🔶 AEW News

Darby Allin completes the New York City Marathon

Darby Allin has added another impressive accomplishment to his growing list of adventures outside the wrestling ring by completing the New York City Marathon. The annual event takes runners through all five boroughs of New York City and featured more than 60,000 participants this year, with Sweden's Andreas Almgren winning the race with a time of two hours and six minutes. Allin participated in the race and completed the 26.2-mile course, officially covering 26 miles and 385 yards with a time of five hours, 34 minutes and 34 seconds, maintaining an average pace of about 12 minutes and 46 seconds per mile.

Reflecting on the grueling experience on social media, Allin shared that he wanted to challenge himself by not training at all to see what would happen. Calling it a wild experience, he immediately looked ahead to his next obligation by declaring it was time for blood and guts.

The achievement is even more staggering given Allin's recent travel schedule and physical punishment. AEW referee Aubrey Edwards highlighted in the social media comments that Allin was in Mexico City just two days prior, wrestling Hechicero on Friday night at Arena Mexico. Edwards expressed utter disbelief, noting that Allin got kicked down the stairs in Mexico and then hopped on a flight a few hours later so that he could achieve another crazy milestone.

This latest feat adds to a unique athletic resume that includes reaching the summit of Mount Everest last year to fulfill a longtime personal goal. Observers noted that while Allin is not the first person to have finished a marathon and climbed Mount Everest, that exclusive club is extremely small. In fact, he is certainly the only man to have done both while also wrestling at Arena Mexico and Wembley Stadium, and holding a major professional wrestling championship.

Allin will not have much time to recover from his marathon endurance test. He is scheduled to return to the ring for the upcoming Blood and Guts special event, where he will team with Steven Borden, The Young Bucks, and Will Ospreay to face the Don Callis Family in the signature match.

Mansoor comments on AEW benched wrestlers taking outside opportunities

During a recent interview with Self Made Pro, AEW star Mansoor shared his thoughts on taking advantage of professional opportunities outside of his primary promotion, particularly addressing wrestlers who find themselves benched or waiting for television time. Reflecting on his past tenure, Mansoor noted that while WWE treated him very well and compensated him extremely well, especially for the Saudi Arabia shows that secured his family's life, sitting at home doing nothing was emotionally grueling. He acknowledged that while not being able to perform comes from a place of privilege, the inability to do what he loves in front of live crowds was incredibly difficult.

Transitioning to his current status, Mansoor stated that there is simply no excuse for wrestlers who are benched in AEW to sit idly. He expressed that if he were single and childless, he would be flying himself all over the world to wrestle on as many independent shows as possible and getting out there. Highlighting his own active schedule, Mansoor revealed that he and his partner Mason were heading to Japan in two days to work for DDT at Ryogoku Sumo Hall, where they would be staying for two extra weeks to do some really cool things over there.

Mansoor also pointed to their earlier international ventures this year, including a successful two-week tour with CMLL in Mexico, which he considers some of the best work of their careers. Furthermore, he praised the unique opportunities afforded to him, such as traveling to India multiple times a year and even being permitted by Tony Khan to promote and book a show for a completely different company in India. Concluding his thoughts, Mansoor emphasized that receiving a guaranteed deal from AEW while still having the freedom to travel, book shows, and work globally is a phenomenal opportunity, noting that it would be absurd for him to complain.

Ric Flair responds to questions about how he is still alive

Ric Flair has the perfect response for anyone wondering how he is still alive after all these years. Having survived decades of brutal wrestling matches, a devastating plane crash, and a life-threatening medical emergency, a recent fall that left him with a serious eye injury is certainly not going to scare the wrestling legend. The two-time WWE Hall of Famer recently shared his candid reaction to the ongoing concerns about his health and longevity.

On October 23, Flair suffered a fall outside a restaurant in Florida. The unfortunate accident left him with serious injuries around his face and eye. He was rushed to Tampa General Hospital for medical treatment and was released the very next day. The incident was particularly concerning because Flair was already dealing with a serious blood clot in his leg, a medical issue that had previously prevented him from flying. On October 28, he revealed to fans that he would require yet another surgery, meaning he still has several medical hurdles to clear before he can completely put this incident behind him.

Taking to social media to address the persistent questions regarding his health following the accident, the seventy-seven-year-old wrestling icon reminded everyone of the incredible punishment his body has absorbed throughout his storied career. Flair posted a message on X highlighting his legendary workload, noting that his career includes five thousand matches, with two thousand four hundred of those being one-hour draws and five being ninety-minute bouts. He also recalled a brutal twenty-four-hour round trip to Japan solely to wrestle three one-hour draws.

Flair went on to reference other major trials in his life, including the infamous 1975 plane crash, thirty-one days spent in the intensive care unit, fourteen days on life support, and thirty-five days in rehab. Concluding his message with his characteristic defiance, he asked his followers if they were seriously worried about him just falling down and hitting his eye.

Along with his statement, Flair included a graphic comparing his staggering career workload with several other big names in the industry, including Brock Lesnar, Triple H, John Cena, and The Undertaker. The comparison heavily emphasized the sheer volume of matches he competed in, serving as a reminder of the physical toll he willingly endured to become one of the most influential performers in professional wrestling history. In 1975, the plane crash left him with a broken back and doctors questioning if he would ever wrestle again, yet he returned to change the industry. This recent health scare and his fiery response also arrive closely on the heels of other discussions surrounding his lifestyle, such as fellow veteran Rob Van Dam recently defending Flair and his drinking habits. Despite his advancing age and ongoing medical challenges, Ric Flair continues to prove that his legendary toughness remains entirely intact.

Thunder Rosa reflects on emotional CMLL Grand Prix appearance

Thunder Rosa has experienced plenty of big moments throughout her wrestling career, but her recent appearance at the CMLL Womens Grand Prix in Mexico hit her differently. The AEW star admitted that she actually broke down in tears while representing her home country, and it all happened before the competition even got underway. The October 23 CMLL Womens Grand Prix featured talent from multiple promotions, with Thunder Rosa competing as part of Team CMLL. The tournament ultimately saw Skadi secure the victory for her team after pinning Megan Bayne, but for Rosa, the experience meant much more than the result.

During an interview, Rosa opened up about what it meant to participate in the Grand Prix for the first time. She revealed that hearing the Mexican national anthem was enough to bring her to tears, especially knowing she was representing her country on such a major stage. Yes, it was. It was very emotional for me, to hear the national anthem. I was actually crying. I said, they actually took a couple of pictures and put me in there. I was really moved because, it was my first time doing the Grand Prix, it just feels so nice to represent my country, and it was weird because I was representing CMLL.

Rosa also explained that representing CMLL initially felt a little unusual, but she quickly reminded herself that she had every reason to be standing alongside the promotions wrestlers because of her own championship status. But at the same time, I have to remember that I am a champion in CMLL, so it only makes sense. So, yeah, it was very special for me.

For Rosa, the Grand Prix wasnt simply another opportunity to compete in Mexico. Getting to represent her country while participating in the event for the first time gave the occasion a personal significance that she clearly wasnt expecting to hit quite so hard. And while Skadi walked away with the decisive victory for Team CMLL, Rosa left with a memory that had nothing to do with wins or losses. Hearing her countrys anthem and realizing what the moment represented was enough to make this one of the more emotional experiences of her wrestling career. Mina Shirakawa comments on confidence without holding a championship

AEW wrestler Mina Shirakawa has opened up about how going months without holding a championship has negatively impacted her confidence. Speaking in a recent interview ahead of her October 30 match at Arena Mexico, Shirakawa expressed genuine surprise when CMLL Japan Women's Champion India Sioux personally selected her as an opponent for the showcase.

Reflecting on her recent career stretch without gold, Shirakawa admitted to questioning her abilities. She explained that because she has not held a title for a long time, she sometimes feels down and wonders what is wrong with herself. However, being specifically chosen by Sioux served as a powerful wake-up call, reminding her that top international talent still wants to step into the ring with her.

Shirakawa noted that someone wanting to wrestle with her meant it was time to shine again all over the world. She viewed the booking as a major opportunity not just for her opponent, but for herself to shine even brighter. Embracing this renewed sense of purpose and vowing to do her absolute best, Shirakawa successfully backed up her words by defeating India Sioux at CMLL's Rey del Inframundo event on October 30.

Will Ospreay credits WCW influence on his wrestling style

AEW World Champion Will Ospreay recently revealed his affinity for one of WCW's top divisions and how it shaped his career. The Aerial Assassin sat down ahead of his Clockwork Carnage match at AEW WrestleDream 2026, where he ultimately successfully defended his gold, and spoke about how he began to hone his craft when he was young.

My mom put a trampoline in my back garden and I used to watch the WCW cruiserweights, Ospreay said. I used to watch them practice their tricks. I would like pause, rewind, see where their arms were and how did they move their bodies? Then I'd go on the trampoline and try it. I got sick of it and my mom saw that I was getting into trouble at school, and on the outside, so she was going, How do I keep him in my garden? How do I keep him safe in the house? Put a wrestling ring in his back garden.

Ospreay joked that one could only imagine how wonderful his family was to all the neighbors, who were hearing all the crashing and banging around of him practicing stuff in the ring. Following his neck surgery in September 2025 that kept him out of the AEW ring for an extended period, Ospreay has had to change up his style a bit, but those changes didn't prevent him from finally capturing the AEW World Championship at Wembley Stadium at AEW All In London 2026.

🔄 המשך AEW Dynamite scheduled not to air head-to-head with WWE SmackDown on New Year's Day

Initial plans pointed toward a direct television clash between the two rival promotions on January 1.

However, that scheduling conflict has been averted. AEW Dynamite will instead air earlier in the day between approximately 3:30 PM and 4:00 PM Eastern Time, taking advantage of a lead-in following the NCAA college football playoffs. This early start ensures the program will conclude well before Friday Night SmackDown begins its broadcast.

The upcoming edition of SmackDown is scheduled to be taped in advance on December 28 in Tampa, Florida, alongside a taping of Monday Night Raw. Meanwhile, the corresponding New Year's Smash episode of Collision is slated for a 7:30 PM Eastern start on January 2, pointing toward its regular 8:00 PM time slot on TNT.

In separate scheduling news, another night of direct competition remains on the calendar as WWE NXT and AEW Dynamite: Blood and Guts are set to face off on Wednesday, November 4, temporarily reviving the Wednesday Night War environment.

🔄 המשך Andrade to defend AEW National Title on Jericho Cruise

The upcoming rematch between Andrade El Idolo and Tommaso Ciampa for the AEW National Championship has received a major location update. AEW announced that the high-stakes title bout will take place on next week's episode of Collision, which will air live from the Chris Jericho Rock 'N' Wrestling Rager at Sea.

Ciampa secured his spot in the rematch by bouncing back from his WrestleDream loss with a victory over Matt Sydal. The seventh iteration of the Jericho Cruise will set sail from Miami to Bimini, Bahamas, running from November 7 through November 11. The special Collision broadcast is scheduled for November 7, with other notable talent such as Chris Jericho, Harley Cameron, Kofi, and Austin Creed also advertised to appear on the floating show.

For the reigning champion, stepping onto the cruise ship will mark his ninth defense of the AEW National Championship. Since capturing the belt by defeating Mark Davis at AEW Redemption, Andrade has successfully turned away challenges from a lengthy list of top contenders including Daniel Garcia, PAC, The Beast Mortos, and Jack Perry.
