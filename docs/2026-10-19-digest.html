Billy Gunn on Early AEW Career Plans and Backstage Drama

Billy Gunn wasn’t supposed to spend nearly as much time wrestling in AEW as he eventually did. In fact, when he first signed with the company, his talent contract only called for 12 wrestling dates for the entire year. Speaking in a recent interview, Gunn explained that his original AEW role was mainly supposed to happen behind the scenes. He had separate talent and employee contracts, with most of his work centered around producing and coaching rather than getting back inside the ring every week. He noted that he really wasn't going to wrestle much, but told Tony Khan that if they needed him to, he would do some here and there. Gunn wasn’t completely closing the door on wrestling, telling Khan he’d be available whenever AEW needed him, which is why the company kept a smaller talent deal in place alongside his regular employee contract.

That arrangement didn’t exactly go according to plan. Instead of occasionally throwing on his gear for one of those 12 dates, Gunn suddenly found himself being used much more regularly. He joked that they worked him constantly across both shows until he felt like he was about to fall apart. Gunn went from somebody AEW expected to use sparingly in the ring to working all the time while still handling his responsibilities behind the scenes. What started as a dozen potential wrestling dates quickly turned into a much bigger in-ring role than he originally signed up for, and his willingness to step in whenever Tony Khan needed him ended up turning those occasional appearances into a regular part of his AEW run.

Shifting to backstage culture, Gunn thinks wrestlers today could do a much better job handling their problems. While discussing the drama surrounding CM Punk’s time in AEW, Gunn compared the current environment to his WWE days and emphasized that you do not have to like somebody to get in the ring and do business with them. Gunn noted that Vince McMahon was very good at keeping backstage issues under control. While there were obviously people who didn’t get along, Gunn remembers most wrestlers being able to put personal feelings aside when it was time to work, estimating that 99.9 percent of the guys always just did business.

Of course, there were still times when things got personal. Gunn said the difference was that those problems weren’t supposed to become everybody else’s issue. If two wrestlers had serious heat, they would be pulled into a room, the door would be shut, and they would deal with it privately. Gunn shared that this is also how he handles things as a coach when he is angry about something a wrestler has done. If he has something harsh to say about coaching and is about to lose his temper, he does not do it in front of the boys, instead taking the person somewhere private because it is nobody else's business.

The goal was never to have an argument, walk away, and stay mad. Gunn explained that if there was a problem, they were supposed to figure it out right there and ensure it was finished before leaving the room so that once they walked out the door, the problem no longer existed. Gunn took a direct shot at how some backstage drama gets handled nowadays, stating that it is sad that modern wrestlers struggle to act like grown-ass people who can confront each other, work through a problem, and move on. Gunn is not saying everybody in a locker room needs to love each other, but his philosophy remains simple: hate each other if you want, but when there is business to do, handle the personal stuff privately and get the job done.

Ringside News

Will Ospreay Childhood Wrestling Influences and Cardio

AEW star Will Ospreay has opened up about the origins of his high-flying wrestling style, revealing how a childhood fascination with WCW cruiserweights and an unusual addition to his family backyard helped shape his professional career. Speaking with WKMG News 6 ClickOrlando, Ospreay discussed the athleticism and endurance required to compete in demanding matches such as AEW Clockwork Carnage. The British star began with a cheeky remark about his conditioning, stating that he is a cardio machine and very good at that, before adding, ask the wife.

Ospreay then explained that his interest in aerial wrestling began when his mother installed a trampoline in their backyard, giving him an opportunity to imitate the moves he watched on television. He recalled watching WCW cruiserweights practice their tricks, noting that he used to pause, rewind, and see where their arms were and how they moved their bodies, and then go on the trampoline and try it.

As his enthusiasm for wrestling continued to grow, Ospreay revealed that his mother became concerned about him getting into trouble at school and on the outside. Her solution to keep him in school, keep him in the garden, and keep him safe in the house was to put a wrestling ring in his back garden. Ospreay joked that the constant noise from his training sessions probably did not make his family particularly popular with their neighbors, who had to hear crash, bang, wallop all the time while he practiced stuff in the ring.

Looking back, Ospreay credited those early experiences with helping him develop the skills that would eventually become central to his professional wrestling career. He explained that this was how he was able to do it, noting that he just had a little bit of a trampoline background, only a small bit, and then brought it into pro wrestling. Ospreay comments offer valuable insight into the foundations of his signature in-ring style, which has become a defining feature of his performances throughout his career.

Max Caster Steps Away from Ring

Former AEW star and former AEW World Tag Team and Trios Champion Max Caster will be stepping away from the squared circle for the foreseeable future, just one week after dropping a controversial freestyle rap promo at TNA Bound for Glory in Tampa, Florida.

Caster was one of the most hyped recruits heading into the pay per view event, where he made his debut answering Moose's open challenge contest and received a warm initial reception from the fans. However, the rap he performed during the appearance contained unapproved lines that made allusions to recent missile attacks that occurred at the King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia.

TNA management, specifically its President Carlos Silva, made the immediate decision to terminate Caster and his pay per appearance deal. TNA released an official statement noting that making light of violent attacks is wrong, accepting responsibility that it should never have made it into the show, and offering sincere apologies to victims, their families, and everyone affected. Two days later, Caster issued a public apology across his social media platforms, admitting that he went too far.

Appearing at Awesome Championship Wrestling's Mischief Night II event on Saturday, Caster addressed the fallout and thanked the fans for their unwavering support during a difficult time. Reflecting on the swift turn of events, Caster stated that in the span of one day, he started a contract and ended a contract with TNA Wrestling. He noted that while he put out a statement and apologized to many people, he wanted to directly address the fans who were disappointed by his departure, while also expressing thanks to Awesome Championship Wrestling for giving him a platform.

Caster then broke the news that he will be taking an indefinite hiatus from the wrestling business to go to therapy and rebuild his life. He told the live crowd that he does not know what his next move is or where he will be, but confirmed he will be stepping away from the ring for an extended period of time. He plans to go home and go to therapy to get things right so that the next time he is in the ring, it feels like home. Caster finished the address with his catchphrase and again indicated he will be out for a long time.

Tommaso Ciampa Discusses Move to AEW

Tommaso Ciampa has only been in All Elite Wrestling for less than one year, but the Psycho Killer has made a huge impact since his arrival back in January. He has won the AEW TNT Championship, challenged for multiple other titles, and featured on some of the promotion's biggest cards this year, but one thing that he has been very happy about since jumping ship from WWE is that he and his family are happy.

During a recent interview, Ciampa explained why making the move from WWE to AEW at the start of the year was something that has opened new opportunities for his family, particularly his daughter Willow. He detailed the amount of times they have traveled together, mentioning trips to London, Scotland, and Montreal. His hope is to get them out to Mexico next time, or even France, because it was so cool out there too, noting that they have been doing a lot of traveling lately.

Ciampa pointed out that Willow and his wife came to Chicago, resulting in a big on-screen moment with Kazuchika Okada. There are so many things that have happened in the last 10 months that would not have happened if they did not make the move when they did. Looking back at it all, he finds it incredible to think of the experiences that they are all gaining, specifically Willow, because a lot of what he does nowadays is pretty much all for her, making the entire journey a total blessing.

The AEW production team went the extra mile to accommodate Ciampa's family at AEW All Out 2026. Not only were his wife and daughter at ringside for his match against Kazuchika Okada, but before the event began, Willow received her own special entrance to celebrate her birthday, complete with her dad's current entrance music, before transitioning into a rendition of Happy Birthday.

🔄 המשך 🔥 AEW WrestleDream Reviews and Discussion

Following the chaotic main event at WrestleDream, Will Ospreay took to social media to reflect on the inaugural Clockwork Carnage match. Ospreay, who pitched the original idea for the bout, thanked fans for their response and expressed gratitude toward Tony Khan and the promotion for putting faith in an unprecedented concept.

The match itself saw Ospreay successfully defend his championship against a star-studded field including Samoa Joe, Speedball Mike Bailey, Konosuke Takeshita, Darby Allin, Adam Copeland, Ricochet, and Kazuchika Okada. Bailey and Takeshita were eliminated early before all competitors entered, and the frantic finish saw Adam Copeland surge late before Ospreay landed a decisive hidden blade to secure the victory with seconds remaining.

Beyond the main event, the rest of the WrestleDream card delivered plenty of fallout and action. Jon Moxley defended his Continental Championship in an emotional war against Mark Davis, while Hangman Page and Swerve Strickland reignited their bitter rivalry alongside their trios partners. Other matches featured Andrade El Idolo putting his National Championship and his pants on the line against Tomasso Ciampa, Persephone defending the TBS Championship in a four-way match, and the Young Bucks defending the World Tag Team Championships against Kyle Fletcher and Kevin Knight.

Additionally, Mercedes Mone defended the AEW Women's World Championship against Thekla, Nick Wayne renewed his rivalry with Chris Jericho, and the Motor City Machine Guns squared off with FTR to kick off a new best-of-five series. The event also featured a major surprise debut, with former WWE standout Ilja Dragunov making his first appearance for the company.

🔄 המשך 🔥 Kairi Sane AEW Signing Rumors and Delay

While the ink is still not dry on a contract, a clearer picture is forming around why Kairi Sane has yet to officially debut for the promotion.

According to recent updates, the holdup is not due to a breakdown in negotiations, but rather immigration paperwork. She is reportedly waiting for her U.S. work visa to be approved before she can move forward. This logistical hurdle explains how she remains linked to the company without an official signing, and it could also account for her slated appearance on Chris Jericho's upcoming wrestling cruise, as international waters present different visa requirements than domestic events.

Tony Khan recently addressed her free agent status during a post-show media scrum. While avoiding specifics on ongoing negotiations, Khan openly praised her abilities, calling her a fantastic wrestler and admitting he is very intrigued by the possibility of bringing her into the fold.

Additionally, while light discussions with the company remain active, those talks are currently holding up substantial interest and potential bookings from promotions in Japan. Until her visa clears and a formal deal is locked in, her exact timeline for stepping into an American ring remains on hold.
