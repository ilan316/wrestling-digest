Chris Jericho cast as Gorgeous George in Muhammad Ali series

Current AEW star Chris Jericho will be appearing on the small screen in an acting role once again, playing a part he is familiar with and portraying someone many wrestling fans know well. Deadline reported that Jericho will play the legendary Gorgeous George in the upcoming Prime Video series chronicling the life of boxer and mainstream icon Muhammad Ali. The full eight-episode series comes out on Wednesday, November 4, with Jericho s appearance coming in the fourth episode. Jaalen Best portrays Ali in the production.

George debuted in 1932 and retired in 1962, becoming famous for his flamboyance, swagger, and promo ability. The two men met in 1961, with Ali taking inspiration from the wrestling star on how to rile up crowds and adopting a style of reciting poems and phrases that eventually caught on in mainstream pop culture.

The series, titled The Greatest, features defining moments in Ali s life and career, both in and out of the ring, that have been well documented, as well as moments that took place outside the spotlight. Producers note that while the world remembers an icon, the project is ultimately about a man, a husband, a father, a brother, and a son.

Jericho has made several television appearances outside the wrestling ring recently. Earlier this year, he appeared in CBS Tracker and Apple TV Margo s Got Money Troubles, and he is also set to make an appearance in the second season of Dexter Resurrection this fall.

Judge denies AEW and Jon Moxley request to move production worker lawsuit

AEW and Jon Moxley have suffered a legal setback in an ongoing civil lawsuit filed by a former production worker. A judge has denied a request from AEW and Moxley to transfer the lawsuit filed by Christopher Dispensa from Michigan to Florida. Wayne County Judge Catherine Heise denied the request in an October 5 order following a September 17 hearing over where the lawsuit should actually play out. The case is staying in Detroit because that is where the whole thing allegedly happened.

AEW and Moxley had argued for Florida because Dispensa lives there, most of his treating physicians reside there, and AEW is headquartered there, making it more practical to handle the case in a Florida court. However, the judge saw things differently. The alleged incident happened during the May 10, 2023 episode of AEW Dynamite at Little Caesars Arena in Detroit, and several potential witnesses are also in the local area. Judge Heise pointed out that the courthouse handling the lawsuit is within healthy walking distance of Little Caesars Arena, while any doctors in Florida can simply testify remotely. Furthermore, keeping the case in Michigan avoids the issue of a Florida court having to sort through Michigan law over an event that happened hundreds of miles away.

There was also the issue of timing, as AEW and Moxley did not file their motion asking to move the case until July 7, nearly 10 months after AEW answered Dispensa's complaint in September 2025. The defendants tried to address potential complications by stating they would not use Florida's three-year statute of limitations as a defense if the case was moved, but Heise was not willing to gamble on whether a Florida court would actually honor such an agreement or an order from a Michigan court.

Dispensa originally sued AEW and Moxley over an incident that allegedly happened during Moxley's match against Kenny Omega on Dynamite. Dispensa, who worked on AEW's production crew, claims Moxley went away from what had been planned and shoved him to the floor. He alleges that the shove left him with serious neck and shoulder injuries that eventually required cervical spine and shoulder surgery. His lawsuit includes assault and battery claims against Moxley, negligence and gross negligence claims against Moxley and AEW, along with negligent hiring, training, supervision, and retention claims against the company. This ruling does not mean Dispensa has won any of those claims, and it does not decide whether Moxley or AEW actually did anything wrong. It just means AEW and Moxley did not get the change of scenery they wanted, so the legal fight stays in Michigan.

There could still be a way for everyone to avoid taking this all the way to a jury. Another settlement conference is scheduled for December 22 after the two sides previously met on July 29 and left those discussions held for further settlement. An initial settlement conference was held in July, but the lawsuit remains unresolved. For now, AEW and Moxley are stuck fighting this one in Detroit, but the actual allegations at the center of the lawsuit still have to be sorted out as the case moves either toward a deal or closer to a jury hearing the whole story.

His original operation, where the shunt was inserted, took place in August to help with balance issues. However, he subsequently began to experience blistering headaches, and a CT scan revealed bleeding that necessitated a second operation. Falls were a significant issue for Ross before the shunt was inserted, so he is eager to get things resolved and have the shunt turned back on.

Amid these considerable health challenges, Ross has expressed deep gratitude. Being in and out of constant doctor's appointments has shown him that, no matter what he is going through, there is always someone facing an even more difficult battle. Swerve Strickland claims he performs at highest level for a Black wrestler in history

AEW star Swerve Strickland has established himself as one of the most popular characters in the promotion since its inception in 2019, despite not officially signing with the company until 2022. Prior to his time in AEW, Strickland was already a heavily decorated champion who held gold across numerous prominent promotions, including CZW, wXw, DEFY, MLW, and WWE NXT.

In a recent interview with Billboard, Strickland discussed his multifaceted career as both a professional wrestler and a hip-hop artist, noting how he continues to grow and find new successes in both fields. He shared with the outlet that when future generations look back on his journey in professional wrestling, they will recognize that his in-ring ability was the primary driving force behind his rise.

Strickland emphasized that his accomplishments are not the result of being a caricature of hip-hop culture or a stereotypical Black wrestler. Instead, he attributes his success to simply being himself and performing at an elite level. He stated that his push in the industry is not about a company trying to push an agenda, but rather because he performs at a remarkably high level, asserting that it is probably the highest level ever seen from a Black wrestler in the history of the business.

While acknowledging that many Black talents became massive household names in professional wrestling eras past, such as Dwayne The Rock Johnson and Booker T, Strickland pointed out that those performers could rarely be described as the absolute best in-ring workers on their respective shows. He explained that this was largely due to the legendary competition they faced at the time, going up against icons like Shawn Michaels and Eddie Guerrero.

In contrast, Strickland feels confident that his in-ring work stands shoulder to shoulder with anyone in AEW today. Listing top-tier opponents such as Kazuchika Okada, Kenny Omega, Will Ospreay, and Hangman Adam Page, Strickland concluded that he must remain as pure and genuine to himself as possible, reaching the pinnacle of the industry through actual wrestling ability rather than relying merely on backstage co-signs.

The Hurt Syndicate allegedly pushed back against putting wrestlers over in AEW

The Hurt Syndicate has found itself at the center of backstage discussions following reports of creative friction within All Elite Wrestling. Questions regarding the strange booking and inconsistent television presence of Bobby Lashley, Shelton Benjamin, and MVP have persisted, especially as their presence has cooled off significantly in recent months. He confirmed that there have indeed been situations where members of the faction did not want to lose to certain opponents.

While Sapp noted they have not refused every creative idea thrown their way, he acknowledged that creative pushback has been a tangible issue. Sapp did not identify which specific members were involved, who management wanted them to put over, or whether these disagreements directly caused their recent lack of television time, leaving several aspects of the situation unknown.

This update follows a noticeable decline in their television prominence. Another recent report suggested fans should not expect that dynamic to change anytime soon, claiming the group would appear intermittently on Collision for random things rather than returning as a serious weekly act. The report went further by arguing that The Hurt Syndicate run as a major act has essentially already ended in real terms, even if the faction has not officially broken up, noting that a lot of people runs end way before they actually end.

For a group loaded with seasoned veterans like Lashley, Benjamin, and MVP, protecting their credibility clearly matters immensely. However, the larger question facing the promotion is how to find meaningful opponents and storylines for The Hurt Syndicate if there are certain finishes and outcomes the group is ultimately unwilling to accept.
