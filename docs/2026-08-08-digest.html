🔥 Jim Ross Brain Surgery and AEW Contract

Jim Ross Scheduled for Brain Surgery as AEW Contract Expires

The legendary professional wrestling announcer Jim Ross revealed during the latest episode of his Grilling JR podcast that he will undergo brain surgery on Wednesday, August 12, in Norman, Oklahoma. The procedure comes after Ross spent more than a month hospitalized between May and June, and doctors will be inserting a shunt to help drain excess fluid from his brain.

"I'm good, man. I got a big week coming up," Ross said at the beginning of the episode, which was recorded in front of a live audience. "My surgery is Wednesday in Norman, so I don't want to say I'm looking forward to it, but I guess I really am, to get to that point and get it over with."

The 74-year-old expressed surprise at how quickly he will be able to leave the hospital following the procedure. "So I have never had brain surgery before. So they're going to put in what they call a shunt. So I'm getting a shunt put in my brain, and they said I'm only going to be in the hospital a day. So that surprised me and made me happy, but still, I would have guessed longer."

Despite the seriousness of undergoing brain surgery, Ross recorded the full Grilling JR episode and spent approximately 90 minutes answering questions from the audience. One consequence of the procedure is that Ross does not expect to be able to travel immediately afterward, which may prevent him from attending the funeral services of legendary wrestler Dory Funk Jr., who died on Tuesday at the age of 85.

"I don't know when the funeral services are going to be. I don't know if I'll be able to go. My surgery is, as I said, it's going to be next Wednesday," Ross explained. "So me travelling is probably not going to be very likely, so I just hope I walk out of the hospital healthy."

Co-host Conrad Thompson expressed optimism that the procedure will help improve the memory and balance issues Ross has been experiencing. Ross remained cautiously optimistic about the outlook, responding, "Well, let's hope so. We'll see. I hope so too, Connie. Quite frankly, I hope so."

Prior to his surgery, Ross made a trip to Minneapolis for an appearance at WrestleCon during WWE SummerSlam weekend. While he enjoyed the event, he acknowledged that extended appearances have become more taxing on him at this stage of his career. "I hate to sound soft, but, you know, you gotta be a character and you gotta be nice to people, because that's the only way to do it. It puts a little pressure on you, but I had a good time. But I've come to the conclusion that doing four-hour shifts is a little more than I want to handle."

Beyond the immediate health concerns, Ross addressed the status of his broadcasting career as his AEW contract approaches expiration this month. The legendary announcer has not yet signed a new deal with the promotion. When asked about the end of his career, Ross emphasized that he hopes for a planned conclusion rather than an abrupt departure from the industry that has defined his life.

"What I would like, I hope that my ending, my career ending, is planned, and so I just don't show up one Wednesday night on Dynamite and say, well, I got some news for you folks, I'm done. That might sound cool and edgy, but I would like to be able to know that I'm leaving. I think that's important for my psyche," Ross explained.

After more than four decades as one of professional wrestling's most recognizable broadcasters, Ross acknowledged that there is little left for him to accomplish in the industry. "Just have a good, clean ending, and if I could pull that off, then I'll be. I've done everything else. I've done every major event for every major promoter," he said.

Ross has dealt with a number of health issues in recent years while continuing to work when possible and regularly keeping listeners updated on his condition. He reflected on his overall career achievements and expressed contentment with what he has already received.

"I've had a blessed career. You know, I've been battling some health issues. I'm getting a shunt put in my brain on Wednesday, so we'll see how that works out, no pun intended. It'll have had a unique life, and I realize that it's coming to an end. I get it," Ross said.

When discussing his AEW contract status specifically, Ross was pragmatic about the possibility that Khan may not renew his agreement. "I think my contract's up this month, and so I'm done, just pretty much. I don't want to be done. But if Tony Khan said, you know, JR, thanks for the efforts and thanks for being here and all that stuff, then I'd have no problem with that."

Ross stressed that he would harbor no resentment toward Khan if AEW decided his current contract should be his last. He emphasized that his already extraordinary career makes it difficult for him to feel entitled to anything more. "Because how can I be such a greedy bastard and want more in my career than I've already received? Sometimes you stumble onto a great job and a great career. My career as a wrestling broadcaster has been nothing but wonderful."

Previously in May, Ross had revealed that his AEW contract would expire in August. At that time, he said he had discussed the situation with his agent, Barry Bloom, and was leaving the decision in Khan's hands. He expressed hope that he would remain with AEW but said he was no longer worrying about his contractual status.

Ross remains AEW's senior commentator, although his appearances have become more sporadic in recent years. His most recent commentary appearance came at Double or Nothing in May, where he called Jon Moxley's Continental Championship match against Kyle O'Reilly.

Ross has been with AEW since the promotion launched in 2019. He initially took on a full-time commentary role before transitioning to his current part-time duties, assisting with commentary at pay-per-view events. His health issues have limited his availability over the past several years.

Prior to joining AEW, Ross had a storied career with WWE dating back to 1993, where he eventually rose to the position of Executive Vice President of Talent Relations before being released in 2013. He briefly returned to WWE in 2017 on a two-year deal that saw him return to commentary duties in a limited capacity before leaving the company once again.

The wrestling industry continues to await an announcement regarding Ross's future with AEW following his surgery and as his contract expires this month. Whatever the outcome, Ross has made clear that he hopes for a dignified and planned conclusion to his legendary broadcasting career.

Claudio Castagnoli on Influencers in Wrestling

Claudio Castagnoli explains why influencers in wrestling are part of the business

Celebrities have long been integrated into professional wrestling, and AEW's Claudio Castagnoli recently provided insight into why they remain a necessary component of the industry. While fans have frequently criticized WWE for their extensive use of celebrities and influencers on their programming, AEW has also regularly featured celebrities on their television shows. Despite this, AEW's incorporation of celebrity talent still lags significantly behind WWE, though Castagnoli believes that the addition of these outside personalities brings valuable new audiences to wrestling.

Speaking to Sky Sports, Castagnoli acknowledged that celebrity involvement in wrestling is not a recent development. He pointed to historical examples of prominent figures crossing over into the sport. "This is not a brand new thing. There was always maybe celebrities or maybe football, like in the past was more like football players, for example, or bodybuilders or stuff like that," he explained. "Now it's influencers because of how everything evolved."

Castagnoli emphasized that while celebrity appearances serve a business purpose by attracting fresh viewership and maintaining audience interest, the fundamental nature of professional wrestling must not be compromised. "As much as that is part of the business because it brings a new audience in and it keeps things, I guess, interesting, I feel the core of the wrestling business is still wrestling, and you shouldn't lose that," he concluded.

WWE has recently featured several high-profile celebrities in their programming, including rapper Jelly Roll, musician Travis Scott, and professional boxer Ryan Garcia, among others. AEW's recent celebrity appearances have included collaborations with TikTok personalities A.J. & Big Justice and The Rizzler, as well as other entertainment figures.

With Wyatt already in the ring and Undertaker waiting in the Gorilla position for his entrance, Triple H noticed something was wrong. "I think he could sense it, that I was struggling." Undertaker recalled being crouched in the corner when Triple H quietly approached him. "He just comes up and he goes, 'Hey,' he says, 'Don't forget who the f*** you are. You're the f***ing Undertaker.'"

For Undertaker, that was all he needed. "It was like it was just enough to like, you know what, you're right, you know, I've done this a million times, everything's gonna be fine." He went through the curtain, defeated Wyatt and proved to himself that the previous year's concussion had not marked the end of his career.
