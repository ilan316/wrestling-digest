🔥 Will Ospreay and the United Empire name rights in AEW

Will Ospreay has brought the United Empire name into All Elite Wrestling, and a new report has shed light on who actually controls the name, putting Ospreay firmly in the driver's seat. According to reports, Ospreay owns the rights to the United Empire name after filing a trademark for it in April 2026. He reportedly received legal assistance from trademark attorney Michael Dockins when registering the name.

The report states that Ospreay has the freedom to use United Empire wherever he chooses, including AEW. That makes the situation different from Bullet Club Gold, which led to AEW using the Bang Bang Gang name because NJPW owns the Bullet Club trademark. Sources familiar with the situation confirmed Ospreay controls the name, which he trademarked earlier this year.

Ospreay announced on the September 2 episode of AEW Dynamite that he was creating an AEW version of United Empire. Speedball Mike Bailey and Francesco Akira emerged as the other names connected to the expansion, with Bailey becoming the latest addition in AEW. The faction's AEW presence also includes Andrade El Idolo, who has a guaranteed title opportunity that he can cash in on one week's notice.

Meanwhile, the original Japan chapter remains led by Callum Newman and includes Akira, Great-O-Khan, HENARE, Jake Lee, Jakob Austin Young and Zane Jay. Ospreay, O-Khan and HENARE currently hold the NEVER Openweight 6-Man Tag Team Championship. Ospreay is also scheduled to return to NJPW for King of Pro-Wrestling on October 12, where the trio will defend the titles.

With Ospreay controlling the United Empire name, AEW can continue building its version of the faction without needing NJPW to hand over the keys. For now, the empire has more than one address, and Ospreay owns the sign.

Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery merger impact on AEW

The massive Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery merger has cleared its final legal hurdle, a development with obvious relevance to All Elite Wrestling. U.S. District Judge Araceli Martinez-Olguin has approved the settlement between Paramount and a group of state Attorneys General who previously sued to block the merger. With the settlement approved, the deal is now on track to formally close as soon as next week. Paramount has been preparing for this final stretch by launching a major bond offering to help finance the acquisition while executives pitch six billion dollars in cost savings as part of its strategy to manage debt. Leadership changes are already underway behind the scenes as well, including the departure of Paramount streaming chief Cindy Holland ahead of the merger, with more executive shakeups expected and leadership teams slated to be announced before the deal officially closes.

For AEW, there are no immediate changes to its television or streaming agreements resulting from the corporate shift. The company remains securely under a multi-year media rights deal with Warner Bros. Discovery, with Dynamite and Collision airing on TBS and TNT while also streaming on HBO Max. Additionally, AEW pay-per-view events continue to be distributed through HBO Max.

The merger is nevertheless profoundly noteworthy for the wrestling business because Paramount already has extensive ties to TKO Group Holdings, the parent company of both WWE and the UFC. Paramount previously entered into a seven-year agreement with TKO that will make Paramount+ the exclusive United States home of the UFC beginning in 2026, including all numbered events and Fight Nights, alongside select events airing on CBS. Once the merger closes, AEW primary media partner will therefore be controlled by a company that also maintains a major long-term relationship with WWE corporate sibling. There has been no indication that the merger will alter AEW existing agreements, but the corporate alignment places the promotion in a unique position within the broader sports and entertainment ecosystem.

Bryan Danielson comments on social media usage

AEW star Bryan Danielson recently opened up about his total lack of interest in social media, admitting that he simply cannot understand why anyone would want to use these platforms. Social media remains a complicated tool in the modern era, acting as a boon for tracking current affairs while frequently proving to be a nightmare for celebrities.

During a recent appearance on the Nikki and Brie show alongside his wife Brie Bella, Danielson discussed his detachment from the digital world. The wrestling star claimed he is rarely on social media and revealed that someone else has handled his accounts for a long time, even dating back to before he was retired. I only get on social media when somebody at the company I work for gets in trouble, Danielson wrote. When Brie Bella asked about the last time he was on social media, Danielson responded that he does not even know, adding that a person named Meech used to run his accounts.

Describing how it feels to live largely offline, Danielson admitted it is great, largely because he has never been deep in the social media game. I can not even understand why people would want to be on social media, it makes no sense to me, Danielson responded. While acknowledging that the platforms serve as a business tool, he questioned the motivations of everyday users who are not promoting products. I mean, it is a business tool, but what about the people who are not selling people things? Like, there are people who are selling things and there are people who are being sold things. And then there is really it is just the attention economy, right? As far as these platforms go, he finished.

Aside from his digital habits, Danielson has also been making headlines in the squared circle. Having been away from in-ring action for months now, Danielson recently made his in-ring return on an AEW Dynamite PAC tribute show, where he competed against members of The Don Callis Family.

WWE stars to reform tag team: Three reunions that could boost the division

With Raw’s World Tag Team Titles going almost three months without a defense and the SmackDown tag team scene feeling just about as lifeless, it is evident that WWE needs to invest a lot more energy in the division. The work required goes beyond just adding new teams, but some fresh blood certainly wouldn't hurt. Thankfully for WWE, they have several teams already signed to the company, so they wouldn't even need to open their check book to make positive changes. Here are three former tag teams to potentially reform in WWE.

We start the list with a pairing that came together in All Elite Wrestling rather than WWE: Ricky Saints and Big Cass. The former Ricky Starks and Big Bill captured the AEW World Tag Team Titles in October 2023 and held them until February the following year. Much like Cass' time with Enzo Amore, he and Saints complimented each other, with Cass acting as a perfect protector for the loud-mouthed New Orleans native. With both Cass and Saints struggling a bit on their own in WWE, they would be wise to revisit this team as soon as possible.

Josh Briggs and Brooks Jensen were a foundational part of NXT's tag team division between 2021 and 2023, with the pair holding the NXT UK Tag Team Titles for just under three months in 2022 and putting up many challenges for the NXT Tag Titles in that time. They never quite reached the top of the brand's tag division, and as singles stars they have certainly struggled to find the same amount of success. Briggs hasn't been seen on NXT since April, but did recently make his in-ring return on WWE Main Event against Big Cass, so there's perhaps some reason to be hopeful for a return. Jensen hasn't had a layoff like Briggs, instead competing across Evolve, Main Event, and NXT live events, but he hasn't been featured regularly on TV for quite some time. Bringing the pair back together would be mutually beneficial, whether back in NXT or on the main roster. If it's the latter then WWE would have to invest some time in their characters, perhaps even giving them a full repackaging. Either way though, they are two very talented in-ring performers and should be utilized somewhere in the company.

After a first year back in WWE where he failed to really find his feet, Rusev saw his fortunes turn around when he began teaming with All Ego Ethan Page. The pair of former AEW stars made for an entertaining odd couple, and their union allowed the Bulgarian to show more of his personality that we all know is there from his first WWE run. Unfortunately this run was derailed when Rusev suffered an injury in late July, but he has recently been cleared to return according to his wife CJ Perry. Page is currently hunting Chad Gable's Intercontinental Championship and has a big match with Rey Fenix set for next week's Raw on October 5. Rusev returning in this match and helping Page win would be an excellent way to reintroduce him to TV and get his allegiance with Page back underway.

A hot tag to Hangman Page shifted the tide, allowing a fired-up Omega to enter the fray and take down Knight with a series of suplexes and a stunning mid-air V-Trigger.

Down the stretch, The Elite overwhelmed their opponents with a flurry of signature tandem offense. Page and Omega combined for a devastating Buckshot and V-Trigger combination before the Young Bucks followed up with a BTE Trigger. Omega ultimately sealed the victory by hitting the One-Winged Angel on Beretta, securing an emotional win dedicated to their fallen friend.
