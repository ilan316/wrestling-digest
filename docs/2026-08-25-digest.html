🔥 Will Ospreay discusses AEW All In main event plans, match against Kenny Omega, and special gear

Will Ospreay has confirmed that AEW considered several possible directions for the World Championship picture heading into the 2026 All In pay-per-view before ultimately settling on his highly anticipated showdown with Kenny Omega. Ahead of challenging Omega for the AEW World Championship at Wembley Stadium, Ospreay discussed the road to the marquee event. Discussions included potential matches involving MJF or Jon Moxley, and even a possible triple-threat match, confirming that multiple options were on the table before the company landed on the match.

Ultimately, Ospreay believes putting him against Omega was the most natural and right choice because they have been preaching to the world that this is where the best wrestle. Ospreay noted that he and Omega are two of the most influential wrestlers of the last decade, pointing to performers like Callum Newman and Nick Wayne who took inspiration from them and followed in their footsteps.

Ospreay also reflected on the different stories AEW has presented in previous All In main events. He contrasted his upcoming match with the humorous build of MJF and Adam Cole, noting it was more to engage the audience than a full sports contest. He then mentioned the crowning of Bryan Danielson against Swerve Strickland and the story they told, followed by the guts and drive of Hangman Page trying to strip away Jon Moxley's championship. In contrast, Ospreay views his match with Omega as two of the greatest wrestlers of this generation going head-to-head to finally find out who is the best.

When Ospreay steps into Wembley Stadium for the August 30 event, he will be sporting special ring gear to represent his home country. Ospreay is moving away from his traditional green color scheme and wearing red and white gear modeled after England's historic 1966 World Cup victory, which remains the only time England has won the tournament by defeating West Germany in the final at Wembley. Additionally, Ospreay joked that the number of friends and family attending has gone way and truly over the usual allotment of complimentary tickets.

Beyond potentially capturing the title on home soil, Ospreay has ideas about what he would like to accomplish with the championship. He has frequently approached AEW President Tony Khan with ideas aimed at incorporating a legitimate sports atmosphere into professional wrestling. Inspired by the World Cup, Ospreay wants to show there are different ways to present sports and wrestling in that sphere. If he is lucky enough to win the World Championship at Wembley, his legacy will be to create something no other wrestler has done before and bring back a sports-film mentality. Ospreay returned to the ring earlier this year after undergoing neck surgery in late 2025 and now prepares for the Sunday main event.

🔥 Darby Allin discusses history with Kevin Knight, AEW title reign, and future goals

In a wide-ranging interview, Darby Allin previewed his upcoming Falls Count Anywhere match against TNT Champion Kevin Knight at AEW All In: London at Wembley Stadium. The two have been locked in a fierce feud since Knight turned heel on Allin at the end of Double or Nothing, where Knight also recaptured the AEW World Title from Allin following a short reign. Allin detailed his history with Knight, noting that they both came up through the Buddy Wayne Academy in Washington. Allin recalled meeting Knight when Knight had only been training for two months while Allin was already five years into his career. Recognizing his potential, Allin kept an eye on him, noting that Knight has now reached the same career crossroads Allin faced when he won the TNT Championship in 2020. However, unlike Allin, who refused outside help to reach the top, Knight joined the Don Callis Family and went a different route, prompting Allin to state that it is time to humble him in front of everybody and show him that his foundation will not last. Their Wembley clash follows escalating tensions, including Knight retaining his title over Allin on Dynamite by hitting him with the belt and later injuring Allin with exploding pyro that required him to be stretchered out of the arena.

Allin also reflected on his monumental AEW World Championship victory at Dynamite: Spring BreakThru on April 15, which lasted less than 40 days before he lost it back to MJF at Double or Nothing. Despite the locker room coming out to the ring to celebrate his victory, Allin revealed that he felt completely numb at the time because his body was so depleted and beat into the ground from the strenuous buildup. Once he got to the back and decompressed, he realized he had climbed to the top of AEW without compromising himself, emphasizing that becoming a world champion loses its meaning if a wrestler is merely an industry plant whose work is not pure and from the soul.

Reflecting on his future goals and enduring approach to wrestling, Allin explained that he has no plans to slow down despite calls from others to do so, stating that he physically and mentally cannot change. Every time he steps into the ring, even for a short match, he goes ballistic because he refuses to take anything for granted. He credits Sting as a major inspiration, pointing to how Sting handled his 2014 injury and eventually returned to wrestling to go out on his own terms. Allin aims to lead by example and do things his own way without ever phoning it in. Beyond in-ring style, Allin noted that the most important lessons he learned from Sting involved how to carry oneself outside the ring, praising the legend for remaining humble, chill, and devoid of ego despite achieving everything in the industry.

Looking back at the broader landscape of the company, Allin discussed signing with AEW as one of its original talents in 2019. He observed that while many early wrestlers had little TV experience and sometimes treated the promotion like a fun spring break job with a lifetime guarantee, the bar has now been set much higher. With the talent pool and television standards significantly elevated, Allin believes nobody can coast or take their position for granted, ensuring that only the absolute best make it onto TV as AEW continues to shape the professional wrestling landscape.
