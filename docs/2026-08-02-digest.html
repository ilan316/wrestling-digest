Tay Melo requests AEW release

Tay Melo has officially parted ways with AEW after requesting her release from the company. AEW President and CEO Tony Khan confirmed that Melo approached him following her match on Collision and asked to be released, explaining that she wanted to step away from professional wrestling. Khan said he honored her request.

Since then, however, there have been conflicting interpretations regarding the circumstances surrounding Melo's departure. The uncertainty intensified after Melo shared a post on Instagram about a tree that struggled while confined to a pot but flourished once planted in the ground. Some fans viewed the message as a metaphor for a fresh start, while others believed it reflected a personal decision, noting that Melo and her husband, Sammy Guevara, have a young daughter and suggesting she may be choosing to focus on family life instead.

According to multiple sources, the wording of Melo's post has also fueled another theory. The carefully worded and somewhat cryptic message gave the impression that Melo may still want to wrestle, but perhaps outside of AEW after becoming frustrated with a lack of opportunities. While it was stressed that Melo never explicitly stated that was the case, the wording left room for that interpretation. Dave Meltzer pointed out in the Wrestling Observer Newsletter that her choice of words implied she wished to wrestle elsewhere rather than step away from the profession entirely.

Khan maintained that the situation has been exaggerated. He said Melo told him she was not attempting to push that narrative about wanting to wrestle elsewhere, though he stopped short of directly disputing the speculation itself. As a result, questions remain about Melo's future and whether her departure truly marks the end of her wrestling career or simply the end of her time in AEW.

AEW Redemption buy rate

AEW Redemption appears to be heading toward becoming AEW's worst-performing pay-per-view of 2026 based on current buy rate projections. According to Dave Meltzer's reporting in the Wrestling Observer Newsletter, early estimates currently place the July 26 event between 115,000 and 125,000 buys, significantly lower than other 2026 pay-per-views.

For context, AEW Dynasty generated approximately 145,000 buys, while Forbidden Door trended slightly below that figure, making Redemption's current projection notably lower if the estimates hold. Despite the disappointing buy rate projection, several other metrics surrounding the event performed surprisingly well. Meltzer noted that early crowd reaction numbers for the show itself were surprisingly positive. Additionally, Google search interest increased substantially, up 41 percent domestically and 25 percent worldwide compared to Forbidden Door. Poll responses also showed improvement, tracking 16.5 percent higher than the San Jose event.

The critical factor dragging down the overall projection appears to be streaming performance. HBO MAX and other streaming platform numbers reportedly underperformed compared to both Forbidden Door and Dynasty, which were previously among the year's lowest performers. However, Meltzer expressed some optimism about the final numbers, noting that the positive indicators across early reviews, Google searches, and poll responses have historically been good predictors of overall performance and may result in final numbers closer to Forbidden Door and Dynasty.

The lower buy rate was partially anticipated by industry observers. The once-planned Kenny Omega versus MJF match, which was originally scheduled for the event, was moved up to Beach Break television programming instead. Kevin Knight received the AEW World title match against Omega following Omega's victory over MJF. Additionally, several major AEW stars were absent from the Montreal card, including Swerve Strickland, Mercedes Mone, Kazuchika Okada, Konosuke Takeshita, Hangman Page, Darby Allin, Mark Briscoe, Mistico, FTR, and Ricochet. Scorpio Sky recalls learning about AEW

Scorpio Sky Recalls First Learning About The Existence Of AEW

With new years typically come new opportunities. For Tony Khan and crew, the sentiment rang especially true when they unveiled All Elite Wrestling, a pro wrestling company aimed at being an alternative to the industry-juggernaut of WWE, in January 2019.

During an interview with Tagging In with Chris Harris, AEW original Scorpio Sky recalled learning about the potential existence of AEW in the midst of a car ride with The Young Bucks, Matt and Nick Jackson. "I heard about it far before the public did," Sky said. "I was on the ground floor. I remember being in the car with the Young Bucks where Matt is like, 'Hey, I got a message from this guy named Tony Khan. He wants to start a wrestling company.' I was, 'Okay, sure.' He's like, 'Yeah, I don't know. It's probably not real, but he seems legit.' I was there and watched it all progress from just a few words to where it is now."

Sky went on to emphasize the significance of those formative moments. "Those early days are irreplaceable," he continued. "That was some of the most fun times I've had in my entire career. I'll cherish those first few years. I'll think about those for the rest of my life. People are going to write books about that. What more can you say about that?"

The initial announcement of AEW's launch came in a special Being The Elite episode that dropped on New Year's Day 2019. AEW talent and executive signings were made public in the weeks following, with Khan, a figure in the NFL's Jacksonville Jaguars franchise, being named as the AEW CEO and President. Cody Rhodes and The Young Bucks emerged as EVPs and in-ring wrestlers for the brand. Sky, amongst other names, were revealed as wrestlers for the promotion as well.

Sky described the atmosphere of AEW's launch using a sports analogy. "It was like an expansion team in sports," Sky said. "We all kind of came together and we felt like underdogs. When I say expansion team, there was really a team environment. It genuinely felt like we're all in this together, the odds are against us, and we're all working towards the greater good. That was awesome. It was such a cool, exciting feeling. For years after WCW closed, I'm sure every wrestler had this thought of 'Boy, I sure wish some billionaire would get into wrestling and open another wrestling company, but that'll never happen.' We always made that joke, and then it happened."

With a roster stacked with talents such as The Young Bucks, Dustin and Cody Rhodes, Kenny Omega, the Lucha Brothers, and Britt Baker, AEW hosted its first pay-per-view event, Double or Nothing, in May 2019. AEW Dynamite, its flagship television show, then premiered in October, the same month in which Sky and Frankie Kazarian, known as SoCal Uncensored, cemented themselves as the inaugural AEW Tag Team Champions.

Fast forward to the present day, Sky is reportedly approaching the end of a five-year contract with the promotion, with his future plans being held close to the vest. In addition to the Tag Team Championships, he has held the TNT Championship twice in his AEW career.

TRIPLE H ADDRESSES BROCK LESNAR'S IN-RING FUTURE AFTER WWE SUMMERSLAM

At the SummerSlam Saturday post-show press conference, Triple H was asked about Brock Lesnar's in-ring status following his Hell in a Cell match against Oba Femi. Lesnar lost the match and afterward called Femi the future of the business in a passing of the torch moment.

Triple H stated that he doesn't know whether Lesnar will continue wrestling. He acknowledged that Lesnar retired a few months ago, came back, and then faced Femi at SummerSlam. According to Triple H, the fact that Brock walked back to the ring to bring Oba back in there with him and put him over both verbally and physically as "the future of the business" represents a pretty bold statement. Triple H noted that Brock doesn't just say things, and when Lesnar said "I am the past," that carries significant weight.

Triple H emphasized that Brock does what Brock wants to do, so fans should take what he said at face value and see what happens next. At the same time, Triple H praised Lesnar's performance and the emergence of Oba Femi as a new star in WWE.

TRIPLE H GIVES BROCK LESNAR HIGH PRAISE

During the press conference, Triple H discussed how Brock Lesnar doesn't get enough credit for being as good as he is. He explained that Lesnar is not a prototypical pro wrestler and isn't traditionally looked at as one of the greats technically in the business. However, Triple H said he would put Lesnar up there as one of the best of all time.

Triple H praised Lesnar's psychology, in-ring performance, and unique approach to wrestling. He noted that Lesnar did it completely his own way that was believable and distinctly Brock Lesnar. Sometimes when you saw Lesnar in there doing what he does, fans would wonder if he was off script. Triple H credited Lesnar with bringing realism, uniqueness, and a bit of UFC to WWE, calling it awesome to watch.

Triple H expressed that he has watched Lesnar from the day he walked in the door all the way to the present, and it has been incredible. He called Lesnar one of the best psychology-wise and one of the best gameday gut guys he has ever seen, describing him as an incredible, incredible, incredible performer.

TRIPLE H'S PHILOSOPHY ON EARNING TOP SPOTS

During the press conference, Triple H was asked about Damian Priest's recent comments expressing frustration about seeing the same wrestlers occupy WWE's biggest spots. Priest was tired of seeing Roman Reigns, Cody Rhodes, CM Punk, and Seth Rollins continuing to dominate the main-event scene while names such as Oba Femi, Trick Williams, Carmelo Hayes, and Bron Breakker fight for their place.

Triple H responded that WWE is in a period where there are many top performers in the top spots who over time have earned those spots, are selling tickets, and are incredibly popular. He stated firmly that wrestlers must really do something to get there. Triple H rejected the narrative of "it's my turn," declaring that there are no turns in WWE. "That's not how it works. You earn the spot," he said.

Triple H explained that if somebody is getting over more than somebody else, WWE will move that person into the spot and move the other person down. However, he emphasized that it's not just about crowd reaction. WWE also considers whether someone will be good in the spot, hold up their end of the bargain, or potentially screw it up in a way that would make the company regret giving them that spot. 🔥 The Undertaker legacy reflections

THE UNDERTAKER ON LOYALTY, BUSINESS, AND THE ROAD NOT TAKEN

The Undertaker has provided candid reflections on some of the most controversial and pivotal moments in professional wrestling history, offering insight into his decision-making during WWE's most turbulent period and his evolving perspective on the industry.

During his podcast, The Undertaker confirmed that he seriously considered leaving WWE for WCW during the mid-1990s when the company was struggling both creatively and financially. Kevin Nash directly recruited him, emphasizing the significant money WCW was offering to top talent. Undertaker acknowledged the appeal of WCW's presentation at the time, noting that the rival promotion was producing cutting-edge, more mature storylines that resonated better with mainstream audiences, while WWE was dealing with over-the-top characters that weren't connecting with viewers.

The financial disparity between the two companies made the decision genuinely difficult for Undertaker. Working 20 to 25 days per month while barely making enough money to survive, he was well aware that WCW was essentially giving money away to talent simply to keep them unavailable to WWE. When Nash presented the opportunity, Undertaker's initial reaction was tempted. He admitted, "Wow," was his genuine response to the financial possibilities.

However, Undertaker ultimately decided to remain loyal to Vince McMahon and WWE. He cited a pivotal moment from earlier in his career when someone from WCW had told him he would never draw money in the business. Though different people were running WCW by the time of his consideration, that previous rejection lingered. More importantly, Undertaker remembered that Vince McMahon was the person who had given him his original chance, even when McMahon himself wasn't completely sold on the character. That loyalty and belief in McMahon's vision ultimately outweighed the guaranteed financial relief that WCW could have provided. Undertaker had confidence that WWE's ship would right itself and that jumping to WCW would have resulted in regret and misery.

On a different but related topic, Undertaker offered his perspective on The Kliq's infamous 1996 MSG Curtain Call, where Shawn Michaels, Triple H, Kevin Nash, and Scott Hall publicly embraced in the ring before their matches. Undertaker characterized the incident as "selfish," explaining that it violated his understanding of professional wrestling at that time. He acknowledged understanding the deep friendship and history shared by those four men, who had traveled together extensively and built genuine relationships. However, he felt they had put themselves ahead of the business during a period when protecting the illusion of professional wrestling was paramount.

Undertaker distinguished between his feelings in 1996 and how such a moment would be viewed in today's wrestling environment. In the modern era, where professional wrestling's worked nature is openly acknowledged, the Curtain Call moment would barely register. But in 1996, during an era focused on maintaining kayfabe and protecting the business, Undertaker viewed it as a serious violation of wrestling's fundamental principles. He stressed, however, that his criticism was specific to that time period and that he holds no personal grudges about the incident today. Business has moved forward, and he has no lingering resentment toward those involved.

When the nWo eventually returned to WWE in 2002, following WCW's collapse, Undertaker's perspective had evolved considerably. He expressed support for Vince McMahon's decision to bring Kevin Nash, Scott Hall, and Hulk Hogan back into the company, praising McMahon's ability to set aside previous disputes when he believed someone could contribute to WWE's success. Undertaker acknowledged that Nash and Hall had previously defeated WWE in the Monday Night Wars, but he did not harbor grudges based on that competitive history.

He articulated McMahon's business philosophy as being rooted in pragmatism: regardless of what WCW competitors had said or done to WWE in the past, what mattered was whether they could make the company better in the present. Undertaker noted that McMahon could separate personal beef from business necessity, exemplifying the principle Undertaker described as "what's best for business," which he emphasized was not merely a catchphrase but a genuine operating principle for McMahon.

Undertaker's reasoning for supporting the nWo's return was straightforward: Hogan, Nash, and Hall were significant drawing attractions in professional wrestling, and if WWE could tap into their residual star power and mainstream recognition, it would benefit the company. Having already won the competitive battle against WCW, Undertaker felt confident in trusting McMahon's judgment. If bringing in former rivals could help WWE, then he was supportive of the decision.

These reflections reveal an evolution in Undertaker's thinking about professional wrestling business. He moved from strict adherence to protecting kayfabe and the business's illusion, to understanding the pragmatic realities of wrestling as an entertainment business where talent decisions must be based on what serves the company's interests. His loyalty to Vince McMahon during WWE's darkest hours proved prescient, as the company not only survived but eventually thrived, validating his decision to remain with the promotion despite WCW's lucrative offers and his serious consideration of making the jump.

Boogeyman character creation story

Marty Wright came up with The Boogeyman character concept himself while working in WWE's developmental system, a period he describes as one of the darkest times of his life. During an interview with The F Y'All Podcast, Wright explained the circumstances that led to the creation of the character, which debuted in 2005 following his appearance on the reality series "Tough Enough."

Wright recalled being in a difficult position when he entered professional wrestling at an age when most wrestlers are retiring. He was training alongside wrestlers who were his contemporaries in age, creating additional pressure and competition. However, the turning point came during a particularly brutal match against a non-contracted wrestler in WWE's developmental system.

"I was in a very, very dark motherf***ing place," Wright said during the interview. "I knew I had some evil forces out there against me. ... I was entering the business [when] people would normally be retiring at that age, and now I'm in a class with motherf***ers that's teaching [at] my age."

The match proved to be life-altering when Wright's opponent struck him three times in succession: two punches followed by an elbow strike that cracked him in the mouth. The impact knocked out nine of his teeth, causing WWE officials to stop the match and separate the wrestlers to prevent further violence.

"Bottom line, I had my teeth knocked out. Nine of my teeth kicked out of my f***ing head," Wright recalled, going into detail about the devastating incident.

The aftermath of this traumatic event set the stage for The Boogeyman's creation. According to Wright, WWE initially fired him following the incident, but surprisingly, the company retained the wrestler who had broken his teeth. This injustice could have ended Wright's wrestling career before it truly began. However, his situation changed when two legendary WWE figures intervened on his behalf.

"That's when Boogeyman was created," Wright explained. "I reverted back to a very dark place, because it done changed my whole game plan. ... I thought I was a ladies' man, now I ain't got no motherf***ing teeth."

Booker T and The Undertaker stepped in to help Wright regain his position with WWE after he had been fired. While Wright was recovering from his injuries and getting his teeth replaced, he channeled his pain and anger into developing the dark, menacing character that would become The Boogeyman.

"Meanwhile, while I'm getting all my [teeth] all situated, that's when that character came. And I drew it down, I manifested that motherf***er," Wright said, describing how he intentionally created the persona during his recovery period.

The character that emerged from this dark period became a memorable part of WWE history, with The Boogeyman making an impact on the roster starting in 2005. Wright's ability to transform his trauma into a compelling wrestling character demonstrated his creativity and resilience during one of the most challenging periods of his life.
