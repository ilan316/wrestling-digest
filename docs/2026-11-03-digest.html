Swerve Strickland Booked for Indie Match

Former AEW World Champion Swerve Strickland is returning to the independent wrestling scene after about two years. He has been announced for an upcoming Fourth Rope Heelmatic event. Fourth Rope, the independent promotion founded by rapper Westside Gunn, is teaming up with record label Mass Appeal for an event called Heelmatic. The show will take place at the Melrose Ballroom in New York City on Thursday, November 12. Strickland’s opponent has now been confirmed, and the AEW star will face Ninja Mack.

Mack is known across the independent wrestling scene for his high-flying moves and athletic style. He has also competed internationally for major Japanese promotions such as Pro Wrestling NOAH and NJPW, and made several appearances for ROH between 2022 and 2023.

Strickland’s last match outside AEW took place in July 2024, when he faced independent wrestling veteran Amazing Red at House of Glory’s High Intensity event in New York, while still serving as the AEW World Champion. He and Red put on a competitive match that lasted more than 20 minutes, with Strickland eventually picking up the win with his signature House Call and later breaking character to recognize Red’s influence on his career and modern high-flying wrestling.

The upcoming appearance is also notable because of AEW’s schedule that weekend. The company’s Full Gear pay-per-view will take place in Phoenix, Arizona on Saturday, November 14, just two days after the Fourth Rope event. Strickland is currently advertised for the show, where he will team up with Austin Creed and Kofi to challenge Hangman Page, Brody King, and Bandido for the AEW Trios Championship.

Strickland’s involvement with Fourth Rope also brings together two of his biggest interests, music and pro wrestling. Music is one of Strickland’s passions outside of the ring, having released his own hip-hop music and recently being featured on On The Radar Radio. He previously told Billboard that wrestling will always be his one true love, noting that even winning a Grammy wouldn’t mean more to him than his reign with the AEW World Championship.

In that same Billboard interview, Strickland praised Westside Gunn and Fourth Rope for how they’ve helped merge wrestling and hip-hop cultures, stating that Wale and Westside Gunn were really the first ones to put their blood, sweat, tears, and real money into wrestling. He shouted out Westside with Fourth Rope and WaleMania, calling them the forefathers of hip-hop and wrestling culture because they were really in it. Strickland expressed that while he has performed at WaleMania and views this as a peak moment, he wants to see wrestlers crossing over further into the hip-hop world on red carpets, at major awards shows, and concerts.

Ricochet Reflects on Emotional AEW Tribute Match for PAC

Ricochet has opened up about the deeply emotional experience of wrestling Will Ospreay during AEW's tribute show honoring the late PAC. Speaking in a recent interview, Ricochet reflected on the September 30 episode of AEW Dynamite, explaining how preparing for his match helped him navigate a difficult day following the death of his longtime friend.

Honestly, the match kind of helps you, Ricochet said. The match helps the day go by, because without having to think about your match, you are stuck thinking about sad times, basically. At least for me specifically, having something to put my mind toward helped.

Ricochet explained that while the emotions surrounding the tribute show remained present throughout the day, having a match to concentrate on provided a necessary distraction. You do not turn off your emotions, but you have to focus on this, or otherwise you could get hurt. Having something like that to focus on for the day kind of helped.

The AEW star also discussed the chemistry he shares with Ospreay, revealing that the two were able to put their tribute match together remarkably quickly because of their familiarity with each other and PAC. It is kind of criminal how quickly he and I got all that together, Ricochet said. It is kind of criminal how quickly we were like, Oh yeah, okay, well, I guess that is it. I will see you later.

Despite the limited preparation time, Ricochet said both performers understood the significance of the occasion and wanted to deliver something meaningful for those in attendance. Because we work so well together, and we just knew Ben so well, it was about going out there and putting on for the people we knew were going to be there and appreciate it.

The September 30 tribute edition of Dynamite took place at the Petersen Events Center in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, just three days after PAC passed away on September 27 at the age of 40. Ospreay defeated Ricochet in the tribute match after delivering PAC's signature Black Arrow finishing maneuver.

Ricochet and PAC shared an extensive history dating back to their time competing in Japan's Dragon Gate promotion. Their rivalry famously included Ricochet defeating PAC to capture the Open the Brave Gate Championship in November 2011. Ahead of the emotional tribute event, Ricochet took to social media to express his feelings about being chosen for the spot, writing that to even be an option for this is an honor, and dedicating the performance to his late friend with the message, Tomorrow. For you, Ben.

Cody Rhodes Names Strongest Opponents He Has Faced

During a recent appearance on the Double Coverage Podcast, Cody Rhodes offered deep insight into some of the strongest opponents he has shared the wrestling ring with, naming several WWE legends, current WWE stars, and AEW talent. Rhodes pointed to Mark Henry, highlighting his historic achievements in weightlifting and strongman competitions. He also recalled a memorable and intense encounter with Big Show.

Mark is going to be on there, Rhodes said. We mentioned Big Show earlier. Big Show got upset with me one night and he hit me on the chin and it was maybe the only time I ever went out and I came back quick. He also shoved me. Big Show had reserve strength. Those two for sure.

Rhodes continued his list by highlighting several other powerhouse performers, including Big E, Claudio Castagnoli, John Cena, and Bron Breakker. He specifically praised Big E for setting gym records nationwide, calling him an unbelievable anomaly, and noted the exceptional strength of Castagnoli, who performs as part of the Death Riders in AEW. Furthermore, Rhodes placed John Cena high in the strength category and suggested that rising star Bron Breakker belongs in the same elite tier as the powerhouses of previous generations.

Another prominent name Rhodes brought up was Brock Lesnar, though his assessment focused on an attribute even more intimidating than Lesnar physical size and strength. According to Rhodes, what made Lesnar truly frightening was his incredible movement velocity.

It wasn t the fact that this giant hulking fearsome looking individual was coming after you, Rhodes explained regarding Lesnar. It was how fast he was. His speed was genuinely, it s terrifying from a performative standpoint.

Beyond pure strength and power, Rhodes also took the opportunity to praise overall athleticism within the industry. He singled out Bianca Belair, boldly declaring her to be maybe WWE greatest athlete ever.

Aside from discussing his toughest physical adversaries during the podcast appearance, Rhodes also touched on other topics behind the scenes. He admitted to stealing a large portion of his current persona from a WWE legend and revealed the underlying reasons why he felt hesitant and afraid to ask Triple H for time off in order to film movies.

Ricochet Discusses His Performer Growth in AEW

Ricochet explains why he is a better performer now than earlier in his career. A part of the AEW roster since 2024, Ricochet now believes himself to be a better performer than he was earlier in his career. A pro-wrestling veteran, Ricochet was part of the WWE from 2018 till 2024, before which he wrestled for NJPW, Lucha Underground, and several other indie promotions. After parting ways with WWE in 2024, he joined AEW.

Recently, speaking in an interview, Ricochet opened up and shared why he believes himself to be a better performer now. Before, it was always physical, Ricochet said. You are going to see something spectacular. I am going to do something that is going to make you get out of your seat, which I can still do. But now I also have that emotional connection.

Throughout his entire WWE run, Ricochet maintained a babyface character. However, Ricochet turned heel soon after joining AEW and started exploring his villainous side. Ricochet's heel character has found an emotional connection with the fans, where he is regularly loudly booed by the crowd, which he is interested in hearing. When I come out and people do not start clapping, they start booing; that is just as much cheering as when Will Ospreay or Kenny Omega comes out and people cheer for them, Ricochet said.

I do not want to be cheered. My job as the villain of the story is to get you to dislike this character so you can like the person I am in there facing, whoever that may be. I do not want to be cheered. I want to do everything I can to make you think, This guy is annoying. This guy is stupid. I hate this guy. Whatever that may be. Because at the end of the day, I am going to do something in the ring, and you are going to want to cheer. And then you are going to be like, Oh, crap, I cannot cheer this guy. I gotta boo him. So that is always kind of cool, he finished.

Bryce Remsburg Suggests AEW Could Hold Stadium Show in Philadelphia

Bryce Remsburg: AEW Could Hold A Stadium Show At Home Of The Philadelphia Eagles

Over the last few years, AEW has not been shy when it comes to running shows in stadiums, holding three All In events at Wembley Stadium since 2023, and the 2025 All In at Globe Life Field in Arlington, Texas. But as of yet, the promotion hasn't tried running in an American football stadium, though one such stadium wasn't entirely dismissed by referee Bryce Remsburg. In an appearance on The Certified Mark Show, Remsburg was asked about the possibility of AEW running a show in Lincoln Financial Field in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, the home of the NFL's Philadelphia Eagles, and while he didn't see it happening soon, he could see it happening eventually.

I will say the fact that they've hosted wrestling before, which was WrestleMania, is a positive, Remsburg said, providing credit to The Certified Mark Show with a h/t to Wrestling Inc. That means they know what they're doing, they know what to do. And I will say Philadelphia has been one of the best AEW markets since it started. So if we were looking for a domestic stadium to host an All In at, I do think The Link would be in the conversation. I don't think it's going to happen in the next year, but maybe after that. I'm not sure we have the demand for a Link show...But never ever say ever, I will say that.

The timing of the question comes as AEW has been rumored to be running a stadium show of some kind this upcoming April, in response to WWE deciding to hold WrestleMania 43 in Saudi Arabia instead of North America. While AEW owner Tony Khan has himself hinted at the idea, no formal announcement confirming such a show has been made, and most of the rumors have suggested the show would be held in New York City, seemingly eliminating any Philadelphia venue from being a possibility.

Paul Wight Praises AEW's Kyle Fletcher

During a recent appearance on the Going Ringside podcast, All Elite Wrestling commentator and veteran star Paul Wight was asked to identify which wrestler currently impresses him the most. Wight did not hesitate to single out rising talent Kyle Fletcher as the person building incredible momentum and possessing huge star potential.

According to Wight, Fletcher completely checks every single box required to be a top-tier professional wrestler. The wrestling veteran specifically pointed to Fletcher's interview ability, wrestling skill, tenacity, aggression, and his overall attitude toward the industry. Wight also commended Fletcher for his willingness to work hard, the way he presents himself, and his positive interactions with other wrestlers behind the scenes in the locker room.

Describing Fletcher as a very humble, quiet, and good kid, Wight expressed that he genuinely enjoys watching the young star's progress. Wight concluded his praise by emphasizing that when he looks at Fletcher, he sees a performer who has money written on him.

Gabe Kidd Takes Shot at WWE Roster

Gabe Kidd isn't interested in stepping into the ring with anyone from WWE. At all. The AEW star made that clear during a recent Instagram Q and A session, where he answered questions from fans while waiting for a connecting flight. Kidd, who has never been one to shy away from speaking his mind, was asked which WWE Superstar he would most like to face in a potential dream match. His response was on-brand.

None of em, Kidd wrote, adding that they are all shit. The blunt response is the latest example of Kidd stirring up controversy on social media, where his outspoken personality has frequently generated discussion among wrestling fans.

Kidd has also been dealing with some developments of his own in AEW, as former Dogs stablemates David Finlay and Clark Connors recently turned against him. Finlay and Connors instead aligned themselves with The Bang Bang Gang to form a new faction known as The Cut, leaving Kidd on the opposite side of his former allies.
