AEW releases video teasing return of Toni Storm

All Elite Wrestling may have started dropping official hints about the highly anticipated return of a major star, former champion Timeless Toni Storm. The promotion released a mysterious 11-second video on its official social media accounts, featuring a package with the words Play Me written on it alongside the outline of a lipstick kiss. The brief video quickly fades to black without revealing anything further. While company leadership has not officially confirmed the exact meaning behind the teaser, the distinct imagery appears to be directly connected to the potential comeback of Storm.

Similar clues reportedly surfaced during the AEW WrestleDream pay-per-view, when select media members discovered physical cassette tapes that had been strategically placed at the event. Those promotional tapes featured the exact same Play Me message and lipstick kiss design.

This latest digital teaser dropped just hours after AEW President Tony Khan addressed Storm's eventual return during the media scrum following the Full Gear pay-per-view in Phoenix, Arizona. Khan praised the current women's division, stating that there are so many great women wrestlers in AEW right now, and noted that one of the greatest women wrestlers and champions the company has ever had will someday return, which is Timeless Toni Storm. Khan elaborated on how much the women have delivered for the company over the years, emphasizing that Storm's return is still looming on the horizon.

Storm has been entirely absent from television programming since March, when she defeated Marina Shafir at AEW Revolution. Following that victory, Storm was involved in a heated pull-apart confrontation with Ronda Rousey before being written off television after she was discovered unconscious backstage. To this day, the identity of the person responsible for that storyline backstage attack has never been revealed. AEW has yet to announce an official return date for Storm, keeping fans guessing as the promotional campaign continues to unfold.

Deonna Purrazzo criticizes lack of character development in AEW women's division

During a recent appearance on Behind The Turnbuckle Studios, AEW star Deonna Purrazzo expressed frustration with the lack of character development in women's wrestling. She argued that performers need more opportunities to establish meaningful connections with audiences through storytelling and backstage segments. The former TNA Knockouts Champion explained that while wrestling ability remains important, audiences also need to understand who the performers are and what motivates them before they can become emotionally invested in their matches and rivalries.

Purrazzo elaborated on the specific shortcomings in current programming by pointing out the absence of foundational character work. She stated that they have talked a lot about being a character versus being the real person and thinks what is missing for a lot of women's wrestling is that character development. According to Purrazzo, they are not getting vignettes to explain who they are, nor are they watching the women interact with each other in backstage, real-life scenarios. Without that foundation, she notes that the audience does not invest because the audience simply does not know them.

Using her own career as a primary example, Purrazzo stated that the AEW audience at this point does not truly know who The Virtuosa, Deonna Purrazzo, is. She noted that they have not seen a vignette for her in roughly 18 months. Therefore, if she suddenly gets put on television, fans are not going to react by thinking it is awesome and cheering for her because they do not have that established relationship yet. She specifically pointed to the absence of promotional vignettes explaining her Virtuosa persona, suggesting that viewers unfamiliar with her previous work are not given enough opportunities to understand her character, and that simply appearing on television is not enough to generate a strong reaction.

Purrazzo believes this issue extends far beyond her own presentation and affects women's wrestling more broadly across the board. She emphasized that the industry needs to focus on developing characters that people either relate to or hate, as that is what ultimately generates compelling storylines, earns more television time, and secures main event spots. Rather than focusing solely on increasing the number of women's matches, Purrazzo emphasized the importance of building characters that audiences either want to support or actively dislike, concluding that this investment is essential for the future of women's wrestling.

Max Caster discusses expecting heat on break from pro wrestling

Former wrestling star Max Caster is currently on a break from professional wrestling after facing severe backlash for a controversial line in his ring entrance rap at TNA Bound for Glory, where he faced Moose. Despite the ongoing fallout, the self-proclaimed Best Wrestler Alive recently participated in a live online signing where he took questions from fans.

During the appearance, Caster was asked if he expected to face backlash from Karen Jarrett for a specific line he delivered back in December 2022. On an episode of AEW Dynamite, Caster threw out a line suggesting Jeff Jarrett stole money like it is Kurt Angle's wife, referencing the Last Outlaw's marriage to Karen, who was formerly married to Angle, a real-life saga that previously played out in a TNA storyline. Reflecting on his provocative style, Caster noted that he generally figures he will attract heat for his bars. A lot of people are angry at me for a lot of things, Caster said. Do I expect it? I just have come to accept it, let's say that.

The most recent and damaging heat on Caster came following Bound for Glory, where he used a rap referencing a missile strike on Riyadh, Saudi Arabia's King Khalid International Airport, an attack that killed 12 people and injured 300 others just before the pay-per-view event. Caster ultimately went on to lose his match against Moose at the show.

In the days following the event, fans quickly noticed that TNA had deleted all references to Caster on social media. The promotion subsequently released a statement confirming they were severing ties with the star. Caster later offered a public apology on his own social media channels and formally revealed that he would be stepping away from professional wrestling for an extended period during an independent wrestling event on October 17.

Steven Borden explains why he doesn't wear Sting's face paint in AEW

AEW star Steven Borden has quickly become a fan favorite since wrestling alongside Darby Allin, often paying homage to his legendary father, Sting.

But unlike Sting, Steven doesn't sport face paint or a moniker like his father, even though he was introduced to AEW fans that way. Recently, Steven commented on why he's presenting himself differently during an interview alongside Allin on 101XL – Jacksonville's Sport Radio.

The last thing I want to do is be playing The Greatest Hits, Steven argued, as transcribed by Wrestling Inc. I think, you know, you can do callbacks when time – when it makes sense, at the right time, but I think if I were to come out with face paint, and a trench coat, and a bat, like, I probably could have been booed out of the building in the beginning; and I'm still in the beginning right now.

While Steven might want to establish his own legacy in pro wrestling, the parallels between him and his father at least physically are undeniable. Veterans like Jim Ross, who watched Sting's entry and rise in the industry, have pointed out just how similar the father and son look, and how they both have impressive athletic careers outside of pro wrestling.

Steven might only be less than a year into his wrestling career, but the second-generation star already had a brief setback when he suffered a concussion mid-match. In an interview ahead of his Blood and Guts bout, Borden brushed off the injury, noting that the brain is important, but he felt fine after regaining consciousness, and chalks his battle readiness down to his attitude of never wanting to run from pressure.

Andrade El Idolo discusses Ric Flair's last match

Ric Flair had a storybook ending to his career at WWE WrestleMania 24 back in 2008, losing to Shawn Michaels in an emotional bout. However, for The Nature Boy, this would prove to not be enough, as he would step back into the ring in 2022 for what he dubbed as his true Last Match.

Facing Jeff Jarrett and Jay Lethal, Flair teamed up with then-son-in-law Andrade El Idolo, suffering a heart attack mid-match. According to Andrade, he didn't realize what happened to Flair for some time. I heard later, like, a couple of months later! At the time, I thought he was selling!, Andrade exclaimed during an interview on Talk Is Jericho. Before the match, I talked to my ex-wife, I said I was scared. Andrade noted that Flair asserted that he was strong and capable of performing, but Andrade couldn't shake the dread of something happening mid-match.

If something happened, what would I say to my ex-wife? Hey, I was there? What could I do? he recalled. I'm happy that nothing happened. Andrade pointed out that Flair was fine later that night and drinking backstage afterwards, and it was only once he visited a doctor months later that Flair found out he suffered a heart attack. That was surprising! Like, oh, that's why he stayed down? I was scared in this moment, I remember in this moment thinking he was selling, but I didn't know he had a heart attack!

Scare aside, Andrade agreed with Chris Jericho that the match and the fact that Flair wrestled through a heart attack only adds to his legend, and still believes his former father-in-law is a great person.

Chris Jericho discusses playing Gorgeous George in Amazon Prime series

AEW star Chris Jericho recently opened up about playing professional wrestling pioneer Gorgeous George in an upcoming Amazon Prime limited series titled The Greatest, which focuses on the life of Muhammad Ali. Gorgeous George, whose real name was George Wagner, was a massive star in the 1940s and 1950s and stands as one of the very first national icons of the industry due to his groundbreaking character work. Over sixty years after his death, Wagner is being featured in the streaming service project, which briefly explores how the wrestling legend inspired a young Muhammad Ali.

During an interview with Christopher Smith, Jericho detailed how the opportunity came to be and what he hopes audiences will take away from his performance. I auditioned and it turns out they were looking for Gorgeous George for a while and couldn't find anybody, Jericho said, with a tip of the hat to Wrestling Inc. for the transcription. To prepare for the role, Jericho drew inspiration from an old movie starring Wagner, studying it to accurately imitate the late wrestler's unique personality and vocal cadence.

The series delves into a crucial historical connection between the two combat sports legends. It turns out the reason why it was such an important part is that Ali, Cassius Clay, was a great boxer, but didn't have the personality, Jericho explained to Christopher Smith, with transcription via Wrestling Inc. Jericho recounted the famous story of Ali meeting Wagner in Las Vegas, where the wrestling pioneer helped shape the persona that Ali would use to captivate the world throughout his legendary career.

When asked why he believes he ultimately secured the role, Jericho theorized that the casting directors chose him not simply because he is a professional wrestler, but because he was capable of portraying Gorgeous George with genuine accuracy. I wanted to remain true to who he was as a person, Jericho told Christopher Smith, with an h/t to Wrestling Inc. for the transcription. Of course I understand what he was saying to Cassius about connecting with the audience and about being that next-level, larger-than-life person, cause thats what people want to see when they watch wrestling or boxing.
