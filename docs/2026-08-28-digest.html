🔥 MJF Comments on Modern Wrestling, Veterans, and Tribalism

Ahead of AEW All In, Maxwell Jacob Friedman has been making the media rounds, offering sharp and candid assessments of the professional wrestling industry, his peers, and media landscapes. Speaking on the state of the business, MJF acknowledged that pro wrestling is more financially successful than ever, pointing to historic television deals and live gates. As a 30-year-old, he noted he has already sold more million-dollar houses than most Hall of Famers, proving the business is thriving. However, he does believe the product suffers from a creative standpoint.

MJF criticized what he views as a homogenized standard for what constitutes a good match bell-to-bell. Comparing the current in-ring style to cake batter ice cream, he explained that the flavor is so rich that eating it constantly becomes exhausting, leading people to yearn for the simplicity of vanilla. He believes his own meteoric rise succeeded because he offered fans a different flavor. To highlight this lack of variety, MJF offered high praise for four rising stars in AEW who stand out to him. He commended Nick Wayne for his work ethic and aesthetic improvements, called Kevin Knight and Fletcher future main-event mainstays for the next decade or more, and singled out Mark Davis as his favorite big man in wrestling. MJF praised Davis for bringing a unique style reminiscent of old-school legends like Stan Hansen and Bruiser Brody, with both competitors set to enter the Casino Gauntlet match at All In.

In addition to in-ring styles, MJF delivered a controversial take on veteran wrestlers in the locker room. He warned young talent that 99 percent of veterans do not have their best interests at heart because they want to protect their spots and prevent others from taking food off their plates. According to MJF, young wrestlers must rely solely on their own instincts, noting that while followers can make good money, only leaders become true stars.

Friedman also addressed the topic of industry tribalism and WWE's business tactics. While discussing WWE running shows in Chicago and at Nassau Coliseum at the same time as AEW events, MJF took an all-in-the-game approach, acknowledging it as smart business counter-programming even if he finds it a bit funny and slightly transparent. He directed a message toward AEW's most diehard supporters, urging them to stop writing thinkpieces attacking WWE and instead focus on putting their own promotion over with logical reasons.

Expanding on media coverage, MJF did not hold back when discussing Busted Open Radio, declaring that the show has turned into a WWE propaganda machine and is no longer real journalism following its WWE Radio deal. While he expressed personal respect for Dave LaGreca and acknowledged him getting paid, MJF argued that the show's coverage of AEW noticeably shifted and can no longer be viewed as impartial.

Finally, addressing negative press and his reputation, MJF maintained that while wild, fake stories come with the territory of being a household name and an athlete-actor, he draws a firm line at actual misconduct. He confidently stated that in the history of his career in AEW, he has never once done anything to genuinely make the company look bad, emphasizing that his primary goal is to be the best possible representative for his promotion.

🔥 Mercedes Martinez Announces Retirement Following Injury

Veteran professional wrestler Mercedes Martinez has announced her immediate retirement from in-ring competition after suffering a serious knee injury. The 45-year-old wrestling veteran shared the heartbreaking news in a video posted to social media, confirming that her final year in the ring has come to an abrupt end.
