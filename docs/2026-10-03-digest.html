🔥 AEW Collision Live Broadcast from Chris Jericho Cruise

For the first time in company history, AEW and its Saturday night flagship show, AEW Collision, will broadcast live from the Chris Jericho Rock 'N' Wrestling Rager at Sea: Seventh Cruise of a Seventh Cruise, on Saturday, November 7, 2026. Confirming Jericho's recent announcement, AEW took to social media to break the official news with a Collision banner featuring Jericho, Harley Cameron, and the current AEW World Trios Champions The New Level, all of whom are scheduled to be there.

Starting in 2018, this year marks the seventh sail of the rock and wrestling rager cruise experience. Running from November 7 through the 11, attendees will sail from Miami to Bimini, Bahamas, aboard the Norwegian ship, experiencing views of the deep sea and a plethora of star power from talents featured across AEW, TNA, the independent scene, and more. Advertised names include Matt and Jeff Hardy, The Great Muta, Elayna Black, Luther, Carlito, Ted DiBiase Sr., Indi Hartwell, Shotzi Blackheart, Dean Malenko, Buff Bagwell, and Saraya, alongside musical guests Fozzy and Downstait.

This milestone marks the first time AEW has aired a live event from the cruise. Back in January 2020, an episode of Dynamite was taped aboard the ship, famously featuring Kenny Omega and Hangman Adam Page defeating SCU to capture the AEW World Tag Team Championships. While talent has frequently appeared on subsequent cruises, logistical issues prevented further television tapings until now.

Jericho explained in a recent interview that extensive planning went into the broadcast, noting the logistical hurdles of satellite connections, Wi-Fi, generators, and thousands of feet of cable. He stated that lots of logistics, bells and whistles, and problem-solving went into making it work. The Collision episode will air on the same day as WWE Crown Jewel.

According to Jericho, Tony Khan can use whoever he wants on the live show at sea, and the cruise will start off with an actual all-star Collision lineup. Afterward, certain performers will stay for the remainder of the cruise while others depart. The event takes place just a few days after Jacksonville, Florida hosts the 2026 Blood & Guts edition of Dynamite on Wednesday, November 4.

The voyage will also feature special attractions like the Jericho Men's Oceanic Tournament, the Women's Oceanic Tournament for a newly created Oceanic Championship belt, MicroMania, and a live recording of a podcast with guests Kofi and Austin Creed of The New Level. Additionally, Jericho announced that this year's cruise will be dedicated to the late PAC, paying tribute to him much like the promotion honored the late Jay Briscoe, who wrestled on the inaugural cruise. Spots remain available for the cruise through official channels, while AEW Collision typically airs Saturday nights at 8/7c.

David Finlay on Choosing AEW Over WWE NXT

During a recent appearance on Chris Jericho’s Talk Is Jericho podcast, David Finlay opened up about his decision to make the move to AEW instead of WWE NXT.

Discussing the overall factors that influenced his career path, Finlay pointed to the overall package of obviously pay and schedule, as well as the fact that all my friends are in AEW. While noting that his brother and dad are in NXT and that working with them would have been cool, he ultimately decided that he wanted to keep doing what he was doing and have the freedom to do what he wanted to do. Being able to work alongside his friends served as a great bonus.

Finlay also addressed the potential creative and personal hurdles he would have faced if he had gone the other route. He admitted that he thinks he would have struggled with re-humbling himself to the level that they would have wanted him to humble yourself to, noting his mindset was that he had already been there and already proven himself.

Finlay is currently a member of The Dogs in AEW alongside his stablemates Clark Connors and Gabe Kidd.

While there has never been an official reason revealed for the disappearance of Timeless Toni Storm from television, there was a compelling mystery revealed when she was taken off of TV in March 2026. On the March 18 episode of AEW Dynamite, there was a backstage angle revealing a dastardly attack on Timeless Toni Storm, leading to the extended absence of the fan favorite star.

Putting together an iconic photoshoot reportedly with very short notice, continual creative force behind the Timeless Toni Storm character, RJ City helped to pull together a masterpiece. Sharing an image to social media pages featuring a variety of compelling suspects, the disappearance of Toni Storm was revealed in such a way that led fans to assume the star would be returning at a finite point in the future. Reportedly putting together the creative angle backstage on the day of the Dynamite taping, not only was the crime scene shown on television, but there was even an AEW t-shirt released.

Seemingly bashed with her own shoe, Toni Storm is laid out as the central visual, but there are plenty of clues hanging around behind her. With a mask by her foot, a bouquet of flowers left next to her body, a bloody heel, shards of glass, sneakers, a golden steel chair, bull rope, cowbell, and a kendo stick, there are plenty of details for at-home sleuths to work with. Chris Jericho Favorite Tag Team Partner

Chris Jericho has shared the ring with some of the biggest names in wrestling history, but when it comes to the tag team partner he enjoyed working with more than anyone else, one man still sits at the top of his list: Paul Wight.

Speaking during an appearance on TMZ, Jericho was asked about Wight's recent work behind the commentary desk in AEW. Wight has continued contributing to AEW through commentary appearances while spending considerably less time wrestling, and Jericho believes the role fits his longtime friend perfectly.

Jericho explained that Wight has found another way to contribute after decades in the ring, while admitting that even he sometimes forgets just how massive the former Big Show really is.

I mean, it is perfect. Once again, here you have this seven-foot guy that is now making a second career as a commentator. You know, you do not want to get too many shots of him because he looks so ridiculously big, and you forget. Like, we have known him for so many years, and he is one of my best friends in the business.

Jericho had plenty of praise for Wight away from the ring as well. He described him as a great person while joking that Wight certainly has another side when his temper gets going.

He is just a sweetheart of a guy. He gets pretty angry and cantankerous too, but, you know, I will see a picture of him and I from, you know, 2010 or whatever, like, Oh my gosh. Holy, is he big. You forget.

The conversation then shifted to a recent AEW moment when Jericho ended up sitting on Wight's lap at the commentary desk during his match with Kevin Knight in Las Vegas.

To see him at the commentary table, we had a great moment, the Kevin Knight match in Vegas, where I flew off the top and landed on Big Show or on Kevin Knight and then I went and sat on Big Show's lap, and it was such a great moment.

Jericho also had some fun with the screenshot that came out of the moment. He joked that he looked great before and after sitting down, but one awkward frame was all critics needed to jump on him.

It is funny too, because I was kind of slumped over sitting on his lap, and, you know, sometimes you get a picture which is not very flattering. So it is abs Jericho, abs Jericho, sit down on Big Show's lap, look like Jabba the Hutt for two seconds, get up and abs Jericho. And of course all the haters are like, Is this what you wanted to see back in WWE? And it is just like, I am looking like a big blob because I am sitting like this.

That story eventually brought Jericho back to their history together, and that is when he revealed just how highly he ranks Wight among the countless wrestlers he has teamed with during his career.

But it was a really great moment that, you know, he is still my all-time favorite tag team partner that I ever had. We just really we had great, great chemistry together.

Jericho and Wight became known as Jeri-Show in WWE after joining forces in 2009, and their size difference ended up becoming one of the biggest strengths of the team. Jericho explained that playing the cowardly heel became much easier when he had a seven-foot giant standing behind him ready to handle anyone who came after him.

Here I am, the cowardly heel, and who is going to beat me when I got this guy behind me? You got a problem with me? Talk to my friend. And that is all I needed.

Jericho said having Wight as backup also allowed him to stand his ground against people who physically towered over him.

Like, I was going toe-to-toe with Shaq, toe-to-toe with freaking, you know, whoever. And it is like I can go toe-to-toe with Shaq because you got to deal with my guy.

For Jericho, however, their connection clearly went beyond simply having complementary wrestling characters. He once again stressed their chemistry together before describing himself and Wight as two veterans from another generation who have somehow managed to keep finding roles in the business.

Following the initial wave of grief across the industry, further details have emerged regarding the unprecedented backstage efforts and widespread reactions surrounding the memorial broadcasts.

The September 30 tribute edition of AEW Dynamite experienced a massive surge in viewership, drawing 899,000 viewers on TBS and a 0.22 rating in the key 18 to 49 demographic. Behind the scenes, the broadcast underwent significant last-minute restructuring to accommodate wrestlers wanting to honor their close friend. Referee Bryce Remsburg also shared a backstage moment where Jon Moxley offered quiet support to the locker room during the intense evening.

Other members of the wrestling community shared personal reflections. Tony Schiavone recalled his final conversation with PAC during All Out, noting that the star jokingly asked what they were doing out on the floor during a chaotic spot. Meanwhile, Josh Alexander praised PAC in a recent interview, placing him in elite company by calling him the closest thing professional wrestling has had to Eddie Guerrero due to his unmatched versatility.

Corporate lines were also crossed when Dwayne Johnson shared a video tribute praising AEW and Tony Khan, which reportedly caught some officials inside WWE by surprise given his position on the TKO board of directors.

The global remembrance extended to Japan as well, where Dragon Gate held a dedicated memorial event at Korakuen Hall. Dragon Kid led a ten-bell salute carrying a photo of PAC, and his team later entered to the late star's former theme music, dedicating their victory to their former locker room companion.

🔄 המשך Jim Cornette on Steven Borden Injury at AEW All Out

Jim Cornette has weighed in on the exact mechanics behind the scary accident.

During a recent episode of his podcast, Cornette analyzed the botched mid-air spot between Borden and Kevin Knight. He pointed out that the setup failed because Borden climbed to the top rope with both feet, while Knight used the safer technique of keeping one foot on the middle rope to better gauge the timing. This led to Borden jumping early and colliding awkwardly in mid-air, resulting in him landing hard on the back of his head and knocking himself out.

Cornette criticized the entire concept of the coast-to-coast style spot, calling it inherently risky because it forces a major flat-back bump even when executed correctly. When comparing it to a similar famous spot between Logan Paul and Ricochet, Cornette noted that those performers possessed superior experience, athleticism, and a bit of luck.

While acknowledging that Borden is a former college athlete who has received good training, Cornette reiterated his belief that Borden should have spent more time on the independent circuit before stepping into a major promotion at this stage in his career. However, he made it clear that he was not minimizing the severity of the injury and expressed relief that Borden is expected to make a full recovery without ongoing symptoms.
