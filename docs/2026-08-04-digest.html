🔥 AEW WrestleDream 2026

AEW has officially announced the location and date for WrestleDream 2026, one of the company's most anticipated shows of the year. The event will take place on Saturday, October 17 from Addition Financial Arena in Orlando, Florida. This marks the third time Orlando has hosted an AEW pay-per-view, following Revolution 2022 and Worlds End 2024.

Addition Financial Arena is a 10,000-seat venue located on the campus of the University of Central Florida. General public ticket sales are scheduled to begin on Monday, August 10. Premium seating and presale options became available earlier, starting Tuesday, August 3 at 10 a.m. AEW Insiders can sign up for presale access and additional benefits through the company's website.

Speaking with the Orlando Sentinel following the announcement, AEW President Tony Khan reflected on Florida's rich wrestling history and expressed his enthusiasm for the company's involvement. Khan recalled his excitement as a young fan discovering classic matches through tape trading on the internet during his junior high years. He specifically mentioned the match between Ric Flair and Barry Windham from the 1986 Battle of the Belts event in Orlando as one that particularly inspired him.

"One of the matches that I was so excited to see as a young fan trading tapes of wrestling on the internet, when I was in junior high, was Ric Flair vs. Barry Windham from the Battle of the Belts in Orlando," Khan said. "That's just one of so many great matches and rivalries in Florida, and we're glad to add to that history."

WrestleDream first took place in 2023 as a tribute to Japanese wrestling legend Antonio Inoki, whom Khan describes as "pro wrestling's greatest dreamer." The annual event has previously been hosted in Seattle, Tacoma, and St. Louis. The 2025 edition was headlined by an "I Quit" match between Jon Moxley and Darby Allin.

The announcement of WrestleDream 2026 completes AEW's confirmed pay-per-view schedule through November. All In will take place on Sunday, August 30 at Wembley Stadium in London, England. All Out follows on Saturday, September 26 at NOW Arena in Hoffman Estates, Illinois. WrestleDream 2026 will then occur on October 17, with Full Gear scheduled for Saturday, November 14 at Mortgage Matchup Center in Phoenix, Arizona.

Additional intrigue surrounds the event due to potential free agency. WWE star Bayley's contract comes up for renewal sometime before the end of 2026, creating the possibility she could be available before the October 17 date. Mercedes Mone shared a fan-made graphic in June teasing a potential matchup between herself and Bayley at WrestleDream, though it remains unclear whether Bayley would be a free agent by then or if she has interest in joining AEW. If she does test the market, reports indicate AEW would be heavily interested in acquiring her services.

🔥 Max Caster AEW Contract Expiration

Max Caster's time with All Elite Wrestling appears to be coming to an end. According to reports from Fightful Select, Caster's current AEW contract is expected to expire around October 2026. An October expiration would mark the conclusion of Caster's AEW tenure at just under six years, having been signed by Tony Khan in November 2020 alongside Anthony Bowens.

The situation surrounding Caster has been developing over recent months. He has not competed in an AEW match at any point during 2026, with his most recent appearance coming on the December 17, 2025 edition of AEW Dynamite, where he participated in the Dynamite Diamond Battle Royal. Compounding the issue, Caster has rarely, if ever, been seen backstage at AEW television in recent months, and there has been no indication that the two sides have discussed a contract extension or new deal.

While absent from AEW programming, Caster has remained active on the independent wrestling scene. He has wrestled for promotions including Beyond Wrestling, Create A Pro, WrestlePro, Wrestling Open, and Gotham Wrestling. In May, Caster declared that Beyond Wrestling was his "new home," suggesting a commitment to the independent circuit. Most recently, he faced Bobby Orlando on July 6. These appearances demonstrate that Caster is fit and healthy, choosing to work elsewhere rather than for AEW.

During his AEW tenure, Caster achieved considerable success. He and Anthony Bowens formed The Acclaimed, one of AEW's top homegrown tag team acts. The team captured the AEW World Tag Team Championship, defeating Keith Lee and Swerve Strickland in December 2022 in one of the company's biggest feuds that year. They held the championship until February 2023, when they lost it to The Gunns. Later, in August 2023, Caster, Bowens, and Billy Gunn won the AEW World Trios Championship from the House of Black at All In. They successfully defended the titles 11 times before dropping them to the Bang Bang Gang at Dynasty in April 2024.

The Acclaimed's story took a turn in January 2025 when the team officially split. Following the breakup, both wrestlers were repositioned as singles competitors. Caster adopted a new five-minute challenge gimmick and went on an extensive losing streak as AEW attempted to repackage both men as solo acts. However, neither caught on in their new roles. The two men failed to gain traction as singles competitors, and AEW teased a reunion throughout much of last year that ultimately fell through.

Attempts at reconciliation between Caster and Bowens on AEW programming were abandoned. The storyline of an unwanted reunion was dropped, and while the team did reunite with The Acclaimed for a House of Glory event in March, this reunion has not extended to AEW television. Instead, Bowens has since aligned himself with a new faction called The Opps, joining the group in April. In early May, Bowels and Caster wrestled each other in a singles match for indie promotion WrestlePro X, with Bowens winning the match. The two made peace with each other afterwards, though the outcome indicated that working together again was doubtful.

The reports make clear that Caster's return to AEW appears unlikely. Not only is there no word regarding the two sides discussing a contract extension, but Caster has actively committed himself to the independent wrestling scene. His prolonged absence from AEW television, combined with his visible activity elsewhere and his public declaration that Beyond Wrestling is his new home, all point toward a permanent departure.

Neither Max Caster nor AEW has publicly commented on the reported contract status.

AEW's Rebel Details Fight Against ALS

Since revealing her ALS diagnosis earlier this year, the wrestling world has flocked around AEW's Rebel. In the last few weeks alone, TNA paid tribute to her on their live broadcast of Impact, WWE's Kevin Owens shouted her out following SummerSlam, and AEW announced a special Dynamite: Rebel Heart event dedicated to her in September, all while many of their stars took the Ice Bucket challenge to raise money and bring awareness to ALS research. In the video, Rebel reflected on her journey to professional wrestling. "I'm just a good old girl from a small town in Oklahoma who never stopped going for her dreams. They don't call me Rebel for nothing. I tried out for the Dallas Cowboys cheerleaders while I was still in high school, and I made the team. I went on to cheer for them for three years. And that's when I knew I could kick some ass. I was there at the very beginning of AEW. AEW has changed my life, and I will be forever grateful," she said.

As clips of her AEW career and AEW wrestlers taking part in the Ice Bucket challenge were shown in the background, Rebel detailed her battle with ALS and her determination to keep fighting. "I first started showing symptoms in late 2024, but I was not diagnosed until early 2026. That is very common in ALS, and why it's so important to have research to find out why this is happening. In order to get research, we need funding. We need awareness, and that is why I feel compelled to fight, advocate, and pick up the torch from the people before me, like Eric Dane. I felt so honored that my family at AEW and I, we're going to make a difference. I feel like I can keep going. I've always stood up for myself, and now it's time for all of us to stand together," Rebel said.

AEW Kazuchika Okada Appearance

AEW wrestler Kazuchika Okada made a surprise appearance at PRODUCE Wrestling in Brooklyn to honor the retiring Tiger Mask IV. Following Tiger Mask's tag team match, Okada came out to pay his respects to the legendary junior heavyweight competitor. He flipped off the crowd, presented Tiger Mask with a bouquet of flowers, and bowed to the retiring star in a touching tribute.

The appearance was particularly meaningful given the extensive history between the two wrestlers. Okada and Tiger Mask competed many times during their time together in New Japan Pro Wrestling, with most of their encounters taking place in various tag team match formats. However, Tiger Mask did manage to defeat Okada in a singles match in Osaka in December of 2009, making their rivalry one of mutual respect.

Tiger Mask IV's final match was a tag team bout that pitted him and "Dynamite Kid" Tom Billington against Black Tiger IV (Rocky Romero) and Black Tiger IX (formerly Magnus in CMLL). The pairing was particularly fitting given the historical connections between the competitors. Billington's uncle was the original Dynamite Kid, who was a notable rival of the original Tiger Mask decades earlier.

Tiger Mask IV had an exceptional professional wrestling career spanning nearly three decades. He was trained by Satoru Sayama, the original Tiger Mask, and began his wrestling career in Michinoku Pro from 1995 to 2001 before moving to New Japan in 2002. He remained with New Japan Pro Wrestling thereafter, establishing himself as one of the most accomplished junior heavyweight wrestlers of his generation.

His championship resume is extensive and includes six IWGP Junior Heavyweight Championships and two Best of the Super Juniors tournament victories. He also won IWGP Junior Heavyweight Tag Team Championships alongside notable partners Jushin Liger and Robbie Eagles. Beyond New Japan, Tiger Mask IV captured championships in All Japan and Pro Wrestling Noah in Japan, as well as in CMLL and UWA in Mexico, demonstrating his versatility and skill across different wrestling promotions worldwide.

Tiger Mask IV also competed in the United States, most notably appearing in TNA Impact Wrestling. He competed in a four-way match at Sacrifice in 2007 against Alex Shelley, Jerry Lynn, and Senshi, also known as Low Ki, further expanding his international wrestling resume. Although Tiger Mask IV had already enjoyed a retirement ceremony in Japan a few weeks prior, he had continued to honor previous commitments, with Monday's match marking what could be his final professional wrestling appearance.

Wrestling Title Changes History

August 3rd stands as one of the most active dates for professional wrestling title changes in history. According to available records, approximately 251 major and notable title changes have occurred on this specific date, demonstrating its significance in wrestling history.

The earliest recorded August 3rd title changes date back to 1996. On this date, Mustafa Saed and New Jack won the ECW Tag Team Titles, while Shinjiro Otani captured the NWA World Welterweight Title. The following year in 1997, August 3rd proved even more significant when Bret Hart won the WWF World Heavyweight Title and Stone Cold Steve Austin won the WWF Intercontinental Title, both at SummerSlam 1997.

From 2001 to 2005, the date continued to see championship activity across various promotions. Cibernético won the AAA Campeón de Campeones Title in 2001. The year 2002 was particularly notable for the WWE Hardcore Championship, which changed hands twice on August 3rd under its 24/7 rule, with Tommy Dreamer and Bradshaw both holding the title on that date. In 2005, the Mexican National Middleweight Title was vacated.

Between 2012 and 2018, several tag team titles changed hands on August 3rd. Atlantis and Diamante Azul won the CMLL World Tag Team Titles in 2012, while Davey Richards and Eddie Edwards captured the ROH World Tag Team Titles in 2013. La Silueta won the CMLL/REINA International Junior Title in 2014, and Volador Jr. won the NWA Historic Welterweight Title in 2018.

The year 2019 saw mixed championship activity. Tessa Blanchard won the AAA Reina de Reinas Title on August 3rd, though the title was also recorded as vacated that same year. Lady Maravilla and Villano III Jr. won the AAA World Mixed Tag Team Titles. Similar to the WWE Hardcore Championship in 2002, the WWE 24/7 Title changed hands twice in 2020 under its 24/7 rule, held by Akira Tozawa and R-Truth on this date.

August 3rd, 2024 proved to be an exceptionally busy day for WWE, with four major titles changing hands. Nia Jax won the WWE Women's Title, Bron Breakker won the WWE Intercontinental Title, Gunther won the WWE World Heavyweight Title, and LA Knight won the WWE United States Title. This concentration of title changes in a single day represented an unusually active period for WWE championship transitions.

Most recently, August 3rd, 2025 continued the tradition with Cody Rhodes winning the WWE Championship at SummerSlam 2025 and Hideki Suzuki and Kengo Mashimo winning the AJPW World Tag Team Titles. Cody Rhodes's championship victory marked a significant moment in recent WWE history and further cemented August 3rd as a consistently important date for professional wrestling title changes across multiple decades and promotions.
