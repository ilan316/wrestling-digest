Spotlight on Cameraman Austin Cowen's Career Behind the Scenes

Some of the most important people at an independent wrestling show never step through the ropes. For nearly nine years, Austin Cowen has been one of the people responsible for determining how EMERGE Wrestling looks to everyone watching through a screen. Cowen works as one of EMERGE’s roaming camera operators, moving around ringside throughout matches while trying to anticipate dives, signature moves, facial expressions, crowd reactions and the moments that can transform a wrestling match from a collection of moves into a story. He has also increasingly become involved in editing EMERGE’s completed shows, giving him a perspective that extends from the moment an image enters the camera to the moment a finished event reaches viewers online. In an exclusive interview, Cowen discussed how an accidental trip to an event began a nine-year relationship with the promotion, learning wrestling psychology through the camera, the danger of working at ringside, why facial expressions matter, the enormous amount of time required to edit a show and why he believes production can help independent wrestling reach people who may never enter the building. His story also adds another chapter to an ongoing look at the people responsible for making independent wrestling happen through Indie Wrestling MATTERS.

Cowen’s path into wrestling production began while he was attending Ball State University. He and fellow EMERGE roaming camera operator Ethan met through a student group centered around professional wrestling called the University Wrestling Club, or UWC. The group also included Justin Myers, who later competed for EMERGE, and future EMERGE World Champion Paragon. Ethan was already involved in professional wrestling and frequently talked about the shows he worked. Cowen eventually decided to see one for himself, although the decision was almost accidental. He had originally planned to visit a friend, mixed up the timing and missed the opportunity. Rather than simply going home, he drove to Columbus, Indiana, to see Ethan and attend an event. That event was EMERGE 23: Downtown Throwdown 3 in August 2017. Historical records confirm that the show featured Raven, who teamed with Karma and Khris Kaliber against Bomb Shelter.

Eventually, Cowen found himself on the floor working as a roaming camera operator, noting that nine years later he is still there doing the thing. Cowen had no formal production experience when he began. What he did have was an interest in wrestling and a growing fascination with how wrestling could feel different when experienced through the lens of a camera. That distinction became increasingly important as he learned the job. The promotion's development has been documented dating back to its first numbered event in 2015, with its historical record now stretching toward its milestone 100th show.

Cowen said one of the biggest misconceptions about filming professional wrestling is that the camera operator simply stands at ringside and watches the match. His experience is almost the opposite. While the audience is following the action, Cowen is evaluating framing, positioning and where the next important moment may occur. He explained that he does not watch the matches per se, but is instead making sure that his shots are in frame, looking for spots, and figuring out how each shot can be better.
