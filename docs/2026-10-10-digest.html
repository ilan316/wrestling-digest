🔶 AEW News

🔥 AEW announces 3-hour Dynamite and Winter Is Coming special in Mexico

All Elite Wrestling is preparing for a busy December, with multiple special episodes of Dynamite planned before the company wraps up 2026. The schedule includes an international stop for Winter Is Coming, with another notable change coming just a week earlier. Before heading south of the border, AEW will visit Laredo, Texas, for a three-hour live episode of Dynamite on Wednesday, December 2. The event will take place at Sames Auto Arena, marking the promotion's first visit to Laredo since February 2023, which was also the company's only previous event in the city. Early access premium seating starts Tuesday, October 13, and tickets go on sale Friday, October 16. Andrade El Idolo, Persephone, Thunder Rosa, and Bandido are featured on the official promotional graphic for the Laredo event.

One week after the Texas stop, AEW will make its debut in Monterrey, Mexico, for the annual Winter Is Coming special. The Wednesday, December 9 edition of Dynamite will take place live from Arena Monterrey, while a Collision taping is scheduled for the same evening to air on Saturday, December 12. Both shows will carry the Winter Is Coming branding. While AEW previously held Grand Slam Mexico events in Mexico City in 2025 and 2026, this marks the promotion's first show in Mexico outside of the capital city, expanding the reach of Tony Khan's goal to bring AEW to more Mexican cities. The announcement was initially shared by FOX Sports MX. Tickets for the Monterrey event go on presale Tuesday, October 13, before general sales begin Friday, October 16, via SuperBoletos. Promotional materials for the Monterrey shows feature Andrade El Idolo, Bandido, Persephone, Thunder Rosa, and The New Level members Austin Creed and Kofi.

No individual matches have been officially signed yet, but the 2026 Continental Classic tournament will feature matches in Monterrey, adding another important stop to the annual competition as it progresses toward the Worlds End pay-per-view on December 19 in Minneapolis, Minnesota. Before then, AEW has its WrestleDream pay-per-view in Orlando, Florida, on October 17 and Full Gear in Phoenix, Arizona, on November 14. Winter Is Coming has delivered several significant moments since its 2020 debut, including Sting's AEW arrival and Kenny Omega winning the AEW World Championship from Jon Moxley. The special has also featured a 60-minute draw between Hangman Page and Bryan Danielson in 2021, Toni Storm's return in 2024, Samoa Joe defending the AEW World Championship against Eddie Kingston, and The Babes of Wrath becoming the first-ever AEW Women's World Tag Team Champions, alongside major championship bouts and Continental Classic action in subsequent years. With a three-hour Dynamite in Texas followed by two branded shows from Mexico, AEW’s December calendar is taking shape to set the stage for a busy month on the road to Worlds End.

🔥 Max Caster removed from AEW roster page following exit

Max Caster's run with All Elite Wrestling has officially come to an end, with the former multiple-time champion being removed from the company's official roster page following the expiration of his contract at the beginning of October. The promotion typically updates its roster page when talent are no longer with the company, making this removal a significant development. Caster had been absent from AEW television for several months prior to his departure.

Caster is already preparing for his next chapter in the wrestling industry. TNA Wrestling announced that he will make his promotional debut at Bound for Glory 2026 on Sunday, October 11, where he will face Moose after answering the former TNA World Champion's open challenge. Caster answered Moose's challenge through a rap posted on social media, bringing his trademark musical approach into the buildup to his first TNA match. While this gives him a confirmed destination for the weekend, there has been no confirmation that he has signed a longer-term deal with TNA.

While Caster is moving on, the status of his former tag team partner Anthony Bowens remains unclear. Bowens also became a free agent when his contract expired at the start of October, but unlike Caster, Bowens' current contractual status remains unconfirmed and he is still listed on the AEW roster page. Bowens remained active on AEW programming leading up to his contract expiration, with his most recent appearance coming at All In in late August. Reports indicated that neither Caster nor Bowens had been involved in discussions regarding new contracts as their deals approached expiration, and there was no initial word on whether AEW intended to make new offers to either performer.

The two wrestlers enjoyed considerable success during their time together as The Acclaimed, capturing both the AEW World Tag Team Championships and the AEW World Trios Championships, holding the tag team titles for one notable reign in 2022 and 2023. With Caster heading to TNA and Bowens' future unresolved, their paths now appear to be heading in different directions, making a future reunion doubtful as the next verse of their careers unfolds.

Juice Robinson reflects on leaving WWE developmental system in 2014

AEW star Juice Robinson recently recalled the turning point in his career that led to him leaving the WWE developmental system in 2014 and early 2015. There is an alternate universe where the Bang Bang Gang member never became an international star for AEW or New Japan, and instead spent his career in WWE, where he originally wrestled as CJ Parker during the early days of NXT. Robinson ultimately left WWE in 2015 for NJPW. During a recent appearance on the What Happened When podcast, he explained that he had been looking to leave as early as late 2014.

His departure was heavily motivated by being around other NXT talents who had established strong reputations outside the corporate umbrella, such as Kevin Owens, Sami Zayn, PAC, Seth Rollins, and Jon Moxley. With all of them having succeeded outside of WWE, Robinson felt it was necessary for him to get the same diverse experience they had acquired. At the time, he felt that he really just needed to go out and be a wrestler, noting that he never truly had that independent experience. He went straight from making no money on the indies to being paid while training in the developmental system.

Robinson felt he had enough experience and had been in the WWE developmental system long enough that he should just go out and try to do it on his own. Listening to stories about how wrestling operated in England, Japan, and Mexico fueled his desire to travel and just wrestle. He admitted he was overtraining and was completely over the restrictive nature of the system.

Fortunately, Robinson was aided in his exit by WWE Performance Center trainer Matt Bloom, with whom he shared a good working relationship. Having previously worked extensively with NJPW himself, Bloom connected Robinson with the NJPW office, effectively putting him on the path that led him to where he is today. Bloom introduced him, NJPW watched his work, and when they asked if he wanted to go there, Robinson immediately agreed. He had that major opportunity set up just as he was leaving WWE. Alongside Japan, he knew he was heading to England and had several independent dates lined up. Robinson simply wanted to get out of the system, feeling it was finally time to be free.

Will Ospreay discusses using PAC tribute move on Dynamite

AEW World Champion Will Ospreay recently opened up about utilizing a special tribute move during his match against Ricochet on the September 30 episode of AEW Dynamite. The bout was designed to honor their late friend Pac, a man whom both Ospreay and Ricochet have credited as being a major influence on their careers. On a night filled with tributes and emotion, Ospreay dug deep into his bag of tricks to pay homage to Pac in a way only the AEW World Champion could, hitting Pac's iconic Black Arrow finishing move.

Ospreay discussed the experience during an appearance on Speedball Mike Bailey's video blog, which was recorded backstage at Dynamite. He admitted that the maneuver required an immense physical effort and noted that he had not performed the move in eight years, back when he was significantly lighter. Despite the physical toll and the lingering competitive tension between him and Ricochet, Ospreay felt compelled to execute the tribute properly.

That was a tough one, Ospreay stated in the video. I ain't done that in eight years is the last time I did it and I was ten kilos lighter, I don't know what that is in pounds. But that was one of the most difficult matches I've ever done in my life, and when it comes to myself and Trevor, obviously there is always hard feelings between us because egos always get in the way and you always want to be sitting on top of the throne and I know he wants this championship. But to be able to put our differences aside and do a great tribute to a very good friend of the pair of us, I wanted to do it right.

Leading up to the broadcast, many observers pointed out that Ospreay and Ricochet were the only people who could realistically hit the Black Arrow due to how difficult it is to pull off. Ospreay ultimately managed to deliver the move to punctuate the emotional encounter. Bailey closed out the backstage clip by praising the match as a perfect tribute to Pac.

🔄 המשך Dutch Mantell gives thoughts on Sting's son Steven Borden in AEW

Dutch Mantell has weighed in on the recent in-ring injury situation involving Steven Borden, offering insight into what might be driving the second-generation star.

Speaking on his podcast, Mantell discussed Borden's approach to his matches and why he may be pushing himself so hard. While stressing that he was offering his own perspective rather than speaking directly for Borden, Mantell suggested that the young wrestler is fighting against the perception that things will be handed to him simply because of who his famous father is.

According to Mantell, Borden likely wants to go out and bust his butt to prove he belongs in the industry on his own merits. Although he congratulated Borden for having that kind of dedicated drive, Mantell also expressed concern over the high-risk moves a newcomer might attempt to validate themselves. He emphasized that someone backstage needs to talk Borden off the ledge and help him better judge the danger of overly ambitious spots before he suffers a more serious setback.

🔄 המשך Dave Meltzer assesses potential entrants for AEW WrestleDream Clockwork Carnage match

Dave Meltzer has shared his speculation on who might fill out the remaining slots for the upcoming eight-person Clockwork Carnage match at AEW WrestleDream.

With Will Ospreay already guaranteed a spot after retaining his title and Samoa Joe qualifying by defeating Jack Perry, the field is beginning to take shape. An upcoming match between Speedball Mike Bailey and Lee Moriarty will determine the next participant.

Looking at the rest of the roster for the final five spots, Meltzer pointed to a group of logical contenders that includes Adam Copeland, Darby Allin, Claudio Castagnoli, Nick Wayne, Gabe Kidd, Josh Alexander, Konosuke Takeshita, Orange Cassidy, Ricochet, Rush, and Wardlow. While Meltzer did not detail the specific reasoning behind each pick, he did note that Rush recently stated on social media that he does not expect to wrestle for AEW in the near future.

The inaugural match takes place on October 17 at the Addition Arena in Orlando, Florida, where the chaotic two-stage structure will ultimately crown the AEW World Champion.

🔄 המשך Update regarding cause of death for PAC / Neville

Following the release of initial police findings regarding the passing of Benjamin Satterley, new details have emerged surrounding the timeline for the official medical conclusions.

The cause of death will not be finalized until additional studies are completed. Toxicology results are expected to take anywhere from several weeks to a few months to process. Furthermore, the Cook County Medical Examiner's Office has not yet released preliminary autopsy findings, with the high-profile nature of the case potentially contributing to the delay.

While earlier police reports detailed the scene where Satterley was found unresponsive in a rental car in a Chicago parking lot, investigators still have not released a definitive explanation for what transpired prior to his passing. Authorities continue to process the remaining evidence and test results before making any final determinations public.
