However, the Don Callis Family continued to add fresh members to the match as Doyle and Davis soon joined the fight and overwhelmed their opponents. Matt Jackson brought a trash can into the match, but Doyle powerbombed Matt onto the can and continued to attack the Young Bucks.

Adam Copeland then entered with two steel chairs and immediately started swinging them at everyone in sight. Copeland also put Davis through a table, but Doyle and Davis soon joined forces to throw him into a chair wedged in the corner. Wardlow entered next and took out many of his opponents before hitting a Swanton Bomb on Copeland through a table. Steven Borden then arrived and brought the fight back to the Don Callis Family. Borden also introduced thumbtacks into the match and tried to send Knight onto them, but Davis and Doyle stopped him before powerbombing Borden onto the thumbtacks.

Rob Van Dam on the AEW product: “I think the inmates are running the asylum”

While speaking on Wrestling with Rip Rogers, WWE Hall of Famer Rob Van Dam opened up about his issues with modern professional wrestling, offering specific critiques regarding match pacing and company culture.

Addressing how modern wrestlers execute their moves, RVD explained that if the setup for a desired maneuver takes so long that it ruins the flow and destroys the feeling of competition, the move should be abandoned to avoid exposing the business. He noted that this issue is particularly obvious among younger talent, specifically pointing toward the smaller of the major promotions. Acknowledging he sometimes gets heat for allegedly bashing AEW, RVD clarified his perspective by stating that the promotion operates under a dynamic where the inmates are running the prison and the boys have more control and power.

RVD expanded on this metaphor, stating that the inmates are running the asylum and that AEW is built around wrestlers getting to do more of what they want. He explained that this freedom creates more variety, but in the big picture, it results in a style that to him is more amateurish and comparable to backyard matches. This he contrasted with WWE, which he feels is returning to telling stories with wrestling, keeping it simple, and making it make sense after things were getting out of hand for a while.

The veteran performer also expressed his longstanding distaste for certain extreme elements seen in the industry, mentioning instances where someone burned a house down or put staples in their face. While noting these types of stunts have been around for a long time, he emphasized they have never been his preference. Addressing anyone calling him a hypocrite, RVD concluded by stating that he is actually the opposite and has always remained consistent in his views.
