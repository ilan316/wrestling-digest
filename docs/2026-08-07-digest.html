🔥 AEW Grand Slam Mexico 2026

AEW Grand Slam Mexico 2026 delivered a packed wrestling program from the historic Arena Mexico, one of the most prestigious venues in professional wrestling. The event featured eight matches on the broadcast with a combined in-ring bell-to-bell time of 1 hour, 42 minutes, and 35 seconds across an overall show length of approximately 2 hours and 53 minutes.

Every match on the broadcast exceeded the 10-minute mark, demonstrating AEW's commitment to giving wrestlers adequate time to tell their stories. The longest match of the evening was the Street Fight between Will Ospreay and Mark Davis, which went 17 minutes, with Ospreay earning the victory. The second-longest bout was Kyle Fletcher defending the AEW International Championship against Mike Bailey, a hard-fought contest that lasted 16 minutes and 45 seconds, with Fletcher retaining his title. The shortest match on the show was Persephone's victory over Kris Statlander at 10 minutes and 5 seconds.

The opening match saw the Death Riders, comprised of Jon Moxley, PAC, Wheeler Yuta, and Daniel Garcia, defeat Adam Copeland, Christian Cage, and The Young Bucks in an eight-man tag team match that went 11 minutes and 50 seconds. Copeland took the loss in a way that advanced his storyline heading into his upcoming AEW Tag Team Championship defense against The Young Bucks at All In on August 30 at Wembley Stadium in London.

For Copeland, competing at Arena Mexico represented the fulfillment of a major wrestling goal. The legendary athlete had listed Arena Mexico as one of the final items on his wrestling bucket list before his eventual retirement. After 34 years in the business, Copeland finally stepped into what many call the Wrestling Cathedral. It had been 18 years since his last performance in Mexico, and the significance of the moment was not lost on him.

Following the show, Copeland took to Instagram to reflect on the historic night, posting: "What. A. Night. Finally, finally after 34 years I was privileged to step into The Wrestling Cathedral. Arena Mexico. And man, Mexico you did not disappoint. I felt the vibrations in my bones when I came out. It'd been 18 years since I last performed in Mexico and to finally do it in those hallowed halls, the perfect way to cap off my love affair with Lucha Libre and Mexico. I'll take last night to the grave with me. Gracias Mexico."

Copeland's wrestling bucket list includes several other iconic venues and opponents. Beyond his Arena Mexico achievement and his upcoming All In defense at Wembley Stadium, he has named Maple Leaf Gardens and the Tokyo Dome as venues he hopes to perform in before retirement. He also listed Kenny Omega as a dream opponent he still hopes to face. Notably, Copeland is scheduled to work a show at Maple Leaf Gardens, now called the Mattany Athletic Centre, for Maple Leaf Pro Wrestling in October.

In other championship action at Grand Slam Mexico, Adam Page, El Bandido, and Brody King captured the AEW World Trios Championship, defeating the defending champions The Demand, which consisted of Bishop Kaun, Ricochet, and Toa Liona. This match lasted 13 minutes and 40 seconds.

The card also featured a Casino Gauntlet qualifier three-way match where Andrade El Ídolo defeated Komander and Tommaso Ciampa in 11 minutes and 10 seconds. A six-woman tag team match saw Alex Windsor, Jamie Hayter, and Willow Nightingale defeat Lena Kross, Megan Bayne, and Mercedes Moné in 11 minutes and 30 seconds. Additionally, Darby Allin, Chris Jericho, and Místico defeated The Don Callis Family, represented by Hechicero, Kazuchika Okada, and Kevin Knight, in a six-man tag team match lasting 10 minutes and 35 seconds.

The average age of wrestlers on the card was 36 years old, reflecting a blend of experienced veterans and talented performers. The wrestling community received the show positively, with coverage and analysis noting that despite its lengthy runtime, the event delivered quality matches and storytelling throughout. The Bryan & Vinny Show and Wrestling Observer Radio both provided detailed recaps and analysis of the Grand Slam Mexico event, with Bryan Alvarez also filing a comprehensive subscriber report on the show.

🔥 Buddy Matthews AEW Return Cleared

Buddy Matthews has finally received the news he has been waiting for throughout his lengthy recovery period. The AEW star has confirmed that he has earned medical clearance to return to in-ring action after dealing with a serious ankle injury that sidelined him for nearly 18 months.

Matthews suffered the injury during his match against Kazuchika Okada for the Continental Championship at AEW Grand Slam: Australia on February 15, 2025. The injury appeared to occur during his entrance to the ring and forced him out of action completely. After dealing with surgery, nerve damage, numbness, and extensive rehabilitation, Matthews has finally reached the end of his recovery journey.

The AEW star shared the significant update in a video posted to Instagram on August 6, 2026. The video gave fans a behind-the-scenes look at the final steps he had to take before getting back into the ring. He showed his medical tests, rehab work, and some of the physical and mental challenges he faced during his recovery. The video also explained how medical clearance was the final hurdle standing between him and an in-ring return.

In his video's description, Matthews wrote: "Before stepping back into the ring, there was one final hurdle. After suffering a serious ankle injury at AEW Grand Slam: Australia, Buddy Matthews begins the final stage of his comeback—getting medically cleared to compete again. This video follows the process behind the scenes, from medical evaluations and rehabilitation to the moment he receives the news every athlete waits to hear. It's a look at the physical and mental challenges of returning from injury, and what it takes to get back to doing what you love. If you've ever faced setbacks, injuries, or doubted whether you'd make it back, this one's for you."

Matthews also dedicated the video to anyone who has dealt with an injury or setback and wondered if they would ever get back to doing what they love. Following the announcement of his clearance, Matthews expressed his readiness to return with the powerful statement: "I'm cleared, and you should be scared."

In May 2026, Matthews had appeared for an Australian promotion and got physical in the ring, noting at that time that he expected to be officially cleared to compete by October. That expectation has now been realized ahead of schedule.

The AEW star had to go through significant challenges during his recovery, learning how to walk again after his devastating injury. His comeback process demonstrates the physical and mental resilience required for professional wrestlers to return from serious injuries.

There is no word yet on a specific return date for AEW or when Matthews will step back into the ring for his first match since February 2025. Much has changed in AEW during the time Matthews has been away, and his return could potentially impact the company's roster dynamics and storyline directions.

Rob Van Dam Says Becoming a Father at 54 Completely Changed His Life and Wrestling Perspective

Rob Van Dam spent decades risking his body inside the ring, but becoming a father for the first time gave him a brand-new reason to protect himself. The WWE Hall of Famer says his twin daughters completely changed his priorities and made him look at his future in a different way.

Rob Van Dam Blasts Modern Wrestlers for Copying the Same Moves in Every Match

Beyond his personal life changes, Rob Van Dam also weighed in on current wrestling trends, offering criticism of how modern performers approach their craft. RVD loves the insane athleticism in modern wrestling, but he is sick of watching the same moves get recycled over and over again.

The WWE Hall of Famer gave today's wrestlers credit for pulling off moves that would have seemed impossible during his prime. RVD specifically praised the reverse hurricanrana and the gymnastic ability of performers doing twisting shooting star presses and other high-risk spots.

"When I see a move like that, I'm impressed, and I think, 'Holy crap, that's a great moment. Take your bow. Connect with the crowd.' However, RVD said that excitement disappears when every wrestler on the card starts using the same offense. In his eyes, moves that were once special have been watered down because fans see them several times during the same show. RVD then explained that his biggest problem is not the athleticism itself. It is the lack of individuality. He said too many wrestlers are borrowing moves from one another, leaving matches filled with repeated RKOs and Destroyers.

"For me, it's too much seeing everybody do everyone else's moves. It seems like there have to be three RKOs in every single match and two Oklahoma Destroyers—or whatever that one move is called. It just seems like that move isn't even that special anymore."

RVD understands why wrestlers keep doing those moves. The crowd still reacts, so performers know they can use them to get an easy response. That does not mean RVD enjoys watching the same spot repeatedly.

"The people still pop, and that's why they do it, because they're just going for the pop now. But for me, when I see it—just because of my position in the business, I guess—I'm not looking at it like a fan, because I'm not. It doesn't strike a happy chord with me."

The former WWE Champion said the constant repetition also makes it nearly impossible for any wrestler to protect a move as a legitimate match-ending weapon.

"It's like, 'Dude, that's the eighth one I've seen tonight.' How can someone ever have that as a finishing move if every single person on the card does it?"

RVD built his career around a move set that immediately separated him from everyone else. Now, he believes modern wrestlers are hurting themselves by chasing a quick crowd reaction instead of protecting their offense and developing something fans can identify as their own.
