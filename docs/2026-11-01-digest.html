🔥 Wrestlers added to AEW Women's Blood & Guts match

AEW has announced a late change to the upcoming Blood & Guts episode of Dynamite, with two prominent names being officially added to the women's match. The company previously held a Fright Night Dynamite episode headlined by a TBS Championship match as Queen Aminata challenged Persephone for the gold. Aminata ended up picking up the upset win, capturing the first title of her AEW career.

On a subsequent episode of Collision, a backstage promo aired featuring Persephone reacting to her defeat alongside a warning to the new champion. Persephone declared that she wants her title back and noted that the two teams for this year's women's Blood & Guts match were currently unfinished, with one spot remaining on each side. She challenged Aminata to join her in the match the following week to settle the score.

Tony Khan has since confirmed the change, making the bout a six-on-six encounter. Persephone has joined the heel team consisting of Divine Dominion featuring Megan Bayne and Lena Kross, and the Triangle of Madness featuring Thekla, Julia Hart, and Skye Blue. Meanwhile, Queen Aminata joins the babyface squad featuring Willow Nightingale, Wrathlander consisting of Kris Statlander and Harley Cameron, and the Brawling Birds consisting of Jamie Hayter and Alex Windsor.

Internal tension surrounds the babyface crew as Aminata joins their ranks. Wrathlander remains annoyed that the Brawling Birds are absent from certain alignments, and trust issues linger because Wrathlander challenged the Birds for the women's tag titles at Full Gear. Nonetheless, Willow Nightingale has been doing her best to get everyone on the same page, and Aminata is ready to make a splash after missing last year's Blood & Guts match.

The villains appear to be a much more cohesive bunch, cutting an intimidating team promo closed out by Thekla playing on the Blood & Guts tagline of one cage, two rings, and three words with a blunt declaration. The Triangle of Madness and Divine Dominion put in heavy work on Collision to build their momentum. Julia Hart spit black mist to help the Triangle retain the Artist of Stardom Championship over Hazuki, Koguma, and Fuwa-Chan, while Megan Bayne and Lena Kross obliterated Nixi XS and Leela Feist in a dominant squash match.

Meanwhile, the men's Blood & Guts match remains unaltered, featuring Will Ospreay leading Adam Copeland, Steven Borden, the Young Bucks, and Darby Allin against the Don Callis Family squad of Kyle Fletcher, Kazuchika Okada, Mark Davis, Jake Doyle, Kevin Knight, and Wardlow.

Despite the star-studded lineups, critics have pointed out similarities between AEW and WWE regarding the build to their respective cage war matches. Both major promotions have faced criticism for a seeming lack of care, originality, and TV time dedicated to building their women's multi-person matches compared to the men. AEW's match largely features the same names that competed last year in a story with minimal television buildup, mirroring WWE's reliance on recycled angles for Survivor Series War Games. Both matches are expected to be stellar given the immense talent involved, but the parallel builds highlight a persistent disparity in how major wrestling companies treat their marquee women's storylines.

Adam Copeland on wrestling in Maple Leaf Gardens vs Wembley

AEW star Adam Copeland has explained how wrestling at Maple Leaf Gardens has meant more to him than wrestling at any other venue. Copeland is winding down his legendary career and has ticked off many items on his pro wrestling bucket list, one of which was wrestling at Maple Leaf Gardens, a venue where he watched many wrestling shows in his youth. After wrestling at the venue for Maple Leaf Pro Wrestling, the AEW star looked back fondly on the experience and reflected on how much it meant to him while speaking to Maple Leaf Pro Wrestling's YouTube channel.

For me, this, Maple Leaf Gardens, was easily the most important venue for me to wrestle in, over any other, over Wembley Stadium, over any of them, Copeland said. Maple Leaf Gardens has always been my one place that I wanted to get to. And here we are today. No, I never thought I'd wrestle in Maple Leaf Gardens. Between the fact that I had retired for nine years, and then even once I got my career back, just with the landscape of what wrestling is and where I was working, Maple Leaf Gardens was a pipe dream. I thought if I were going to ever wrestle there, I'd have to run my own show.

The dream of wrestling at the venue that holds so many great memories for him seemed within reach once he joined AEW. He said that Scott D'Amore, former TNA president and owner of Maple Leaf Pro Wrestling, played a key role in helping him realize that dream.

And then Scott D'Amore and I, we started talking and realized, wait, this is now a possibility now that I'm with AEW, he said. We could actually do this.

Copeland announced that 2027 will be his final year in wrestling, and he seems to have wrestled at every venue he has dreamed of, with the Tokyo Dome potentially being the only one left.

Bryce Remsburg compares refereeing in indies vs AEW

Bryce Remsburg Compares Referee Role On The Indies Vs. AEW

Since the first AEW Double or Nothing took place in May 2019, Bryce Remsburg has been a constant presence in AEW's referee core, while also serving as a travel manager for the promotion. Remsburg's consistency as a ref is no surprise, given he had been one of the most notable referees on the independent wrestling circuit for over a decade before signing with AEW. To most, that would seemingly make his transition from the indies to a major league wrestling promotion pretty easy. As Remsburg would explain during an appearance on The Certified Mark Show, however, it was anything but smooth.

Refereeing on the independents and refereeing on television for AEW are two different beasts, Remsburg said. I did seventeen years on the indies from 2002 to 2019. And I feel like I had kind of gotten my bachelor's degree. But when AEW started, just really, I began my master's program.

According to Remsburg, the key difference turned out being having people in his ear, literally, as the earpiece Remsburg wears during matches allows him to receive instruction from AEW owner Tony Khan, agents, and television producers. That level of communication was new enough for Remsburg that he didn't get entirely comfortable in his new role until several months into AEW's tenure.

It took me a while to feel like I was being present in the moment because I was hearing things and talking, Remsburg said. It definitely...I am not saying that a global pandemic was a good thing in any shape, way, or form. But I will say we did 23 weeks of Dynamite before COVID happened and we went to no crowds. And then doing those Dynamite's in front of no crowds, sometimes they were taped in Jacksonville, really gave me an opportunity to start feeling confident for refereeing for television.

Orange Cassidy details worst injury of his career

AEW star Orange Cassidy recently detailed the worst injuries of his professional wrestling career during a recent appearance on The Rizzuto Show.

Cassidy recalled suffering a broken orbital bone and a severe sinus injury during a match that left him physically ill and vomiting blood. Explaining the gruesome aftermath, Cassidy said that when the sinus ruptures, the blood drains down your throat and into your stomach, which he was unaware of at the time. He felt extremely nauseous and went behind a small venue where they were wrestling. He leaned over, and a friend captured a video that he humorously compared to The Exorcist because blood was shooting out. It was just straight blood because all of it was just going down in his stomach and came back up. He explained that he took a hard knee to the face, got knocked out, and left blood everywhere.

Cassidy also shared the terrifying details of a previous shoulder dislocation that differed significantly from a typical joint separation. He noted that when people think of a dislocation, the shoulder usually pops outward, but in his case, his shoulder went directly into his body. The medical procedure to fix it required him to be completely knocked out while two nurses wrapped bed sheets around his arm and pulled while the doctor held his body to extract the joint. He remained out of it for a while afterward because they had to heavily sedate him for the procedure.

On the television front, Cassidy recently teamed with Jay White to face The Death Riders on the October 21 episode of Dynamite. Additionally, he is scheduled to team with Kyle O'Reilly and Roderick Strong to take on The Cut on tonight's episode of AEW Collision.

Austin, the day he graduated, walked from the stage, up the balcony where I was sitting, handed me his diploma, and goes, When do we start?
