🔥 AEW Fight Forever video game financial losses

AEW Fight Forever Cost Company at Least $30 Million in Losses

AEW Fight Forever may have been a much bigger financial hit than anyone realized, and that could explain why Tony Khan hasn't rushed into making another video game. Insider Gaming executive editor Mike Straw revealed that sources told him AEW took a "massive loss" of at least $30 million on Fight Forever after development dragged on longer than planned and the game failed to hit its sales targets.

According to Straw's reporting, the extended development timeline was a significant factor in the enormous losses. Fight Forever was originally expected to arrive in 2022, but multiple delays pushed the release all the way to June 2023 around the time of AEW x NJPW Forbidden Door. All that extra development time came with a substantial price tag. "When games take that long, they take more to make," Straw explained in his report.

That wasn't the only problem facing the project. Straw said Fight Forever ultimately sold around half of what AEW initially expected, leaving the company with a huge gap between what it spent developing the game and what it brought back in revenue. The combination of ballooning development costs and disappointing sales numbers created the perfect storm for financial disaster.

The poor performance puts a very different spin on all the questions Tony Khan has received about a Fight Forever sequel. Khan has repeatedly left the door open for AEW to do more in the gaming world, but there hasn't been another full-scale console game announced since Fight Forever's release.

There is also another issue standing in the way of a quick return to gaming. AEW currently does not have an agreement with Yuke's, the developer behind Fight Forever. However, AEW owns the game engine, which would give the company the option of taking that technology elsewhere and working with another studio if they chose to pursue a new project.

Straw said AEW is still interested in revisiting video games somewhere down the road, but there doesn't appear to be anything locked in right now. Speaking with sources who were privy to the information, Straw reported that the company is not planning anything imminent. He also addressed rumors that AEW had been speaking with a third party about licensing its wrestling game rights. Back at WrestleMania XL, Cody Rhodes defeated Roman Reigns to finish his story in the Bloodline Rules main event, winning the Undisputed WWE Championship for the first time. The match was praised as one of the greatest main events in the history of the event, with the moment the sole defining moment of WWE over the past few years.

The main events of WrestleMania 39 and WrestleMania XL have been often compared to Marvel's Avengers Infinity War and Avengers Endgame, with years of storytelling coming together in a major moment that rewarded long term fans of each respective product. In the early years of the Paul Levesque era, WWE's commitment to long-term storytelling was arguably WWE's greatest strength, with the card for WrestleMania 39 being planned out as far back as the early Fall of 2022, and all naturally coming together.

However, WWE has been unable to reach the heights of WrestleMania XL in the years since, and as more and more questionable decisions are made, one thing is now pretty clear: WWE has forgot how to finish the story.

The Bloodline Story Saved WWE

In 2020, Roman Reigns made his return to WWE television after taking several months off due to the shut down caused by the COVID-19 pandemic, but he did so as a heel, a role many fans had hoped to see him in for a number of years. Following his return, Reigns was incredibly hands on with his creative after recent frustrations with his booking, which included such 2019 Vince McMahon genius as the Big Dog mascot and being covered in dog food by Baron Corbin. It's really hard to imagine why Reigns may have been frustrated.

Reigns' return would lead to him taking on a new moniker in WWE: The Tribal Chief, and a few weeks later during his 2020 rivalry with Jey Uso, the Bloodline storyline officially began. The story would make for great television for the rest of 2020, all of 2021 and the first half of 2022, but it wasn't until the change in creative regime in the Summer of 2022, and Sami Zayn's major addition to the storyline, that we would start getting the cinema we all remember.

Sami joining the group, Jimmy Uso immediately accepting him and Sami having to win over Jey Uso over the next few months, leading to War Games where Sami would choose the Bloodline over his own best friend Kevin Owens, before ultimately pulling the trigger on Reigns at the Royal Rumble when Roman forced him to assault his best friend. This all led to the perfect crescendo at WrestleMania 39, where Zayn and Owens defeated the Usos to win the Undisputed Tag Team Championship to end their record breaking run with the gold.

What made the moment so great was that, after years of Vince McMahon tearing up scripts on the fly, WWE finally saw out a long term storyline plan, with viewers being rewarded for watching the shows weekly.

Cody felt like the perfect person to dethrone Reigns for the belt at WrestleMania, and it was clear what WWE was setting up for: Cody Rhodes would return to the main event of WrestleMania a year later and avenge this loss to finally defeat Roman Reigns, and after the past eight months, WWE had earned that goodwill to fans. 🔥 Historical wrestling matches and events

August 20 has proven to be a significant date throughout professional wrestling history, hosting a variety of memorable events and title changes spanning several decades and promotions.

The most prominent match associated with August 20 occurred in 1995 at the IWA Kawasaki Dream event held at Kawasaki Baseball Stadium. This show featured the King of the Death Match tournament, with the final pitting Cactus Jack against Terry Funk in a no ropes, exploding barbed wire, exploding ring time-bomb match contested before 28,757 fans. Cactus Jack emerged victorious, pinning Terry Funk in 13 minutes and 21 seconds to claim the honorary title of King of the Death Matches.

The match was noted for its exceptional brutality. Cactus Jack took a devastating bump off a ladder into barbed wire that nearly caused serious injury to his remaining ear. Terry Funk suffered serious burns when his arm caught fire from one of the explosions. The bout was designed to culminate with the ring exploding at the ten-minute mark with an explosion billed as more powerful than anything Atsushi Onita had previously executed, with Jack positioned outside the ring for safety while Funk remained inside. However, this climactic explosion failed to detonate properly, resulting in heavy crowd booing. The wrestlers responded by taking increasingly insane bumps to recover the crowd's interest and successfully brought them back for the finish.

Dave Meltzer's coverage noted that this was Funk's first match in Japan since his famous 1993 encounter with Atsushi Onita in the same stadium, and the victory was expected to significantly elevate Cactus Jack's reputation in Japan. This tour marked Funk's return to wrestling after he had withdrawn from all commitments in June due to a severe back injury. Following the tour, Funk reported that his back held up well during competition, though he planned additional rest. While some observers wondered if this might represent Funk's final match, he indicated his intention to tour Japan three times over the subsequent year period.

The decision was reportedly driven largely by WWE and TKO's desire to get AAA in front of potential television partners. With a number of network and sports industry executives expected to be in Las Vegas for the boxing event, the idea was to bring AAA to the city beginning Friday night in hopes of attracting additional attention to the promotion.

TKO is said to be pleased with AAA's rollout over the past year, though there is reportedly interest in finding the promotion a more permanent television home outside of YouTube. Holding TripleMania in Las Vegas during a weekend when industry executives will already be in town could provide an opportunity to make an impression on potential partners.

The expanded weekend is also said to be creating a demanding schedule for WWE's production team. SmackDown will take place Friday from Mexico City, followed by AAA that night, Zuffa Boxing on Saturday, the second night of TripleMania on Sunday and a major episode of Raw on Monday.

Several wrestlers have also reportedly been informed that they will be working both nights of TripleMania, differing from the approach WWE typically takes with its two-night premium live events.

Je'Von Evans Says WWE Tried To 'Surprise' Him With Randy Orton Match Announcement

Awesome Kong (Kharma) Reflects On Pregnancy Announcement On WWE TV, Possibility Of A Return

AWESOME KONG REFLECTS ON PREGNANCY ANNOUNCEMENT ON WWE TV, POSSIBILITY OF A RETURN

Awesome Kong has opened up about her experience with WWE during one of the most significant moments of her career. Kong, who performed as Kharma in WWE, made her official debut in April 2011 but was forced to step away just weeks later after learning she was pregnant. The timing proved difficult for everyone involved, particularly given the heavy investment Triple H had made in bringing her to the company.

During an appearance on the No Contest Wrestling Podcast, Kong recalled the exact moment she discovered her pregnancy. The realization came during a WWE tour of Mexico, when Kong found herself unable to function normally. "Just got to WWE and we had just went on a tour of Mexico. I couldn't like move or wake up. It got to a point where I was like, I'm just going to live in Mexico now. Then I was like, something's wrong. There's something going on with me because I'm this tired when I should be so excited that I'm on the road and whatnot. I went to the doctor and they [told me I was pregnant]. So I called the office right away and I was like, 'Yeah, this is what's going on.'"

The manner in which WWE chose to handle her departure did not sit well with Kong. The company wrote her off television on an episode of Raw in May with an emotional storyline segment. Kong remained away from the ring until January 2012, during which time she gave birth and handled her maternity obligations. When discussing the angle, Kong was candid about her dissatisfaction. "They had wrote that angle, which I didn't think was the most creative way to do." Despite her reservations, Kong understood the position WWE was in and felt particularly bad about the timing given Triple H's excitement about her signing.

Though Kong was not thrilled with the creative decision, she ultimately agreed to go through with it. However, she did make one important request. "But I was like, I felt bad that's what they had to do because at the time, Hunter had put — he was at the beginning of his reign, you know what I mean? He had put everything into me, he was so excited for me. Then, it was like, I pop up pregnant. They asked me to do that and I was like, 'I'll do it' but I did ask them to change some words cause I'm not going to say that or have that said about my child, if they were to see that later. They accommodated me and we went out and did that."

Kong expressed a strong preference for how she would have handled the situation differently. Rather than an emotional on-air segment, she would have preferred to simply vanish from television without fanfare. "I wanted to just moonwalk quietly home. Like, can you have me just disappear for a while? Why we got to do all this crying and whatnot. Okay, I'll go out there and cry. If y'all want me to cry on cue, I'll go out there and cry on cue."

Years later, Kong has begun to reconsider the possibility of returning to professional wrestling. During her podcast appearance, she opened the door to a potential in-ring comeback, provided she can dedicate time to getting back into proper ring shape. "I would go back to wrestling if I got back in shape. If somebody wanted to work out with me and get me back in shape, I totally would."

The conversation also seemed to reignite Kong's passion for the sport. She revealed that discussing wrestling during the podcast had affected her emotionally and professionally. "Heck no. I told y'all, you put it back in my heart and I can't even say dang you because wrestling is in my blood, which is why I didn't want it in the house because once you get the bug, it doesn't leave. It will stay in you forever and I needed to heal. But I feel like I'm pretty healed up. Now, it'd just be about getting in shape. I won't rule it out, but it's not going to be my main focus. It won't be my main focus but I will not rule it out."

Despite being close to 50 years old, Kong remains confident in her physical abilities and competitive potential. She believes that women wrestlers in her age group do not receive adequate respect in the industry, and she is certain she could perform at a high level with minimal preparation. "I'm close to 50. I feel like women of our age don't get enough respect. I could go in there and hang with the best of them if I went and trained for two or three months. Yes, I could. I believe it."

Eric Bischoff Says There “Might Be A Chance” Brock Lesnar Competes In RAF

Eric Bischoff believes there could be a chance Brock Lesnar returns to his amateur wrestling roots, though he isn't counting on it. Bischoff was asked during a recent Heavy Sports interview about the possibility of the former NCAA Division I Heavyweight Champion, UFC Heavyweight Champion and WWE Champion competing in RAF. While Bischoff knows Lesnar personally, he noted that they aren't particularly close.

"I know Brock, we're friendly, let's put it that way. But it's not like we call each other and chat, wish each other Merry Christmas, stuff like that," Bischoff explained. He described Lesnar as someone who has always preferred life away from the spotlight, particularly when he's able to spend time at home with his family.

"He's a very unique individual. He's a loner. He just would prefer to be on his farm, hunting, maybe fishing, or on his tractor plowing a field. That's the highlight of his life," Bischoff said. "He wants to spend time with his daughter. She's on a mission to win an Olympic gold medal as a shot putter. That's why he retired from WWE."

With Lesnar seemingly content focusing on his family and avoiding public appearances, Bischoff initially leaned against the idea of him competing for RAF. Still, he acknowledged that Lesnar's wrestling background makes it difficult to completely dismiss the possibility. "He could focus on his family and children's athletic aspirations and knowing how much he doesn't like making public appearances. And it's just not that he doesn't like people as much, he's just not comfortable in that environment. I get that. For that reason, I would probably say he does not wrestle in RAF. But, he's also a former wrestler and you know what that means."

Bischoff pointed to amateur wrestling as the foundation for virtually everything Lesnar accomplished during his athletic career. "There might be a chance because amateur wrestling and collegiate wrestling was a big part of Brock's life and a big part of the reason why he was successful as he was, whether it be in the NFL as a walk on for the Minnesota Vikings or a UFC champion or a multi-time, multi-decade force of nature in WWE. It started with amateur wrestling."

Lesnar won the NCAA Division I Heavyweight Championship while competing for the University of Minnesota in 2000 before embarking on careers in professional wrestling and mixed martial arts. Bischoff's comments leave the door open to Lesnar potentially revisiting the sport where his rise began, even if he believes the odds are against it.
