🔥 Swerve Strickland discusses the possibility of a WWE-AEW crossover

AEW has actively worked to break down metaphorical forbidden doors since its inception, hosting its Forbidden Door pay-per-view alongside New Japan Pro-Wrestling since 2022 and later expanding to include CMLL and Stardom, while WWE has established partnerships with TNA Wrestling and acquired AAA ahead of WrestleMania 41. Despite this collaborative landscape, AEW star Swerve Strickland recently addressed the perennial topic of a potential crossover event between WWE and AEW, offering a bleak outlook on the possibility. During a recent interview that also touched on his hip-hop pursuits, including his dream of winning a Grammy Award and his pride in being the only former 205 Live star to become an AEW World Champion, Strickland was asked if fans will ever see the two rival promotions work together on a joint show. Though AEW leadership has previously sounded open to collaboration if logistics made sense, Strickland's response was direct and definitive, stating that it will not happen in his lifetime at least.

Strickland explained that the primary obstacle to a supershow is not a lack of interest from the talent, but rather strict business interests involving corporate control and marketing. Comparing WWE's protective stance to the NFL shielding its brand, he argued that major promotions would never allow competing wrestlers to look superior to their own top tier. He specifically highlighted marquee WWE stars whose presentations must be closely guarded, including Roman Reigns, Cody Rhodes, Gunther, and rising powerhouse Oba Femi, whom Strickland called a world beater and publicly supported on social media following a recent premium live event. Rhodes presents an even more complicated scenario given his history with AEW before returning to WWE.

The former world champion noted that these booking and presentation complications would only intensify when trying to coordinate matches for the women's divisions, mentioning top WWE names like Rhea Ripley, Liv Morgan, and Chelsea Green who also require careful protection. Addressing fantasy matchups that fans crave, such as a generational clash between Seth Rollins and AEW World Champion Will Ospreay, Strickland questioned how the companies could possibly agree on a winner, manage the booking, or navigate issues of corporate leverage.

Despite the intricate political and business roadblocks that make agreeing on outcomes nearly impossible, Strickland shared that the sentiment inside the locker room is entirely different. He revealed that he maintains close personal friendships with several WWE talents, including Carmelo Hayes, Je'Von Evans, and Oba Femi, calling them his boys. According to Strickland, the performers simply want the opportunity to step into the ring together and do not care about who wins or loses, even joking that every match could end in a double disqualification.

Beyond dream matchups with other rosters, Strickland noted that he has evolved past creating his own fantasy opponents, expressing a desire to be the definitive fantasy match that others strive to face at this stage in his career. While the talent in the locker rooms would warmly welcome the chance to tear down industry barriers, Strickland's insights make it clear that competing front office priorities and the rigid demands of brand protection will likely keep the door between WWE and AEW firmly shut.

🔥 AEW factions and original name discussions

Former New Japan Pro-Wrestling standouts David Finlay and Clark Connors recently shed light on their transition to All Elite Wrestling, discussing everything from faction naming disputes to the financial perks of their new contracts. During an appearance on the What Happened When podcast, the wrestlers revealed the behind-the-scenes story of how their faction, formerly known as the Bullet Club War Dogs, settled on their current AEW moniker.

When Connors, Finlay, and Gabe Kidd arrived in AEW as The Dogs, the group actually had a much different title in mind. Connors explained that they initially wanted to pitch the name Raw Dogs for the stable. However, they quickly realized that a major obstacle stood in their way regarding the moniker. Connors noted that there was an obvious issue concerning WWE and its flagship Monday Night programming. While neither wrestler elaborated on specific legal or trademark hurdles, the strong connection to Monday Night Raw made the preferred name difficult to use. Connors admitted they still loved the original idea simply because they thought it sounded cool, but they recognized the practical roadblocks.

Beyond potential conflicts with competitors, observers also noted that the provocative nature of the phrase might have faced pushback from network executives or AEW leadership. Despite dropping their original choice, the group remains thrilled with their current identity. Connors emphasized that The Dogs is a unique name that no major wrestling team has used in the same capacity before, making it a distinctive brand as they establish themselves on television. The podcast host agreed, predicting that fans will quickly grow accustomed to hearing the name as the faction gains more television exposure.

The conversation also touched upon the practical benefits of jumping to AEW, with Finlay and Connors expressing clear satisfaction with their new professional environment. Finlay stated that he has few complaints about his schedule, noting he gets to kick ass, travel to a different city twice a week, and make the announcer's life miserable on television. Connors bluntly highlighted the financial upgrade by stating that he likes the money quite a bit. Finlay reinforced this sentiment by making a direct comparison, explicitly stating that AEW pay is way better than New Japan money. This builds on Finlay's previous remarks about NJPW's past contract offers around the time AEW first launched in 2019.

On the television front, the trio has been actively making their presence known in AEW since officially signing. Connors and Finlay recently challenged Matt and Nick Jackson, the Young Bucks, for the AEW World Tag Team Championship at Collision Grand Slam France. Furthermore, the group teamed up with Death Riders members Claudio Castagnoli, Daniel Garcia, and Wheeler Yuta to secure a victory over the Bang Bang Gang and the Conglomeration in a twelve-man tag team match at the Tailgate Brawl ahead of WrestleDream in Orlando, Florida. As The Dogs continue to make waves in the ring, they do so with a solid identity and lucrative contracts that cement their place in the promotion.

🔥 Ric Flair praises Tony Khan's generosity and promoter skills

During a recent interview with Biff Poggi on NewsNation, WWE Hall of Famer Ric Flair offered glowing remarks about AEW President Tony Khan, going so far as to suggest he may be the greatest professional wrestling promoter of all time. Flair, who is 77 years old, has been under contract with AEW since 2023 when he initially joined the promotion during Sting's retirement run. Although his arrival also included a sponsorship agreement for his Wooooo! Energy drink that has since ended, and despite making very limited appearances on AEW programming, Flair remains on the roster and receives steady financial support.

Flair openly admitted that his workload is practically non-existent, believing that Khan simply takes care of him out of personal respect and nostalgia. Tony is a great guy, generous to a fault, Flair said. With all the work that he has, he does three TV shows a week, and the Jaguars, who are kicking ass right now, he will be in England with the Jaguars. I mean, he just keeps moving, man. He has that Learjet, he has a beautiful girlfriend, and he is a nice guy. He is generous. I work for him, and I don’t do anything. I think he takes care of me out of respect for me. He grew up on me, and he is just a wonderful guy. Flair also praised Khan's collaborative leadership style, noting that Khan gathers a trusted circle of people to make decisions on the spot rather than getting bogged down by complicated corporate politics.

When asked if Khan is the greatest promoter across all forms of entertainment or specifically in wrestling, Flair clarified that he meant within the wrestling industry. He acknowledged that while Khan may not have decades of historical positioning like Vince McMahon, who was very smart and clever through years and years, Khan still stands out as one of the most wonderful people he has ever known and a truly elite promoter.

Transitioning to a discussion about WWE, Flair contrasted his comfortable AEW position with what he perceives as a pervasive anxiety regarding job security among WWE personnel. He pointed to the numerous layoffs over the years, expressing bewilderment at why certain integral figures have been let go. It is not that the guys in WWE are not great, Flair explained. I just think they are all trying to hang on to their jobs because you never know. They have fired so many people. They were an integral part of my career when I was there, and I don't understand the rhyme or reason for it. Some of the people who did my documentary on Peacock, which was fantastic, by the way, have been fired. Two of the guys who helped produce it have been fired, though obviously not Tom Rinaldi. They just do what they want to do.

Flair was careful to emphasize that WWE has always treated him well and that he does not want to speak negatively about the company, especially given that his daughter, Charlotte Flair, remains a prominent star on their roster. However, he admitted confusion regarding the current power structure following the formation of TKO. He noted that while Nick Khan used to be the definitive boss, he is unsure how much authority he currently holds, pointing out that Paul Levesque, known as Hunter, is now running the creative side as the head booker.

🔥 The New Level AEW contract length and details

Former WWE stars Austin Creed and Kofi, widely known throughout their careers as The New Day, have officially locked in their long-term futures with All Elite Wrestling. According to backstage reports, the duo signed five-year contracts with the promotion, finalizing the paperwork just one day before their long-awaited debut at AEW All In.

Plans for the duo's arrival and creative direction were discussed well before the contracts were officially signed. Swerve Strickland played a significant role in connecting the performers with the promotion, helping facilitate the initial relationship. AEW President Tony Khan strictly avoided direct discussions with Creed and Kofi until they were entirely contractually free from WWE, ensuring proper protocol was followed before the creative pitches began.

The early creative plans immediately placed them alongside Strickland, forming a powerful alliance that led to them capturing the AEW World Trios Championships at All In. While they have since dropped the titles to Hangman Page and Brodido at AEW WrestleDream on October 17 after a 48-day reign, reports indicate that this short championship run was always part of concert with the original creative design rather than a sign of wavering commitment from the company.

Logistics for their debut also came down to the wire. AEW successfully secured the rights to use the entrance music New Level, but Sony granted final approval on the exact day of the All In pay-per-view. Industry insiders note that such last-minute clearances are fairly common when wrestling promotions license external music.

Beyond their presence on weekly television, the five-year agreements reportedly permit Creed and Kofi to take outside bookings, granting them the flexibility to wrestle for other promotions while remaining active in AEW. With their long-term future secured and their partnership with Strickland firmly established, The New Level are positioned to remain major fixtures on the roster for years to come.

Adam Copeland recalls teenage Christian Cage jumping into Demolition’s car

Adam Copeland recently recalled a wild parking lot encounter from 36 years ago involving a teenage Christian Cage brazenly jumping uninvited into Demolition member Crush's car. Speaking in a documentary released by Maple Leaf Pro Wrestling, Copeland remembered attending a WWE house show at Maple Leaf Gardens that featured Demolition members Ax, Smash, and Crush facing off against the Ultimate Warrior and the Legion of Doom. According to wrestling records, the specific event took place on October 21, 1990.

Copeland explained that he and Cage used to frequent the venue's entrance and parking lot as teenagers to stage ambushes and annoy wrestlers arriving for the shows. On this particular occasion, which Copeland noted was the last show he attended there as a fan, the duo targeted their favorites, Demolition. As the wrestlers were packing their car, Brian Adams, known as Crush, was sitting in the front seat.

Cage unexpectedly hopped right into the front seat with Crush and immediately began reciting all of Crush's career highlights and wrestling trivia to him. Meanwhile, Copeland stayed at the trunk of the vehicle, annoying Ax and Smash. Years later, the chance encounter came full circle when Cage found himself on a flight sitting right beside Crush. When Cage retold the story, Crush actually still remembered the bold teenage fan who had jumped into his car.

The documentary release and Copeland's storytelling coincided with his own return to the historic venue, now known as the Mattamy Athletic Centre, where he defeated Steve Maclin in the main event of MLP Northern Rising. During the post-event press conference, Copeland mentioned that wrestling at Maple Leaf Gardens is likely a white whale achievement for Christian Cage as well.

In other wrestling news, Copeland previously revealed on AEW Dynamite that Cage suffered three broken ribs and a collapsed lung during a ladder match at AEW All Out. Looking ahead, Copeland is scheduled to team up with Will Ospreay to face Kyle Fletcher and Kevin Knight on an upcoming episode of AEW Dynamite.

Willow Nightingale is celebrating four years as a member of the All Elite Wrestling roster, reflecting on the accomplishments she has achieved since officially signing with the company.

Nightingale’s signing was announced during an October 2022 episode of AEW Rampage, where she defeated Leila Grey in singles competition. Following the match, Tony Schiavone entered the ring and surprised Nightingale with the news that she was officially All Elite.

Since then, Nightingale has established herself as one of the promotion's most accomplished female performers, capturing the AEW TBS Championship twice, along with the AEW Women’s World Championship and AEW Women’s Tag Team Titles. Those accomplishments have also earned Nightingale a place in the company’s history books as its first women’s Triple Crown Champion.

Taking to social media on the fourth anniversary of her signing, Nightingale looked back at the moment that started it all and reflected on how far she has come. You think she knew she’d end up being the first women’s triple crown champ in company history, Nightingale wrote on X.

🔄 המשך 🔥 AEW Dynamite October 21 reviews and coverage

Ilja Dragunov successfully kicked off his AEW career in the main event of the live broadcast, putting away Mike Bailey with a pair of devastating D-Bomb finishers after targeting his opponent's injured knee throughout a hard-hitting singles contest.

The night was marked by major surprises and faction turmoil, highlighted by a shocking opening match where Don Callis Family members Mark Davis and Jake Doyle completely decimated AEW World Tag Champions The Young Bucks in a quick eliminator bout. That dominant victory officially secured Davis and Doyle a title opportunity against the champions for Full Gear.

Later in the evening, Steven Borden made his triumphant return to television after being sidelined with a concussion since All Out. Borden joined forces with Darby Allin, Will Ospreay, Adam Copeland, and the Young Bucks to clear the ring of the Don Callis Family, officially challenging them to a massive six-on-six Blood and Guts match scheduled for November 4 in Jacksonville.

The card also featured a dramatic shift in the locker room when Jay White and the Bang Bang Gang betrayed their allies, turning heel and merging with David Finlay and Clark Connors, known as The Dogs. The newly unified mega-faction launched a brutal attack on Jon Moxley and the Death Riders, as well as members of The Conglomeration, leaving Claudio Castagnoli outnumbered despite a resilient last stand.

In the women's division, Queen Aminata earned a future TBS Championship opportunity by defeating a returning Maya World in a number one contender match, setting up a title showdown against Persephone for next week's Fright Night Dynamite. Meanwhile, Marina Shafir interrupted an in-ring interview to confront AEW Women's World Champion Mercedes Mone, issuing a direct and aggressive challenge for the belt.

🔄 המשך 🔥 Updates surrounding the passing of former AEW star PAC

Official findings have now clarified the final moments of the late wrestler's life. The Cook County Medical Examiner’s Office officially ruled the cause of death as bronchial asthma, classifying the manner of death as natural. Investigators recovered inhalers at the scene inside the Chicago-area rental car, and subsequent toxicology testing returned no significant findings. Records also noted that PAC had informed his wife he planned to stop at a restaurant before heading to O'Hare International Airport.

In addition to the medical updates, fellow wrestler Bandido has opened up about his final interaction with PAC just one day before the tragedy. The two originally bonded during their time in Japan and recently reunited to exchange football jerseys, with PAC gifting a Newcastle United shirt and receiving a Santos Laguna jersey in return. Bandido shared that the meaningful exchange concluded with PAC telling him, I love you, brother, words that the AEW star vowed to keep in his heart forever as a lasting reminder of their friendship.

🔄 המשך 🔥 Tama Tonga addresses social media post about Ilja Dragunov

While Ilja Dragunov remains focused on his new chapter, his past WWE work has unexpectedly sparked a backstage and online debate.

The social media chatter began when WWE wrestler Tama Tonga posted a clip from a previous SmackDown match showing him absorbing Dragunov chops without selling the impact. Because the upload closely followed Dragunov appearing on AEW television, fans and observers immediately began dissecting the timing and questioning if it was a subtle dig.

During an episode of Wrestling Observer Radio, Dave Meltzer weighed in on the video. Meltzer questioned whether the post was a calculated message or an attempt to score brownie points, comparing the move to promotional strategies from the 1980s territories where promoters like Bill Watts and Ole Anderson would highlight departing stars getting beaten up.

Tonga quickly caught wind of the commentary and took to social media to shut down the theories. He admitted the post stirred up drama, but insisted he simply thought it was a good clip worth sharing. He dismissed Meltzer by referring to him as Great Uncle Dave and rejected the idea that he was following any kind of corporate or historical playbook.

Additionally, Tonga had sharp words for journalists who flooded his direct messages asking for confirmation on the situation. He told reporters to get out of here and maintained that nobody inside WWE instructed him to publish the footage, leaving the online speculation without any official backing.

🔄 המשך Jeff Jarrett defends comedy spot from AEW WrestleDream

Jeff Jarrett has offered his perspective on the divisive comedy spot featuring Andrade El Idolo and Tommaso Ciampa, pushing back against critics who argue that humor has no place in professional wrestling.

Speaking on his podcast, Jarrett dismissed the notion that comedy does not draw money, calling it a highly situational concept and emphasizing that creative choices are ultimately subjective. He pointed directly to the live audience's loud reaction at WrestleDream as proof that the segment achieved its intended goal, even if the humor was not to every traditionalist's taste.

To support his stance, Jarrett compared the moment to the career of Mick Foley, noting how Foley regularly used comedic elements like the Mr. Socko gag to generate massive crowd pops without ever undermining his credibility as a fierce, serious competitor in his prime.
