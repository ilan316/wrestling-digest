🔥 Maya World TBS Championship

MAYA WORLD BECOMES NEW AEW TBS CHAMPION AT REDEMPTION

Maya World has captured the AEW TBS Championship, defeating Hikaru Shida in an upset victory at the inaugural AEW Redemption pay-per-view on Sunday, July 26, 2026, from the Bell Centre in Montreal, Quebec, Canada. The win marks World's first singles championship in All Elite Wrestling and comes at a significant moment in her career, as the match took place on her 24th birthday.

World entered the match as the challenger against Shida, who had held the vacant TBS Championship for only 25 days after winning it in a six-way Survival of the Fittest match on July 1. The title reign came after Shida successfully retained the championship over Queen Aminata on a recent episode of Collision, with Shida continuing to assault World until the challenger came down for the save, setting up the championship match at Redemption.

The match itself told a story of a hungrier challenger fighting through adversity against an experienced, decorated former AEW Women's World Champion. Shida controlled the early portions of the contest, taking World down to the mat and even biting her head in the corner. The champion then shifted her strategy to target World's knee, applying various submission holds including the Stretch Muffler, single-leg Boston Crab, and the TBS Lock throughout the match. World's knee injury became a central element of the match, affecting her ability to execute certain moves effectively.

Despite the sustained attack on her knee, World showed tremendous heart and determination, fighting back with double stomps, enziguiris, and a series of Shining Wizards, some performed with her knee pad removed. The challenger managed to land multiple near falls with her striking combinations, refusing to stay down despite the damage inflicted by Shida. When Shida attempted to use her kendo stick late in the match, the referee intervened to stop her, giving World the opportunity to kick the weapon out of Shida's hands.

The finish of the match came when Shida appeared to have the contest won after connecting with the Katana, multiple running knee strikes, and a Falcon Arrow. However, World managed to keep the match alive by reaching the bottom rope at the crucial moment. Shida also attempted to apply the TBS Lock with running knee strikes, but World escaped that submission hold. The pivotal moment came late in the contest when Shida thought she had won the match after the Falcon Arrow, causing her to turn her attention to arguing with referee Paul Turner. This distraction proved costly, as World capitalized by hitting Shida with a bridging fallaway slam to score the pinfall and capture the championship.

Following her victory, World appeared somewhat in disbelief about what she had accomplished. During the post-show media scrum, when asked if the TBS title win had hit her yet, she responded with "You know, almost," noting that she kept seeing the graphic and thinking "That's insane. I can't believe that's me." World expressed gratitude for the opportunity, saying "I'm just really grateful for everything. AEW is such a great place and I'm just glad that, you know, Tony Khan believed in me so much and now I'm here and I did it and the future is now."

This championship victory comes on the heels of an impressive run for the young wrestler at AEW Forbidden Door last month. World had been competing as a tag wrestler alongside her partner Hyan when she was suddenly inserted into the Owen Hart Foundation Tournament as a replacement for the injured Sareee. This substitution occurred just weeks after World's brother tragically passed away in a car accident, making her tournament run even more remarkable. She advanced through the tournament and reached the finals, where she faced her idol Mercedes Mone but ultimately came up short in a match that drew widespread praise from the wrestling community.

Regarding her match against Mercedes Mone at Forbidden Door, World reflected on rewatching the contest during the AEW Redemption press conference. When asked if she had seen the match again, she responded "Somebody has to," adding with a laugh that she had indeed watched it "many, many times." She explained her approach to reviewing the match, saying "I tried not to at first, I wanted to stay in the moment. I had a few things happen and I wanted to remember it for what it was, which was a great match and a great feeling after, but I definitely have rewatched it a lot of times."

Looking forward to her championship reign, World has already expressed interest in specific matchups. When asked who she would like to face at AEW All In on August 30, she named ROH Women's World Champion Athena as a potential opponent. "I feel like my story with Athena isn't done," World said. "I would love to face Athena. But whoever, whoever. Anybody can step up... But Athena for sure."

Despite capturing her first championship, World remains committed to her tag team work with Hyan. Both wrestlers were signed to AEW in December 2025 and have developed a strong working relationship. When asked about continuing to team with Hyan after winning the TBS Championship, World expressed enthusiasm for maintaining the partnership. "Of course. That's my — yes, for sure," she said. "I love tag wrestling and I'm so glad that — honestly, I would never imagine that I'd be signed as a tag wrestler but, me and her love it so much. We always are picking each other's brains. I'm like, 'Did you watch this?' We send each other matches to watch, study, and I love tag wrestling so definitely wanna stay with my girl Hyan." World and Hyan last competed together in a six-woman tag team match on the July 15 episode of Dynamite.

The match itself received mixed reviews from the wrestling media. While the finish and the moment for World were celebrated as a great achievement for the hard-working talent who persevered through personal tragedy, some observers felt the match itself could have been better executed. One reviewer noted that while the story was acceptable, with World having an injury to overcome and Shida playing a vicious, tormenting villain, the match ran longer than necessary for the narrative it told and failed to continuously engage the Bell Centre crowd. However, critics generally agreed that the emotional payoff of World's victory on her birthday made it a great moment regardless of the technical quality of the contest.

World's victory at Redemption marks a significant turning point in her AEW career. Having entered the company as a tag team specialist, she has now proven herself capable of competing at the highest levels of singles competition in the women's division. Her journey from substitute tournament entrant to championship contender to TBS Champion in the span of roughly a month represents one of the more impressive rapid rises in recent AEW history. As she moves into her championship reign, World will look to prove herself as a worthy titleholder while continuing to balance her singles success with her passion for tag team wrestling alongside Hyan.

🔥 AEW Redemption Media Scrum and Coverage

AEWS REDEMPTION DELIVERS MIXED RESULTS IN MONTREAL

The first-ever AEW Redemption took place on Sunday, July 26, 2026, from the Bell Centre in Montreal, Quebec, Canada, capping off an ambitious pay-per-view that lasted nearly five hours including the Buy-In pre-show. The event marked a transitional moment for the company as it builds toward All In at Wembley Stadium on August 30, but received mixed critical reception regarding its necessity on the packed AEW calendar.

KENNY OMEGA RETAINS AMID DRAMATIC ANGLE

The main event saw Kenny Omega successfully defend the AEW World Championship against rising challenger Kevin Knight. The match lived up to the billing with numerous near falls, as Knight, the TNT Champion, looked to capture a second title. After Knight nearly won following a slam onto the title belt and a top-rope splash that Omega countered with his knees, Omega caught Knight in the One Winged Angel for the pinfall victory.

The post-match angle proved even more significant than the match itself. The Death Riders immediately attacked Omega, with Claudio wrapping a chair around his head. Will Ospreay, who had only recently joined the Death Riders group, ran down to save Omega and chased the stable away, wanting to defeat Omega on his own terms at All In. Jon Moxley then presented Ospreay with a clear plastic bag, presumably containing a symbolic gift from the group, but Ospreay rejected it by tearing up the bag and attacking Moxley with a forearm. Ospreay then took out the entire Death Riders stable, including a Hidden Blade to Wheeler Yuta, before removing the Death Riders logo kneepad he had been wearing.

However, the evening was not over. As Ospreay celebrated in the ring, Kenny Omega plucked him off the top rope and delivered another One Winged Angel to a stunned crowd. The Young Bucks subsequently entered the ring appearing somewhat confused before the group posed together as The Elite, setting up a complex dynamic for the All In main event.

WILLOW NIGHTINGALE REACHES THE TOP

Willow Nightingale captured her first AEW Women's World Championship, fulfilling a goal she had expressed to her sister years earlier. During the post-show media scrum, Nightingale opened with a personal story about telling her sister she would one day be at the "top of the wrestles," which she felt she accomplished at Redemption. The victory was particularly meaningful given the challenges she had faced over the past year, including an AC sprain that prevented her from competing at Double or Nothing, an absence that was emotionally difficult for her.

Nightingale acknowledged that she had self-diagnosed herself with depression and had been dealing with related issues, but received help through AEW. She noted that the business brings her joy and that she appreciated the ability to show authentic emotions rather than maintaining a constant happy persona. She credited wrestling for helping her through difficult personal times, and Tony Khan announced plans to celebrate her championship victory on the following Wednesday edition of AEW programming.

The women's championship match featured Mercedes Mone, Nightingale's scheduled opponent at All In, watching from the crowd, though no direct confrontation occurred between the two. Critics noted that the title change, while important, felt somewhat lacking in build and intensity, and suggested it could have been held at All In with similar impact.

MAYA WORLD WINS TBS CHAMPIONSHIP

In an upset victory, Maya World defeated Hikaru Shida to capture the TBS Championship. During the media scrum, World thanked Tony Khan for believing in her and credited Mercedes Mone for elevating her throughout their program. When asked about potential opponents at All In, World mentioned Athena's name but indicated willingness to face anyone. She expressed interest in continuing to team with Hyan despite initially being signed as a tag team wrestler, and was asked by media whether she had rewatched her championship-winning match, to which she responded that "somebody has to." This title change was also viewed by some critics as something that could have been saved for All In, particularly since it could have taken place on the Zero Hour pre-show at the Wembley event.

SPEEDBALL MIKE BAILEY WINS LADDER MATCH IN HOMETOWN

From Injury to Gold: Willow Nightingale Captures AEW Women's World Title and Makes History

Willow Nightingale has reached the pinnacle of the AEW women's division. Nightingale's path to championship gold was complicated by an AC sprain that kept her out of action and forced her to miss the 2026 Women's Owen Hart Foundation tournament. In her explanation of the injury, Nightingale disclosed that while she initially thought it would not be severe enough to keep her from Double or Nothing, she quickly learned otherwise. The AC sprain proved more problematic than she anticipated, especially considering the varying severity levels of such injuries. Despite the initial uncertainty surrounding her recovery timeline, Nightingale worked diligently to rehabilitate the injury. She explained her decision-making process regarding her championship status, stating that if she could not defend the TBS Championship whenever opportunities arose, she felt she should not hold the title. This principled stance led to her relinquishing the championship while she recovered. Her patience and commitment to proper rehabilitation paid off when she returned to in-ring action in July and earned a shot at the AEW Women's World Title at Redemption.

The match itself was a competitive affair that showcased the abilities of both competitors. Nightingale came out aggressively early on, using her power advantage to control the opening portions of the match with a Pounce and Spinebuster that sent Thekla to the outside. The victory at Redemption marked a historic achievement for Nightingale.
