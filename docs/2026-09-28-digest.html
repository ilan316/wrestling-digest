🔥 AEW Star PAC Passes Away at Age 40, Wrestling World Mourns

All Elite Wrestling announced the sudden passing of Benjamin Satterley, known to fans as PAC, at the age of 40. The tragic news arrived on Sunday night, less than 24 hours after Satterley competed in the opening match at the AEW All Out pay-per-view in Chicago, where he challenged Andrade El Idolo for the AEW National Championship. He also made an appearance during the main event, competing alongside the Death Riders in the match between Jon Moxley and Will Ospreay. AEW issued an official statement highlighting that Satterley, a native of Newcastle upon Tyne, England, began his career in 2004 and captivated fans worldwide with his innovative high-flying moves and in-ring ability after signing with the promotion in 2019. No further details regarding the cause of death have been made public.

Satterley carved out an extraordinary legacy across two decades in professional wrestling. He initially built his reputation on the British independent circuit and international promotions like DragonGate and Pro Wrestling Guerrilla before signing with WWE in 2012. Performing as Adrian Neville, he became known as The Man That Gravity Forgot and made history by winning the inaugural NXT Tag Team Championship with Oliver Grey. He later defeated Bo Dallas to capture the NXT Championship and eventually moved to the main roster, where he won the WWE Cruiserweight Championship in 2017. Following his departure from WWE in 2018, he joined AEW upon its launch in 2019, becoming the inaugural All-Atlantic Champion, a two-time World Trios Champion with Death Triangle, and a prominent member of the Death Riders faction.

The wrestling world reacted with immediate shock and profound grief. AEW President Tony Khan paid tribute, writing that Satterley defined excellence through his wrestling knowledge, unprecedented athleticism, professionalism, consistency, and his role as a beloved husband and friend. Scores of wrestlers from AEW, WWE, TNA, and other promotions shared emotional statements. Will Ospreay called him the greatest export from their country and a hero to many, while Paul Heyman, Rhea Ripley, Saraya, Adam Pearce, Dustin Rhodes, Matt Hardy, and Mustafa Ali expressed deep devastation. Taz shared a touching final memory of shaking hands and giggling with him backstage just a night prior. Fans also flooded a social media post made earlier by Andrade El Idolo—who had praised PAC and predicted he would be a future world champion—with heartbreaking messages.

At a Warrior Wrestling event held shortly after the announcement, Marina Shafir and Lance Archer delivered emotional addresses to the live crowd. Shafir tearfully recalled hugging PAC and telling him she loved him just twenty-four hours earlier, urging everyone to tell their loved ones how much they mean to them. Archer revealed that he spoke with Jon Moxley to ask if they should still perform, sharing Moxley's advice that they should go out and honor their fallen brother by entertaining the fans to the absolute best of their ability. Meanwhile, former wrestling star Val Venis made headlines after posting about PAC's passing and subsequently sharing a screenshot showing he had been blocked by AEW's official social media account, demanding answers from the promotion.

AEW Star Brian Cage Defends Performance-Enhancing Drug Use

AEW star Brian Cage relies on his imposing physique to stand out amongst his peers, which is a large part of his presentation as The Machine. However, fans have often speculated on his possible use of steroids, which Cage admitted to during an interview on The Smoothvega Podcast.

I don't care, I want to be more and more open about this, Cage said regarding performance-enhancing drugs. However, he clarified that steroids aren't a magical fix to make one muscular, and it still requires a great deal of hard work. Do steroids work? Absolutely. Have I taken PEDs? Absolutely! I've never said I haven't!

Cage stated that his success isn't due to PEDs, though he acknowledged that he has used testosterone replacement therapy to help him maintain his physique at 42. According to Cage, he's far from alone in that regard, and he pointed out that the federal government has changed the classification of many PEDs in recent years, and newer research has denied any connection between steroids and cardiac arrest.

Despite this, Cage has one important caveat about steroid usage. There's obviously a difference between use and abuse with everything, right? he pointed out, citing how someone can consume alcohol responsibly or begin abusing it on a regular basis. Everyone's on peptides, and they're great, they're freaking phenomenal! Again, are they magic? No. Do they work overnight? No.

Steroid usage is controversial in the world of pro wrestling for multiple reasons, but especially with regard to the Chris Benoit double murder-suicide in 2007. Benoit's actions have often been publicly tied to steroid use as well as extreme levels of CTE, but Cage doesn't believe that steroids were at the root cause of Benoit's actions that weekend.

Even when it happened, I'm like I wasn't taking steroids then, but right away I go, That's not a roid rage thing. First of all, like I said I don't fully believe in it, but also a roid rage thing wouldn't be a three-day premeditated setup, he argued, sharing his belief that there were other factors involved in Benoit's crimes.

Cage also recalled his earliest history with steroid usage, claiming that he never touched it until after his very first bodybuilding contest, denying that he was on anything during his indie run. I had a really bad temper in high school and stuff when I was younger, and that was one of the reasons, too, why I never wanted to do it. I thought, because of roid rage, I was going to lose it which is also, afraid to tell you, it's kind of bullshit, he opined.

Cage then added that people who do have roid rage are likely just using steroids to justify the anger issues they already have, while admitting that some people will react differently to the chemicals they inject into their bodies.

AEW/ROH Star Anthony Henry Uncertain About In-Ring Career Future

In 2021, I was in a match and I got hurt, Henry said. Turns out, I actually herniated two disks in my cervical spine C5, C6. Once we figured out what it actually was, I got an epidural shot and it helped a ton. Within a few months I was good to go. Let's be real though, these injuries never really fully heal, do they? And my newest MRI shows bulging or herniated discs through my entire neck, bone spurs wedged between them, and a spinal cavity that keeps getting narrower.

Honestly, it's f***ing scary, he continued. Unfortunately, this has been a reoccurring issue, especially over the last year. I've done the PT and the epidurals have just stopped working. I'm getting two separate opinions, but everything that I have read points towards neck fusion surgery.
