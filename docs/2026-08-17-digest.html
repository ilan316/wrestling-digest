🔶 AEW News

Christian Cage and AEW vs WWE Rivalry

Chris Jericho and Eric Bischoff have found themselves at odds over the true nature of AEW's relationship with WWE and how the company has positioned itself within the professional wrestling landscape.

In recent comments, Jericho discussed AEW's trajectory and relationship with its biggest competitor. He believes AEW has always followed its own path and focuses on its own product. Jericho pointed toward the company's matches, ratings, and ticket sales as evidence of this independent approach. He also stated that AEW has never copied WWE and was never a WWE-lite version of the company.

Eric Bischoff, the WWE Hall of Famer, took strong issue with Jericho's characterization of events. Speaking on the 83 Weeks podcast, Bischoff was asked about Jericho's comments on AEW's rivalry with WWE. He responded bluntly, saying "I think that is the most bullshit statement. I love Chris. I have nothing but respect for Chris, but that was a comment, Chris."

Bischoff pointed to Jericho's own history as evidence contradicting the current stance. He referenced Jericho's earlier prediction that within six months, AEW would overtake WWE. Bischoff also cited examples of other AEW talent, such as Will Ospreay, who he suggested frequently reference WWE in their commentary and promos.

Bischoff did acknowledge the possibility that AEW management may have recently instructed talent to change their approach regarding WWE references. "Maybe like within the last 30 days they've made a, you know, executive decision and instructed talent, you know, not to talk about WWE," Bischoff said. However, he expressed skepticism about the longevity of any such shift.

Looking at AEW's entire history since 2019, Bischoff maintained that the company and its talent have consistently tried to create an "us versus them" narrative. "If you look at the last since 2019, that's all they freaking do from Tony Khan to the talent. That's all they've been doing is trying to create an us versus them. We're the underdogs, and we're going to take over, and they're evil. I mean, come on, Chris," Bischoff said.

Bischoff expressed surprise at Jericho making such a statement, noting that Jericho is typically a smart operator in professional wrestling. "There's nothing. I'm surprised he made that statement. Chris is a smart guy, but that's that wasn't a smart thing to say," Bischoff concluded.

In a separate interview, Jericho explained his decision to remain with AEW rather than pursue other opportunities. Speaking with Dave Meltzer and Garrett Gonzales on Wrestling Observer Radio, Jericho discussed the factors that influenced his choice to stay with the company.

Jericho emphasized his personal stake in AEW's success, noting that he helped start the company. "When it was time to kind of start thinking about coming back, obviously you weigh your options. And there is a lot of factors, but the bottom line is this, and this was the one that really clinched it for me, was I've got skin in the game in AEW. I helped start AEW, and in a lot of ways, if there was no Chris Jericho, there might not have been an AEW," he said.

Jericho acknowledged that other major promotions had histories before his involvement. "And there certainly wasn't one before me. There was a WWE before Chris Jericho. There was a New Japan before. There was a CMLL before. And there'll be an AEW after I leave, obviously," Jericho stated. Despite this, he expressed enthusiasm about the company's future prospects.

Jericho also credited his working relationship with Tony Khan as a contributing factor to his decision. "And I can't wait to see how much bigger the company gets over the next 10 years, etc. But when it came down to it, it was like, I like working for Tony. We had to work some things out, which we did. It wasn't hard to do," Jericho explained.

When discussing potential future feuds and collaborations, Jericho expressed interest in working with various AEW roster members. "Kevin Knight, I would love to do more with him. I'd love to be a tag team with Ricochet. I'd love to be a tag team with Ciampa. I mean, I could do something with Jack Perry. I could work with Mark Davis. I could work with Kyle Fletcher. I could work with The Rascalz. The Rascalz could be like my minions," Jericho said.

Kyle Fletcher and Mark Davis Individual Success

Kyle Fletcher and Mark Davis have successfully navigated one of professional wrestling's most challenging transitions: breaking up as a successful tag team without the typical dramatic betrayal angle.

The two Australian wrestlers spent six years together as Aussie Open before transitioning to All Elite Wrestling, where they have begun establishing themselves as singles competitors. Both men remain signed to AEW and continue to work together in a positive capacity within the company.

"They always called Hulk the Immortal Hulk Hogan. And I — that was the one. I mean, I'll never get over Scott because he was my best friend. But I just never pictured Terry dying," Nash explained.

Getting older has changed his perspective on time itself, and that shift has manifested in both his emotional reflections on lost friends and his personal health decisions. As he approaches the 30-year anniversary of the NWO, Nash carries the weight of being the last surviving original member, a responsibility that comes with both the honor of preserving those memories and the pain of remembering those who can no longer share in them.
