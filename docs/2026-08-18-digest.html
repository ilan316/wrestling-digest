🔥 Mercedes Mone WWE Negotiations During AEW All In 2023

Mercedes Mone has revealed the pivotal moment that led to her signing with AEW. In the latest edition of her Mone Mag newsletter, the former WWE star reflected on attending AEW All In 2023 at Wembley Stadium, disclosing that she was still in active negotiations with WWE at that time.

"Let me take you all the way back to 2023," Mone wrote. "At the time, I was still going back and forth in talks with WWE when I got an invitation to go to All In London. Now, you guys know me. I LOVE professional wrestling. So there was absolutely NO WAY I was going to miss the opportunity to witness something this big—a wrestling show of this magnitude, outside of WWE, at Wembley Stadium. I love history. I love being around history, and even more than that, I love being a part of it."

Mone explained that the moment she arrived in London, something shifted for her. "The second I got to London, something just felt different. The energy was right. From the way I was treated to the moment I arrived at Wembley, I remember thinking: Wait... I Love this!"

When Mone made her way backstage, she crossed paths with AEW President Tony Khan, who was thrilled to see her in attendance. Khan's enthusiasm led to an unexpected proposition for the unsigned wrestler. "Then I saw Tony backstage, and he was SO excited to have me there. So excited, in fact, that he wanted me to do a backstage segment with… WILLOW! I was like— WOWOWOWOW. WAIT! I am NOT signed. I do NOT work here. I'm just here to enjoy the show!"

The suggestion to appear with Willow Nightingale carried particular significance given their shared history. Mone had suffered an ankle injury during a match against Nightingale at NJPW Resurgence in May 2023, an incident that kept her sidelined for approximately a year. Despite Khan's enthusiasm, Mone politely declined the opportunity, choosing instead to spend time catching up with old friends backstage, including Amanda Huber, Renee Paquette, and Rusev (Miro).

Rather than appearing on the broadcast, Mone was escorted to a box seat where she could experience the event as a spectator. This proved to be a transformative experience for someone accustomed to performing. "Then I was escorted to my box seats, and for once… I got to be a FAN. Do you understand how rare that is for me? I never really got the opportunity to sit in the crowd and just be a fan at WrestleMania. Okay… MAYBE there was one time I went in disguise for Bayley's match. But that's another story."

Mone described the unique experience of being able to simply enjoy a wrestling show without the pressures that typically come with her profession. "This time, I could actually sit there. No match to prepare for. No entrance to think about. No pressure. Just Moné and more than 80,000 screaming wrestling fans celebrating the thing I love most in this entire world."

The overwhelming atmosphere and warm reception she received ultimately proved to be the deciding factor in her career path. "I was in awe. I wanted to cry. And somewhere inside of me, I think I already knew. When I got back home, there was one thing I couldn't stop thinking about: AEW WAS THE PLACE FOR ME."

Mone made her official AEW debut at the Big Business episode of AEW Dynamite in March 2024. Her decision to join the promotion has since led to significant career momentum, including her victory in the Owen Hart Tournament. Cody Rhodes Four Horsemen Heel Turn

Wrestling author David Shoemaker believes Cody Rhodes could be the perfect fit to lead a modern version of one of professional wrestling's most iconic factions, The Four Horsemen. Shoemaker made these comments while appearing on Cheap Heat with Peter Rosenberg to promote his book, Why Hulk Hogan Matters, and the conversation naturally turned toward the possibility of Rhodes eventually embracing a heel role in WWE.

Rather than envisioning Rhodes undergoing a dramatic personality change or completely turning against the WWE audience, Shoemaker suggested that many of the qualities already associated with "The American Nightmare" could naturally translate into a villainous character. He floated the idea of Rhodes using those existing traits to relaunch The Four Horsemen as a heel faction.

Shoemaker articulated his vision by explaining that a modern Four Horsemen led by Rhodes wouldn't require him to fundamentally alter his character. According to Shoemaker, Rhodes simply needs to be presented as a man who dresses well, takes pride in himself, and surrounds himself with like-minded individuals who share that philosophy. This presentation would make him functionally a villain and a heel for the purposes of professional wrestling storytelling, without requiring a dramatic shift in personality or an explicit rejection of the audience.

Shoemaker used this example to illustrate a larger point about professional wrestling storytelling. He emphasized that the traditional distinction between babyfaces and heels remains an important part of how professional wrestling matches tell stories, even as modern characters increasingly operate in shades of gray. He expressed frustration that this fundamental storytelling concept is often overlooked in contemporary discussions about heel turns, with many observers arguing that the modern era has moved beyond the face versus heel dynamic.

This idea would provide an intriguing connection to Rhodes' wrestling lineage. Cody's father, the late "American Dream" Dusty Rhodes, was famously one of The Four Horsemen's greatest rivals during the faction's dominant run in Jim Crockett Promotions. The original Horsemen became synonymous with Ric Flair, Arn Anderson, Ole Anderson, and Tully Blanchard, although numerous wrestlers joined different incarnations of the group over the years.

Shoemaker emphasized that professional wrestling as a medium operates within traditions and storytelling conventions that stretch back centuries in various forms and decades within the modern context. He argued that these traditional narratives work because they establish clear antagonists and protagonists, and that when the bell rings to start a match, these roles matter for creating compelling television.

A Cody Rhodes heel turn has been the subject of fan discussion at various stages of his career in professional wrestling. However, Shoemaker's concept would not require Rhodes to completely reinvent himself or fundamentally change how he presents himself to audiences. Instead, his polished presentation, confidence, and high-status persona could simply be reframed in a way that positions him as the antagonist in the narratives WWE creates.

🔄 המשך Nikki Cross Vince McMahon Superhero Character

Nikki Cross opened up about the creative process behind her superhero persona, crediting herself as the driving force. She explained that when Alexa Bliss began working with Bray Wyatt, she needed a fresh direction and drew inspiration from characters like Hurricane Helms and Mighty Molly, combined with her lifelong love of superheroes and the X-Men.

"I always loved superheroes, I needed something different," Cross said in an interview on "INSIGHT with Chris Van Vliet," noting that despite the popularity of superhero movies at the time, WWE hadn't featured such a character in years. She spent considerable effort putting together a presentation on the concept and the broader appeal of superheroes before pitching it directly to McMahon.

"I just basically gave it to Vince and was like: 'Here, I've got an idea, this will make you money!'" she recalled.

After several weeks of silence, McMahon texted Cross calling the idea "genius" and instructed her creative team to get to work. However, McMahon made a crucial addition: the word "Almost." He wanted her original vision of an elaborate, blockbuster-style superhero costume modified to reflect a more DIY, Spider-Man-inspired approach.

"He wanted the character to instead take a Spider-Man approach to her gear and make it seem like she had made it herself instead of being a blockbuster film superhero, because it would be more relatable to fans," Cross explained. McMahon's vision ensured that young girls in the audience could easily recreate her costume without difficulty.

"Almost is like, she's not quite a superhero, she's a superhero in training but not what you've seen before," Cross said. "She knows that she doesn't have super powers, but her mindset is that she's a superhero and can take on the world, and that's how I approached it."

Cross embraced McMahon's refinement of her concept, appreciating how it made the character aspirational and relatable. "I really liked the idea of being relatable and I loved that idea of a little girl in the audience can look up to me," she added.

Brock Lesnar starts his final boss era: WWE SummerSlam 2014 | WON Flashback

On August 17, 2014, WWE SummerSlam featured one of the most significant matches in modern wrestling history as Brock Lesnar dominated John Cena in the main event to capture the WWE World Heavyweight Championship. The match marked the beginning of Lesnar's presentation as the "final boss" of the company, though some trace this character's origins back to WrestleMania 30 when he defeated The Undertaker. However, it was the complete dominance displayed at SummerSlam that truly established Lesnar as an unstoppable force, booking him with the presentation of M Bison from the Street Fighter video game series. This run would continue until his loss to Goldberg at WWE Survivor Series 2016.

According to Dave Meltzer's analysis of the match, Lesnar systematically destroyed Cena from the opening bell, throwing him around the ring at will. The match featured Lesnar delivering 15 different released German suplexes, with several of these throws seeing Cena land badly on his already damaged neck. Lesnar's dominance was complete and psychological, laughing at the minimal offense Cena managed to muster. When Cena attempted his finishing moves, Lesnar countered them with ease, kicking out of the Attitude Adjustment before powering out of the STF submission hold. Lesnar finished the match by delivering vicious ground and pound strikes before executing an F-5 for the pin. The aftermath was so significant that Cena was kept off Raw the next day to properly sell the severity of the beating.

Meltzer placed this booking decision in historical context, drawing comparisons to territory-era wrestling in Memphis. He noted that Jerry Lawler occasionally worked similar one-sided matches when establishing new top heels who would be pushed for extended runs. Lawler worked such matches against the debuting Kamala and the original Lord Humongous, portrayed by Mike Stark. The Lawler versus Kamala program proved to be a significant success in drawing business and generating a longer run of singles matches between the competitors. However, there was a crucial difference in those historical examples: Lawler was not the reigning world champion and top babyface when working those matches. Lawler's status meant he could absorb the loss without suffering long-term damage to his drawing power.

Meltzer emphasized the unprecedented nature of Cena's booking at SummerSlam 2014 by noting that modern wrestling history provided virtually no comparable examples at the world championship level. He examined major babyface champions and top stars throughout wrestling's history, including Dusty Rhodes, Hulk Hogan, Bill Goldberg, Triple H, Shawn Michaels, Bruno Sammartino, Giant Baba, Jumbo Tsuruta, Mitsuharu Misawa, and Kenta Kobashi, concluding that none of these legendary performers had ever worked a match where they put their heel opponent over in such a dominant, one-sided fashion while holding the top championship. This made Cena's decision to absorb the Lesnar assault a significant and rare booking choice in professional wrestling's modern era.
