🔥 Nixon Newell AEW Collision Walkout Controversy

Nixon Newell has set the record straight regarding the controversy surrounding her and Miranda Alize's departure from an AEW Collision taping on November 8, 2025. Speaking with CryptoCasinos.com, Newell stated that the initial reporting was largely based on assumptions from people who had no actual knowledge of what transpired backstage. She emphasized that wrestling news outlets had no factual understanding of the situation, leading to speculation that gained traction rather than the true story.

According to Newell's account, neither she nor Alize had any issue with losing their scheduled match against TayJay. The actual disagreement centered on the amount of time allocated for their match. Newell explained that they had been promised at least six minutes to work with something of substance, but AEW ultimately planned to give them only three minutes. She clarified that they were not asking for an extended fifteen-minute time-limit extravaganza, simply requesting what they had been initially promised.

Newell stressed that the outcome of the match was never the problem. She noted that losing has been a consistent part of her career and that both she and Alize understood AEW's position. She characterized the disagreement as a straightforward business matter, stating that they and the company's higher-ups reached an understanding. Rather than creating a major confrontation, she indicated that AEW management was respectful of their decision and even extended an open invitation for them to return to the promotion in the future. Management reportedly told them, "Cool, we understand. Thank you so much. We'd love to have you guys back, but that's fine. We respect you coming to us and not kicking up a fuss."

The fallout from the walkout, however, became significantly more serious than the initial disagreement warranted. Newell revealed that the story spiraled due to people without knowledge of the situation adding their own narratives and creating scenarios that never actually occurred. She mentioned that some reports included accusations of her arguing with people she had never even met. One particular headline made her realize how distorted the narrative had become: when someone posted "Nixon Newell pounds beer after walking out of AEW," Newell found the characterization absurd, noting that as a British person, it was merely a single Michelob Ultra.

Beyond the inaccurate reporting, Newell disclosed the darker consequences she faced. For approximately four months following the walkout, she received death threats on a daily basis. The vitriol from some wrestling fans reached disturbing levels, with messages explicitly telling her to kill herself because she had walked out on AEW over what was essentially a booking disagreement on a single match.

Newell also addressed what she perceives as a pattern of unwarranted animosity directed toward her online. She noted that there exists a small group of people who appear to have despised her for years, seemingly stemming from her serious knee injury during WWE's Mae Young Classic in 2017. Despite her efforts to be a good person and remain polite, she expressed bewilderment at the deep-seated hostility some individuals harbor toward her for reasons she cannot comprehend.

The sustained harassment and negative attention following the AEW incident appears to have contributed to Newell's recent announcement that 2026 will be her final year as an active in-ring competitor. When asked whether the backlash from the walkout played a role in her retirement decision, she confirmed that it did accelerate her thinking on the matter. The combination of inaccurate reporting, public misunderstanding of a straightforward business disagreement, and the subsequent months of daily death threats and harassment created an environment that influenced her decision to step away from professional wrestling.

Mick Foley and Darby Allin AEW

Mick Foley, now part of AEW's broadcast team, has opened up about the striking similarities between his wrestling style and that of Darby Allin, revealing insights into how the younger star has channeled his influence while making it entirely his own.

During an interview, Foley reflected on his first night in AEW and how he initially remembered Allin as "the quiet kid who didn't speak up much." It was through John Moxley's insistence that Foley began to truly appreciate Allin's abilities. "Then it was only John Moxley saying you have to see this kid, he can do anything, that I began to see an image of Darby Allin as a younger fan maybe not quite fitting in and seeing someone who made it cool to not fit in and who took chances," Foley explained. He continued, "So I see Darby as someone who took the inspiration from Cactus Jack and added a whole lot of athleticism to it. Did things that I could have never done. He's created a hybrid style that borrows from me a little bit and from others who were far more athletic than me. He's made it his own."

According to fashion outlet Women's Wear Daily, the partnership ended on good terms. Under Armour will continue to distribute Project Rock merchandise through October 2026. Johnson and his ex-wife, entrepreneur Dany Garcia, own the Project Rock trademark and will be seeking another manufacturing partner to produce the line going forward. Garcia and Johnson, who met in college and were married for 11 years, have continued their business relationship, serving as cofounders of the studio Seven Bucks Productions and as partners in the UFL football league they purchased along with RedBill Capital Partners.

The Brahma Bull logo synonymous with Johnson has been prominently featured throughout the Project Rock line during its run with Under Armour. The brand was created in 2017 with Johnson's philosophy that training extends beyond physical strength to build discipline, clarity, confidence, and peace. The Project Rock line created performance apparel, accessories, and footwear, and during the partnership, it had a multi-year collaboration with the UFC.

Johnson expressed his appreciation for the partnership in a statement, saying: "I'm grateful to Under Armour and to every person who helped us build this epic chapter of Project Rock. I'm proud of what we created together, and even more proud of all the people behind the scenes who poured themselves into Project Rock and took great pride in making it their own. As we sharpen our focus and strengthen our core business, we're creating a more unified expression in our train category. We're incredibly grateful to DJ and his entire team for their partnership, friendship and everything we've built together over the past decade. We wish him and his team nothing but continued success."

There's an innate discipline that training breeds in all of us, that allows us to tap into those responses. And watching our Project Rock community empower themselves with this mindset has been one of the most rewarding parts of this long, ever growing journey."

When Undertaker responded, he said, "Trick, I'm going to make you famous," before delivering the chokeslam. August 14 holds a special place in professional wrestling history as a date that has consistently produced memorable matches, historic title changes, and significant moments across multiple promotions and decades. The date's significance spans from major international wrestling events to surprising career turns and championship transitions that have shaped the landscape of the industry.

The most celebrated match to take place on August 14 is widely considered to be AJ Styles versus Hiroshi Tanahashi from New Japan Pro Wrestling on August 14, 2015. According to Cagematch ratings, this match earns a 9.41 rating, and Dave Meltzer awarded it ****3/4 stars. The match, which lasted 27 minutes and 56 seconds, saw Tanahashi defeat Styles to win the A block in what was built as a match of the year candidate. The bout began slowly but escalated into an epic encounter featuring multiple near falls and false finishes.

The story of the Styles versus Tanahashi match centered on each competitor being unable to defeat the other using their opponent's signature move. Styles executed a high fly flow for a near fall, while Tanahashi hit the Styles clash but could not get the victory. The match featured several memorable moments, including Styles hitting Tanahashi with a low blow and Tanahashi reciprocating the illegal tactic. Tanahashi sold a calf killer submission hold with great emotion. The decisive finish came when Tanahashi caught Styles attempting a Pele kick with a dragon screw, followed by a crossbody off the top and finally the high fly flow for the pin. According to Meltzer's detailed coverage, the match featured smooth execution of signature moves, particularly praising Styles' springboard quebrada into a reverse DDT. The match's psychology was strong, with both competitors attempting to finish with the other's moves, creating a narrative that elevated the bout beyond a simple athletic display.

Alex Wright WCW Ric Flair Push

Alex Wright's rise in WCW came with crucial guidance from Ric Flair and an unconventional character element that would define his career. In interviews with Bryan Asbury on Developmentally Speaking, Wright detailed how these two factors shaped his trajectory in professional wrestling during a formative period.

Wright's journey nearly ended before it began when personal struggles threatened his career prospects. After moving to the United States, isolation and financial difficulties led to heavy drinking and significant weight gain. He estimated gaining roughly 40 pounds during this difficult stretch, a physical transformation that caught the attention of company leadership for the wrong reasons. Ric Flair, recognizing Wright's potential as a performer, decided to intervene backstage with a direct message about what needed to change. "Hey, listen kid. I know you're talented and we really want to do something with you, but right now, how you look... you need to change. Otherwise we can't do anything with you," Flair told him. Wright described the intervention as exactly what he needed at that moment, calling it "the foot on my ass" that motivated real change. He responded by overhauling his lifestyle, changing his diet and committing to training twice daily. The results were dramatic. Wright dropped from 250 pounds down to 190 pounds, a transformation that eventually earned Flair's approval. When Flair saw the results, he acknowledged the change in clear terms: "Okay, now you look like Das Wunderkind." With his physical presentation finally matching the vision WCW had for him, the company began developing serious plans for his career.

While Flair's tough love intervention created the physical foundation for Wright's push, the character element that would make him truly memorable came from an unexpected source. The famous dancing that became synonymous with Das Wunderkind originated from a casual night out at a nightclub with WCW personnel. Wright was simply dancing the way he normally would to German techno music, a natural expression of his cultural background. Someone from the company noticed something different about his movements and asked what it was. From Wright's perspective, there was nothing unusual about it. "Well, that's how I dance in Germany," he explained. The WCW response was immediate and direct: "You have to do that in the ring." Initially, Wright resisted the idea completely. Having been trained with a traditional wrestling mentality, he was reluctant to blend that type of entertainment into his presentation. "No, I'm not going to do that," he initially responded. However, WCW eventually convinced him to try it, and Wright's opinion changed dramatically once he witnessed the live reaction from audiences. "That was the best decision what I could have done," he reflected years later. Wright believes the dance remains a major reason fans remember him decades after his wrestling career ended, stating that "One big part is the dance. It was different." He also noted that "It made me very popular and everybody knew me very quickly."

The very element that made Wright so recognizable, however, came with a significant drawback. The dancing became so closely associated with him that some viewers overlooked his actual wrestling ability. This overemphasis on the character's entertainment value eventually led Wright to seek something completely different. His attempt to escape that image resulted in the darker Berlyn reinvention, a character transformation that represented Wright's desire to be recognized as a serious competitor rather than just an entertainer. This later evolution in his career demonstrated how the foundation of his early WCW success, while incredibly effective, ultimately shaped the trajectory of his entire professional wrestling identity.
