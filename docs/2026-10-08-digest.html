🔥 Swerve Strickland and wrestling world react to the passing of PAC

The sudden passing of AEW star Pac is something that fans, wrestlers, and everyone in between are still attempting to come to terms with. Everyone grieves in their own way and at different speeds, but for those in the AEW locker room like Swerve Strickland, moving forward after such a devastating loss can be extremely difficult. Strickland revealed that what has been helping him and those around him is being open and honest about their emotions rather than staying quiet. He noted that he heard the news through Darby Allin, which led him to immediately call their good friend Ricochet. Communication became a powerful tool for the entire locker room during this difficult period. Everyone honored Pac with a special tribute episode of television, where Strickland and The New Level laid their AEW World Trios Championships on the mat with one of Pac's signature towels over them in honor of his historic time with the titles.

Strickland also shared fond memories, going back to the first time he witnessed Pac wrestle in 2011 at a Dragon Gate USA and CZW double header in the ECW Arena, where Pac faced Brodie Lee. Strickland later learned that this exact match was what originally landed Brodie Lee a contract. The last time Strickland shared the ring with Pac was in the Trios Roulette Royal at All In London 2026.

As the wrestling community heals, a comprehensive 39-minute tribute video has been released featuring emotional messages and personal stories from numerous superstars who worked with him during his six years as Neville. Kevin Owens remembered working with Satterley for the first time in the United Kingdom in 2006, where Satterley was initially nervous before putting on an unforgettable performance that led Owens to call him the best wrestler he had ever seen. Owens admitted Satterley would have hated everyone speaking so highly of him, but stressed that sharing these memories is therapeutic. Owens was also the driving force behind setting up a backstage camera at television tapings so talent could record their personal memories and tributes for the family.

Sami Zayn, who had known Satterley since they were kids, delivered an emotional tribute, reflecting on how they grew into men together and lived a dream life across twenty unbelievable years. Zayn sent his love to Satterley's wife Natalie, his mother, and his dog, noting he would always see him as the five-year-old kid he grew up with. Bayley remembered Satterley's personality during the black and gold era as a total goofball who eventually let his colleagues in after seeing how much his wife Natalie enjoyed their humor. Penta and Rey Fenix of Death Triangle expressed deep gratitude for his friendship and career advice, calling him their big brother. William Regal stated that outside of his own family, there were very few people he thought more of than Ben. Finn Balor shared memories dating back to their childhood in Newcastle, recalling church basement wrestling mats, trips to Japan, visiting parents in Ireland, and talking about everything except wrestling while enjoying tea and discussing football and comedy.

Other colleagues, including Becky Lynch, Cody Rhodes, Big Cass, Tyson Kidd, and Corey Graves, highlighted Satterley's incredible humility, his desire to elevate everyone around him, his genuine kindness, and his deep love for his family and hometown of Newcastle. Tributes also touched upon his love of dancing, ska, and reggae music, as well as the recent tragic loss of his close friend The Butcher, Andy Williams.

On the investigative side, new details have emerged surrounding his death through the release of a 911 call made by the woman who first found him unresponsive. The incident occurred on September 27, approximately 15 minutes before Satterley was officially pronounced dead at 408 p.m. The caller initially believed the person in the running rental vehicle was a woman due to long hair, but quickly realized it was a man after getting a closer look. Witnesses spent about five minutes trying to get his attention by banging on the window, describing him as completely unresponsive with his face straight down and eyes open. First responders attempted life-saving measures at a CAVA restaurant location on Mannheim Road in Des Plaines, where he had planned to stop for lunch before catching a delayed flight back to the United Kingdom. Police reports confirmed no signs of foul play, with his phone still displaying Google Maps directions to O'Hare International Airport. Medication was found inside the vehicle, but the official cause of death remains pending further testing by the Cook County Medical Examiner's Office.

Chris Jericho addresses potential WWE Hall of Fame induction

AEW star Chris Jericho has once again addressed his chances of eventually entering the WWE Hall of Fame, expressing that he is completely fine with whatever happens regarding the honor. The former WWE Undisputed Champion is widely considered one of the most accomplished wrestlers of his generation, having spent nearly two decades with the company with a few years off. However, his ongoing run with AEW could make a potential WWE Hall of Fame induction complicated due to the current landscape of professional wrestling.

Speaking in a recent interview, Jericho stated that his extensive WWE career gave him more than enough accomplishments to deserve a place in the Hall of Fame. Looking back at his legacy, Jericho believes he did enough to be considered one of the industry's true legends. He noted that it was always his dream to work in WWE, questioning if he created enough memorable moments, matches, characters, creations, and rivalries to earn the nod. He answered his own question with an absolute yes, adding that this is all he needs.

Jericho also understands that his current AEW affiliation, along with potential industry tribalism and politics, could make a WWE induction difficult. Having been in the wrestling business for 36 years, he understands how the industry works, emphasizing that it is not personal and he is not insulted by any delays or omissions. While he remains confident about his legacy, he is not overly concerned about receiving an official plaque or corporate recognition from his former employer.

The former AEW World Champion explained that he already considers himself to be in a hall of fame of sorts because of the lasting impact his career has had on wrestling fans. He added that if he finishes his career with AEW and becomes the first, second, or tenth inductee into an AEW Hall of Fame, that would be great too, but ultimately he is secure in his status. He stated that he is in the hall of fame in his head and in the heads of anybody that appreciates the work that he has done.

Reflecting on his blessed career traveling the world and doing what he always wanted to do, Jericho noted that making people happy or even getting people angry through his craft is essentially the same thing. He concluded that experiencing all of that represents a true hall of fame career all across the board. Previously, Jericho had even shrugged off the concept of the WWE Hall of Fame, noting that there is no real physical hall of fame and that such accolades did not mean that much to him.

Eric Bischoff comments on PAC tribute show and recent AEW injuries/deaths

The professional wrestling locker room suffered back-to-back tragedies in recent weeks, marked first by the loss of Andy Williams at age 48, followed by the death of Benjamin Satterley, known as PAC, just three weeks later at age 40. Eric Bischoff, who is no stranger to passings in the wrestling industry, discussed these recent deaths and the subsequent tribute shows on his podcast. Bischoff noted that it is emotional for everyone involved, even for peers who did not have a close relationship with the deceased. To have to go out and perform as a character and focus on what you are doing in the ring for your own safety as well as the person you are in there with has got to be challenging. Co-host Conrad Thompson suggested that while there was no foul play and sometimes life just throws a curveball, the passings reminded him of an era in the 1990s and early 2000s when a lot of wrestlers died in their early forties. Bischoff was not as convinced that it was merely a coincidence. Every once in a while, someone will get struck by lightning, but having two guys pass away at age 40 means something is going on, Bischoff stated, adding that it is not natural and he hopes they are not revisiting the pattern of frequent early deaths experienced back in the 1990s. Official causes of death for Williams and Satterley have yet to be released by officials, though initial reports indicated Satterley's death did not appear suspicious.

Aside from locker room tragedies, Bischoff severely lambasted AEW for putting Steven Borden, the son of the legendary Sting, on television prematurely. Borden made his in-ring debut last month after several dark matches and, in just his third match on television, suffered an injury after a nasty high spot at the All Out pay-per-view. Although reports indicated that Borden is fine, Bischoff expressed anger that Borden was allowed to perform the spot and appear on television so soon into his career. Questioning the role of the veterans collecting a paycheck backstage, Bischoff asked why nobody stepped in to stop a kid from doing something inappropriate for his stage of development. He argued that Borden shouldn't even be wrestling on television yet, describing him as a deer in the headlights, a feeling Bischoff and his own son Garrett experienced before gaining necessary experience, confidence, and developmental time. The Hall of Famer expressed worry over the head injury and argued that the young star is being set up for failure, emphasizing that people only have so many opportunities in life and must protect them. Bischoff concluded by stating that he has a lot of respect for Sting, and that Tony Khan should have known about the spot, taken responsibility for it, and prevented it from happening.

Big Vinny V: Why next week’s Monday Night Raw is so important for the 946

As the saying goes, you only get one chance to make a first impression. For the 946, that chance comes next week on Monday Night Raw. Next week's match against the Bloodline will be the first time many fans see the 946 in action, particularly Bronco Nima and Lucien Price.

Though it is not the first high-profile match in Keys career, having performed on fifteen pay-per-views and premium live events across AEW and WWE, it remains critical for him to show he can deliver at a main event level. This high-stakes scenario makes it especially curious that their very first opponents as a unit are going to be Solo Sikoa and the Usos. Nima and Price have wrestled the Usos exactly one time previously, which was a dark match before SmackDown in Denver, and their only prior encounter with Sikoa was in a dark match battle royal in NXT over four years ago.

For two competitors who came up through the NXT system where matches are often rehearsed for several days, stepping onto a live Monday Night Raw broadcast represents a true sink-or-swim opportunity. The dynamic differs slightly for Keys. While it feels outwardly like he has been feuding with the Bloodline for months, he has actually shared the ring with them in a relatively limited capacity. He has faced the Usos about a half-dozen times in various singles and tag combinations, alongside exactly three matches either teaming with or wrestling against Sikoa.

Obviously, Keys brings significantly more experience to the table than his partners, but he is still navigating new waters within WWE. All of these statistical matchups are guaranteed to shift over the coming months, as the 946 and the Bloodline are expected to lock horns in plenty of matches for the rest of the year. Even so, only one of those encounters can happen first. The 946 must capitalize on that initial chance to prove immediately that they belong on the exact same stage as the premier WWE faction of this era.

🔄 המשך Samoa Joe qualifies for Clockwork Carnage at AEW WrestleDream over Jack Perry

At Grand Slam France, Samoa Joe punched his ticket to WrestleDream by overcoming Jack Perry in a hard-fought qualifier. In the closing moments of the bout, Perry climbed to the top turnbuckle before Joe cut him off with an enzigiri. Perry spat in Joe's face in a final act of defiance, only for the veteran to drop him with a devastating Muscle Buster. This match marked Joe's first appearance back in the ring since his May loss to Will Ospreay, successfully re-establishing him as a dominant force following a nearly five-month hiatus.

The newly introduced Clockwork Carnage match at WrestleDream will feature unique rules to test its competitors. Two wrestlers will start in the ring, with new participants entering randomly every three minutes. Eliminations occur via pinfall and submission until the final entrant joins, at which point the bout transitions into a 15-minute iron-man scoring period. The competitor with the most falls at the end of that period will walk away with the AEW World Championship.

For Jack Perry, the defeat marks another setback in his stated mission to challenge and defeat every former AEW World Champion. Having previously fallen to Jon Moxley and Swerve Strickland, Perry has now lost to a third of the company's historical world champions. With formidable names like MJF, Kenny Omega, and Bryan Danielson still left on his checklist, Perry faces an uphill battle to prove he belongs in the undisputed title picture after accumulating multiple losses against the roster's elite.

🔄 המשך The future of Clockwork Carnage after AEW WrestleDream

Clockwork Carnage is coming to AEW WrestleDream, and there is a strong possibility it will become a recurring staple on the company's calendar rather than a one-off. The match rules feature eight wrestlers across two stages. Two competitors start at random, with another wrestler entering every three minutes. Until all eight participants have entered, any pinfall or submission results in elimination. Once the final wrestler enters, the remaining competitors enter a fifteen-minute scoring period where every fall counts, and the wrestler with the most falls at the expiration of time wins the match and the AEW World Championship.

Will Ospreay, Samoa Joe, and Mike Bailey have already qualified for the inaugural bout, with Samoa Joe securing his spot by defeating Jack Perry. Because the stipulation originated in the all-women promotion Pro Wrestling Eve, speculation has also arisen that the concept could eventually be utilized for the AEW Women's Championship.

Beyond the upcoming pay-per-view, Ospreay's current title reign and the new match concept have sparked heavy speculation about who will eventually dethrone the champion. Samoa Joe is positioned as a top threat if he capitalizes at WrestleDream. Other heavily discussed contenders include Darby Allin, whom Ospreay has expressed a desire to face given their history, and Andrade El Idolo, who holds a guaranteed title shot from winning the Casino Gauntlet at All In London. MJF also remains a looming threat to capture the gold as he continues to stake his own claim to the top position in the company.

🔄 המשך Chris Jericho's future plans and role in AEW

Continuing his mission to build the future of the company, Jericho is currently locked in a heated feud with 21-year-old Nick Wayne, with the pair heading to AEW Collision to settle their differences once and for all. Wayne has been a prominent fixture across AEW and Ring of Honor, previously holding gold and learning valuable experience from established veterans like Christian Cage. Entering a program with Jericho adds another layer of prestige to the young star's development, offering an in-ring education that comes from decades of television experience.

While Jericho insists that winning is no longer a priority, he concedes that he still needs the occasional victory so he is not just losing all the time, which would hurt the credibility of the stories. Rather than chasing championships for personal glory, his focus remains entirely on match quality and helping younger talent discover hidden potential. With a career spanning over three decades, Jericho sees his future in AEW defined by high-profile feuds that ultimately pass the torch and bring out the best in the next generation of stars.
