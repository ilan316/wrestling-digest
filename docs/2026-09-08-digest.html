AEW Announces Debut in Green Bay for Collision

All Elite Wrestling is heading to a brand new market later this year, making its first-ever trip to Green Bay, Wisconsin. The company officially announced that the Saturday, November 21 episode of AEW Collision will take place live from the Resch Center.

Tickets for the historic event will go on sale to the general public on Monday, September 14, with early-access premium seating starting the day prior. Fans looking to secure presale access can sign up as an AEW Insider through the promotion's ticketing website. Promotional material for the debut features advertising graphics highlighting wrestlers Kevin Knight, Hikaru Shida, Kyle Fletcher, and Thekla.

This upcoming Green Bay edition of Collision is scheduled to take place one week after the AEW Full Gear 2026 pay-per-view event. As of the announcement, the exact location for the post-Full Gear fallout episode of Dynamite has not yet been revealed, nor has the venue for the November 25 Thanksgiving eve episode of Dynamite. Notably, that week will feature standalone live broadcasts for both Dynamite and Collision, deviating from the common format where Collision is taped immediately following the live Dynamite show.

While Green Bay represents a completely new territory for the company, the state of Wisconsin is no stranger to All Elite Wrestling. The promotion has visited the state five times previously, though all of those prior events were held in Milwaukee. AEW's very first show in Milwaukee took place in August 2021 and famously included CM Punk's historic first-ever appearance on Dynamite. The most recent Wisconsin event prior to this announcement was a Collision episode hosted in Milwaukee in March 2025.

Opinion: Champion Scenarios in AEW

Can’t Knock The Hustle: Let’s Crown Some New Champions (AEW Edition)

If you read my previous column, you already know what’s up here. For the rest of you, I received a request to write a column that would have me placed in charge of WWE and AEW. My job in that new role would be to choose new champions for every title in the company based on what I wanted to do, and not on what the current storylines may or may not be indicating. The request wanted me to start with AEW, but the time crunch coming out of All In(nit) made that a tough one for me pull off, so I went with WWE and NXT first. Now, it’s finally AEW’s turn. This isn’t me saying that the current champions are doing a bad job. In some cases, I’m saying that the current champion should remain champion for a very long time. We’re simply talking about title changes, whether they’re a week away or a year away. As I said, this is about who I would crown as new champions, not predictions on who I think will be the new champions. Time for some belts to change hands.

AEW National Title (Currently Held By Andrade El Idolo)
Writer’s Note: I wrote this section before Josh Alexander returned on Dynamite and got involved in Darby Allin’s storyline. I’m keeping this here, though, because all of my points remain. One of the biggest problems that AEW has right now is the amount of titles they have. Yes, the company has a massive roster, but there’s not much of a difference between the National, International, and TNT Titles, are there? Because of that, you could pencil people in for any of those titles, with no issues whatsoever. I’m going to go with a name that I think has been seriously underutilized since he debuted for AEW in April 2025. He’s someone that needs some work, though, as he hasn’t won a match on AEW programming in seven months, and hasn’t won a singles match on AEW programming in 11 months… and that match was a count-out victory. You have to go back to May 21st, 2025 to find the last time Josh Alexander won a singles match on AEW programming by pinfall. AEW fans are smart, though. They don’t view him as a no-talent type of guy just because he loses more often than he wins. Give him some momentum-building wins, and he’ll be in position to compete for a midcard title in no time.

AEW Women’s Tag Team Titles (Currently Held By Jamie Hayter & Alex Windsor)
Let’s shake things up a bit. I like the business relationship between AEW and Stardom, and I would continue that business relationship here. AEW needs more instances where the promotions they work with come out on top, even for a brief period of time. Give me the current Goddesses Of Stardom, Stardom’s Tag Titles, Champions, AZM and Miyu Amasaki, in this spot. Unless they’re going to come over for a longer excursion, the 02line duo isn’t going to be a team that holds the AEW belts for an extended period, but that’s fine. Not all reigns need to be record-breaking.

AEW Trios Titles (Currently Held By Swerve Strickland, Austin Creed & Kofi)
Generally, this isn’t a championship that is held for a lengthy period of time. Counting the trio of Samoa Joe, Powerhouse Hobbs, and Katsuyori Shibata, who entered the year as champions, Swerve and New Level are the ninth set of champions in 2026. Of the previous 16 champions, six of them saw reigns that lasted less than a month. Combine that with the fact that Kofi and Creed are definitely in AEW to compete for the Tag Team Titles, and I think this is probably going to be another relatively brief reign. I could probably go in a few different directions here, but I’m going with a trio that really needs it for different reasons. Let’s go with Switchblade Jay White, Juice Robinson, and Ace Austin here. Switchblade is someone who had people thinking he would be a World Champion when he first signed with AEW, but that obviously hasn’t happened. Juice is someone who gets attention because of the way he looks and the wild antics that his character delivers on-screen, but he’s actually one of the more underrated guys on the AEW roster when it comes to in-ring work.
