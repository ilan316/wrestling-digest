🔥 AEW Full Gear travel impacted as flight groundings leave wrestlers stranded

AEW Full Gear 2026 faced last-minute uncertainty after the Federal Aviation Administration issued a rare nationwide ground stop on Friday. The FAA ordered departing flights across the United States to remain grounded following a communications issue and telecommunications outage affecting air traffic control centers. The agency cited safety concerns and extraordinary circumstances while technicians worked to investigate the problem and restore normal operations, which resulted in nearly 7,000 flights facing delays or cancellations.

According to Bryan Alvarez, several AEW wrestlers had already traveled to Phoenix following the week's Dynamite and Collision tapings. However, others who had not yet departed were stranded, raising initial concerns about whether everyone scheduled for Full Gear would arrive in time for the Saturday pay-per-view at the Mortgage Matchup Center. Alvarez reported that many AEW wrestlers flew to the pay-per-view straight from Dynamite, but others who had not left yet were stranded. He noted that the longer the ground stop lasted, the greater the likelihood of major delays and people missing the event entirely, adding that some individuals were told the ground stop might not be lifted until 8 PM, which would be a disaster.

The timing added to a week of travel headaches for AEW. During his Full Gear media call, Tony Khan explained that several wrestlers had already arrived late for the previous tapings because of flight problems following the Chris Jericho Cruise, forcing last-minute changes to the show. Furthermore, internet personalities Big Boom AJ, Big Justice, and The Rizzler expressed determination to make the event despite the ongoing flight issues.

Fortunately, the situation improved later in the day. Dave Meltzer reported that although the situation was initially scary for AEW, the ground stop was eventually lifted. Meltzer noted that with the shutdown over, everyone scheduled for the event was expected to make it, including Bandido, who had a booking working Arena Mexico on Friday night and would need to fly out after his match.

Other wrestling promotions also monitored the situation closely. WWE managed to avoid major disruptions for Friday night's SmackDown in Sunrise, Florida, as the company typically flies talent in the day prior. NXT crews also made it safely to their house show locations for the holiday live tour, which featured appearances by stars like Bayley in Cleveland and Detroit, and CM Punk in Detroit and Chicago. Meanwhile, TNA Wrestling faced potential travel complications ahead of its Destination X pay-per-view in Edmonton, Alberta, Canada, since most talent and personnel were scheduled to fly in on Saturday, leaving the promotion vulnerable if the flight restrictions had persisted longer.

Despite the initial panic and severe travel hurdles across the country, the FAA successfully lifted the ground stop, averting a major crisis for AEW Full Gear 2026.

🔥 AEW's Bobby Lashley wins 4th Rope Heavyweight Championship

AEW star Bobby Lashley has added another championship to his collection by capturing the 4th Rope Heavyweight Championship during the promotion's Heelmatic event on November 12 in New York.

The title change came after Joe Alonzo was initially awarded the vacant championship by 4th Rope Wrestling commissioner and General Manager MVP. Alonzo had argued that he was cheated out of a previous opportunity to compete for the title, leading MVP to award him the championship.

His reign wouldn't last long. Immediately after presenting Alonzo with the title, MVP announced that the new champion would have to defend it on the spot. That is when Lashley made his surprise appearance as Alonzo's challenger.

The former WWE Champion made quick work of the newly crowned titleholder, winning the match in just two minutes. Lashley quickly took control, connecting with a belly-to-belly suplex before delivering his signature spear and scoring the pinfall victory to become the new champion. Following the victory, MVP came to the ring and personally placed the championship around Lashley's waist.

The victory gives Lashley his second active singles championship outside of AEW, as he also currently holds the ACW World Championship in Australia Championship Wrestling. During his current AEW run, Lashley previously held the AEW World Tag Team Championships alongside fellow Hurt Syndicate member Shelton Benjamin during a 214-day reign. Before winning the 4th Rope Heavyweight Championship, Lashley last wrestled on the October 28 edition of AEW Collision, teaming with Benjamin to defeat Aaron Solo and Nick Comoroto.

Mercedes Mone reflects on attending Forbes Power Women's Summit

AEW star Mercedes Mone recently reflected on her experience attending the Forbes Power Women's Summit in New York City, where top women from various industries gathered to inspire others on using their influence for good. Fresh off a notable win at All In, Mone brought along her AEW Women's Championship and a nose cast to the September 10 event.

In the latest edition of Mone Mag, Mone described her experience as incredible. She noted that she was not walking into the room just as Mercedes Mone, but as the AEW Women's World Champion representing an industry and a division of women who deserve recognition on that level. She brought her championship to show what women's professional wrestling represents, including the dedication required to become a world champion, the built business, the audiences reached, and the global impact. She emphasized that the championship carries much more than her name.

The 2026 Forbes Power Women's Summit centered around the theme the future of power and spotlighted leaders within sports, entertainment, business, beauty, and science. Mone specifically recalled having a conversation with entrepreneur Haley Sacks, also known as Mrs. Dow Jones, about negotiating, being The CEO of wrestling, and her reign as AEW Women's World Champion. For Mone, these discussions provided a rare opportunity to showcase the businesswoman behind her wrestling persona and served as a reminder of the immense progress women's wrestling has made.

Mone emphasized that for years, women in professional wrestling fought to be taken seriously within their own industry, fighting for more time, bigger opportunities, and the chance to headline the biggest shows, which they are now achieving. She noted they are drawing global audiences, building brands, creating businesses, and inspiring young girls, questioning why they should not also be in these prestigious rooms.

According to Mone, the figures surrounding her at the summit hailed from different industries, yet all understood the struggle of working for opportunities. In her case, Mone wants women's wrestling recognized on a larger scale far beyond the squared circle, and she is eager to see other women's wrestling champions invited to big events to have their stories heard alongside the world's most influential women. She expressed her desire for the next generation of women in wrestling to walk into these rooms knowing they belong there, which is why the invitation meant so much to her.

Alongside Mone, the summit featured high-profile figures such as actress Jennifer Garner, singer Natasha Bedingfield, supermodel Cindy Crawford, NASA astronaut Christina Koch, and tennis legend Serena Williams, all of whom contributed to discussions about power, leadership, and the future.

In her immediate wrestling future, Mone is scheduled to defend her AEW Women's World Championship against Marina Shafir at AEW Full Gear, which is slated for November 14 in Phoenix.

Stevie Richards criticizes crowd reaction to Darby Allin's Blood & Guts cage fall

Stevie Richards recently shared his critical perspective on Darby Allin’s dangerous fall from the Blood and Guts cage, noting that the stunt drew far less crowd response than he expected for such a high-risk maneuver. Speaking on his eponymous program, the former WWE and ECW wrestler contrasted the muted reaction he heard with the sustained concern and shock that followed Mick Foley’s famous Hell in a Cell fall. The incident occurred when Wardlow sent Allin crashing from the cage roof through tables after the match had already concluded. Richards clarified that he was offering his own interpretation of the audience rather than an independently measured account of every fan's reaction.

Describing the impact, Richards expressed sympathy for Allin while breaking down the audience's lukewarm reception. He noted that Allin actually overshot the tables slightly, making it a very rough fall from the cage to the floor. According to Richards, the audience reaction resembled a routine high-impact move like a top-rope superplex or a standard setup for a finish rather than a genuine spectacle. He characterized the response as a subdued murmur of acknowledgment rather than a massive crowd pop, describing it as a simple wow followed by general arena noise.

Richards went on to question whether the frequent repetition of cage falls has dulled their overall effect on viewers, while emphasizing that the physical danger to the performers remains just as high. He admitted that the lack of energy from the audience made the situation feel like a shame, especially considering that the physical toll on the wrestler does not decrease just because the crowd is getting used to it. He pointed out that the bump actually hurts more when there is no vibrant energy or explosive reaction from the arena to validate the immense risk being taken.

Observing the fans in the arena right after Allin landed, Richards noted that many people remained sitting in their seats. He pointed out that nobody seemed to be rushing up or leaning anxiously over the railings to check on the wrestler's condition. Rather than blaming the fans for the lackluster atmosphere, Richards placed the responsibility squarely on creative booking and the overexposure of dangerous spots. He argued that if an entire crowd is not standing up when such a massive stunt is executed, it is a clear sign that the promotion is doing the spot too often and needs to pull back or completely reset its approach. Richards' commentary focused strictly on the psychological and entertainment impact of the stunt on the live audience rather than any confirmed injury to Allin.

Page did a moonsault off the top rope to the floor. Page used a brainbuster. Omega used palm strikes and a springboard sunset flip into a power bomb off the top rope, followed by two snap dragon suplexes and a third snap dragon suplex on the apron. Omega hit his first V trigger, went for a One Winged Angel but Page turned it into a victory roll. Page gave Omega a German suplex on his head, though landing on your head like that is questionable for neck problems and vertigo. Omega blocked the Dead Eye and hit the J driller.

Omega bit Page in the head and Page came back and bit Omega in the head. Page was on the top rope and hit the moonsault fallaway while standing on the top rope. Page climbed to the top of the post and dove to the floor with a clothesline that put Omega through a table on the edge of the setup. Page went for the Buckshot Lariat but Omega collapsed. Omega hit a V trigger and a German suplex but Page landed on his feet and hit a power bomb. Page accidentally hit the referee with a Buckshot Lariat when Omega put referee Paul Turner in the way. Omega went for a belt shot but Page ducked and hit the Dead Eye, resulting in Omega kicking out. Page had the belt, but threw it away. They traded elbows and punches. Page came back with a big clothesline. Omega used head-butts and a back suplex.

The Young Bucks came out. Page was on one side, looked at Nick, who didn’t interfere, and hit a Buckshot Lariat to the back of the head. He went to the other side of the ring, looked at Matt, who nodded his head signaling it is your time, and hit the final Buckshot Lariat to pin Omega. This was a classic world title change match and stands out as one of those encounters where you notice more and more things on repeat viewing.
