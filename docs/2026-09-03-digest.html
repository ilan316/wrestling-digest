🔥 Will Ospreay to defend AEW World Championship against Jon Moxley at All Out 2026

Fresh off his monumental AEW World Championship victory over Kenny Omega at AEW All In London at Wembley Stadium, Will Ospreay already has his first title defense locked in. During the closing moments of the September 2 episode of AEW Dynamite in Allen, Texas, it was officially announced that Ospreay will put his newly won gold on the line against Jon Moxley at AEW All Out. The major pay-per-view event is scheduled for Saturday, September 26, at the NOW Arena in Hoffman Estates, Chicago, putting it head-to-head with the WWE, NXT, and AAA Worlds Collide event headlined by CM Punk at the Allstate Arena.

The shocking title match announcement came on the heels of a chaotic 10-man tag team main event on Dynamite. Ospreay teamed up with Andrade El Idolo, HENARE, Callum Newman, and Francesco Akira of the United Empire to defeat a combination of The Demand, featuring Bishop Kaun, Ricochet, and Toa Liona, alongside The Dogs, featuring Gabe Kidd and Clark Connors. Ospreay secured the victory for his side by connecting with the Hidden Blade.

However, the celebration was immediately cut short. Andrade and Gabe Kidd continued brawling after the bell, forcing security to intervene. When Ospreay attempted to step in and help Andrade, Kidd caught the AEW World Champion with a low blow. Kidd then grabbed a steel chair and was hell-bent on attacking Ospreay, but Jon Moxley and the Death Riders rushed to the ring to cut him off. Moxley physically blocked Kidd from striking the champion, buying enough time for Andrade to take Kidd out and drag the fight backstage.

Left alone in the center of the ring, Moxley picked up the AEW World Championship, placed it on Ospreay's shoulder, and stared him down face-to-face as neither man backed down. Just as the former allies appeared to be back on the same page, the arena big screen unexpectedly flashed an official graphic revealing that Ospreay will defend the world title against Moxley at All Out. The announcement drew a massive reaction from the live crowd and left both Moxley and Ospreay visibly shocked, with Moxley quickly distancing himself as the broadcast went off the air.

This upcoming encounter marks their third-ever singles match, with Moxley currently holding a 2-0 advantage. Their first meeting took place at NJPW Windy City Riot in 2022, followed by a rematch at AEW Dynasty in April where Moxley successfully defended the Continental Championship. Ospreay also spent several months as part of the Death Riders faction earlier this year following his return from neck surgery before eventually splitting from the group. Moxley, who also defeated AR Fox in a Continental Championship Eliminator match earlier on Dynamite and retained his Continental Championship against Nigel McGuinness at All In, is now chasing his fifth AEW World Championship reign, having last held the belt before losing to Hangman Page at All In: Texas in July 2025.

In addition to the world title main event, AEW confirmed another massive match for the All Out card. AEW World Tag Team Champions Adam Copeland and Christian Cage will defend their titles against FTR and The Young Bucks in a three-way tables, ladders, and chairs match. The bout was set up after Matt and Nick Jackson confronted the champions on Dynamite over how they retained their titles at All In using a wrench, which prompted an interruption by the returning FTR. Christian Cage also took shots at the recent WWE departures of the New Level, and Copeland ultimately proposed the chaotic multi-team stipulation to settle the score, officially adding a second high-stakes championship showdown to the Chicago pay-per-view lineup.

Editorial opinion on the importance and psychology of championship belts

The simplicity of pro wrestling has always been what made it so appealing to me. There are good people and bad people and their conflicts are solved in the ring with the best of the best landing in that top spot with gorgeous golden belts around their waists. These belts signify not only their skill, but also their struggle and story. In recent years as the curtain has been pulled further and further back in wrestling, some of the things that we have come to know and love about it are showing the seams. You can see the holes cut in the Hell In a Cell for climbing spots. Tables are sponsored for breaking. Shiloh Hill has completely killed the kayfabe of poison mist, though the man likes to learn. The one thing that has always remained in the magical golden area of reality to me has been the title belts, jeweled trophies with lineage and prestige attached to them that are not given out anew every time someone wins one. They are passed along in a tribute to the history of wrestling.

Lately, something has been happening in WWE with championships that has me concerned and it started with Roman Reigns. After winning the World Heavyweight Championship, Reigns began arriving to the various arenas without his belt. As he entered, a production assistant would hand him his title belt and he would take it out to the ring. To my knowledge, it has usually been the case that a champion carries their belt with them when they travel to and from events. The care and protection of belts has provided endless hilarious, and sometimes cringe-worthy stories about wrestlers losing, protecting or wearing them. That added to their prestige that it meant enough that a champion personally took care of it during their reign.

On an episode of WWE NXT, new champion Grayson Waller also was handed his belt by a production assistant as he went to the ring. This completely takes me out of what is supposed to be an important story because it makes the title belt seem like simply a prop, one that is easily replaceable and only important for the scene it is in. I do not like that. Wrestling title belts are the pinnacle of the trophy game and should be held in high regard. I do not like belts that are wearable logos, I am not a fan of belts that have flashy side plates and I think it hurts a promotion credibility when a belt is devalued as a piece of wrestling lore. Champions, take care of your belts. TV producers, do not relegate them to something that can be put in a warehouse and thrown out later like a set or costume. Make them the symbols and important markers of the best of the best in your promotion because you know what, that is what they are.
