🔶 AEW News

🔥 Steven Borden details scary concussion suffered at AEW All Out 2026

Steven Borden has opened up about the scary concussion he suffered at AEW All Out 2026, revealing that he was knocked unconscious during the match and had to wait for medical clearance before returning to the ring. Borden teamed up with Darby Allin to face Kyle Fletcher and Kevin Knight at the September 26 pay-per-view. However, the match took a turn after Borden suffered a head injury during a mid-air collision involving a top-rope spot. Specifically, the injury occurred when Borden struck his head while attempting a double coast-to-coast maneuver with Allin against Kevin Knight and Kyle Fletcher, leaving him unable to continue and forcing the referee to stop the match live on pay-per-view, awarding the victory to Knight and Fletcher.

Speaking to First Coast News ahead of AEW Dynamite Blood and Guts, Borden confirmed that he suffered a concussion and was unconscious for some time during the match. It feels great to be back. So I got concussed, and I was knocked out for a little bit, Borden said. However, Darby Allin quickly corrected his tag team partner about how long he was unconscious. A little bit? You were snoring for six minutes straight. I was there in the ring. We had to stop the match live on pay-per-view. Stop the match and he is out there snoring and I was like, Steven? It wasn’t a little bit, Allin recalled, adding some levity while reflecting on how scary the situation was for everyone involved.

Steven Borden then explained why recovering from the concussion was a strange experience for him. With physical injuries like a torn ligament, wrestlers usually know right away that they cannot compete because they can feel it in a knee or elsewhere. However, Borden felt completely fine while recovering from his concussion. This made staying away from the ring even more frustrating, as he wanted to return but had to wait for medical clearance. It is weird though. With injuries, you are sidelined. Normally, you can feel it. You tore something in your knee or whatever, and so you know as much as you wanna get back in the action, you can’t. But for me, I felt fine. So it is just this weird thing of, like, I wanted to be back out there, but I couldn’t. And now that I am back, it feels pretty dang good, he explained.

Tony Schiavone later revealed that Borden was not even thinking primarily about himself afterwards. Despite experiencing his first concussion after years of playing football at the University of Kentucky, Borden was more worried that the whole thing had somehow ruined AEW is pay-per-view. He did tell me, he said, Of all the years that I played football for the University of Kentucky, I never had a concussion. I never experienced anything like that. And he was more upset that he thought that he ruined the pay-per-view by what happened, according to Schiavone.

After several weeks on the sidelines, Borden eventually returned to television on the October 21 episode of AEW Dynamite. AEW is not giving him a gentle warm-up match for his return, as he is set to compete in the men's Blood and Guts match. A guy who was desperately waiting to get off the sidelines is coming back in one of the most violent environments AEW has to offer. Borden will team with Allin, The Young Bucks, Adam Copeland, and Will Ospreay to take on Knight, Fletcher, Mark Davis, Jake Doyle, Wardlow, and Kazuchika Okada. Getting thrown straight into Blood and Guts might be a hell of a way to return, but after weeks of waiting around, Borden finally gets to stop watching and start fighting again.

🔥 AEW Senior Vice President Justin Street leaves company to return to TKO

Another executive has reportedly departed All Elite Wrestling, bringing an end to a relatively brief tenure with the promotion. Justin Street, who served as AEW's Senior Vice President of Corporate Partnerships and Sales, officially exited the company in October. Street had originally joined Tony Khan's promotion in January 2025. Additional details regarding the exact circumstances surrounding his departure from AEW have not been disclosed.

Following his exit, Street has returned to his former employer, Professional Bull Riders. The organization operates under the TKO Group Holdings umbrella, which is notably the parent company of both WWE and UFC.

Street is a University of Michigan graduate who originally started with Professional Bull Riders as their Director of Partnership Marketing in 2017. He spent a total of seven years with the company, climbing the ranks to hold the position of Senior Vice President of Corporate Partnerships and Media Sales for nine months before briefly switching companies to join AEW. Now, he has returned to his previous professional home within the TKO-owned corporate structure.

Will Ospreay accepts Callum Newman's challenge for NJPW Wrestle Kingdom 21

AEWs Will Ospreay has made a public request to the United Empire ahead of his newly confirmed match against Callum Newman at NJPW Wrestle Kingdom 21. After nearly a year of simmering tension, the friction between United Empire stablemates Will Ospreay and Callum Newman exploded during an IWGP Tag Team Title match against the Knockout Brothers at NJPW Power Struggle in Hiroshima. The match ended in disqualification after Newman hit OSKAR with a steel chair. Ospreay and Newman had argued throughout the contest and came to blows immediately afterward, culminating in Newman challenging Ospreay to a match at the Tokyo Dome.

A few hours later on social media, Ospreay accepted the challenge while admitting his shame over how bad the relationship had deteriorated. He has informed New Japan matchmakers that he will face Newman one-on-one at Wrestle Kingdom 21 on January 4, 2027. In his statement, Ospreay addressed the state of the stable, stating he sits embarrassed about what took place in Hiroshima and how their frustrations cost the team victory in front of loyal fans. He added that he cannot fully say what lies in the future for himself, Callum, or anyone else in the United Empire.

Ospreay also issued a public request to all United Empire players, asking them to allow him and Newman to uphold the code they set when the group first started, requesting seconds be welcomed but no interferences. He emphasized that they were never there to fight each other's battles, but rather to make each other strong enough to win them, expressing hope that the fractured group will heal and return to the top of the wrestling world.

Seeds of dissension between Ospreay and Newman first appeared at New Years Dash in January, when Newman teased an attack on Ospreay before being talked down by the stable. Further friction arose due to Ospreay's association with the Death Riders and his decision to create an AEW branch of the United Empire, which Newman had angrily questioned, though the two had maintained their friendship until the recent boiling point.

Several other marquee matches were also set up for the upcoming Wrestle Kingdom event during the Power Struggle show. Kenny Omega will challenge Yota Tsuji for the IWGP Heavyweight Championship, YOH will face SHO in a high-stakes stipulation match where the loser of YOH must leave NJPW and the loser of SHO must leave House of Torture, and MJF is set to face Ryohei Oiwa.

Wrestling Inc · F4W/WON

MJF set to make NJPW debut at Wrestle Kingdom 21 against G1 Climax winner

AEW star MJF is headed to Japan for the first time in his professional wrestling career. The top star is expected to make his New Japan Pro-Wrestling debut at Wrestle Kingdom 21, where he will face 2026 G1 Climax winner Ryohei Oiwa at the Tokyo Dome on January 4, 2027.

During Tuesday’s NJPW Power Struggle event, another video featuring MJF aired as a follow-up to his challenge to Oiwa from the previous month. This time, the former AEW World Champion specifically named Wrestle Kingdom as the location for their potential showdown.

Hell, I will even sweeten up the deal. I will let you wrestle me on the one show that everyone in New Japan kills to be on: Wrestle Kingdom, MJF said in the promo. If you choose to accept my challenge, I will beat you in the middle of the ring — in the middle of that crappy little Tokyo Dome — with a move that is as simple as a headlock takeover.

Oiwa subsequently accepted the challenge, effectively setting the stage for the match, although NJPW has yet to formally announce it as part of the Wrestle Kingdom 21 lineup. An official post from NJPW Global confirmed the direction, stating that MJF issued a challenge for the Tokyo Dome and it will be Ryohei Oiwa versus MJF at Wrestle Kingdom 21.

The headlock takeover reference carries additional significance, as both competitors have regularly utilized variations of the maneuver. Oiwa notably used the move during his successful G1 Climax tournament run earlier this year, defeating Yuya Uemura in the finals. The 27-year-old subsequently challenged Yota Tsuji for the IWGP Heavyweight Championship but was unsuccessful. Despite that loss, Oiwa remains one of the brightest young stars in NJPW and was in action at Power Struggle, teaming with fellow TMDK member Hartley Jackson to defeat The Swirl, featuring Blake Christian and Lee Johnson.

Meanwhile, MJF continued his familiar criticism of NJPW during the video, taking shots at the promotion’s wrestling philosophy and declaring AEW the home of the industry’s top performers. He also made disparaging remarks, claiming that NJPW has become a destination wrestlers eventually leave for bigger opportunities, while asserting that the United States is the only country that matters.

Despite participating in previous AEW and NJPW Forbidden Door crossover events in the United States, MJF has never competed in Japan. His upcoming appearance at Wrestle Kingdom will mark his first match in the country.

The January 4 event at the Tokyo Dome could also feature another prominent AEW name, as Kenny Omega has expressed interest in challenging Tsuji for the IWGP Heavyweight Championship. While NJPW has not yet officially confirmed any matches for Wrestle Kingdom 21, the developments at Power Struggle have successfully laid the groundwork for one of the most anticipated cross-promotional matches of the coming year.

Death Riders declare war on NJPW ahead of Wrestle Kingdom 21

New Japan Pro Wrestling's Power Struggle event featured more AEW participation than any show in recent memory, setting the stage for massive inter-promotional action at the upcoming Wrestle Kingdom 21 Tokyo Dome show. Maxwell Jacob Friedman followed up on his recent trash talk with a big screen appearance after TMDK's Hartley Jackson and Ryohei Oiwa defeated The Swirl consisting of Blake Christian and Lee Johnson. G1 Climax winner Oiwa was the target of MJF's ire, and Max issued a direct challenge for the Tokyo Dome. Not wanting to hear Friedman badmouth New Japan any longer, The Grip accepted, making it official that Ryohei Oiwa will face MJF at Wrestle Kingdom 21.

Jon Moxley returned to New Japan on the card, and United Empire's leaders finally booked a grudge match for the Tokyo Dome. Will Ospreay and Callum Newman failed to reclaim the IWGP Tag titles from Knockout Bros after Newman was disqualified, and things got testy with Ospreay once again, setting up a singles match between the AEW World champion and his former protege, Callum Newman. Looking to follow his recent AEW World title run with another IWGP run, Kenny Omega faced Oleg Boltin and reigning IWGP Heavyweight king Yota Tsuji in a non-title Triple Threat. Omega pinned Tsuji and called his shot for another Wrestle Kingdom main event, stating he wants to challenge for the belt if there is no opponent set for January 4.

Power Struggle also featured the finals of the Super Junior Tag League, which saw Unbound Co.'s Robbie X and Taiji Ishimori defeat Ichiban Sweet Boys consisting of Kosei Fujita and Robbie Eagles. Both teams will get a shot at the IWGP Jr. Heavyweight Tag belts. Mistico, who wrestled in a preliminary multi-man match, came out to vacate the titles as his partner El Desperado is sidelined with an injury. Ishimori and X would not accept the belts handed to them, instead agreeing to fight for them against Mistico and a partner of his choosing at Wrestle Kingdom. Ichiban Sweet Boys will be in that Tokyo Dome match too, so the Tag League final ultimately served to shake up the division.

In the main event, Gabe Kidd defended his IWGP Global championship against Taichi in a Kings Road-style classic, utilizing an avalanche Canadian Destroyer, lariat, and leg trap piledriver to outlast the veteran. Following the match, Kidd demanded that New Japan boss Hiroshi Tanahashi strap his title back around his waist. After President Ace did so, Kidd hit him with a forearm, put him down with a DDT, and threw his belt out of the ring. Yuya Uemura raced in to jump the disrespectful Brit, but Moxley snuck in behind him. Moxley, along with Wheeler Yuta, had lost to Uemura and Shingo Takagi earlier in the night, setting up an upcoming match with Takagi. That brought out Takagi, who also fell to the Death Riders duo. Standing over the three fallen Japanese stars, Gabe Kidd officially declared war, stating that the Death Riders have declared war on New Japan.
