Mike Bailey discusses modern wrestling character work

AEW wrestler Mike Bailey has opened up about the modern requirements of professional wrestling character work, asserting that the industry no longer relies on traditional personas confined strictly to television screens. Speaking in a recent interview, Bailey claimed that wrestling should be an immersive experience, which is something that will ultimately help the industry grow as it heads toward 2026.

According to Bailey, the days of people playing characters that were only able to exist on television in fifteen-minute weekly increments, such as undead creatures that cannot really have a conversation, are no longer what the wrestling world needs. He noted that the concept of kayfabe and the traditional secrecy behind wrestling have evolved. Bailey believes that these days, if you look at social media, being open and honest and cultivating an audience that knows you well is the way to go.

The entire topic of conversation emerged when Bailey was asked about the importance of maintaining his vlogging and streaming channels upon joining AEW. Elaborating on the motivation behind his content creation, Bailey explained that he started his vlog for the same reason he started Twitch streaming. During the pandemic, when the world was shut down, he was unable to travel, engage with his live audience, or see many of the friends he had made around the world of professional wrestling. To try to reform that connection, he decided to leverage the internet to make up for the fact that he could not be there in person, which is kind of what he has had to do throughout his entire career.

Bailey pointed to his past struggles with immigration as a major factor in shaping his digital presence. If you are familiar with his story, he was unable to enter the United States for five years. During those five years, his focus was on growing his skill set as a professional wrestler, but also building his brand and trying to get as much global recognition as he could. Those five years really highlighted to him the importance of putting yourself out there as much as possible.
