Jon Moxley AEW Leadership

Jon Moxley 'Turned the Ship Around' After AEW Lost Fan Goodwill, Says Daniel Garcia

Daniel Garcia believes Jon Moxley played a big role in helping AEW get back on track after the company lost some of its fan goodwill. Garcia joined Moxley's Death Riders faction in late 2025 and has since worked closely with the former AEW World Champion. The former TNT Champion recently spoke about AEW's place in wrestling and explained why he thinks Moxley helped the company find its direction again.

Speaking with Alex Sagot on Thrive, Garcia looked back at a difficult period for AEW, especially around 2024. At the time, some fans felt the company had moved away from the alternative identity that made it popular in the first place. AEW was brought into the wrestling world as an alternative, as something to be different from WWE. However, Garcia feels the company eventually started following some of the same patterns as other wrestling promotions.

"AEW, I would say like a year ago or two years ago, was in an interesting spot where I feel like it lost kind of a lot of goodwill with the fans, and a lot of the fans didn't really feel like it was the AEW that first belonged to them. AEW was brought into the wrestling world as an alternative as something to be different from WWE. And I feel like AEW got to a place where the fans felt like we weren't really an alternative anymore," Garcia said.

Garcia then pointed to Moxley as one of the people who helped change AEW's direction. Garcia believes the former AEW World Champion had a personal reason for wanting to help the company get back to what made it special. Garcia, however, did not reveal the full story Moxley had shared with him. "We were just kind of falling into the same patterns that other wrestling companies have fell into. And Mox, in my opinion, turned the ship around, man. He turned the ship around. And I won't share his story completely; it's amazing, the thing that he told me about this. But he really decided that he needed to take control and turn the ship around, and he did exactly that. And AEW is in a much better place right now than it was when it was a year ago," Garcia said.

For Garcia, Jon Moxley did more than just lead a faction. He turned the tide around before a storm threatened to flare up.

🔄 המשך 🔥 AEW Dynamite Grand Slam Mexico - August 5, 2026

MAJOR TITLE CHANGES AND STANDOUT PERFORMANCES

The Demand's reign as AEW World Trios Champions lasted only seven days. After winning the belts from The Conglomeration on July 29, Ricochet, Toa Liona and Bishop Kaun dropped them to Hangman Page, Brody King and Bandido at Arena Mexico on Wednesday. The challengers, billed as "The Good, The Bad and The Ugly," overcame a devastating sequence where Liona powered all three men onto his shoulders for a massive triple fallaway slam. In the closing moments, Bandido and Page connected with a combination of the 21-Plex and Buckshot Lariat on Liona to secure the victory. Page celebrated by finally completing the Macarena dance with his new championship partners and their manager Kemalito.

The three-way match between Andrade El Idolo, Komander and Tommaso Ciampa for the number one spot in the Casino Gauntlet at All In lived up to the billing with several highlight-reel moments. Komander delivered a reverse hurricanrana to Andrade and walked the ropes for an impressive flip to the outside. Despite all three men giving everything they had in front of their home crowd in Mexico City, Andrade secured the victory with the DM on Komander. He then delivered a heartfelt babyface promo, speaking in both Spanish and English about his pride in being home and his goal of winning the Casino Gauntlet and AEW World Championship for himself, his fans and all of Mexico.

MJFS EXPLOSIVE RETURN AND CHAMPIONSHIP CLAIM

The celebration did not last long. MJF's music hit, but instead of a traditional entrance, he emerged from behind the curtain and blindsided Andrade with his custom Triple B championship belt. The former AEW World Champion continued the assault with the Dynamite Diamond Ring, busting open Andrade and leaving him bloodied. He then drove Andrade off the stage, sending him crashing down onto Komander below. With medical personnel rushing to assist, MJF grabbed a microphone and declared himself the one true champion of AEW. He blamed Will Ospreay for costing him his championship and his spot in the All In main event against Kenny Omega at Beach Break. MJF called Omega a paper champion carrying around a toy belt and claimed that the Omega versus Ospreay match at Wembley is a fake main event.

Rather than stay bitter about his loss, MJF announced that he will enter next week's number two qualifier for the Casino Gauntlet in Las Vegas. He promised to win that match, travel to Wembley and defeat Andrade again before capturing the Casino Gauntlet contract. MJF exited through the crowd while jawing at fans as the show continued.

FLETCHER RETAINS AGAINST BAILEY

Kyle Fletcher successfully defended his AEW International Championship against Speedball Mike Bailey in one of the night's most physical contests. Fletcher came into the match having defended against Mascara Dorada at a CMLL event just 24 hours earlier. Bailey went high risk early, attempting a tope suicida but getting caught and launched into the guardrail. Fletcher took control with an Avalanche Michinoku Driver as the match headed into a commercial break. During the break, Fletcher punished Bailey repeatedly on the outside to the crowd's disapproval.

Bailey fought back with an Asai Moonsault to the floor and later hit a Moonsault off the entrance steps. He narrowly missed the Ultima Weapon but connected with the Spanish Fly for a near fall. Fletcher answered with another Michinoku Driver before powerbombing Bailey into the apron. After a running boot in the corner and a Sheer Drop Brainbuster attempt, Bailey managed to counter into a Poisonrana and landed the Ultima Weapon for the closest near fall of the match. Fletcher finally put Bailey away by landing the Brainbuster on the top turnbuckle to retain his title.

DEATH RIDERS PULL OFF CHAOTIC UPSET

The night opened with an eight-man tag team match pitting AEW World Tag Team Champions Cage and Cope and the Young Bucks against the Death Riders. The action immediately spilled outside ringside with both teams brawling. The Death Riders controlled much of the match by isolating Nick Jackson early. Adam Copeland eventually made a hot tag and nearly put Moxley away with a spear, but Wheeler Yuta distracted referee Bryce Remsburg to break up the count. Marina Shafir attempted to interfere from ringside before Christian Cage neutralized her.

The decisive moment came when Matt Jackson and Copeland accidentally crossed paths on the apron, creating the opening Moxley needed. He rolled Copeland into a schoolboy pin while grabbing a handful of tights to steal the victory for the Death Riders. After the match, a frustrated Christian Cage confronted the Young Bucks about what happened, though the four men eventually walked away together. The Death Riders left Arena Mexico with momentum heading into All In.

CRITICAL RECEPTION AND ALL IN IMPLICATIONS

The show drew mixed reactions from observers. While the Andrade versus Komander versus Ciampa match was widely praised for its athleticism and the emotional investment of the crowd, criticism focused on Kenny Omega's complete absence from the card. With All In less than a month away, the AEW World Champion appearing only in video packages felt like an odd promotional choice that may have inadvertently signaled that Omega versus Ospreay may not be the direction AEW wants to emphasize heading into their flagship London show. The rehashing of MJF's "real world title" storyline from previous years also drew criticism from some analysts who felt it was retreading old ground.

The All In card continues to take shape with a stacked top lineup. Andrade now sits at number one in the Casino Gauntlet, MJF will qualify for number two next week in Vegas, and the Continental Cup final will likely feature Jon Moxley. With three former All In main event headliners already added to the card in significant positions, AEW has assembled considerable star power for the August 30 show at Wembley Stadium.

🔥 WWE/AAA Cross-Promotional Tournament for Roman Reigns Challenger

WWE has announced a historic cross-promotional tournament that will determine Roman Reigns' next World Heavyweight Championship challenger. The tournament, featuring luchadores from Raw, SmackDown, and AAA, was announced by Rey Mysterio in his capacity as AAA General Manager following a backstage conversation with Reigns on the August 3 episode of Raw.

The tournament will begin on the August 10 episode of Raw and will culminate with a championship match against Reigns on September 14 during Raw at Arena Ciudad de Mexico in Mexico City. This marks WWE's return to televised programming in Mexico for the first time in fifteen years, with the company's last televised event from the country taking place in October 2011.

Rey Mysterio revealed that he promised Reigns he would bring him a worthy challenger and noted that Reigns understood the importance of Lucha Libre to Mexican culture. The announcement comes as part of WWE's larger September 2026 tour of the region, which includes live events in Guadalajara on September 9 and Monterrey on September 10, followed by SmackDown from Arena Ciudad de Mexico on September 11.

According to Bryan Alvarez on Wrestling Observer Live, El Grande Americano is currently the rumored favorite to win the tournament and challenge Reigns, though Alvarez emphasized that this plan is unconfirmed and not locked in. The rumored matchup takes on additional significance because El Grande Americano is already scheduled to challenge Dominik Mysterio for the AAA Mega Championship at TripleMania 34 on September 13, just one night before the Raw championship match. If Americano wins the AAA title at TripleMania, he could potentially enter his Reigns match as the reigning AAA Mega Champion.

However, Alvarez expressed reservations about whether Roman Reigns should definitively defeat El Grande Americano so soon after a potential AAA Mega Championship victory. Alvarez stated that WWE could use a schmoz or non-finish if necessary, or alternatively pivot to a different challenger such as Penta. He emphasized that WWE's plans could certainly change before the tournament begins and that nothing has been finalized.

Several major WWE and AAA stars have already been advertised for the Mexico City edition of Raw, including Stephanie Vaquer, Penta, Seth Rollins, Dominik Mysterio, El Grande Americano, Oba Femi, GUNTHER, and The Usos. However, WWE has not yet officially announced the specific participants who will compete in the tournament.

The cross-promotional event occurs as WWE continues its integration with AAA following the company's acquisition of the Mexican promotion in August 2025. Mysterio has been serving as AAA's General Manager since the acquisition, overseeing several roster additions. Additionally, WWE and AAA will co-host the Worlds Collide Premium Live Event on September 26, which will be held at the Allstate Arena in Chicago on the same day as AEW's All Out pay-per-view.

Reigns has held the World Heavyweight Championship since defeating CM Punk on night two of WrestleMania 42 and most recently successfully retained the title against Seth Rollins in the main event of SummerSlam.

🔥 WWE Stars Rebranding After Departures

Sheamus has officially rebranded his YouTube channel to reflect the name he is now using professionally, moving away from the Celtic Warrior identity that helped build one of wrestling's most successful fitness channels. The channel, previously known as Celtic Warrior Workouts, has been renamed S. Farrelly Workouts, derived from his real name Stephen Farrelly.

Alongside the new title, the channel's description has been updated to remove the WWE references that had remained for years. It now welcomes viewers to the official channel of S. Farrelly and features the phrase "Brave Change" prominently. The slogan, which is familiar to longtime viewers, appears set to take on a larger role going forward as "Brave Change" will become the name of a new series, although no details regarding its format or launch date have been announced.

The rebrand has also come with a new logo and represents a complete shift from the channel's former branding. The rebrand follows similar changes across Farrelly's social media platforms, where he has also adopted S. Farrelly for his professional identity since his WWE exit.

Launched in 2017, the channel grew into one of the most popular wrestler-run YouTube channels, featuring workout sessions with fellow wrestlers and athletes.
