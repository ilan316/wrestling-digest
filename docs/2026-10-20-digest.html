🔥 Billy Gunn calls Triple H's Hall of Fame 'pissant company' joke a dick move

During an interview on the Inside The Ropes YouTube channel with Kenny McIntosh, WWE Hall of Famer and AEW veteran Billy Gunn reflected on the controversial remarks made by Triple H about All Elite Wrestling during D-Generation X’s induction into the WWE Hall of Fame in April 2019. Gunn had signed with AEW earlier that year in January, just months before being inducted into the WWE Hall of Fame alongside his fellow D-Generation X stablemates.

During the April ceremony, fans in attendance began chanting AEW, prompting Triple H to take the microphone and joke that Vince McMahon would purchase the newly formed promotion simply to fire Gunn again. During the exchange, Triple H infamously referred to AEW as a pissant company. Looking back, Gunn explained that he was not particularly surprised by the comments because he had already expected something similar to happen since AEW was hot at the time and he was working there.

Gunn revealed that AEW President Tony Khan had been completely supportive of his participation in the Hall of Fame ceremony, despite Gunn already being signed to the rival promotion. When Gunn went and asked Khan if he could attend, Khan responded one hundred percent and stated he would never hold him back from that opportunity. Gunn explained that while he did not know the exact details of what WWE had planned, he knew that Hunter was going to have something to say about it. Once he prepared himself for that reality, he just let it go.

Despite being prepared for the moment, Gunn admitted he was not impressed by how Triple H handled the situation. Gunn stated that the only one who got hurt out of that whole deal was Triple H himself. Calling it a dick move, Gunn added that it was kind of an attempt to save face that was not actually needed.

Gunn went on to suggest that the exchange reflected the competitive and provocative nature of the professional wrestling business. He noted that when a wrestler is associated with another company, it triggers an unwritten rule in the industry where the opposition feels compelled to say and do something. Despite being critical of Triple H at the time, Gunn indicated that he had long since moved past the incident rather than allowing it to become a lasting issue.

Gunn initially joined AEW as a coach in 2019 and made sporadic in-ring appearances for the promotion. Later in 2022, when D-Generation X celebrated 25 years of the faction, Gunn was notably absent from the on-screen reunion. However, Road Dogg later revealed that Gunn had texted him expressing a desire to be part of the celebration and was ultimately disappointed when he could not be included.

🔥 Billy Gunn reflects on the 2022 AEW All Out backstage fight

WWE Hall of Famer and current AEW coach Billy Gunn has reflected on the infamous Brawl Out incident involving CM Punk and The Elite. The controversy began during the AEW All Out 2022 post-show media scrum, where CM Punk made controversial comments criticizing Hangman Page, Colt Cabana, and AEW Executive Vice Presidents The Young Bucks. Those comments led directly to a physical backstage altercation involving Punk, Ace Steel, Kenny Omega, and The Young Bucks. AEW President Tony Khan ultimately suspended everyone involved in the fight.

During an interview with Kenny McIntosh on the Inside The Ropes YouTube channel, Gunn discussed the situation despite not being present when it occurred. He explained that he steers clear of wrestling media coverage because it has nothing to do with him. Regarding the clash itself, Gunn offered a blunt assessment of the dynamics between the individuals involved, stating that certain veterans simply will not tolerate disrespect. He remarked that if a young buckaroo wants to poke the bear, they should get ready to get bit, adding that the full details remain strictly between the parties involved.

When asked how backstage disagreements were handled historically and whether they could turn into wrestling storylines, Gunn compared the current era to his time working under Vince McMahon in WWE. He noted that McMahon maintained strict control over the locker room. According to Gunn, wrestlers do not need to like each other to work together, explaining that 99.9 percent of the time, talent simply focused on doing business regardless of personal feelings.

Gunn expressed frustration with how backstage conflicts are sometimes handled in the modern wrestling business compared to past generations. He detailed the proper way to resolve a dispute, stating that if a problem arises, individuals should be pulled into a room with the door shut. He emphasized that arguments should never happen out in public or in front of the boys, noting that even when he is frustrated with coaching and about to lose his temper, he removes himself and others to a private area where nobody else can hear.

The veteran coach stressed that grown adults should address and fix problems immediately so that once they walk out the door, the issue is completely resolved and no longer affects their work. The 2022 All Out altercation remains one of the most controversial moments in AEW history, leading to major suspensions and eventually culminating in Punk returning to television in June 2023, being terminated in September 2023 following another incident at AEW All In, and subsequently returning to WWE in November 2023. Additionally, during the same interview session, Gunn addressed other topics such as calling out Paul Triple H Levesque for an AEW reference made during a WWE Hall of Fame ceremony.

WWE's internal reaction to Ilja Dragunov signing with AEW

Ilja Dragunov’s jump to AEW apparently isn’t causing any hard feelings inside WWE. In fact, the latest backstage word is that people who worked with Dragunov are happy for him and WWE management isn’t holding his decision against him either. During a recent wrestling Q and A session, industry insiders were asked how WWE reacted to Dragunov showing up at WrestleDream and officially becoming All Elite. According to these reports, the people who know Dragunov personally were happy to see him land somewhere else, with sources noting that everyone in WWE that are friends with him are happy.

That attitude apparently goes beyond just Dragunov’s friends. Reports indicate there isn’t some automatic resentment backstage whenever somebody leaves WWE and decides they can make better money somewhere else, establishing that there is no animosity when it comes to talent leaving and going to get their bag. Insiders pointed to the recent Sheamus situation as another example of that mentality. According to these reports, there were even people within WWE who felt Sheamus should consider AEW rather than accept an offer they believed wasn’t good enough, encouraging him to go out there and get his money.

When it comes specifically to Dragunov, the feeling is basically the same from both sides of the curtain. His friends are happy for him, and even management apparently isn’t bitter about seeing him become part of AEW, wishing him well from both personal and management sides of things.

There is one interesting wrinkle, though. While WWE apparently isn’t angry with Dragunov for leaving, reports indicate that losing him wasn’t exactly something Triple H wanted to happen. That makes Dragunov’s departure a little more interesting than simply WWE deciding it was finished with him. The latest information suggests the company would have preferred things to work out differently, but once Dragunov made his move, there wasn’t any backstage grudge over it.

Dragunov officially debuted for AEW at WrestleDream after his WWE contract expired in August and visa issues delayed his arrival. Tony Khan quickly made things official afterwards, confirming that the former WWE star had signed with the company. Dragunov has since explained that he wanted a fresh environment where he could breathe, find new competition and avoid feeling stuck. He didn’t specifically blame WWE for that feeling, instead framing AEW as the new landscape he needed at this stage of his career.

So while Triple H apparently didn’t want to lose Dragunov, WWE isn’t treating his AEW move like some kind of betrayal. The backstage reaction sounds much more like wishing him well, letting him chase what he wants and moving on without any bad blood. 🔄 המשך 🔥 Update on Kairi Sane's AEW negotiations and visa status

While fans previously believed bureaucratic visa delays were holding up Kairi Sane from debuting in AEW, the actual situation is quite the reverse.

Sane remains entirely unsigned following the expiration of her WWE contract earlier this year. Reports have clarified that her U.S. work visa has indeed expired, but the renewal process cannot even begin until she officially signs with a promotion or agency that can sponsor her. Once a deal is finalized, an expedited visa can be approved in as little as fifteen business days, meaning the paperwork is not the primary hurdle preventing her debut.

In addition to ongoing talks with American companies, Sane currently has multiple standing offers on the table from promotions in Japan as she weighs her next career move.

Although she has not yet inked a deal for U.S. television, fans will have an opportunity to see her in the ring soon. She is officially booked to take part in Chris Jericho's upcoming Rock N Wrestling Rager at Sea in November. Because the event takes place entirely in international waters, Sane will not require a U.S. work visa to participate in the cruise.
