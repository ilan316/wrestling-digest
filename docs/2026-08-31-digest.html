🔥 Darby Allin Wins TNT Championship at AEW All In with Return of Sting

Darby Allin won the TNT title for the third time at Sunday’s AEW All In at Wembley Stadium, defeating Kevin Knight in a falls count anywhere match that ended Knight's 140-day reign which featured 12 successful title defenses. The bout was fueled by revenge after Knight’s betrayal at May’s Double or Nothing, where Knight turned on Allin after he lost the AEW World title to MJF. In a contest that saw blood and the heavy introduction of tables, chairs, and ladders, Allin looked to get retribution while being willing to put his own life and limb on the line.

Things worsened for Darby when Knight’s Don Callis Family stablemates, Brian Cage and Jake Doyle, got involved. Cage and Doyle blindsided Darby, crotched him on the top of a chair, and later tied him up with tape. Steven Borden rushed the ring to make the save following up on an angle from Saturday's Collision where Callis tried to recruit him, and initially managed to fight off Knight's cronies. However, the numbers caught up when Cage powerbombed Borden through a table. As Knight prepared to deliver a Coast-to-Coast, the lights went out, and Metallica's Seek and Destroy blared as The Icon Sting made a surprise AEW return. Sting took out Brian Cage and Jake Doyle on the way to the ring, had a staredown with Knight, and handed his trademark baseball bat to Allin, who blasted Knight with it before Sting laid Knight out with a Scorpion Death Drop.

Steven Borden and Darby brought a tall ladder into the ring, and both Sting and his son climbed to the top. Steven persuaded his dad to climb back down, telling him he had it covered, and then performed a cross-body splash onto Brian Cage and Jake Doyle on the outside. That allowed Sting and Steven to clear the ringside area, leaving Darby Allin and Kevin Knight to resume their one-on-one contest. Their fight spilled onto the stage, where Allin climbed the side of a lighting rig estimated to be 20 to 30 feet high. Allin then performed a high-risk Coffin Drop that sent both men crashing through part of the stage in a spot reminiscent of the Shane McMahon and Steve Blackman scaffolding spot from WWE SummerSlam in 2000.

Medical personnel placed Kevin Knight on a stretcher and began wheeling him out from under the stage. However, Allin called off the paramedics, climbed the stage, and delivered one final Coffin Drop off the entrance ramp onto Knight on the stretcher to score the pin and secure the victory, marking a full circle moment for the rivalry.

Speaking to the media after the pay-per-view, Allin reminisced about why winning his first TNT title meant more to him than his first AEW World title. Allin explained that it brings him back to a much different, insecure time in his career during year one of AEW when he wondered if he was really going to fit or last in the company. Tapping the title, he noted that it gave him the confidence he holds so close. Tony Khan joked during the media scrum that he hopes Allin is cleared for the upcoming week's TV as it would help him out a lot.

🔥 AEW Announces Dynasty 2027 International Location and London O2 Show

All Elite Wrestling has officially announced its first major pay-per-view event of 2027, revealing a significant international expansion and a major shakeup to its traditional calendar. During the AEW All In London broadcast, it was confirmed that AEW Dynasty 2027 will take place on Sunday, February 7, emanating from the M&S Bank Arena in Liverpool, England.

This booking marks the first time AEW has ever traveled to Liverpool. It also represents the first time in 13 years that a televised professional wrestling event will be held at the M&S Bank Arena, with the last being a November 2014 episode of WWE SmackDown, though WWE NXT UK ran shows in the city in 2018 and the venue has previously hosted various house shows since opening in 2008.

Rather than traveling straight back to the United States after Dynasty, the AEW roster will remain in England for an upcoming television taping. On Wednesday, February 10, the company will host an episode of AEW Dynamite at The O2 Arena in London for the very first time. The O2 previously hosted the AEW x NJPW Forbidden Door pay-per-view in 2025, setting an attendance record for a wrestling event at the venue.

The announcement represents a notable shift for AEW's pay-per-view schedule. Dynasty, which originally debuted in 2024, has historically been held in April. The February 7 date marks AEW's first-ever pay-per-view held during the month of February. This calendar adjustment has already led to fan and analyst speculation regarding where the annual Revolution pay-per-view will land in 2027. Furthermore, with WrestleMania 43 scheduled to take place in Saudi Arabia, AEW President Tony Khan has previously been asked about the possibility of running a WrestleMania-week-style slate of domestic events while WWE is overseas. Khan has called the concept an interesting idea, though no such plans have been officially confirmed.

Before the company returns to the United Kingdom in 2027, mainland Europe will receive its first taste of All Elite Wrestling in October. Paris, France, will host AEW Grand Slam France on Tuesday, October 6, at the Adidas Arena. That event will feature tapings for both AEW Dynamite and AEW Collision, with both shows airing on a tape delay due to the time difference. It remains unconfirmed if Dynamite will air live on that Tuesday, which would put it in a head-to-head broadcast competition with WWE NXT. Additional details regarding Dynasty 2027 and the February London Dynamite will be announced in the coming months.

MVP Contract Extension Details with AEW

MVP has revealed that his current All Elite Wrestling contract takes him through the time he reaches 57 years old. Speaking on the Marking Out with MVP and Dwayne Swayze podcast, the 52-year-old star discussed his long-term contract extension and his evolving role in the wrestling industry.

MVP noted that he views his wrestling persona as a job rather than his entire existence, expressing comfort with transitioning away from active competition. While acknowledging that he is still making good money, he recognizes that his financial peak from his younger years has shifted. He is very content with his current positioning and expects to contribute to the company for a long time as a manager, mouthpiece, consultant, and contributor.

Regarding his in-ring career, MVP is confident that his final match is approaching soon. At 52 years old, he has no desire to sprint across the ring, take powerbombs, or absorb chokeslams. Although he is not officially retired yet, he is targeting next year for his final match. He understands that independent bookings or final appearances do not require him to put on a twenty-minute classic, as fans simply want to see his signature mannerisms like the Ballin elbow and his trademark charisma.

While MVP has secured his long-term future with the promotion, he recently faced travel hurdles that kept him away from one of the company's biggest events. MVP was noticeably absent from AEW All In at Wembley Stadium, where The Hurt Syndicate members Bobby Lashley and Shelton Benjamin competed in a match alongside Big Boom AJ and QT Marshall against Shane Taylor Promotions and The Infantry. Lashley and Benjamin made their way to the ring without their manager.

Reports indicated that MVP missed the major pay-per-view event due to changing visa processes, with efforts currently underway to resolve the matter for future international events. International travel has historically been a recurring hurdle for MVP due to his past criminal record, which includes serving over eight years in prison prior to his professional wrestling career. He previously missed AEW and NJPW Forbidden Door in London due to United Kingdom entry restrictions related to his past, though wrestling companies have frequently assisted him with the necessary paperwork. Despite his absence at All In, The Hurt Syndicate successfully secured a victory.

Since joining the promotion in 2024 to reunite with his former stablemates, MVP has served as the vocal representative of The Hurt Syndicate. While he is open to an eventual in-ring farewell match, he has no ambitions of pursuing a world championship, remaining firmly focused on his managerial and backstage contributions as he locks in his tenure for the years ahead.

Following the action from the AAA Ola de Calor event in Edinburg, Texas, several major matchups and championship pictures for the upcoming TripleMania show have been officially locked in.

La Parka emerged victorious in a high-stakes four-way match to become the new AAA Latin American Champion. Filling the vacancy left by El Hijo del Vikingo's ACL injury, La Parka overcame El Fiscal, El Hijo de Dr. Wagner Jr., and former WWE star Damian Priest, securing the pin on Fiscal after hitting the Bone Breaker. The victory was also accompanied by the debut of a newly redesigned Latin American Championship belt.

The previously scheduled contendership bout between Nathan Frazer, Mini Vikingo, and Jack Cartwheel ultimately failed to produce a definitive winner after reaching a twenty-minute time limit draw. In response to the stalemate, AAA World Cruiserweight Champion Rey Fenix announced that he will now defend his title against all three competitors in a massive four-way match at TripleMania.

Furthermore, the women's division saw La Catalina secure a major championship opportunity by winning a grueling gauntlet match. The bout featured NXT star Thea Hail as a notable surprise entrant, but Catalina outlasted the entire field to earn a Reina de Reinas Championship match against Flammer at the upcoming mega-event.

🔄 המשך Scott Steiner's Son Brock Rechsteiner Waived by Saints

The New Orleans Saints officially waived wide receiver Brock Rechsteiner on Sunday as NFL teams trimmed their rosters to the mandatory fifty three player limit. The release comes on the heels of a tumultuous preseason for the undrafted rookie, which included a viral training camp fight with a Dallas Cowboys player. Compounding his roster struggles, Rechsteiner was recently handed a six game suspension to begin the regular season for violating the league performance enhancing substances policy.

Now that his time in New Orleans has come to an end, speculation is ramping up regarding his inevitable move to the squared circle. Rechsteiner signed a WWE NIL deal in February of last year and has openly stated his intention to pursue professional wrestling once his football days are finished. He has already spoken extensively with his cousin, WWE superstar Bron Breakker, about joining the family business. According to Rechsteiner, Breakker advised him to play football for as long as possible before making the jump to wrestling. Whether this roster cut accelerates that timeline remains to be seen.

Following the events leading up to Wembley Stadium, the actual fallout from AEW All In brought massive title changes and historic moments. Other major happenings at All In included Britt Baker returning, Sting making a return appearance, a former New Day member debuting to win Trios gold with Swerve Strickland, and a Casino Gauntlet match determining the next World title challenger. Tony Khan later reported an attendance of 47,014 with a roughly $6 million gate, noting it was the company's largest PPV on HBO Max by a significant margin.

AAA also hosted events featuring matches for TripleMania 34, including Rey Mysterio versus Omos, alongside a contract signing for Dominik Mysterio and El Grande Americano.
