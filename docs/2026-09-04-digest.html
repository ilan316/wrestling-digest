The TNA Wrestling roster has undergone some significant changes in 2026, with several new arrivals and even more names walking out the door. With former WWE star Zoey Serrano joining up with the company last week, and Bound for Glory on the horizon, we could see even more free agents arrive in the Impact Zone. Here are three potential new TNA Wrestling signings coming soon.

Romeo Malverde (Santos Escobar)
Part of the same group of WWE releases as the aforementioned Zoey Serrano, Romeo Malverde (fka Santos Escobar) was one of the most surprising cuts on the list. Malverde had attempted to depart WWE back in October last year, only for the company to convince him to re-sign with a bumper new contract, suggesting that a major push may be in his future. A move to AAA in the new year came instead, however, Malverde’s run was cut short due to a tricep injury and he was released shortly after his surgery. Now he’s back fit and healthy, fans of the former Legado del Fantasma leader are anticipating his next move, but there’s been no indication of where he will land yet. Having previously wrestled in TNA back in 2017-2018 as El Hijo del Fantasma, perhaps a return to the company could be his best next step, only this time as Romeo Malverde. TNA is in need of some new main event level talent, and Malverde could certainly be elevated to that level with the right push.

Killer Kross & Scarlett Bordeaux
Speaking of TNA alumni, Killer Kross and Scarlett Bordeaux have spent the past year rebuilding their careers on the independent scene after exiting WWE in August 2025. And it’s safe to say the husband and wife pairing have done that to great effect, with Kross holding multiple championships across different promotions, most notably being the MLW World Heavyweight Title which he has now held for over 200 days. While they seem happy where they’re at, Kross and Scarlett have more big moves in them and a return to weekly network television is what they should be aiming for. Despite WWE not shying away from signing released talent in 2026, TNA seems like the likeliest way they could do that right now, and they would absolutely be pushed to the top of the card if they did make their return.

Malakai Black & Xelina (Aleister Black & Zelina Vega)
Another two-for-one signing would be fellow husband and wife pairing: Malakai Black and Xelina. On paper, both should be shoo-ins for All Elite Wrestling, however, given Black’s apparent reputation in the promotion, a return there has been questioned. While Andrade El Idolo has proven that there can be a way back, there has been no word regarding AEW approaching Black and Xelina since they became free agents in July. With that being said, TNA should capitalise on their seeming availability and make them offers. Xelina has history in TNA, as she previously made her name in wrestling through her work as Rosita in the early 2010s, while Black has never appeared in the Impact Zone.

Sonya Deville (Daria Rae) lands role in Netflix series

TNA Director of Operations Daria Rae, formerly known in WWE as Sonya Deville, has reportedly landed a role in an upcoming television series and has already begun filming for the project.

Rae will be a part of a Netflix TV series called Myron Bolitar. The show revolves around a sports agent and features a cast that includes Colin Woodell, Diane Guerrero, and Chloe Fineman. In the series, Rae's character is a pro wrestler who goes by the name Fury, with shooting for the production taking place in New York.

Rae has previous experience in television and film, having appeared on a few shows such as Tape Me, as well as movies like The DNA Murders and His, Hers & Ours.

While pursuing her acting endeavors, she also continues to appear on TNA Wrestling in her Director of Operations role. Her most recent appearance came on the post-TNA Lockdown edition of TNA Impact, where she introduced former WWE star Zoey Serrano, also known as Zoey Stark, to the TNA audience.

Rae originally debuted on Impact in January during the show's first episode on AMC and recently announced that she had re-signed with the promotion. Her role in TNA has remained purely an authority position thus far, as she has yet to wrestle for the company, with her last official match taking place in February 2025 during her tenure in WWE.

🔄 המשך Zoey Serrano loses first match in TNA and reflects on NXT preparing wrestlers

Zoey Serrano's fresh start in TNA hit a major roadblock during the September 3 episode of iMPACT. Teaming with Xia Brookside against Indi Hartwell and Harley Hudson, Serrano found herself on the losing end after a costly mistake. During the bout, Serrano accidentally struck her own partner, creating an opening for their opponents to seize control. The match descended into further chaos when Serrano attacked a fan near the commentary desk. As tensions boiled over, Brookside was knocked off the apron and subsequently forced to tap out to a submission from Hartwell, leaving the future of Serrano and Brookside's partnership in question.

In addition to her in-ring struggles, Serrano recently reflected on her time in WWE and offered a critical assessment of the developmental system. Speaking about whether NXT prepares wrestlers for the main roster, she expressed doubts, explaining that the developmental brand relies heavily on coaches walking talent through every detail of a match or promo from start to finish. In contrast, she described moving up to RAW or SmackDown as a sink or swim environment where wrestlers are left to figure things out largely on their own without that safety net.

🔄 המשך Eric Bischoff discusses WCW nWo booking and Ted DiBiase casting

Expanding further on his 83 Weeks podcast, Bischoff noted that while Barry Bloom originally introduced DiBiase to the fold and everyone had high hopes for his strong voice, the execution fell flat. He stressed that his critique is not a reflection of DiBiase's immense talent, comparing the misfire to Hollywood casting where even great actors fail if paired with the wrong script or director. According to Bischoff, the overly animated WWF character fans already knew simply did not match the authentic tone they were trying to build.

Bischoff also shed light on the psychological tactics used to make the nWo feel genuinely dangerous to viewers. By orchestrating relatively minor acts of vandalism, such as defacing the WCW television production truck in August 1996, the promotion aimed to blur the lines between scripted entertainment and reality. The goal was to make fans genuinely question whether the faction had crossed a line that could lead to real-world consequences like arrests or firings, reinforcing the illusion that management had lost control of the company.
