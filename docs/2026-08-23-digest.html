AEW star MVP has always emphasized fashion and style, but that does not mean the wrestler cannot manage his spending. He noted that while he did not understand his contract the way a lawyer would, he was intelligent enough to understand some legalese. MVP then gave a shout-out to former WWE star Jinder Mahal, also known as Raj Dhesi, praising him as a great and financially sharp guy who has done really well for himself.

MVP also credited a WWE Hall of Famer for offering a piece of advice that has stuck with him throughout his career. Ricky Steamboat gave him probably the best, simplified financial advice by telling him to divide his money into thirds. Steamboat instructed him to take one third and put that away for taxes, which MVP jokingly refers to as Uncle Scam. The veteran also advised putting the second third into savings and investments, and living on the remaining third. MVP pointed out the danger of suddenly making 750000 a year and irresponsibly trying to live entirely on that full amount instead of budgeting wisely.

Chris Jericho Recalls Classic 2001 WWE Tag Team Match

For many, the May 2001 tag match that saw Chris Jericho and Chris Benoit defeat Steve Austin and Triple H to win the WWE Tag Team Championships remains the best match in Raw history. It is also the same match that altered Triple H's career, as he tore his quad muscle, putting him out of action for eight months. It's an evening that remains vivid in Jericho's memory, and during a recent audio interview, Jericho recounted the wild swing of emotions that occurred from when the match was over to learning how severe Triple H's injury was.

The crowd went nuts, we win the titles, and everyone's going crazy, and what an amazing moment, Jericho said. And then we go to the back. And this should've been one of those nights where you get the pat on the back, you get the standing ovation in Gorilla, you get the handshake and a hug from Vince. Those are things in the business that...well, you strive for them. You don't crave them, but you strive for them, the night when everybody, when everything works.

And we didn't get that, because everyone was concerned about Triple H, as we should've been. But we didn't know. I just thought Well, he followed through. He must be okay. It's like He's really hurt. I was like Oh no, is he really? And so it went from this amazing elation to this complete downer, where now we've all got to go to the doctor's room, and he's getting checked, and then we find out that he tore his quad, which then basically ruined everything for the plans they had.

Those plans called for Jericho to resume his feud with Triple H, after the two had previously battled several times in 2000, while Austin was set to defend the WWE Championship against Chris Benoit. Instead, Jericho and Benoit would both challenge Austin for the title at King of the Ring, with Austin retaining.

JBL Suggests Acting Coaches Would Benefit Professional Wrestling

During a recent appearance on the Something to Wrestle podcast, host Conrad Thompson asked WWE Hall of Famer JBL about the potential benefits of professional wrestling promotions hiring dedicated acting coaches. Thompson pointed out that while the industry invests immense time into the physical mechanics of matches, safety, and production, very little focus is placed on camera work and emotional delivery. Thompson questioned why the business does not openly embrace acting coaches to guide performers on their delivery and nuance, asking what would be wrong with utilizing such expertise.

JBL agreed with the suggestion, admitting that he does not understand why acting coaches are not more widely used in the industry. As an example, he noted that legendary wrestler George Steele was not actually a wild animal who ate turnbuckles, but was instead a highly intelligent former high school teacher.

JBL did not see any issue with bringing in professional acting guidance, especially given the current state of television content. He bluntly criticized modern pre-tapes and backstage skits, stating that while some are very good, others are downright horrible and cringeworthy. He pointed out that performers frequently struggle with overacting, underacting, and failing to react properly in the moment, arguing that professional coaches could significantly elevate both inexperienced talent and veterans alike.

Ultimately, JBL emphasized that whether professional wrestling is viewed as real or scripted is irrelevant, as long as the audience perceives the performance as authentic. Because audience perception is the most important element of the business, he concluded that incorporating acting coaches would benefit the industry significantly.

Following the circulation of these remarks, former WWE star Jinder Mahal took to Twitter to share his thoughts on the matter. Mahal publicly agreed with JBL, stating that every WWE talent could benefit from acting classes.
