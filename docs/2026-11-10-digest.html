🔥 HBO Max and Paramount+ merger plans and potential impact on AEW streaming

Skydance has confirmed major changes following its Warner Bros. Discovery takeover, announcing plans to bring HBO Max and Paramount plus together into a unified streaming service. Starting in early 2027, consumers will be able to subscribe to both platforms through a combined bundle as the first step toward full integration. Skydance CEO David Ellison confirmed that the technology behind both platforms will be combined within roughly a year of completing the acquisition, eventually ending the era of HBO Max operating as a separate streaming service. The combined platforms currently boast 203 million subscribers, and Skydance will align with Netflix by no longer reporting total subscriber figures, shifting its focus instead to revenue and financial performance to appease Wall Street investors.

In its first post-merger earnings call, Skydance reported seven billion dollars in total revenue, net earnings of 73 million dollars, and one billion dollars of adjusted EBITDA prior to the Warner Bros. Discovery acquisition. The company outlined plans to cut approximately six to eight billion dollars in costs, bringing CBS Sports and TNT Sports together under one operation. Despite these cost-saving measures, Skydance announced a planned annual content spend of 30 billion dollars, which includes various movie studios as well as televised and streaming content. Skydance also holds a minority stakeholder position in AEW, with an unreported stake under ten percent.

These sweeping media changes have naturally created questions regarding the long-term television and streaming future of All Elite Wrestling. AEW currently airs Dynamite and Collision on TBS and TNT, with both weekly shows also streaming on HBO Max alongside archive content and pay-per-view events. AEW President Tony Khan has spoken positively about his conversations with David Ellison and expressed confidence in Skydance, stating he loves working for the company and expects a great future. AEW's current media rights agreement runs through the end of 2027, with Skydance holding a one-year option to extend the deal through 2028.

Despite Khan's optimism, questions remain about how AEW will fit into Skydance's long-term programming strategy. For example, potential adjustments could arise if Skydance decides to move UFC events to TNT on Saturday nights, which would require scheduling changes for AEW Collision. Skydance has not yet announced pricing for the upcoming Paramount plus and HBO Max bundle, the exact launch date for the unified service, or what the final platform will be called. While AEW programming remains secure under the existing agreement through 2027, the ultimate placement of wrestling and sports properties under the newly merged media giant is still to be determined.

Mercedes Martinez provides recovery update after ACL surgery

Mercedes Martinez is making steady progress in her recovery following her serious knee surgery on October 15. The procedure addressed multiple issues, repairing her fully torn ACL, fixing a small MCL tear, and treating arthritis discovered in the joint.

Three weeks post-operation, Martinez took to social media to share an encouraging message and a video documenting her rehabilitation sessions. The footage featured electrode pads, her surgical scar, and mobility exercises such as lifting her leg over a foam roller. A comparison of her exercises from Tuesday, November 3, to Friday, November 6, showed noticeable improvement as she transitioned from struggling to lift her heel off the ground to performing full leg raises.

Reflecting on her journey, Martinez wrote that some days progress is seen in two days while other times it takes a full week, emphasizing the importance of showing up, keeping the pressure on, trusting the process, and celebrating little victories. She noted that small gains lead to big progress, adopting the mantra of small goals and big gains.

The injury took place on August 15 during a match against Allie Catch, coming at a difficult time as Martinez was set to embark on her retirement tour. The severe knee damage ultimately brought her in-ring career to an end. Martinez made her professional wrestling debut in 2000, spending the early part of the century working for organizations like Shimmer, Ring of Honor, World Xtreme Wrestling, and Women Superstars Uncensored. She later competed in the inaugural WWE Mae Young Classic in 2017, the AEW Casino Battle Royale at All Out in 2019, and debuted on Monday Night Raw in September 2020 before returning to AEW and Ring of Honor until her contract expired.

Because Martinez did not have insurance to cover the surgery and recovery, she faced significant financial challenges. Wrestling fans stepped up by contributing to a GoFundMe campaign, which has raised over 12,000 dollars to help her with medical bills and remains open for ongoing donations.

Booker T advises Steven Borden to limit high-risk moves

Booker T believes Steven Borden can stand out in the professional wrestling industry without making high-risk dives a regular part of his matches. Speaking alongside Brad Gilmore on podcast, the wrestling legend urged Sting’s son to develop a style that does not depend on taking the same risks as smaller competitors. The discussion was prompted by Borden’s dangerous landing at AEW All Out. During that event, Borden teamed up with Darby Allin to face Kevin Knight and Kyle Fletcher, a high-stakes encounter that ultimately ended in a premature doctor's stoppage. Fortunately, Borden has since recovered and returned to AEW television, making an appearance on the October 21 edition of Dynamite.

Expanding on his advice, Booker T stressed that there is simply no need for Borden to take unnecessary aerial risks. He pointed out that many other performers on the card are already dedicated to executing those types of dives, meaning Borden should let those wrestlers bear that physical brunt instead. The veteran advised Borden to focus on learning how to work effectively without leaving his feet until the absolute right moment so the people go, oh man, that was huge. Booker warned that wanting to be a guy put in the position to do that stuff on a weekly basis when it is really not your forte, or wanting to go out and work smaller as one of the bigger guys on the card, is putting yourself in a bad position.

Booker T also expressed a wish that he had been given the opportunity to train Borden himself, offering insight into his own historical approach to aerial offense as a teaching example. He noted that he never viewed himself as a traditional flyer, but he understood how to act like one and how to act like a flyer while always putting himself in a safe position more than anything. Concluding his thoughts, Booker stated that he would have loved to work with and train Borden even for just a little bit to help him understand the five elements of professional wrestling.

Disco Inferno criticizes Mercedes Moné's believability

Disco Inferno is not entirely sold on the believability of Mercedes Mone as a top-tier performer in the women division. Speaking on a recent podcast episode, he compared Mone with Madusa while explaining why he places such heavy emphasis on making professional wrestling look authentic. The conversation initially began with a listener question regarding Dave Meltzers recent assessments of Mone, including her Owen Hart Cup final against Maya World at Forbidden Door. When the panel shifted to discussing Mone standing among American women wrestlers, Disco argued that Madusa possessed a far more convincing physical presence. He noted that meeting someone like Madusa in real life reveals a certain vibe, making it clear that she looks like she can kick a guy ass.

From there, Disco went on to question the level of realism he currently sees in Mone presentation and connected that specific critique to his broader assessment of how modern wrestlers operate in the ring. He stated that the goal should be to try to make the action look as real as possible, arguing that a lot of wrestlers from the current generation fail to convey any real sense of realism in their matches. In his view, the modern style often looks more phony than anything else.

Earlier in the discussion, Disco pointed to specific elements like selling and facial expressions as the true cornerstones of wrestling psychology. He contrasted these crucial details with matches that are primarily built around a sequence of high impact moves and callbacks. Psychology is like the way WWE produces psychology with facial expressions, close ups of facial expressions during the matches, selling, Disco explained. That is the psychology, not these moves and callbacks to spots. That is not psychology. That is like a math equation.

He further elaborated that these guys wrestle like it is a big giant math equation without the emotion. He concluded by criticizing the current generation for failing to sell very well, attributing the issue to wrestlers trying to pack far too much action into their matches. Because they do so much in their matches, they simply do not have time for selling and they just do not sell that much.
