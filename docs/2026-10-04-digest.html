🔥 Clockwork Carnage World Championship match announced for AEW WrestleDream 2026

During Saturday’s episode of AEW Collision, a brand new match stipulation called Clockwork Carnage was officially announced to debut at AEW WrestleDream 2026. Taking place on October 17 in Orlando, Florida, the marquee pay-per-view event will feature Will Ospreay defending the AEW World Championship in this unique multi-person contest.

The mechanics of the Clockwork Carnage match are divided into two distinct stages involving eight wrestlers. The contest begins with two randomly selected participants in the ring, and every three minutes, another randomly selected wrestler enters. During this opening stage, any competitor who is pinned or submitted is immediately eliminated from the match. This process continues until all eight participants have entered.

Once the final wrestler enters, the second stage of the match officially begins, featuring a 15-minute scoring period. Throughout this window, every pinfall or submission counts as one fall. When the 15-minute time limit expires, the wrestler who has accumulated the most falls wins the match and secures the AEW World Championship. While comparisons can be drawn to other multi-person timed concepts like the Iron Survivor Challenge, AEW is introducing its own distinct flavor of chaos to the stipulation.

Aside from current champion Will Ospreay, no other participants for the eight-man field have been announced yet, and it remains unconfirmed whether qualifying matches will take place. Ospreay's path to WrestleDream also includes upcoming defenses, as he is set to put the AEW World Championship on the line against Kazuchika Okada at AEW Grand Slam France, while Andrade El Idolo still holds a contract for a title match following his Casino Gauntlet victory. Additionally, the WrestleDream card in Orlando will feature a Tag Team Championship match between champions The Young Bucks and the team of Kyle Fletcher and Kevin Knight, who earned their spot by defeating Darby Allin and Steven Borden at All Out.

🔥 Scorpio Sky's AEW contract expiring without new deal signed

Former AEW World Tag Team and TNT Champion Scorpio Sky is facing an uncertain future with the company as his contract approaches its expiration this month. When All Elite Wrestling formed back in 2019, the company announced many staples of the independent scene among its inaugural roster, including SCU, the trio of Christopher Daniels, Frankie Kazarian, and Scorpio Sky. Kazarian and Sky made history as the first-ever AEW World Tag Team Champions.

Following the breakup of SCU, Sky went on to become a multi-time TNT Champion and joined forces with Ethan Page as the Men of the Year. However, in recent years, Sky has not been a regular staple of AEW television, enduring nearly two years of inactivity from 2023 to 2025. He returned in July 2025 and aligned with Top Flight, forming the SkyFlight trio, but has wrestled just ten times in AEW since then and none since July of this year. More recently, he has made sporadic appearances on Ring of Honor programming, picking up several victories.

Addressing his situation, Sky has shared that he has been thinking about the circumstances while trying to focus on finishing this next chapter of his career. He admitted it has been hard not to be wrestling in AEW and, while realizing he is getting paid while staying at home, he grew up dreaming about wanting to be a wrestler rather than dreaming about getting paid not to. The 43-year-old is not the only high-profile talent facing an expiring deal, as Max Caster and Anthony Bowens of The Acclaimed also reportedly became free agents when their contracts expired on October 1, alongside other unresolved roster contracts.

If Sky officially departs the promotion, several potential options await outside of AEW. TNA has reportedly been expecting a former tag team champion to debut at Bound For Glory, which could potentially be Sky, allowing him to reunite with Frankie Kazarian to reform a version of SCU. Another alternative involves joining his former Men of the Year partner Ethan Page on the WWE main roster, where the tag team division could utilize a real team. Brock Lesnar inducted into University of Minnesota M Club Hall of Fame

Former WWE and UFC champion Brock Lesnar has added another major accolade to his resume by being inducted into the University of Minnesota M Club Hall of Fame. The official induction took place during a ceremony at Huntington Bank Stadium in Minneapolis, with the class also being recognized during the subsequent Minnesota home football game against Michigan.

Lesnar was one of ten former Golden Gophers athletes included in the Hall of Fame class, which was originally announced in July. Other inductees featured in the group include Guillermo Alvarez in men gymnastics, Luke Appert in baseball, Daniel Croaston in men swimming and diving, Lauren Gibbemeyer Mendes in volleyball, Jody Horak in women hockey, Sean Lumpkin in football, Larry Olimb in men hockey, Sarah Wilhite Parsons in volleyball, and Lezlie Weiss in softball.

Before rising to fame as one of the biggest stars in professional wrestling and mixed martial arts, Lesnar enjoyed a dominant amateur wrestling career. He transferred to the university from Bismarck State College on a scholarship, compiling a 55-3 record with 23 pins at Minnesota and an overall 106-5 record across four years. After finishing as the NCAA heavyweight runner-up in 1999, he captured the NCAA Division I heavyweight national championship in 2000. He was a two-time NCAA All-American and a two-time Big Ten Conference Champion.

This latest honor comes just two months after Lesnar confirmed his retirement from professional wrestling during an appearance on The Pat McAfee Show. His final match took place on August 1 during the first night of WWE SummerSlam at US Bank Stadium in Minneapolis, where he faced Oba Femi inside a Hell in a Cell structure in the main event, with Femi defeating Lesnar to close out his in-ring career. Throughout his combat sports and sports entertainment journey, Lesnar also briefly pursued an NFL career with the Minnesota Vikings in 2004, captured the UFC Heavyweight Championship in 2008, and is already a member of the Bismarck State College Hall of Fame from 2005, with future inductions into the WWE and UFC Halls of Fame expected.

Pictured in a suit and glasses at the recent ceremony, Lesnar had the support of familiar faces. His college roommate, former coach, and former wrestling roster mate Shelton Benjamin, who is currently an AEW roster member, was in attendance to celebrate the milestone and shared public praise for his longtime friend on social media.

Dave Meltzer notes AEW star responsible for NJPW relationship

According to Wrestling Observer Radio, AEW and New Japan Pro Wrestling have maintained a strong working relationship for over five years, highlighted by the annual Forbidden Door pay-per-view event held across the United States, Canada, and the United Kingdom. However, the partnership did not always exist. Initially, relations were strained because NJPW management was unhappy with The Elite leaving the promotion in 2019. Furthermore, former NJPW President Harold Meij saw no upside in collaborating with AEW since NJPW already maintained partnerships with CMLL and Ring of Honor.

Despite these early hurdles, one man proved pivotal in turning the situation around. Dave Meltzer reported on Wrestling Observer Radio that Jon Moxley was single-handedly responsible for forging the connection between the two promotions during the pandemic. Moxley held the IWGP United States Championship while simultaneously being an AEW star, which ultimately opened the door for a working relationship. This bridge-building moment eventually led to crossover moments such as KENTA appearing in AEW, allowing the two companies to construct a robust partnership from that point onward.

Moxley originally captured the IWGP United States Championship during his debut match for NJPW, which occurred less than two weeks after his initial AEW debut. Although travel complications forced him to be stripped of the title after missing a scheduled defense in October 2019, he retained the championship when he won it back in January 2020. Despite the COVID-19 pandemic preventing him from traveling back to Japan throughout that year, his position as champion kept the diplomatic pipeline open. Additionally, Chris Jericho competed in NJPW while reigning as the AEW World Champion, marking Jericho's final official match for the Japanese promotion to date.

Discussions are currently taking place about creating a permanent monument in the city, with Damo sharing that they are looking to see if they can get a statue to him in the city and trying to get permission for that because he means so much to the people there and to wrestling.

Beyond his hometown legacy, Damo also spoke about the overwhelming response from those who knew PAC personally, emphasizing that the loss has been felt throughout the wrestling world. While nothing has been officially approved yet regarding the statue, the fact that these efforts are already underway speaks volumes about the impact PAC had both inside and outside the ring. If permission is ultimately granted, the monument would stand as a lasting tribute to one of Newcastle's most celebrated wrestling stars. Fans and peers continue to reflect on his profound influence, leaving many to consider what the most fitting ways are for the wrestling world to continue honoring his legacy.
