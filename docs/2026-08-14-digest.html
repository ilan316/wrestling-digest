Cody Rhodes Found Unexpected Downtime When American Nightmare Tour Bus Breaks Down

The "American Nightmare" encountered an unexpected obstacle on Wednesday, August 13, 2026, when his tour bus broke down on the side of the road. Rather than let the situation dampen his spirits, Rhodes decided to make the most of his time by engaging with fans on social media. Despite the mechanical issues, Rhodes managed to get the bus moving again and made it to his scheduled WWE house show appearance in Manchester, New Hampshire that evening.

While waiting for repairs, Rhodes took to X/Twitter to conduct an impromptu question-and-answer session using the hashtag CodyBus. The spontaneous interaction allowed fans to ask him about various topics ranging from personal preferences to professional philosophy. Among the questions he fielded was one about his favorite cities to perform in, to which Rhodes responded enthusiastically about several locations.

Rhodes shared his affinity for Philadelphia, Atlanta, Quebec City, Los Angeles (specifically the Sofi Stadium), all of Texas, and New York City. He noted that while he enjoyed most places, there were only two cities where he came in "ready for a fight at." The wrestler also revealed several other insights during the session, including recommendations for cigars and his philosophy about preparing for leadership positions in wrestling.

When asked about potential podcast guests for his show "What Do You Wanna Talk About?", Rhodes mentioned a notable list of peers he admired. He named IYO SKY, Bobby Roode, TJ Wilson, Michael Cole, Raquel Rodriguez, his sister Teil, and his daughter Liberty as dream guests. Rhodes expressed enthusiasm about their respective stories and suggested it was up to the fans to decide who they would like to hear from next.

On the podcast itself, Rhodes recalled a memorable moment involving Greg Gagne and CM Punk. According to Rhodes, Gagne was confused about how the ECW crowd already knew who CM Punk was when he debuted. Punk explained that he had wrestled in that building for ten years prior to his ECW debut, which bewildered Gagne. Rhodes noted that despite Gagne's initial confusion, he became very supportive and would frequently give speeches at the end of events where he would shout out Punk, though he never called him by his "CM" initials. Rhodes described Gagne as a great example and noted that he was very helpful in teaching him old-school wrestling techniques that are no longer commonly taught.

Adam Copeland Receives Tony Khan's Blessing for Maple Leaf Pro Northern Rising Appearance

In a significant development for professional wrestling partnerships, Adam Copeland has received approval from AEW President Tony Khan to compete at Maple Leaf Pro Wrestling's Northern Rising pay-per-view. The event is scheduled for October 3 at the Mattamy Athletic Centre in Toronto, Ontario, Canada, located on the grounds of the historic former Maple Leaf Gardens. This opportunity represents an important career milestone for Copeland, who has long desired to perform at the legendary venue.

During an appearance on MLP Mayhem, Copeland explained the circumstances that led to Khan approving his outside appearance. He praised the AEW president's progressive outlook on professional wrestling and his understanding of the broader wrestling landscape. Copeland stated that Khan recognizes the importance of professional wrestling flourishing across different promotions and territories.

"In terms of doing this show, I'm contracted with AEW, but the beautiful part about Tony Khan and his outlook in terms of pro wrestling is that he wants pro wrestling to flourish," Copeland explained in a video that aired during the August 13 episode of MLP Mayhem. "AEW and Maple Leaf Pro Wrestling are partners. It's a partnership that can help wrestling thrive in a place that needs to have wrestling thrive."

Beyond the business considerations, Copeland emphasized the personal significance of this opportunity. He revealed that Khan understood the deep emotional and professional importance of this moment in his career. Copeland expressed that wrestling at Maple Leaf Gardens represented a crucial item on his professional bucket list that he needed to complete before retiring.

"He also knew what it means to me and how special this place is to me," Copeland said. "And I don't think I was going to be able to retire without a bit of a bee in my bonnet unless I did this. I think this allows me to go, 'Right.'"

The path to this opportunity began when Maple Leaf Pro President Scott D'Amore saw an interview in which Copeland discussed his desire to perform at the historic venue. Copeland had previously referred to Maple Leaf Gardens as his "hallowed ground" that he had to perform in before concluding his wrestling career. D'Amore took that interview as inspiration and began working to make the match happen, eventually reaching out to Khan for approval.

Before his Northern Rising appearance, Copeland will have another significant engagement alongside Christian Cage. The two are set to defend their AEW World Tag-Team Championship against The Young Bucks at AEW All In: London on August 30 at Wembley Stadium in London, England.

The approval of Copeland's Maple Leaf Pro appearance reflects a broader partnership between AEW and Maple Leaf Pro Wrestling. Both Copeland and Bret Hart have made appearances on MLP Mayhem, which airs on TSN in Canada and streams on myAEW.com internationally. Hart has been appearing in videos discussing his protege Mo Jabari, who co-owns Dungeon Wrestling with Hart's sons. Copeland first appeared on the July 29 episode of MLP Mayhem, where he was revealed as the mystery person who had accepted D'Amore's phone call.

Another member of the legendary Mysterio family has joined WWE's ranks. According to reports, Aalyah Gutierrez, the daughter of WWE Hall of Famer Rey Mysterio and sister of former Intercontinental Champion Dominik Mysterio, has officially started at the WWE Performance Center and is now listed on the company's internal roster.

The 24-year-old is currently listed under her given name, Aalyah Gutierrez, rather than using the Mysterio surname that her father and brother utilize. This is standard practice for new recruits who are awaiting WWE's assignment of their in-ring name. Whether Gutierrez ultimately becomes her wrestling name or she eventually adopts the Mysterio name remains to be seen, though some industry observers suggest she may create a distinct identity separate from her family's legacy, similar to how other second and third-generation wrestlers have approached their careers.

Aalyah's path to WWE began after she graduated from the University of California San Diego last year with a degree in human biology. Rey had made a promise to his daughter that once she completed her education, he would introduce her to professional wrestling. When she showed him her diploma, she was ready to take that next step.

Her journey into wrestling started in earnest in October 2025, when Rey brought her to the WWE Performance Center for basic in-ring drills while he was recovering from his own injuries. She loved the experience and returned in February 2026 for an extended two-week training session. During that time, Rey revealed that his daughter had committed to pursuing wrestling on a full-time basis with WWE.

Rey previously discussed the training regimen with his daughter, wanting to ensure she understood the demands of the profession. He said, "So I said, 'Okay, the next step would be, let's see if you can go through a full week of training. And if you can do that and you like it afterwards, then maybe it's something you might want to think about.' I'm actually trying to get her out there pretty soon so she can just feel what it's like to train every day. Wake up, work out all day, sleep, back, same thing. That's insane. Like, I've thought about that and it scares me, but it also excites me."

Aalyah completed a two-week test of the Monday-to-Friday training schedule to determine if she genuinely enjoyed the grind of professional wrestling. She did. Rey recalled, "I said, 'Let's try to set you up to go up there for about two weeks and see if you can wake up every day and do this Monday through Friday, weekends off, and then back again Monday through Friday.' She did two weeks, and she really loved it."

While Aalyah is no stranger to WWE audiences, her previous appearances were primarily non-wrestling in nature. She appeared in several storylines during the Mysterio family's feud with Seth Rollins in 2020, including a romantic angle involving Buddy Murphy that played into on-screen storylines. She also featured prominently during Rey and Dominik's rivalry that culminated at WrestleMania 39, though these appearances were limited to acting rather than in-ring competition.

The addition of Aalyah to the Performance Center roster places her among several other notable second and third-generation wrestling prospects. Earlier this week, she was photographed at the Performance Center alongside Lexis King, son of Brian Pillman; Marie Malenko, daughter of Dean Malenko; Uriah Connors, son of Fit Finlay; Avery Styles, son of AJ Styles; and Brooks Jensen, son of Bull Buchanan. The photo was shared by King with the caption, "We already set up the f'n chairs," a reference to the tradition of second-generation wrestlers assisting with setup duties at wrestling events.

Neither WWE nor the Mysterio family has officially announced Aalyah's signing, though the reports of her being listed on the internal roster are significant. There is currently no word on when WWE plans to make an official announcement or when she might debut on television.

Rey has expressed his excitement about the historical possibilities that Aalyah's wrestling career could create for his family. He previously stated, "To be able to have shared the ring with my son, become the first father-and-son Tag Team Champions in WWE, and then later feud against each other—but now with my daughter, it's like, what can we do there? This is a historical moment."

Rey's own WWE history is extensive, having made his main roster debut in 2002 and becoming one of the company's most iconic performers. Dominik followed his father into WWE in 2020 and has since become a prominent figure on the Raw brand. With Aalyah now joining the Performance Center, the Mysterio family's presence in WWE appears poised to expand further.

For now, Aalyah's focus remains on developing her in-ring skills at the Performance Center before any potential television debut. As she trains and prepares for her wrestling career, fans and industry observers will be watching to see what name she ultimately adopts and what role she will play in continuing the Mysterio family's legacy in WWE.

WWE ID Scouting and Recruitment

What WWE Looks for When Scouting Potential WWE ID Prospects

Gabe Sapolsky is looking for independent wrestlers who can do a lot more than just put on a good match. When WWE ID asked what catches his attention as a talent scout, Sapolsky made it clear that being a great wrestler only gets someone through the door.

Sapolsky currently works as a WWE talent scout across North America while also being involved with EVOLVE and WWE ID. For Sapolsky, it starts with something that can be difficult to teach: the "it" factor.

"What stands out to me are unique qualities. I'm looking for the 'it' factor, but also strong fundamentals. I look for strikes that I still believe. When I see a good strike, I still react to it. Then I look at a hold, and if I cringe, I'll be like a fan. When I'm buying it like that, I know the superiors will also buy it, and the fans will buy it."

But believable wrestling is only part of what gets his attention. Sapolsky wants to see wrestlers bring character, personality and something uniquely theirs to what they're doing. Simply being technically great isn't enough when WWE is ultimately looking for people who can become Superstars.

"So I'm looking for those qualities and looking for people who add character to it, uniqueness, and personality. We're not in the good wrestler business. Being a great wrestler gets you in the door, but we're in the Superstar business. What really matters is what makes you one of one after you accomplish all that."

And once someone has those qualities, Sapolsky is looking at how they work when the cameras aren't necessarily the focus. He wants people who will outwork everyone around them, take coaching and actually learn from what WWE's coaches are trying to teach them.

"At the end of the day, I wanna put qualities in front of the coaches at the PC. People who outwork everyone else, and people who are coachable and listen and learn."

That pretty much lays out the checklist for anyone hoping WWE ID becomes their road into the company. Great wrestling can get Sapolsky watching, but if there isn't personality, individuality and something that makes a wrestler impossible to confuse with everybody else, that apparently isn't enough anymore.

Matt Hardy WWE Career Reflection

Matt Hardy Picks the Underrated WWE Match That Changed Wrestling Forever

Matt Hardy has reflected on one of the defining moments of his legendary career. He argued that a match many fans remember fondly deserves even more credit for reshaping professional wrestling. The TNA World Tag Team Champion believes its influence stretched far beyond the finish, laying the foundation for an entire generation of hardcore classics.

With more than 35 years in the business, Hardy has built a resume that spans WWE, TNA, AEW, ROH, and beyond. Yet when asked by Fox News Digital which achievement doesn't receive enough recognition, he didn't point to a championship reign or a cinematic spectacle. Instead, he highlighted the first-ever tag team ladder match featuring The Hardy Boyz and Edge & Christian at No Mercy 1999.

Hardy believes that bout became the blueprint for everything that followed. Looking back, he said, "A moment still that I don't think gets talked about quite enough… was the first-ever tag team ladder match." He explained that the contest changed how stories could be told with inanimate objects and directly inspired future innovations. According to Hardy, "It was so instrumental in leading the way for change."

The match eventually led to the first tag team tables match with The Dudley Boyz, an idea he pitched to Vince McMahon, before evolving into the first TLC Match at SummerSlam 2000. Hardy noted that the concept didn't stop with WWE, as promotions such as AEW, TNA with Full Metal Mayhem, and ROH with Ladder Wars all embraced similar ideas. "That tag team ladder match… was absolutely the beginning and origin point of the TLC concept," he said, adding that decades from now fans will recognize it as "the moment where all of this insanity started."

Hardy has echoed similar thoughts on The Extreme Life of Matt Hardy podcast. While crediting Shawn Michaels vs. Razor Ramon for inspiring the original ladder match format, he insisted, "The tag team ladder match at No Mercy '99… changed the perception of wrestling."

He also recalled calling Bruce Prichard before a follow-up house show because he worried about topping the match. Prichard reassured him, saying, "It's not a big deal, nobody's going to see it." More than 25 years later, Hardy's argument still carries weight. For many fans, that ladder match wasn't just climbing the ranks but raised the bar for professional wrestling storytelling.

Santino Marella WWE Advice

Former WWE star Santino Marella has passed on some important advice from the legendary Dusty Rhodes for all aspiring wrestlers.

While he was in WWE developmental, Santino Marella wrestled under the name Boris Alexiev with a character based on a legitimate MMA and fighting background. That is of course very different to the Italian comedy character he became known as when he was called up to the WWE main roster.

While he wasn't exactly a serious main event talent or WrestleMania headliner, Marella did become one of the most loved comedy characters in WWE history, and amassed two Intercontinental Title reigns, a United States Title reign, and a Tag Team Title reign alongside Vladimir Kozlov.

That all came because Marella followed some advice that was given to him by WWE Hall of Famer Dusty Rhodes when he was still in developmental: "If they ever ask you if you can do anything, the answer's yes. And then you better learn how to do it."

Appearing on a podcast hosted by Dusty's son Cody Rhodes, Marella explained the origin of this lesson. He recalled the story of "Fearless" Jack Bull, another wrestler in developmental who was portraying a washed-up, alcoholic Evel Knievel character. When Dusty Rhodes called and asked if Jack Bull could ride a motorcycle, the wrestler initially said no. When asked if he could learn, the answer was again no. Jack Bull later realized he had rejected a call from the legendary Dusty Rhodes himself.

Dusty Rhodes subsequently visited the developmental facility and taught a class where he shared his philosophy with all the wrestlers present. He used the example of riding a unicycle, telling them that if asked whether they could perform any task, the answer should always be yes, followed by learning how to do it in the coming days.

Marella took this lesson to heart and immediately implemented it when the opportunity arose. He received a call asking if he could speak Italian. Without hesitation, he said yes, despite having only memorized a few basic phrases from a tourist tape. During that initial conversation, he recited those few lines and the call went well.

Following the call, Marella went to a bookstore and purchased multiple Italian language instruction DVDs and CDs, loading them onto his iPod. He spent his entire flight studying Italian lessons, beginning his journey into the language. While he didn't become fluent during that initial period, he continued studying Italian over time and eventually reached a level where he could conduct live television interviews fluently in the language.

Marella's commitment to learning Italian went beyond professional necessity. He eventually obtained Italian citizenship and fully immersed himself in Italian culture, crediting his father's Italian heritage as an additional motivation for this genuine connection to the language and culture.

Marella reflected on how this single lesson changed the trajectory of his career. He acknowledged that had he not received and implemented Dusty Rhodes' advice, he might have declined the opportunity, telling whoever called that he couldn't speak Italian very well. The willingness to say yes to an opportunity, combined with the determination to actually learn the required skill, led directly to the creation of Santino Marella, one of WWE's most memorable and beloved characters.

The message Marella emphasizes is vital for all up and coming WWE stars and wrestlers in general. Always say yes to opportunities, even if the call isn't necessarily what you were dreaming of, because it could always become something bigger. The opportunity might never come around again, and as Marella's career proves, saying yes to something that seems unusual or outside your wheelhouse could result in a career-defining transformation.

Dave Meltzer's assessment was brutal, with multiple DUD ratings for matches and a particularly harsh -2-star review for Major Gunns (Tylene Buck) defeating Miss Hancock (Stacey Keibler) in a clothes-tearing mud match.

Meltzer noted that aside from a good first and last match, the show consisted of either bad matches or talented workers like Mike Awesome and Lance Storm who were given scripts that fundamentally killed their performances. He highlighted that there is nothing worse than attempting comedy when nobody is laughing. Even the main event suffered greatly, with three referee bumps dampening what should have been an exciting finish, especially given that the crowd had already been largely killed by the undercard's excesses.

The crowd in Vancouver, a city that should have been hot all night given that WCW had never run the market and it traditionally receives strong reactions for professional wrestling, failed to engage with what the company was trying to sell. Meltzer received a letter from a fan after the show making an important point: the people in charge at WCW needed to step out of their roles for a night and go to an average family's home, shut up, and watch a PPV to see how a regular person who spent thirty dollars reacted from their couch.

When Meltzer held a full house viewing of the event, his guests enjoyed the ladder match but then went outside and stopped caring about the show. The only times anyone came back inside for more than a quick few seconds were during the Major Gunns match, with one person leaving halfway through and another initially enjoying it but ending up hating it because of the angle. One person watched the entire Booker T match, but the heavily hyped three-way match filled with shoot references received nothing more than quick glances before people headed outside.

The viewing group consisted entirely of casual fans, not serious wrestling enthusiasts. Every single person was a fan to some degree, and everyone had loved WCW Nitro during the summer of 1997. They had attended numerous live events in the Bay Area over the years spanning decades, some still followed Raw storylines, and they represented the exact demographic that had been glued to WCW PPV events during that peak period. These were precisely the type of casual fans who had responded positively in WCW's most recent marketing surveys, yet the New Blood Rising event failed to captivate them.

When it comes to professional wrestling, there is a difference between working while injured and working while hurt. While there will no doubt be arguments to be had about either condition, sometimes stars will push through an injury to continue performing, even if all the indications are there that they should recover.

Working hurt is likely to be the preferable of the two situations, being a result of day-to-day activity, be it a match or a workout. A bruise, a cut, a black eye, feeling a bit sore—the medical professions are likely to be the one who provide an insight into what is needed to keep going and, for many stars, they are likely to have an awareness of what they can do and how they can do it without making the hurt worse.

When it comes to working injured, whether it be any of the list that Bowens provides or any of the other injuries that would see many of us take time off work, it becomes apparent why these stars would continue to work. A storyline could be derailed, a push abandoned, a championship vacated, and there is no guarantee, in the most serious cases, that a return will happen. Medical professionals are again the ones who will no doubt counsel the star on what is next, with the star no doubt hoping for a return as quickly as possible. Even after that, there is the likelihood that they may have to work with the aftermath of a particularly serious injury.

For some stars, that comeback may be years later, as was seen with Paige, who was cleared for a return to the ring while signed to AEW as Saraya, having suffered a career-ending injury in WWE. Medical science continues to evolve, and what may have been the end of a career a few years ago can have a different result in modern times.

After the match, Johnny Ace questioned Red's experience working in larger rings. When Red mentioned his All Japan Pro Wrestling background, Ace was momentarily impressed but ultimately offered little encouragement beyond "Get bigger" and a vague promise to call him back. Red left convinced WWE had never seriously considered signing him.

The situation became even more frustrating years later. Red was set to compete in the 2016 Cruiserweight Classic, a 32-man tournament he believed represented his best chance at finally earning a WWE contract. A week before the tournament, he was invited to perform motion-capture work for a WWE 2K video game—a dream opportunity that turned into a nightmare. The mocap sessions proved far more physically demanding than expected, requiring performers to repeat moves multiple times to capture different variations and sell reactions. Red was asked to perform AJ Styles' Spiral Tap repeatedly, a move he disliked because it required him to look away from the ring. After seven or eight attempts with his body already exhausted, he had a gut feeling he should stop, but was pressured into one final take. During that last attempt, his foot became caught in the gap between two crash pads, and his leg twisted and broke. Low Ki, who was also present for the session, had to loudly demand that someone help him.

An WWE executive promised him there would be a second season of the Cruiserweight Classic where he could participate, but that tournament never materialized. Instead, WWE launched 205 Live later that year, and Red never received a call for either opportunity. At the time, Red was devastated, viewing it as his last realistic shot at WWE employment. However, he has since reframed the injury as a blessing in disguise that ultimately kept his family together and prevented him from pursuing a path that may not have materialize anyway.

Red's experiences illustrate a pattern of WWE overlooking him despite his undeniable influence on professional wrestling. His Code Red has become a staple move used by John Cena, Sting, and numerous other wrestlers, cementing his creative legacy even without the company's backing. He was recently inducted into the TNA Hall of Fame at Bound for Glory, an honor he initially refused multiple times but accepted after a surprise announcement at Slammiversary.
