🔥 Brodie Lee's Widow Slams AI-Generated Tribute Images of Deceased Wrestlers

Following the tragic passing of AEW star PAC, who died at the age of 40, the wrestling community has been struck by another wave of grief. AEW officially announced the death of PAC, whose real name was Benjamin Satterley and who was also known to WWE fans as Neville, on September 27, 2026. As wrestlers and fans worldwide shared memories and tributes to honor his career and impact on the industry, a specific trend began flooding social media.

Numerous AI-generated images started circulating online depicting deceased wrestlers, including late AEW star Brodie Lee, welcoming PAC to heaven. While some fans may have intended these posts as heartfelt tributes, Amanda Huber, Brodie Lee's widow, made her feelings crystal clear by condemning the trend on X. She expressed how deeply upsetting the images were to her, noting that she frequently ended up tagged in them by users expecting a positive response.

The fucking ill I feel seeing this shit. I hate it so much. It feels like romanticizing this shit. Worst part is I always end up tagged like I am gonna be like I love it so much, thank you for your creativity and doing this UGH. That was how Huber described the impact of seeing the fake artwork on her timeline while she was already grieving.

Huber further explained that the technology itself went directly against the personal values of both PAC and her late husband. According to Huber, PAC actively hated artificial intelligence, and she knows for a fact that Brodie Lee would have felt the exact same way. Both men believed strongly in supporting real art and real artists.

Huber shared that Brodie consistently paid friends for their art during his life. When he passed away in December 2020 at the age of 41, people offered to send her free art, but she insisted on paying for multiple pieces because accepting uncompensated work felt like it would dishonor his memory and his commitment to supporting artists.

Clarifying that she was not trying to make the situation about herself, Huber admitted that losing her friend PAC has been immensely tough, and seeing these AI-generated images on her timeline only adds to her pain. Her frustration goes beyond whether the images look tasteful, as they clash directly with the values of the people being depicted. As the wrestling community continues to mourn PAC, Amanda Huber has drawn a firm line, reminding fans to consider the real-world feelings of those affected before pushing automated memorial images onto their feeds.

🔥 AEW October 2026 Schedule on HBO Max and TV

AEW has officially unveiled its full schedule for October across TBS, TNT, and HBO Max, which features several notable departures from the company's usual weekly lineup.

The biggest change on the calendar takes place on Tuesday, October 6, when a special edition of Dynamite emanates from the Adidas Arena in Paris, France. Titled Dynamite: Grand Slam France, the episode moves to Tuesday night due to Major League Baseball playoff coverage on TBS. This rare shift will place AEW head-to-head with WWE NXT in an unusual mid-week showdown.

AEW will return to its regular Wednesday schedule the following week, though October 14 will feature a combined three-hour Dynamite and Collision broadcast. The Countdown to WrestleDream special will follow immediately after the conclusion of the three-hour block at 11 p.m.

WrestleDream weekend brings further schedule adjustments on Saturday, October 17. The one-hour Tailgate Brawl pre-show will return at 7 p.m. on TNT and HBO Max, leading directly into the WrestleDream pay-per-view event at 8 p.m. The pay-per-view will be available on HBO Max alongside AEW's usual traditional providers.

The rest of the month largely follows AEW’s regular rhythm and time slots, highlighted by spooky-themed holiday programming. Fright Night Dynamite is scheduled for October 28, followed by Fright Night Collision on October 31 to close out the month.

In addition to current programming, HBO Max is expanding its on-demand archive library throughout October. The AEW Dynasty 2026 event, which took place this past April, will become available on the streaming service on October 16. Later in the month on October 30, episodes of Dynamite, Collision, and Rampage from May and June 2024 will be added. These archive updates continue to fill the existing content gap on the platform between April and October 2024, giving subscribers more wrestling history to revisit.

Rebel Advocates for ALS Legislation After Passing of Bill

There has been a positive update regarding the ACT for ALS legislation that former AEW personality Rebel recently urged wrestling fans to support. Rebel previously called on fans to sign a petition supporting the reauthorization of the Accelerating Access to Critical Therapies for ALS Act, commonly known as ACT for ALS. The legislation was scheduled to expire on September 30 unless Congress acted to extend it. There had been concern that reauthorization could be delayed until later this year, potentially putting programs that provide ALS patients with access to investigational therapies at risk. As Rebel noted, many ALS patients simply cannot afford to wait that long.

On Monday, the U.S. Senate passed the ACT for ALS Reauthorization Act of 2026, extending programs established under the original legislation through 2031. The bill will now head to the President's desk to be signed into law.

The organization I AM ALS celebrated the development in a statement, crediting the ALS community for helping push the reauthorization forward. ACT for ALS passed Congress on September 28, the organization wrote, and it is now on its way to the President's desk to become law, happening because our community-powered movement led the way on this reauthorization from the start. Rebel has been outspoken about ALS awareness and the importance of maintaining access to potentially life-extending treatments for patients battling the disease.

The organization further shared on social media that ACT for ALS passed Congress with one day to spare, thanking supporters for emails, calls, meetings, petition signatures, and relentless advocacy that made the achievement possible.

AEW Dynamite Ticket Sales and Previews

AEW Dynamite is taking place this week from the Petersen Events Center in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, and will be a special tribute show dedicated to Benjamin Satterley, also known as PAC, who passed away this week at the age of 40. As of writing, the cheapest standard admission ticket available is $32.70, down from $39.25.

In terms of the match card, only the tag team match featuring Persephone and Britt Baker against Thunder Rosa and Hyan has been confirmed for the show as of writing. Obviously, the entire broadcast will be centered around the tragedy of PAC passing away. The outpouring of love for the Geordie wrestler has been almost universal, with even his beloved Newcastle United offering condolences on social media. Nobody ever seemed to have a bad word to say about The Bastard, and everyone he ever worked with came away a better performer. PAC is widely regarded as one of the greatest innovators of the 21st century in professional wrestling, and his legacy will be visible every week in the ring across WWE, AEW, the indies, Japan, and beyond. Benjamin Satterley was an incredible professional wrestler and by all accounts an incredible human being who will be missed by everyone. All Elite Wrestling will honor his life, career, and legacy this Wednesday night at eight eastern on TBS and HBO Max.

Shifting to business matters, Tony Khan recently spoke about AEW's growth, media rights, and expanded pay-per-view schedule during the All Out post-show. Khan highlighted the accolades the promotion has received from wrestling and mainstream sports media and asserted that the shows have been better and more commercially successful this year. He noted higher numbers of buys and higher live event sales for the pay-per-views, calling it a really great year. Khan explained that as AEW grew its television footprint and media rights deal, expanding the pay-per-view calendar made logical sense. He pointed out that every major wrestling TV company in history has gravitated toward having more pay-per-views as they expand their television product through boom periods.

Khan believes the expanded schedule has been very additive for AEW, helping the business and the bottom line. He emphasized that the company has maintained goodwill with fans by consistently delivering great pay-per-views that offer good value for the money, making it worth the investment for the audience. According to Khan, this value is why AEW enjoys such a strong return rate from its incredible fanbase, whom he thanked for their support. AEW originally began in 2019 with four major pay-per-view events and just two hours of weekly television, but as the product has progressed, the company has transformed its routine into four hours of weekly TV and held around ten pay-per-views this year.

Eric Bischoff Discusses AEW TV Rights Negotiations

Former WCW President Eric Bischoff recently discussed AEW and outlined what he believes the company needs to focus on when selling itself during upcoming television rights negotiations. Speaking on his 83 Weeks podcast, Bischoff drew from his past dealings with television executives to explain how All Elite Wrestling should position itself as it seeks a bigger deal in the future.

Putting on his theoretical AEW salesman hat, Bischoff explained that the primary selling point he would emphasize is consistency. He stated that he would highlight consistency for the wrestling product in general, point to high points when AEW has achieved consistency, and discuss strategies for becoming even more consistent. Beyond just AEW, Bischoff pointed out that professional wrestling has historically been a very consistent performer on a cost-per-program basis, meaning it remains cheap programming for networks.

Elaborating on pro wrestling bringing a consistent audience, Bischoff adopted his salesman persona to mock up a pitch to network executives. For your investment, Mr. Network, my God, this stuff continues to deliver and always will, it is predictable and it is relatively stable, he added. For programmers, that is important, because if you are programming a network or you are the general manager of a network, you have to project numbers, ratings, and all the metrics needed to attract advertisers.

Bischoff concluded his advice by suggesting that AEW should focus heavily on cost comparisons. He advised comparing AEW programming to WWE programming to highlight specific financial advantages, emphasizing that networks can still secure a stable audience while not spending nearly as much money.

🔄 המשך Steven Borden Injury Update After AEW All Out

Medical updates following the All Out pay-per-view indicate that Steven Borden is doing well after his scary collision in the ring.

During the live broadcast, commentary provided reassurance by announcing that Borden was conscious and being transported to a hospital for precautionary checks. Tony Khan later shared during the post-show media scrum that Borden's CT scans and medical tests came back completely clear, and that the injured wrestler even retained his memory of the match. Additional reports confirmed that he was expected to fly home safely the following day.

Weighing in on the online debate surrounding the dangerous spot, Ricochet defended the athletes involved. He compared the incident to his own mid-air collision with Logan Paul at the 2023 Royal Rumble, noting that he and Paul had also only rehearsed their dangerous spot once. Ricochet stressed that injuries can happen on any move regardless of preparation, and insisted that no one is at fault for the unfortunate accident.
