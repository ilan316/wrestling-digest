AEW production and entrance limitations for All In London at Wembley Stadium

AEW is set to hold its All In London pay-per-view event on Sunday, August 30, 2026, at Wembley Stadium in London, England. As the company is holding its biggest show of the year, events of this magnitude typically feature special entrances for the wrestlers, and this year is expected to follow that tradition. Numerous extravagant entrances are currently being planned for the event. Fans can anticipate impressive entrances from top stars such as Mercedes Mone, Will Ospreay, Kenny Omega, Jon Moxley, Willow Nightingale, and MJF.

Indie veteran Eric Draven reflects on 35-year wrestling career

Eric Draven has spent approximately 35 years building a professional wrestling career that he says has taken him through 48 states, produced matches with some of the industry’s most recognizable names and eventually placed him in a position to teach the next generation. The Supreme Wrestling promoter, trainer and veteran recently spoke about his introduction to wrestling, the origin of the Eric Draven name, his return to EMERGE Wrestling and the goals that continue to motivate him decades into his career.

Draven’s interest in professional wrestling began with Memphis wrestling and the National Wrestling Alliance. Jerry Lawler and Bill Dundee were the performers who first made him believe wrestling could become his own career. I have always known this is what I wanted to do, Draven said, explaining that growing up watching Memphis wrestling and the old NWA made Lawler and Dundee the reasons he chose to become a wrestler. He began training at the USWA Wrestling Academy under the guidance of Jeff Jarrett and Wolfie D before continuing his development with Rip Rogers and Danny Davis at Ohio Valley Wrestling. The relationships did not end when his training was complete. Draven said he remains close with his trainers and continues to apply the lessons they taught him. To this day, I remain close friends with all of my trainers, Draven noted, adding that Rip, in particular, instilled in him the knowledge, discipline and ring skills that he now proudly pass on to the next generation of wrestlers.

Despite an assumption that has followed him throughout his career, Draven said his wrestling name did not originate with the 1994 film The Crow. The name instead developed from advice Jarrett gave him during his training in the early 1990s. During my training in 1991 or 1992, Jeff Jarrett gave me some advice that stuck with me, Draven recalled, noting the advice was that if he wanted a name that would last his entire career, he should combine his middle name with something he cared deeply about. Draven had become friends with Scott Levy, better known to wrestling fans as Raven, and held him in considerable regard. He then adapted his own middle name, Edric, to complete the combination. My middle name, which I have always hated, is Edric, Draven explained. I dropped the D to turn it into Eric, then placed it in front of Raven, and Draven was born. Although the presentation has changed over time, the character is now grounded in Draven’s own personality, with the veteran stating that over the years the character has continued to evolve, but at its core I have settled on a heightened, turned-up version of myself.

Draven describes himself as an old-school Memphis-style brawler. That was not always the entirety of his presentation, however. Early in my career I was a high-flyer, Draven said, and while my style has evolved into something more hard-hitting and grounded, I still mix in some of that aerial offense when the moment calls for it. His primary influences include Lawler, Terry Funk, Randy Savage and Eddie Gilbert, each representing some combination of physicality, personality, intensity and audience connection that would become part of Draven’s own work. Among his most valued accomplishments is winning the AIWF World Championship. Draven also cited opportunities to wrestle The Road Warriors, Lawler, AJ Styles and The Rock and Roll Express among the matches he continues to hold closely, stating that winning the AIWF World Championship has been one of his top moments, but he also holds dear matches he has had with those legendary names and many more.

After working throughout the country, Draven does not believe modern wrestling changes dramatically simply because a wrestler crosses a state line. Having wrestled in 48 states and continuing to travel as his only employment, he points out that to be honest, there is not much difference these days between locations, describing it as the same games, just different players, as he looks toward his ongoing return to his second home.
