Indie Spotlight on Teagan Thorne

Teagan Thorne describes herself as a real-life Monster High doll, combining Frankenstein-inspired imagery, gothic glamour and an intentionally layered personality. The darkness surrounding Thorne is not simply a costume or fictional backstory. In a written interview, Thorne said she was pronounced deceased at the scene of a childhood car crash before an emergency responder continued working to revive her. She survived, spent weeks in a coma and faced an extended rehabilitation that required her to relearn basic parts of everyday life. Years later, that survival story became the foundation for the wrestler she now presents to audiences, embracing the reality that she is a literal living dead girl story.

Thorne said she was around eleven or twelve when she was traveling with her father and two of her brothers. According to Thorne, their vehicle was struck by a drunk driver. She believes she fell on top of her youngest brother after losing consciousness and absorbed the most severe impact. Thorne suffered significant injuries to her skull and orbital socket, while her father attempted to protect her injuries until emergency responders reached the scene. She credits an EMT for refusing to let her die and performing the CPR that brought her back after she was initially pronounced deceased on the scene.

Being revived was only the beginning of her ordeal. Thorne remained in a coma for weeks, and after awakening, she had to relearn how to walk and talk while adjusting to vision changes following the repair of her orbital injury. She continued physical therapy for an extended period and stated that the crash left her with permanent brain damage. The psychological consequences proved just as significant as the physical recovery. Thorne can remember information about the early years of her life, but she cannot always mentally return to those memories in the same vivid way she can with experiences after the crash, comparing her mind to having a shelf of old VHS tapes with only a DVD player to view them. That limitation shapes how she approaches her time and relationships, driving her to make every moment count and mean something for herself and her fans.

Professional wrestling was not originally part of Thorne's plan. She did not care for wrestling when she was younger, despite growing up around it through her father. That perspective changed after the crash when she spent more time at home and began watching the product with her family. The breakup of The Shield and the fierce rivalry between Seth Rollins and Dean Ambrose pulled her into the larger storylines, while the women division featuring AJ Lee, Paige and The Bella Twins made wrestling feel like a viable path. Paige story particularly resonated with her, making her realize that if Paige could do it, she could too.

The opportunity to enter the business developed in 2019 after her cousin began training at a wrestling school connected to shows Thorne had attended in her youth. Her father, Wildthing Chuck McRoberts, was already helping her cousin and gradually began guiding Thorne as she participated in more training sessions. After a few practices, her father knew she was hooked because it quickly became her life and almost all she thought about.

Thorne identified McRoberts, Van Martigan and Marksman Fudd as the three most important coaching influences from the beginning of her career. The most lasting lesson she received was not a specific move or a technical sequence, as her trainers taught her to stop fearing the inherent unpredictability of the sport.
