🔥 Sheamus becomes free agent with report of AEW interest

Former WWE star Sheamus, also known by his real name Stephen Farrelly, has officially become a free agent following the expiration of his contract. Back in July, it was reported that Sheamus would be departing WWE after nearly two decades with the company, a move later confirmed when he was moved to the alumni page of the company website. The split came after the company asked him to restructure his deal while he was out of action with an injury, which was swiftly declined by the Celtic Warrior. Sheamus has not competed since he teamed with John Cena and Rey Mysterio on John Cena's final Raw appearance last November, though he was reportedly cleared to return shortly before his departure was confirmed. Despite the pay cut dispute, Sheamus noted in July that he was still working out and felt like he was in the best shape of his life.

With his contract officially ending on October 1, reports have emerged regarding interest in signing him. Management is expected to have interest in securing Sheamus to a full-time deal now that he has hit the open market. This interest extends beyond decision-makers, as a source close to the situation noted that a handful of talents and wrestlers in the locker room are in favor of him becoming All Elite. Despite the anticipated interest, there have been no direct discussions between Sheamus and the company yet, and no deal or potential timeline for his debut has been agreed upon or discussed.

Speculation surrounding his next move suggests that Sheamus likely turned down the WWE pay cut because he believed he could command a similar seven-figure salary elsewhere, likely reaching out to gauge his market value before making his decision. However, his potential addition to the roster also brings up questions regarding whether the promotion has too many wrestlers right now. While some personnel previously expressed concern about a deep roster where contracted talent struggles to secure enough television time, others point out his natural fit. Sheamus brings a very physical style that fits right in with the DNA of the brand, and his arrival could open doors for exciting matches against stars like Kyle Fletcher, Konosuke Takeshita, and Ilja Dragunov. Additionally, he could enter the tag team division by reuniting with his old partner Claudio Castagnoli, who previously stated he would love to see the Celtic Warrior join him. Sheamus' potential arrival follows a wave of recent high-profile departures and signings in the industry, including Kofi Kingston and Xavier Woods (who debuted as The New Level), the Motor City Machine Guns, Dani Luna, and Ilja Dragunov. For now, Sheamus has yet to publicly comment on his future, leaving his next chapter as one of the biggest stories in wrestling.

🔥 Jim Ross details severe post-brain surgery headaches

AEW commentator Jim Ross recently provided a health update during an episode of his Grilling JR podcast following the AEW Dynamite PAC tribute show in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. The 74-year-old WWE Hall of Famer is currently dealing with severe complications following brain surgery earlier this year, which involved having a shunt placed to drain excess fluid from his brain. Although the surgical wound on his skull is healing nicely, Ross revealed that the shunt has become problematic and has already been adjusted three times.

Ross is now experiencing awful, debilitating headaches that routinely wake him up in the middle of the night, usually between two and four in the morning. Describing the pain, Ross said he has watched too many John Wayne movies to complain about a simple headache, but compared the intensity to what he imagines a migraine feels like. He is scheduled for a CT scan followed by a visit with his neurosurgeon later in the week to find a solution. In addition to the shunt issues, Ross also has upcoming medical appointments concerning a spot on his kidney.

The brain surgery was originally intended in part to address balance problems that caused Ross to suffer several heavy falls prior to the operation. Those pre-surgery incidents left him with a broken wrist, a messed-up groin, and two broken toes on the exact morning of his surgery. Commenting on his streak of physical misfortune, Ross remarked that he is breaking shit right and left and admitted that it is not any fun whatsoever.

Despite his ongoing health battles, Ross remains deeply proud to be a part of AEW and hopes to resume his part-time commentary duties once he gets healthy, stating that he still has his playbook and his jersey. Reflecting on his extensive career, Ross expressed gratitude for the past 51 years he has spent immersed in professional wrestling, noting he does not feel burned out and feels very lucky that he is still able to put sentences together from time to time. He also shared gratitude for PAC, praising his professionalism, honor, and dignity, while noting that watching pro wrestling has provided a welcome escape during the stressful times he is currently going through.

Chris Hero discusses origin and pronunciation of PAC's ring name

Following the tragic passing of star wrestler PAC, real name Benjamin Satterley, at the age of 40, his longtime friend and colleague Chris Hero shared the heartfelt origin and correct pronunciation of his famous ring name. AEW had announced the death of the former WWE star known as Neville, which occurred just one day after he wrestled a well-received match against Andrade El Idolo at an AEW pay-per-view.

Hero, who knew the English wrestler for years, competed against him in NXT, and later worked with him in AEW where Hero serves as a coach and producer, took to Twitter to clarify how the name was intended to be sounded out. According to Hero, PAC is pronounced pack rather than pock. The moniker originated from a childhood nickname given to Satterley by his father, referencing Six Pack Ben Satterley.

Hero acknowledged he completely understands why so many people have mispronounced the late star’s ring name over the years, pointing out obvious parallels like 2Pac and X-Pac. He noted that people have been saying it that way for years and that is fine, adding that some will continue to say pock and that is fine too.

At the same time, Hero emphasized that Ben would cringe at the idea of correcting someone, making it clear he did not mean to act as the pronunciation police. He simply felt it was important and respectful to share how to say and spell the name correctly, comparing the phonetic difference between pack and pock to the difference between Max and Mox.

When one user responded by saying they were mortified to learn they had been saying it wrong for years, Hero reassured them that it was all good and happens easily given how diverse wrestling is with many cultures and ethnicities represented. Hero concluded by noting that PAC did not really care about the mispronunciation to be honest, but that fans and peers should still care about getting it right.

TNA wrestler Mila Moore set for MyAEW PPV debut with EVE

TNA name Mila Moore is set to make her debut with Pro Wrestling EVE this Friday as part of the GLOBAL THUNDER QUEENS ATTACK card from the Big Penny Social in London, England. EVE confirmed that the Secretary of Strategic Affairs for Order 4, who appears alongside Mustafa Ali on TNA IMPACT, will be appearing on a loaded lineup.

Friday is a major night for the promotion, as the card also features former WWE performer Kay Lee Ray, AEW name Emi Sakura, and notable UK standouts Lucy Deathmatch, Molly Spartan, Emersyn Jayne, Skye Smitson, and Alexxis Falcon.

In addition to the singles action, Friday's show will host the debut of Will Ospreay's new Clockwork Carnage match. This unique bout features 8 competitors, two stages, and one winner. Two competitors start the match selected at random, and every 2 minutes, another randomly selected competitor enters until all 8 competitors have entered the fray.

Any competitor who is pinned or submits during the initial entry phase is eliminated. Once the final competitor enters, all remaining participants enter a 15-minute scoring period. During those 15 minutes, every pinfall or submission scores one fall, and when time expires, the competitor with the most falls wins the match.

Global Thunder Queens Attack will take place on Friday, October 2nd. Fans can watch the event live on MyAEW PPV or access it as part of EVE's Champion Tier on YouTube.

Reason behind MJF missing the PAC tribute episode of AEW Dynamite

When AEW aired its emotional tribute show for PAC on the September 30 edition of Dynamite, MJF was nowhere to be seen. With so many wrestlers paying their respects throughout the night, plenty of fans expected one of the company's biggest stars to at least make an appearance, and it did not take long for them to start asking why.

Mike Johnson was asked about the omission during a recent Q&A session, and he did not think there was anything unusual about it. In his view, bringing MJF back just to appear on the tribute show would have completely conflicted with the character AEW has spent years building for him on television.

Let us be honest, that would be very out of character for the MJF that is portrayed on AEW programming, Johnson explained. Plus, he has been off TV of late, so it makes the most sense to bring him back when they are ready for his next story.

Johnson also pointed to Bryan Danielson's appearance on the program, noting that someone like Danielson naturally fit the solemn and uplifting tone of the night. MJF, on the other hand, represents a completely different dynamic for the company.

Bryan Danielson wrestling and appearing makes sense as a way to help the audience feel uplifted in the wake of tragedy and to pay tribute to Pac, Johnson stated. MJF, who is supposed to be the most rotten, obnoxious jerk in the history of pro wrestling, to me, does not make the same sort of sense.

MJF has remained off AEW television since the All In pay-per-view event, and he has already indicated that he is simply waiting for the right moment to make his official return. The bottom line is that AEW chose to stick to that long-term plan rather than disrupting it to feature him in a one-off cameo during one of the most emotional shows in the promotion's history.

Jeff Jarrett says Kazuchika Okada needed character evolution for American TV

AEW performer and former owner of TNA Wrestling Jeff Jarrett recently discussed Kazuchika Okada and the changes he has made to be successful on US television.

Speaking on his My World podcast, Jarrett explained why Okada needed to add to and evolve his on-screen character, beyond just being a fantastic pro wrestler, to really get over in the United States. Jarrett noted that Okada is the most decorated wrestler in modern times, acknowledging that while Hiroshi Tanahashi is up there, Okada is right at the tippy-tippy top. He stated that it is obviously a Japanese style that has carried him here, but in America, you are going to have to develop and evolve the character.

Jarrett would go on to praise Okada for how he has adapted to a US television presentation, noting how he interacted with the crowd during his match with Tommaso Ciampa. When he shot the double bird, that look right there, Jarrett pointed out, is the furthest thing from the most highly decorated Japanese wrestler of all time, joking about him flipping off a ten-year-old girl in front of her mother.

Jarrett emphasized that he is entirely here for it because it creates another extension of Okada's actual personality. When you know him, Jarrett explained, he is a nice dude, he is fun-loving, and he likes to joke around. While he will put on his game face with anybody, being able to transition in a match like that gives, as they say, a Hollywood depth to the character, and Jarrett expressed that he loves it.

Will Ospreay recently celebrated the longevity of his corporate relationship with the Assassin's Creed franchise, noting that the company has invested heavily in him over the past three years.

Unlike traditional wrestling crossovers where a game sponsors an entire match or pay-per-view event, such as past video game integrations seen in WWE and AEW, this partnership belongs exclusively to Ospreay and manifests organically through his gear, finishing move, and custom entrances.

Speaking about the future of the collaboration, Ospreay revealed his desire to take the partnership to the next level by appearing as a character via a game cameo, inspiring downloadable content featuring his ring gear, or transitioning into acting for a franchise movie or television series.

🔄 המשך Status and future of United Empire faction in AEW

Will Ospreay has wasted no time expanding the American branch of the United Empire since bringing the group over to AEW. Formed on the September 2 episode of Dynamite, the stable has quickly grown to include Andrade El Idolo, Mike Bailey, and Alex Windsor and Jamie Hayter. Bailey was brought into the fold later in September, while Windsor and Hayter joined the ranks at All Out.

This rapid expansion has not gone unnoticed across the Pacific. Callum Newman has voiced his displeasure over Ospreay's recent recruitment choices, showing visible frustration on social media regarding the additions of Bailey and Francesco Akira. Despite the brewing friction and a distinct lack of communication between Ospreay and the Japan-based members regarding these new decisions, Ospreay maintains that the group functions as a united front, leaving the door open for a future cross-continent reconciliation when a larger threat eventually looms.

Looking ahead, Ospreay is expected to recruit even more talent to solidify the faction as a primary counterpoint to the Death Riders in AEW. Potential future members include the Grizzled Young Veterans duo of Zack Gibson and James Drake, who have been searching for a definitive direction since arriving in the company. Other candidates to bolster the ranks include Kip Sabian alongside Penelope Ford, as well as young star Nick Wayne following his recent tour of Japan.

🔄 המשך Eric Bischoff advises Tony Khan on pitching long-term plans to network executives

Expanding on his previous advice regarding upcoming television negotiations, Eric Bischoff detailed exactly how AEW President Tony Khan should structure his approach when meeting with new network decision-makers. Speaking on his 83 Weeks podcast, Bischoff framed the advice around preparing for potential changes in corporate management.

Bischoff stressed that Khan needs to walk into any initial executive meeting armed with a believable, plausible three-year blueprint for growing the brand beyond its current ceiling. He cautioned that this strategy cannot simply cater to the vocal online wrestling community, arguing that satisfying a niche internet audience will not sustain long-term growth.

According to Bischoff, storytelling must be central to that presentation. He suggested that Khan should enlist someone with genuine professional wrestling storytelling experience to help craft a viable approach, rather than relying solely on his own creative instincts.

While acknowledging that exceptionally strong ratings could alter the conversation, Bischoff urged Khan to be proactive rather than reactive. He advised putting the plan together and requesting a meeting ahead of time to stay in control of the narrative, regardless of whether the network's current data looks favorable or problematic.

🔄 המשך Bryan Danielson’s next match in AEW – Potential opponents for the former world champion following his return on the Dynamite: A Tribute to PAC show

Following his multi-man tag team victory alongside the Death Riders on the tribute show, speculation has naturally turned toward what lies ahead for Danielson should he decide to wrestle again. While he considers himself retired from a full-time schedule, these special appearances leave the door open for high-profile matches.

One major possibility is a future program with Hangman Adam Page. Danielson previously named Page as one of his wrestling soulmates, and a potential encounter could happen in singles action or even the trios division, given Danielson's renewed alignment with the Death Riders and Page currently holding championship gold.

Another fascinating direction would be a rematch against Jon Moxley. Unlike their brutal WrestleDream encounter where Moxley ousted Danielson, a future bout between the former stablemates could feature a friendlier competitive dynamic, potentially with Danielson challenging for Moxley's Continental Championship.

Finally, a chaotic Casino Gauntlet match offers a fun setting for a one-off Danielson appearance. The stipulation relies on surprise entrants and multi-person action, making it an ideal environment for the legend to mix it up with a wide cross-section of the roster without needing to commit to a grueling full-time storyline.
