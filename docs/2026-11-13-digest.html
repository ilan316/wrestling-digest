🔥 Christian Cage injury recovery update from Tony Khan

During a media call to promote the Full Gear pay-per-view event, AEW President Tony Khan addressed the status of Christian Cage, who has been sidelined since suffering severe injuries during a three-way ladder match at All Out. Cage's injuries first came to light on an episode of AEW Dynamite when Adam Copeland revealed that his partner was thrown into a ladder roughly five minutes into the match. The impact resulted in a collapsed lung and three broken ribs, with one rib fractured in three distinct places. Despite the immense damage, Cage managed to finish the match before beginning his lengthy recovery process.

Khan offered insight into Cage's current condition, explaining that the veteran is facing a difficult recovery path. While he could not provide a confirmed return date, Khan emphasized his optimism that Cage will eventually make his way back to AEW television. Calling Cage one of the most important people in the history of the company, Khan praised him both as an all-time favorite professional wrestler and as a phenomenal person outside of the ring. Khan joked about Cage's villainous television persona and his unique sense of humor, noting that while he does not always come across as the nicest guy, he is an incredible human being whom everyone in the promotion is supporting.

Beyond the update on Christian Cage, Khan touched on several other roster and backstage topics during the call. Former WWE ID prospect Marcus Mathers was discussed following his ROH match against Mark Davis and his subsequent AEW Collision debut in a loss to Nick Wayne. After arriving on Chris Jericho's cruise looking for work, Mathers was recommended for an ROH opportunity by Christopher Daniels, and Khan noted that fans should expect to see more of him in both AEW and ROH moving forward.

Khan also explained the reasoning behind the sudden name change for Jay White's group to The Cut. Aiming to try something fresh with a stable capable of threatening top groups like the Death Riders and The Conglomeration, Khan had a meeting with the group members. During the discussion, the wrestlers described themselves as the last outfit of an operation, referencing their past with Bullet Club Gold. Khan felt a key piece was still missing and suggested the name The Cut, a change that Jay White immediately agreed to implement that same day.

Additionally, Khan reflected on Claudio Castagnoli's arrival in AEW and his involvement with the Blackpool Combat Club. Khan revealed that he personally suggested Castagnoli join the faction when it was first established, praising his leadership and deep passion for professional wrestling. Castagnoli arrived during a particularly challenging period for the company that was plagued by major injuries, including one to Bryan Danielson, making his presence invaluable to the roster. The media call concluded with brief discussions regarding AEW's upcoming television negotiations, past issues with New Japan Pro-Wrestling, and the uncertain future of the Wrestle Dynasty pay-per-view event.

🔥 AEW forced to make last-minute changes to combined Dynamite/Collision taping

To the naked eye, the double taping of AEW Dynamite and Collision in Utah went off without a hitch, but behind the scenes, the company faced major logistical challenges. The special broadcast took place in West Valley City, Utah, just days after several performers participated in Chris Jericho's Rock 'N' Wrestling Rager at Sea cruise. The November 7 episode of Collision had aired live from aboard the Norwegian Jewel while the ship was docked in Miami, Florida. With the cruise concluding shortly before the television taping, getting the necessary talent from Miami to Utah became a major hurdle.

Speaking on Wrestling Observer Radio, it was revealed that AEW was forced to rearrange portions of the broadcast because officials were uncertain which wrestlers would arrive in time. A lot of the show was done on the fly and rearranged because they were having trouble getting the people from docking yesterday to get to Salt Lake City. So they docked in Miami, and they had to get to Salt Lake City, and there were issues in that. The uncertainty continued until shortly before the show, leaving officials with little choice but to adjust plans based on talent availability. The show was rearranged literally at the last minute because they didn't know who was going to make it on time and who wasn't going to make it on time.

Despite the travel concerns, several talents who appeared on the Jericho Cruise were able to make it to the arena, including AEW Women's World Champion Mercedes Mone, Andrade El Idolo, The Cut, the Death Riders, Persephone, FTR, Motor City Machine Guns, Speedball Mike Bailey, AEW National Champion Tommaso Ciampa, Blake Christian, and AEW TBS Champion Queen Aminata. All the matches AEW advertised in the days leading up to the event happened as planned. The combined broadcast, which aired live on TBS and HBO Max from the Maverik Center in West Valley City, was originally promoted as a three-hour special but ultimately ran for more than three and a half hours.

These travel difficulties came at a particularly critical time for the company, as the broadcast served as the final television stop before Full Gear. The pay-per-view is scheduled for Saturday in Phoenix, Arizona, making the combined episode an essential opportunity to build anticipation for the event. While it remains unclear which specific matches or segments were altered due to the delays, the final presentation ultimately differed from what the company had originally planned.

🔥 Tony Khan discusses early AEW/NJPW relationship and Wrestle Dynasty future

During the media call ahead of AEW Full Gear, AEW President Tony Khan discussed the history of the working relationship between his promotion and New Japan Pro-Wrestling, while also shedding light on the future of the collaborative Wrestle Dynasty event.

Reflecting on the early days of AEW, Khan acknowledged that the two companies did not always have the best relationship. He described the period between 2019 and 2020 as a bit of a cold war between them. Despite the initial tension, some connections existed through performers like Jon Moxley and Chris Jericho who worked for both promotions. The relationship began to significantly improve in 2021 after Khan developed stronger ties with NJPW officials Rocky Romero and Gedo. Those discussions culminated in a formal working agreement and paved the way for the inaugural AEW and NJPW Forbidden Door pay-per-view in 2022. Fast-forward to the present, and Khan now considers the relationship a great partnership.

The conversation then shifted toward NJPW events at the Tokyo Dome. Khan expressed that he is thrilled to contribute top-tier talent to help NJPW put together a strong show, noting that stars like Jon Moxley, Kenny Omega, and MJF are expected to appear.

When asked about the future of Wrestle Dynasty, the collaborative event featuring talent from AEW, NJPW, and other promotions like ROH, CMLL, and Stardom, Khan suggested the concept may not return as a separate show in the immediate future. Having skipped 2026, Khan confirmed it will not happen next year either, meaning the event will go at least two straight years without another edition following its original January 2025 debut.

Instead of a standalone Wrestle Dynasty event, Khan explained that AEW is sending several of its biggest stars directly to Wrestle Kingdom, effectively providing the crossover attraction that fans would ordinarily associate with the separate collaborative show. Looking at what the upcoming Wrestle Kingdom card has become, Khan openly questioned how different it really is from another Wrestle Dynasty, rendering a separate event less urgent.

Offering a colorful analogy regarding NJPW approach to assembling the Tokyo Dome card, Khan noted that they effectively built the plane out of the black box in this case. He praised the decision, emphasizing it is a great call because AEW has the best wrestlers in the whole world. While the long-term future of Wrestle Dynasty beyond 2027 remains unclear, the heavy integration of AEW talent into Wrestle Kingdom ensures fans will still get the cross-promotional dream matches they desire.

Marina Shafir credits Jon Moxley for personal AEW moment

Marina Shafir has found a new side of herself since joining forces with Jon Moxley in AEW. Moreover, one piece of advice from her Death Riders teammate has also changed how she approaches pro wrestling. Since August 2024, Shafir has been aligned with Moxley, and she was technically the first member of the Death Riders group. Now, Shafir is preparing for the biggest match of her career as she challenges Mercedes Mone for the AEW Women Championship at Full Gear. Ahead of the event, she opened up about Moxley and how his guidance helped her find more enjoyment in wrestling.

Speaking about how Moxley encouraged her to have fun during AEW matches, Shafir revealed in an interview that Moxley always pushed her to enjoy herself, though he also made it clear that enjoying the moment does not always come naturally. Simply, youre supposed to have fun, and sometimes in the moment you have to work to have fun, and it may not happen imminently, Shafir explained. It may not happen immediately. It may not happen within however much time frame you think it should happen. But like having fun is the goal, because if youre having fun, that resonates. However it is you have fun. Maybe its silently. Maybe its conniving. I dont want to say like kind of like in a criminal type of way, but outlaws have fun, right? They just have their own kind of fun, and that was the biggest piece of advice that he gave me from the beginning, and it helped me relax.

The advice helped her relax and become more open to different possibilities in her career. It helped me open up my mind to literally all the possibilities that have led up until this point, and then it opened up my mind for the future because thats what were always like aiming for, the unknown, Shafir added. I like not knowing what the fuck is going on. Ive become very addicted to not knowing what is going on. I dont complain about not knowing what the fuck is going on because I love surprises, and like Ive said before, in professional wrestling, its one of lifes greatest surprises if you choose to not be a fucking know-it-all.

When asked if there was a particular moment when she truly understood what Moxley meant, she pointed to Anarchy in the Arena. The match took place at Double or Nothing 2025, where she competed alongside the Death Riders. One key highlight for Shafir was getting to use a frying pan during the match, but the weapon held a much deeper meaning for her. She revealed that using the frying pan was a way to pay tribute to her mother and her familys journey to the United States.

Anarchy in the Arena was really the first time when I got to throw down with a frying pan, Shafir explained. That was very fun for me. I got to pay respect to my mom. I wanted to let her know that a lot came through a pan. My parents came here with fucking nothing but a cast-iron pan. And like Im not lying. Like when we immigrated here, we could only take immediate things, and one of the things that she packed was her cast-iron pan. She still has it, and I just wanted to pay respect and let her know that her decision was not for nothing. And that was fun for me.

Moxley will once again compete in Anarchy in the Arena at Full Gear. This time, the match will be part of an event where Shafir also has a major opportunity to capture championship gold.

Tony Khan explains reason behind quick Jay White faction name change

Tony Khan has finally explained how Jay White’s new faction managed to get a new name almost as quickly as the first one was announced. According to Khan, AEW wasn’t frantically changing things because everyone online hated The Last Outfit.

During the AEW Full Gear media call, Khan was asked about the lightning-fast switch from The Last Outfit to The Cut. Khan said he’s trying something different with White’s crew and believes they can become a threat to pretty much anybody in AEW, whether that’s the Death Riders, The Conglomeration, or somebody else.

Khan said he had a specific vision for the group and eventually sat down with everyone involved to talk it through. The wrestlers were apparently pretty excited about the idea, and during that conversation, the phrase the last outfit came up as a way of describing the group as the last outfit of an operation. That phrase eventually made on-air television when White introduced his crew on the October 24 episode of Collision.

Almost immediately, fans started roasting the name online. But Khan says the backlash wasn’t what caused him to pull the plug. He felt fans actually liked the angle itself, but something about the group was still missing. That’s when The Cut entered the picture. Khan brought the new name to White, White liked it, and that was basically that. The decision was made that same day, which explains why fans barely had time to get used to The Last Outfit before AEW was already calling them something else.

The name may have changed at record speed, but Khan clearly isn’t treating the faction itself like some throwaway experiment. With White, Juice Robinson, Ace Austin, Austin Gunn, Colten Gunn, David Finlay, and Clark Connors involved, AEW has assembled a ridiculous amount of Bullet Club history in one place, and Khan sounds like he expects them to cause some serious problems. Do you buy Tony Khan’s explanation for the sudden name change, or do you think the fan reaction played a part?

Darby Allin explains why he values TNT Championship over AEW World Title

AEW TNT Champion Darby Allin believes that his current title holds more personal value to him than the promotion's top prize, the AEW World Championship. Allin is currently in his third reign as TNT Champion, having first won the title back in 2020. He recently spoke to Going Ringside, where he discussed the championship's importance to him and explained why winning the TNT title felt more significant than winning the World Championship.

Honestly, I'll be real, the first time I really found my footing in this company was during the pandemic when I became TNT Champion, Allin said. So, this championship means more to me than when I won the World Championship. I felt more winning this than winning that. And I think it's up to me, as the performer that I am, to make this TNT Championship mean more than the World Championship because to me, it's just, it's like what you do with it. There's champions that can sit and do nothing, or there's champions that can do everything. So, that's kind of my whole MO with this title.

Allin, who has often battled against the odds to achieve his goals, views the TNT Championship as AEW's underdog title, which makes it even more special to him. It's kind of the underdog title, per se, you know, in the grand scheme of everything, but that's what I live by. So, yeah, I feel a lot more passionate about this, he concluded.

His world title reign lasted just 39 days, winning it from MJF and losing it to him again, but he seemed to thrive under the pressure of being champion. However, Allin couldn't quite enjoy winning the World Championship, as he was drained of all his energy after his grueling match with MJF, which typified him as a wrestler who gives everything to overcome the odds.
