Marina Shafir cuts post-Collision promo on Mercedes Moné

Following the latest episode of AEW Collision in Winston Salem, North Carolina, Marina Shafir has a strong message for AEW Women's World Champion Mercedes Mone. Shafir appeared in a backstage promo released by AEW, where she addressed Mone and made it clear that she wants an opportunity to face her in the ring. Shafir opened by explaining that her previous comments about Mone were intended to get the champion's attention, while acknowledging the respect she has for what Mone has accomplished.

Mercedes, we all know you are not a pussy, Shafir said. I just needed to get your full attention. I actually have a lot of respect for you. You are in a league of your own. And as much as it might pain you to think or to even say, you know that I am too.

While Shafir acknowledged Mone's status in the women's division, she also pointed to what she believes separates the two competitors, particularly when it comes to how they present themselves. That is where the difference lies between you and me, Shafir noted. You need to swing your dominance in between your legs. And I don't. I can't even compliment myself.

The Death Riders member continued by emphasizing that she does not need the elaborate presentation or recognition that often accompanies Mone's appearances. Instead, Shafir believes the work she has put in over the past three years should speak for itself. She stated that she does not need bells and whistles, affirmations, a dance, or production, but simply the opportunity to work.

All of the work that I have been doing these last three years has gotten me here, Shafir said. But there is something else that I need. I need to stand across the ring from you. And I want to show everyone exactly who I am, but most importantly, I need to show you.

Earlier in the evening, Shafir helped the Death Riders defeat Shane Taylor Promotions in an eight-person mixed tag team match, forcing Trish Adora to submit to PAC's Brutalizer submission hold. She also participated in the chaotic brawl that closed Collision, involving the Death Riders, the Conglomeration, and Jay White's newly formed faction, The Cut, which had briefly been known as The Last Outfit.

This follows a previous confrontation between Shafir and Mone on AEW Dynamite as Mone was celebrating her AEW Women's World Championship title defense against Thekla at AEW WrestleDream. It remains to be seen whether Shafir's challenge will lead to an AEW Women's World Championship match against Mone.

Paige (Saraya) stranded in Scotland following flight cancelations

Paige is having an absolute nightmare trying to get out of Scotland. Between losing her seat, having her flight canceled, and then finding out the trains weren't running either, the WWE Superstar basically ran out of ways to leave the country.

She said she got up early and made it to the airport, only to discover that her ticket had somehow been given away. As frustrating as that already sounds, losing the seat ultimately didn't matter because the flight wasn't going anywhere anyway. Paige said a storm hitting Scotland led to most flights being canceled as the Scottish storm went off.

Paige wasn't ready to give up and tried coming up with another way home. Her backup plan was to take a seven-hour train to London and catch a flight from there, but that plan died pretty quickly when she found out the trains were canceled too.

At that point, Paige was pretty much stuck. She is now hoping to finally make her escape tomorrow and promised fans an update on whether attempt number two goes any better.

The travel headache comes shortly after Paige's last WWE match on the October 16 episode of SmackDown, where she defeated Nikki Bella. Now her opponent isn't another wrestler, it's apparently the entire Scottish transportation system. Hopefully Paige's next attempt goes a whole lot smoother and she can finally get out of Scotland without another flight or train throwing a wrench into things. After the day she's had, simply getting a seat and actually leaving might feel like a win.

Ringside News

Jeff Jarrett defends making his CMLL debut

AEW and WWE Hall of Famer Jeff Jarrett recently addressed the online criticism surrounding his historic debut for CMLL. For the first time in his legendary career, Jarrett stepped inside a CMLL ring to take on Blue Panther at the promotion's 93rd Aniversario event on September 18. While the live Arena Mexico audience lit up for his appearance, many fans online expressed negative reactions.

On a recent episode of his My World podcast, Jarrett addressed this mixed reaction as part of a larger discussion about his current place in the wrestling business. He noted that he picks his spots here and there. While acknowledging the online hate, he compared it to other controversial moments, such as Tommaso dropping the elbow on Andrade's pants at AEW WrestleDream, noting that such things are not for everybody.

Jarrett emphasized that if you were in the arena at the CMLL anniversary show, the crowd liked his match against Blue Panther. He stated that some people will never be satisfied or say he should never wrestle again, but since the crowd liked it, they can agree to disagree and he will continue to pick his spots. At the event, Blue Panther defeated Jarrett in less than nine minutes. Elsewhere on the card, CMLL World Heavyweight Champion Hechicero successfully defended his title against AEW's Claudio Castagnolo and NJPW's Zack Sabre Jr.

For Jarrett, the event marked his first televised match inside Arena Mexico, making him one of a select few who have performed in Arena Mexico, Madison Square Garden, Wembley Stadium, and the Tokyo Dome. It also served as one of his few in-ring outings in 2026, which he indicated was intentional.

Jarrett explained that he knows his role in today's wrestling landscape, stating he is not an every week Dynamite or Collision guy, but knows without a shadow of a doubt that he can contribute in different spots here and there. He believes he can do a right short term storyline to help elevate a guy if called upon, using his verbal skills, wisdom, and connection with the audience to contribute to a storyline's overall growth. At 59 years of age, he recognizes he is not a main event guy who needs to go every week.

Since joining AEW in 2022, Jarrett has balanced two on-screen roles as an in-ring talent and a broadcaster. His broadcasting role typically involves co-hosting AEW pay-per-view pre-shows, such as the WrestleDream Buy In, alongside RJ City and Renee Paquette. As an in-ring talent, the 40-year veteran has wrestled nearly four dozen matches under the AEW banner.

His latest AEW in-ring appearance prior to the CMLL event took place at the Grand Slam Mexico event on August 5, where he teamed with Brillante Jr., Mascara Dorada, Orange Cassidy, and Roderick Strong to defeat Nick Wayne, The Dogs, and The Swirl in a dark tag team match. Prior to that, Jarrett had not wrestled in an AEW ring since January 2025.

Anthony Bowens reflects on joining AEW and forming The Acclaimed

Anthony Bowens spent his early pro wrestling days like many talents do, grinding on the independent circuit. In 2020, though, he received a big break that landed him on global television. During a recent interview, Bowens looked back on that big break, which centered on him inking a contract with All Elite Wrestling.

I think once AEW opened up, I was like this is the place I probably should be, Bowens said. They were putting the focus on the younger guys. It was a different type of style than WWE. It was an alternative and wrestlers were like more free to perfect their craft and I thought that seems like the place I should go. Then, fortunately, I got a tryout with them in September of 2020, I want to say. One thing led to another and ended up signing in October of 2020, and then had a crazy journey ever since.

Bowens made his AEW debut on the October 6, 2020 edition of AEW Dark, which had been taped on September 25. Weeks later, he and Max Caster officially joined forces to form The Acclaimed, earning their first team victory over Aaron Solow and Angel Fashion on the same brand. The 35-year-old initially agreed to sign with WWE shortly before. After flying down to Florida and meeting with AEW CEO Tony Khan to discuss his potential plans for The Acclaimed, however, he ultimately chose to come aboard AEW instead.

Bowens and Caster became a successful and popular act in AEW, with reigns as World Tag Team and Trios Champions to their names. Surprisingly, they weren't too familiar with one another beforehand. Bowens noted that he trained in Create A Pro in New Jersey while Caster trained in Create A Pro Long Island. He knew him from training, occasionally going out to train a little bit in Long Island, and they had wrestled each other once in a multi-man match, but he did not really know much about him.

When Bowens found out they were going to be paired together, he knew they needed to figure it out quickly because if the owner had an idea and a team name for them, they had to be able to deliver. He recognized they needed to be cordial to each other and learn about each other because they were going to be tethered to one another for a long time, forcing them to become friends real quick.

Tony Khan initially saw the Create A Pro graduates compete separately on AEW Dark, where Bowens teamed with Lee Johnson and Caster had singles and trios performances under his belt. Upon feeling pressure from WWE, Khan then made an enticing offer for them to join forces and be regularly featured on AEW programming. Years later, Bowens and Caster are now both free agents once again. Bowens post-AEW moves remain unknown, while Caster has taken a step back from in-ring competition in order to undergo therapy.

Samoa Joe names his hardest-hitting opponent

Throughout his lengthy career spanning many promotions, from Ring of Honor, to TNA, to WWE, and now, AEW, Samoa Joe has faced off against many hard-hitting opponents. The star, recently returned to AEW with his sights set on Will Ospreay's AEW World Championship, sat down with SHAK Wrestling and played a game to determine just who hit him hardest in the ring.

He was given two names to start with, and the winner stayed on. Joe was first tasked with choosing between Brock Lesnar and Kurt Angle. Kurt, I think it's the battle of technique vs. raw, unbridled power, Joe said. I think they both, they have great technique. They both have their own kind of reserved power, but I think when you talk about the balance, I think Brock hits a little bit harder. Kurt, maybe a little bit more technical prowess.

Lesnar beat out Shinsuke Nakamura, Bryan Danielson, and Claudio Castagnoli for Joe, though he had to think a bit when comparing Lesnar to Castagnoli. He said The Beast Incarnate also hit harder than Nigel McGuiness and Necro Butcher, though Butcher was another name Joe to think about for a moment. Minoru Suzuki was also beaten out by Lesnar, as well as Low Ki, with some further contemplation that Joe called the choice of a generation.

We're going to go destructive potential, but extreme enthusiasm, and effort and a very, very close call, but we're going to stick with Brock, Joe said according to SHAK Wrestling, with a transcription provided by Wrestling Inc.

Finally, when Lesnar was compared to Kenta Kobashi, Joe once again choose Lesnar. He said he faced off against an older Kobashi, who was a touch less explosive, and named The Beast his hardest-hitting opponent.

Jeff Jarrett wishes he had a rematch with specific WWE Hall of Famer

During a recent episode of My World with Jeff Jarrett, the veteran star discussed his illustrious career working in WCW, the then-WWF during the Monday Night Wars, and creating his own promotion with TNA. I didnt get to do it! I killed it! Jarrett admitted with a laugh, taking full responsibility for missing out on the opportunity.
