Road Dogg Claims AEW Turned Him Down Over Political Beliefs

Former WWE creative team member Brian "Road Dogg" James claimed on social media that he was turned away from AEW because of his political beliefs, making the allegation during a contentious exchange that began with his criticism of the AEW Tag Team Championship.

The controversy started when James took to Twitter/X to argue that Austin and Colten Gunn had surpassed the level of AEW's tag team division. He directly called on AEW President Tony Khan to recognize the brothers' potential, writing: "Tag titles are beneath them at this point as is EVERY OTHER TEAM and I mean EVERY TEAM AEW @TonyKhan I heard you didn't sleep…… you're sleeping on these boys #OUDK #SUCKIT #Nothing2lose"

Wrestling Observer's Dave Meltzer responded by disagreeing with James' characterization of the AEW tag team titles. Meltzer pointed to the caliber of wrestlers who have held AEW tag championships throughout its history: "I will say this. For all the people who championed him coming to AEW, saying the tag titles are 'beneath' Austin & Colten Gunn shows an lack of understanding of AEW tag titles. They weren't beneath Omega & Page, nor Christian & Copeland, nor FTR, nor Young Bucks. Saying tag titles are 'beneath' anyone is a fed brain issue. Ospreay has the trios titles in NJPW. Mistico has a tag title in AEW. Don't even get me started on history like Funks or Stevens & Patterson."

James responded sarcastically, firing back at Meltzer: "My bad Dave, I forgot, when it comes to wrestling, ONLY your opinion matters 5 star tweet sir! #GOTME #OUCH #SUCKIT"

The exchange then took a significant turn when a fan suggested to James that publicly arguing with someone associated with AEW would not help his chances of getting hired there. The fan wrote: "Sir, if you really want a job in AEW you shouldn't be publicly arguing with their executive vice president of Communications like this!!"

It was at that moment that James made his major claim, stating: "Actually, I was turned away because of my political/ core beliefs That happened IN AMERICA I didn't donate to or vote for Trump, BUT his old man did, why was I punished?"

James did not provide specific details about when he was allegedly turned away, who made the decision, or the exact circumstances. The claim represents solely his account, with no supporting evidence or response from AEW provided.

James had publicly lobbied for Tony Khan to hire him in 2022 after being released from his behind-the-scenes role with WWE. That opportunity never materialized, and James subsequently returned to WWE, where he served as co-lead writer of SmackDown before departing earlier this year.

Beyond his allegation about AEW, James also directed attention toward journalist David Bixenspan, claiming the reporter was responsible for his opportunity falling through. He wrote: "This is the guy that screenshot my social media Post (I am conservative) and then used it to get my opportunity canceled. I feel differently than a lot of you. Im not sorry for who I am and how I was raised."

When Bixenspan responded that he did not know what James was referring to, James walked back the specific accusation, writing: "My bad David. I guess there's another David B out there doing this." However, in another message, James doubled down, telling Bixenspan: "Do you want to know David, cuz you're the one that reported it all. Tell us all right now David you can't hurt me dude, I'm not scared of you and your social warrior friends. I'm unemployed and living my life. Show the world why David and I'll live on my side."

It should be noted that Shad Khan, the father of AEW owner Tony Khan, did donate to Trump's 2017 inauguration. However, Khan has expressed mixed political signals since then, stating he remained a fan of Trump's economic policies while disagreeing with him on social issues including immigration and religion. Khan notably did not donate to Trump's 2025 inauguration. Additionally, several people currently employed at AEW, including recently re-signed World Champion Chris Jericho, likely share similar political beliefs to those James has expressed, raising questions about whether political beliefs alone would be grounds for rejecting him.

Some observers have pointed out that James did not leave his last WWE position in particularly favorable circumstances, suggesting that Tony Khan's reluctance to hire him may stem from professional rather than political considerations.

James' current employment status remains uncertain. He was expected to join TNA's creative team earlier in the year but those negotiations reportedly stalled by the end of July. On August 19, James tweeted that he remains "unemployed and living my life."

🔥 Ricochet Fires Back at Paul Heyman

During the August 17, 2026 edition of WWE Raw, a confrontation between Bron Breakker and Oba Femi was interrupted by Paul Heyman, who is Breakker's manager. Breakker appeared ready to fight Femi immediately, but Heyman stepped in to prevent the altercation. In addressing Breakker, Heyman emphasized WWE's status as a publicly traded company with obligations beyond its immediate audience.

"This is a publicly traded company. This is the biggest promotion on the face of the planet. We don't just answer to the people live and to people at home. We answer to shareholders," Heyman said during the segment.

Heyman's remarks caught the attention of Ricochet, the former WWE star who currently performs for AEW. On August 19, 2026, Ricochet responded via Twitter/X with a post that directly contrasted AEW's approach to business with WWE's shareholder-focused model.

"We're the Greatest Wrestling company in the world. So no we don't answer to 'Share Holders' we answer to OUR fans. Because they're the ones who deserve it," Ricochet wrote.

The response quickly generated discussion among wrestling fans on social media. When one fan suggested that Ricochet would eventually "come crawling back" to WWE, the AEW star fired back with a blunt response, telling the user, "You just keep following like a good little mark ass."

Ricochet spent six years with WWE before departing the company in 2024 and joining AEW later that year. Since making the move, he has occasionally been outspoken about his former promotion and the differences between WWE and AEW's operational philosophies.

Some observers noted that Heyman's promo also contained a line about not doing "impromptu matches" to decide the future, which some interpreted as a jab at AEW's strategy of occasionally putting major clashes on television rather than pay-per-view events. However, Ricochet chose to focus his response on the shareholders comment rather than addressing that aspect of the promo.

The exchange adds another layer to the ongoing discussion within the wrestling community about the fundamental differences between WWE and AEW. While both companies operate in the professional wrestling industry, their business structures and stated priorities appear to differ significantly. WWE, as a publicly traded company under parent corporation TKO, must consider the interests of shareholders and stockholders in its decision-making process. AEW, by contrast, frames its approach as being primarily driven by pleasing its fanbase.

The philosophical disagreement highlighted by this exchange touches on broader questions about how wrestling promotions should balance fan satisfaction with business obligations. In a well-functioning market, these priorities would theoretically align, with shareholder value being built on strong fan engagement and customer satisfaction. However, the tension between short-term profit maximization and long-term fan loyalty has been a point of contention among wrestling observers.

Meanwhile, on WWE Raw, the storyline between Breakker and Femi continues to escalate. Recent episodes have shown Heyman warming up to Femi, who is being positioned as the next big star in WWE. This has apparently frustrated Breakker, who had envisioned himself in that role. Heyman used the August 17 segment to announce that a match between Bron Breakker and Oba Femi would take place at Sunday Night's Main Event on September 6, setting up what is expected to be a significant confrontation between the two competitors.

Ricochet's comments add context to the broader tribalism that exists between sections of the WWE and AEW fanbases, with the former WWE Intercontinental Champion making it clear where his current allegiance lies.

New attendance figures released by the Las Vegas Stadium Authority have provided clarity on WrestleMania 42's draw at Allegiant Stadium on April 18-19, 2026. The year-over-year comparison reveals that WrestleMania 42 saw a drop of approximately 21,515 attendees, representing roughly an 18% decline from 2025 to 2026.

The Stadium Authority figures differ from other attendance measurements for the event. WWE announced a combined attendance of 106,071 across the two nights, calling WrestleMania 42 "one of the highest-grossing events in company history." WrestleTix estimated that 102,092 tickets were distributed across the weekend. While all three measurements show different totals, they consistently point to the same conclusion: attendance was down between 15% and 18% compared to the previous year.

The discrepancy between the Stadium Authority's official count of 97,126 and WWE's announced figure of 106,071 raises questions about how the company calculates attendance. In years past, WWE has been rumored to inflate its numbers by including anyone in the venue, such as staff members and other personnel. The exact methodology used by the Stadium Authority remains unclear regarding whether it represents tickets sold, tickets distributed, or actual tickets used.

However, the year-over-year comparison presents a more difficult metric to dismiss. WWE's live-events revenue was down 33.7 million dollars from the previous year's WrestleMania quarter, with company financial reports attributing the difference almost exclusively to WrestleMania 42 in Las Vegas. This financial decline provides additional support for the attendance drop indicated by multiple independent sources.

WWE President Nick Khan acknowledged the attendance decline and its likely cause during subsequent interviews. Khan revealed that the decision to hold WrestleMania in Las Vegas for a second consecutive year was ultimately his choice. WRESTLING COMMENTARY AND ANALYSIS

Wrestling persists as one of the most accessible and emotionally resonant forms of entertainment available to audiences worldwide. For many fans, the appeal transcends the theatrical presentation and extends into deeply personal territory, where narratives crafted by wrestlers and writers become mirrors for viewers' own experiences. The affordability of modern wrestling content, particularly through streaming services and YouTube archives, has democratized access to the sport in ways previous generations could never have imagined. Where fans once paid significant sums for bootleg recordings of international broadcasts, they now enjoy unlimited catalogs of historical material for minimal monthly costs.

The emotional authenticity of wrestling storytelling represents a unique phenomenon in entertainment. Unlike heavily scripted dramatic productions or sports governed purely by athletic competition, wrestling exists in a liminal space where emotional truth and predetermined outcomes merge. A single comeback moment or title victory can carry profound significance for viewers processing their own grief, loss, or transformation. When figures like Cody Rhodes return to major promotions and pursue narrative arcs involving family legacy and personal redemption, these stories resonate because wrestlers themselves are often drawing from genuine life experiences and losses. The physical vulnerability of performers, combined with the vulnerability of emotional exposition through promos, creates an intimacy that transcends the theatrical scaffolding.

This sense of community unity among wrestling fans remains foundational to the medium's longevity, despite contemporary online discourse often becoming hostile and divisive. Wrestling fandom has historically functioned as a club for those who understand and appreciate the artistry behind what casual observers dismiss as mere entertainment. The tribalism inherent in wrestling programming, with its heroes versus villains and competing promotions, translates into passionate fandom debates that should ideally remain playful rather than vicious. When fans disagree about the merits of different wrestling organizations or performers, these disagreements reflect genuine investment in the medium rather than casual disinterest.

The historical record of wrestling demonstrates consistent excellence across multiple eras and styles. August 19th alone reveals a remarkable lineage of significant matches and title changes spanning decades. The 1987 multi-team main event in New Japan, the 2001 SummerSlam card featuring Kurt Angle versus Stone Cold Steve Austin, and the 2017 NXT TakeOver card showcasing Asuka versus Ember Moon all represent peak wrestling performance. These matches, rated with critical acclaim by industry observers, showcase wrestling's technical and narrative sophistication. The 2001 SummerSlam in particular served as a reprieve during the Invasion angle, which was generally poorly received overall. That August 19th event drew 15,293 fans to the Compaq Center in San Jose, with Kurt Angle and Stone Cold Steve Austin delivering what many consider a near match-of-the-year performance.

The breadth of title changes on August 19th across different promotions and eras underscores wrestling's global presence and consistent productivity. The Rock's WCW World Heavyweight Championship victory, Edge's WWF Intercontinental Championship win, Rob Van Dam's WWF Hardcore Championship ascension, and X-Pac's WWF World Light Heavyweight Championship triumph all occurred on August 19, 2001, during a single SummerSlam event. The Asuka versus Ember Moon match from 2017 received four and a half stars and is considered among the three best women's matches ever performed in the United States. Asuka's technical execution, incorporating hammerlock transitions, STOs, octopus holds, and Fujiwara armbars, demonstrated the sophistication modern women's wrestling had achieved.

However, the legacy of wrestling includes tragic dimensions that some veterans prefer not to revisit through documentary examination.
