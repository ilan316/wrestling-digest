🔥 Anthony Henry to undergo neck surgery and face months of recovery

AEW and Ring of Honor competitor Anthony Henry is facing a significant period on the sidelines after revealing that his neck is kinda screwed and requires immediate surgery.

Henry, one half of the WorkHorsemen alongside tag team partner JD Drake, took to social media to explain his absence from the ring since July. He provided an update to his fans regarding the severity of his condition.

As Henry explained, his current situation is particularly alarming because the herniated disc is located directly on the spinal cord itself. Due to this delicate placement, the 42-year-old wrestler is staring at a recovery window of anywhere from three to six months following the impending procedure.

Despite the daunting diagnosis and a challenging run with injuries in recent years—which previously included a broken jaw and a bicep tear that required surgeries in 2025 and 2026—Henry is maintaining a positive outlook and has firmly shut down any talk of retirement.

Expressing gratitude toward AEW and the medical staff for taking good care of him, Henry stated that he plans on returning as a new, better, stronger, and more motivated version of himself. He added that he cannot wait to be back in an AEW ring as soon as possible once he is fully healed.

With the primary focus now on safely navigating the surgery and the subsequent three to six month rehabilitation process, the wrestling community eagerly awaits further updates on Henry's road to recovery.

Chris Jericho says AEW no longer needs to actively recruit wrestlers

Chris Jericho believes All Elite Wrestling has established itself to the point where the promotion no longer needs to actively recruit talent. Jericho recalled his personal involvement in recruiting Jon Moxley and PAC when the company was first starting out. While AEW President Tony Khan and The Young Bucks were also reaching out to potential signings, Jericho personally contacted both wrestlers. He noted that PAC, whose real name is Benjamin Satterley, was initially hesitant to make the move because of his commitment and loyalty to Dragon Gate. Jericho respected that decision at the time and encouraged PAC to consider joining AEW whenever he felt ready, assuring him he would love the environment because everyone was there for the right reasons and for the love of wrestling. PAC eventually made the transition and proved to be an amazing addition.

According to Jericho, while early efforts required heavy outreach for a few key names, AEW's reputation has since grown to the point where wrestlers are interested in joining the company organically. He stated that they do not have to recruit anybody anymore because people just want to come after hearing about the promotion.

Jericho also discussed AEW's approach to operating differently from WWE, acknowledging that both major companies have their distinct advantages and disadvantages. Despite occasionally disagreeing with Tony Khan's decisions, Jericho believes AEW has succeeded in maintaining the identity and vision that helped establish the promotion from day one, staying true to what Tony wants even if it drives him a little crazy sometimes.

Jim Ross advises Aleister Black to get promotion promises in writing

Veteran wrestling executive Jim Ross has offered some pointed career advice to Aleister Black, suggesting that the wrestler should ensure any future commitments from a promotion are officially documented in writing. Speaking on his podcast, Ross addressed Black's recent public accounts regarding unfulfilled expectations during his return to WWE. While Ross noted he did not have firsthand knowledge of Black's specific negotiations, he spoke from his vast experience as a former head of talent relations and stated he had no reason to doubt the wrestler's claims. Observers have previously highlighted Ross's broader views regarding Black's future, but these recent remarks specifically address how the athlete can protect himself through his next agreement.

According to Ross, the best way to avoid misunderstandings or broken expectations is to demand that verbal agreements become binding parts of the legal agreement. He pointed out how simple it is to protect oneself by adding a clause specifying guarantees. As Ross explained, you can easily add a little addendum to a contract and say that the company hereby guarantees the following for said client. By doing so, any potential disputes immediately become a non-issue because you got it on paper, operating under a completely different set of rules than verbal assurances.

Beyond contract negotiations, Ross also advised Black to distance himself from any ongoing controversies. He emphasized that the wrestler needs to get away from all the drama because nobody wants to hire somebody that they perceive might be a headache. This advice comes on the heels of earlier remarks where Ross praised Black's considerable in-ring ability and striking physical appearance. Expressing high confidence in the star's marketability, Ross concluded that he fully expects Black to secure plenty of new opportunities and find more work in the wrestling industry.

AEW Dynamite October 6 viewership numbers

AEW Dynamite experienced a massive drop in viewership for its October 6, 2026 episode, according to data from Wrestlenomics. The show drew 495,000 viewers on TBS and secured a 0.11 rating in the key P18 to 49 demographic.

For comparison, the September 30, 2026 episode performed significantly better, drawing 899,000 viewers with a 0.22 rating in the same key demographic. This marks a sharp decrease of 404,000 total viewers and a drop of 0.11 in the demo rating week-over-week.

However, looking at the wider annual picture, the show fared better than it did during the same timeframe in the previous year. AEW Dynamite drew 321,000 viewers and a 0.07 demo rating on October 7, 2025. When compared to the 495,000 viewers and 0.11 demo rating recorded on October 6, 2026, that represents a year-over-year increase of 54.2 percent in viewership and a 57.1 percent jump in the demographic rating.

These figures reflect only linear TV viewership on TBS and do not include streaming or digital viewership. Coverage will continue to monitor AEW Dynamite as the company builds toward its next major live event.

Comparing WWE's product style to AEW's target audience

Conrad Thompson made the news again when he said in an interview with Ariel Helwani that AEW is making wrestling for fans and WWE is making wrestling for investors. This is not the first time this quote has appeared in the news, as others working for AEW such as Ricochet and even Tony Khan have said versions of that quote from time to time. This sparked a larger question about whether this quote is an accurate representation of the industry or simply a marketing tool designed to attract more fans to AEW. While there is some truth to the statement, the reality is not as black and white as Thompson and others make it seem, which can be effectively illustrated by comparing both products to movie genres.

When watching WWE, the product feels similar to the Marvel Cinematic Universe and other movies in the superhero genre. The product is made for the general audience that just wants to have a good time. When it works, it makes a lot of money, and when it doesn't, it can become a major flop. Fans of the WWE product generally do not care how many stars a match receives or what those wrestlers did before joining WWE. They just want to have fun and be entertained for a few hours, and that is exactly what WWE provides. This approach is also good for shareholders because the more fans who enjoy the product and spend money on it, the more profitable the company becomes, benefiting the shareholders in the process. This dynamic is similar to how a movie like Avengers Endgame makes a lot of money, benefiting Disney financially and pleasing its shareholders.

On the other side, AEW is similar to the independent horror genre. Those types of movies are primarily made for horror fans and rarely attract audiences outside of that fandom, though occasional crossover hits like Terrifier 3, The Backrooms, or Obsession break into the mainstream. AEW operates in a similar way as a company primarily made for the hardcore wrestling fanbase, often referred to as the Meltzer crowd. These are fans who love watching pretty much anything wrestling related, know everything happening in Japan, Mexico, and other countries, and want to see high impact wrestling. They generally care less about character development and storytelling and more about the wrestling itself, much like how the horror genre focuses more on scares and big set pieces than deep story. While there is nothing inherently wrong with that approach, it may not work as well if the goal is to continuously grow the broader audience, since AEW is primarily booking for its existing hardcore fanbase rather than trying to appeal to casual viewers.

In the end, both companies are actually made for the fans, just different subsets of fans. WWE is mainly targeting casual fans who just want to have a good time and do not want to feel like they need to do homework before watching a wrestling show. AEW is primarily targeting the hardcore wrestling fan who loves independent and international wrestling and places a greater emphasis on in ring work. That division is completely acceptable. It is not a bad thing for WWE to care about giving casual fans a product they can enjoy. Furthermore, the Meltzer crowd tends to generalize too much because while AEW is clearly made with a certain portion of the wrestling fanbase in mind, not everyone in the wrestling fandom likes AEW's product, just like not everyone likes WWE's product. The fun thing about being a wrestling fan is that everyone can like different things, and the fact that WWE is a publicly traded company does not mean the product is being booked solely for shareholders.
