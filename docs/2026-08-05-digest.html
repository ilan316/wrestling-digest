AEW Star MJF Continues Hollywood Expansion With Violent Night 2 Appearance

Professional wrestler MJF is expanding his acting portfolio with a significant role in the upcoming action-comedy sequel Violent Night 2. The official trailer for the film, produced by Universal Pictures, dropped recently and marks the first official look at the AEW star in the movie. The sequel is scheduled to hit theaters on December 4.

In the trailer, MJF makes a strong impression as one of the film's primary antagonists. The AEW star appears in two major moments that showcase his villainous character. In one scene, he is shown putting someone inside an oven, a moment that catches the attention of David Harbour's Santa Claus character, who jokes that the person looks like a gingerbread man. Later in the trailer, MJF comes face-to-face with Santa during an intense fight sequence, attacking the character with a large steel mace. However, the action is interrupted when Kristen Bell's character steps in and catches the weapon during the chaos.

Universal Studios has not yet revealed extensive details about MJF's specific character in the film. However, based on the trailer footage, it is clear that he will be one of the main problems Santa has to deal with throughout the movie. MJF was first announced as part of the Violent Night 2 cast in September 2025, and the role represents another feather in the cap of his growing acting career.

The film stars David Harbour reprising his role as Santa Claus, who in this universe is revealed to be an immortal Viking warrior that inspired the legendary Christmas figure. The sequel builds on the story from the original 2022 movie, with Santa having lost his powers and struggling to remember what Christmas is really about. Things become even more complicated when Santa finds himself on his own naughty list. He eventually teams up with a lively mall community that needs his help against an evil gangster played by Jared Harris.

The cast also includes Kristen Bell as Mrs. Claus, Joe Pantoliano, Daniela Melchior, and Andrew Bachelor alongside MJF and Harris. Tommy Wirkola returns as director, with Pat Casey and Josh Miller writing the screenplay.

MJF's involvement in Violent Night 2 continues his trajectory in the entertainment industry beyond professional wrestling. He has already appeared in notable films including The Iron Claw and Happy Gilmore 2, where he portrayed Gordie Gilmore, the son of Adam Sandler's iconic character. On the wrestling side, MJF has been away from AEW programming since dropping the AEW World Championship to Kenny Omega at AEW Dynamite: Beach Break in July.

Chris Jericho AEW philosophy

Chris Jericho believes that throughout AEW's existence, the company has remained singularly focused on itself rather than on WWE's actions or direction. In an interview with SHAK Wrestling, Jericho was asked about the perception that AEW is gaining momentum while WWE faces criticism for its increasingly corporate presentation and heavy advertising. While acknowledging that WWE's approach could be contributing to AEW's upswing, Jericho attributed the company's success primarily to its own efforts, roster quality, and leadership under Tony Khan.

"I think it's the same as always, in that we've never worried about WWE — ever, ever. We've only worried about our own show, our own product, the quality of what we're doing," Jericho stated. He emphasized that AEW's growth is not merely perceived but backed by concrete metrics. "And it's not a perceived upswing. You can see it in the numbers, tickets sold, the ratings. Our ratings, I think, last week were very, very close to the ratings that they were right before HBO Max became a platform for us. So if we're doing those same ratings, plus there's the HBO Max, that would tell me — common sense would say we're doing bigger ratings now than we were doing three or four years ago."

Jericho stressed that AEW's consistent philosophy has been to maintain focus on quality matches, variety, and building its own identity rather than attempting to emulate WWE. "And I think all of that is just because, no matter what's going on around us, we keep our noses to the grindstone and worry about what we're doing and what kind of matches and quality of matches and variety. We've never been a WWE-lite. We've been AEW since day one. That's never changed. And sometimes you like it and sometimes you don't, but we've never changed it."

Earlier in 2026, speculation emerged that Jericho might depart AEW and return to WWE. When asked whether WWE's current landscape influenced his decision, Jericho acknowledged that he weighed his options but ultimately found the choice straightforward. His unique history with AEW made the decision to remain with the company significantly easier than it might have been otherwise.

"Once again, you weigh your options. But I helped start AEW. I had a stake in the game. There's something special about that, you know? I didn't help WWE. I didn't help start New Japan Pro Wrestling or CMLL or ECW or WCW or any of those places that I worked in the past. I helped start AEW," Jericho explained.

Jericho reflected on the risk he took when signing with AEW at the company's 2019 launch. At that time, there were no guarantees of success, and some observers predicted the promotion would fail. The company's subsequent success has become a source of immense pride for Jericho, representing an important addition to his overall legacy. He views his role in founding and helping build AEW as fundamentally different from his work with any other promotion, past or present. Additionally, Jericho expressed pride in a recent match he had with Tommaso Ciampa at AEW Redemption.

Michael Cole WWE career expectations

Michael Cole has disclosed that his legendary WWE career began with modest expectations. During an appearance on What's Your Story? with Stephanie McMahon, Cole explained that he viewed his signing with WWE as a brief stepping stone rather than a long-term commitment.

Cole began exploring opportunities that would offer a more predictable work arrangement and better quality of life for his loved ones.

Cole approached this audition strategically, viewing WWE as a temporary platform to gain valuable television experience. He believed the exposure and professional development he would gain at WWE could eventually lead to another position in broadcast journalism with a more manageable schedule.

Cole himself confirmed his original timeline, stating "I legitimately thought I was coming to WWE for about a year." However, his temporary plan transformed into an unexpected career spanning nearly three decades. The stable, predictable travel schedule that WWE offered proved far more appealing than he had anticipated, ultimately making it possible for him to maintain family stability while advancing his broadcast career.

Today, nearly 30 years after his initial audition, Cole remains with WWE. What was meant to be a one-year engagement became one of the most significant broadcasting careers in professional wrestling history.

🔥 Dory Funk Jr. passes away

WRESTLING WORLD MOURNS DORY FUNK JR.

The professional wrestling community is grieving the loss of one of its most respected and influential figures. Dory Funk Jr., a WWE Hall of Famer and former NWA World Heavyweight Champion, passed away on Tuesday, August 4, 2026, at the age of 85. His wife, Marti Funk, confirmed to the Ocala Gazette that he died under hospice care at their home in Ocala, Florida, where they had lived for decades. No specific cause of death was announced.

Funk's passing marks the end of an era for one of wrestling's most legendary families. He was the son of Dory Funk Sr., a respected wrestler and promoter who operated the Double Cross Ranch, and the older brother of Terry Funk, who passed away nearly three years earlier on August 23, 2023, at age 79. Both Funk brothers achieved tremendous success in professional wrestling, with each holding the NWA World Heavyweight Championship during their respective careers. The entire Funk family is now represented in the WWE Hall of Fame, with Dory and Terry inducted together in 2009 by Dusty Rhodes, and their father Dory Sr. receiving a Legacy induction in 2025.

Tributes poured in immediately from across the wrestling industry following the announcement of Funk's death. Brian Blair, former member of the Killer Bees tag team, was among the first to announce the passing and share his memories. Blair reflected on how his admiration for Funk evolved from childhood fandom into a lasting friendship and mentorship. "It's hard to find the right words to say goodbye to a man who became a mentor, a leader, and a dear friend, Dory Funk Jr.," Blair wrote. "My love for him started all the way back as a kid at the Bayfront Center, booing him as the ultimate heel while he took on my hero Jack Brisco. He was an absolute legend who commanded respect just by being himself. My heart goes out to his wife, Marti, and his family. You will be deeply missed, Dory. I know God is welcoming you with open arms. Rest easy until we meet again."

All major wrestling promotions issued official statements honoring Funk's legacy. WWE released a statement through Chief Content Officer Paul "Triple H" Levesque expressing sadness at Funk's passing and highlighting his immense contributions to professional wrestling. Levesque wrote: "Saddened to announce the passing of Dory Funk, Jr. From the Double Cross Ranch to the WWE Hall of Fame, Dory's style inspired countless performers in our industry. Whether it was at WrestleMania 2, WCW, ECW, or the dozens of NWA-affiliated promotions, his travels across North America and Asia were a part of the early blueprint that helped build this business into a global phenomena. A hard-nosed and fierce competitor, his rugged in-ring style was only eclipsed by the decades he spent training athletes to perform in the squared circle he loved so much. My thoughts and those of the entire WWE Universe are with his loved ones during this difficult time."

AEW, TNA Wrestling, the NWA, and MLW all released statements mourning Funk's passing and acknowledging his lasting impact on professional wrestling. The NWA specifically highlighted his landmark championship reign, noting that on February 11, 1969, Dory Jr. defeated Gene Kiniski to begin his historic run as NWA World Heavyweight Champion. TNA Wrestling acknowledged Funk's involvement with their early events, including their very first event in 2002. MLW described Funk as embodying "the sweet science of pro wrestling, discipline, and tradition that defined generations of professional wrestling," calling him "a true traveling world champion, master grappler, and respected mentor" whose influence extended far beyond the ring.

WWE Hall of Famer and wrestling analyst Dave Meltzer offered extensive commentary on Funk's wrestling career and legacy. "Dory Funk Jr. was such a fantastic world champion for his time," Meltzer wrote. The Cauliflower Alley Club also paid tribute to Funk, calling him "a champion, a teacher, a gentleman, and an ambassador for our great sport."

Funk's professional wrestling career spanned more than six decades, beginning in 1963 when he debuted in his father's Western States Sports promotion in Amarillo, Texas. Born in Indiana and raised in Amarillo, Funk grew up immersed in the wrestling business. After playing football at West Texas State University, he entered professional wrestling and quickly established himself as a talented technician.

On February 11, 1969, Funk captured the NWA World Heavyweight Championship by defeating Gene Kiniski in Tampa, Florida. His reign lasted more than four years, making him the second-longest reigning NWA World Heavyweight Champion in history after Lou Thesz. He held the title until May 1973, when he lost it to Harley Race. His championship era was marked by memorable rivalries, most notably with Jack Brisco, whose matches against Funk became templates for technical, hard-hitting wrestling during the territorial era.

Unlike his younger brother Terry, who became known for wild brawling and unpredictable violence, Dory built his reputation through calculated strikes, disciplined grappling, and technical precision. His in-ring style was rooted in fundamental wrestling, crisp striking, and control rather than chaos or spectacle. This technical approach made him highly respected by wrestlers across the industry and allowed him to make every opponent look credible, a quality that made him an ideal traveling champion for the NWA.

Funk's impact on professional wrestling extended far beyond his NWA championship reign. He became one of the biggest foreign stars in Japanese wrestling history. Working for All Japan Pro Wrestling from 1973 to 1987, again briefly in 1996, and finally from 2013 through 2017, Funk became a legend in Japan. He and his brother Terry teamed together frequently, winning All Japan's major year-end tag tournament three times. Dory also captured the NWA International Heavyweight Championship during his time in Japan. The Funk brothers battled major Japanese and international stars including Abdullah the Butcher, The Original Sheik, Jumbo Tsuruta, Giant Baba, Bruiser Brody, and Jimmy Snuka.

Throughout his career, Funk competed across multiple territories and promotions. He wrestled in Florida, the Mid-Atlantic territory, Memphis, Smoky Mountain Wrestling, Puerto Rico, WCW, and ECW. In the WWF, he was known as "Hoss" Funk during a run in the mid-1980s. He appeared at WrestleMania 2, where he teamed with Terry against Junkyard Dog and Tito Santana. In ECW, he teamed with Terry against The Public Enemy in 1994. Funk also worked for various NWA-affiliated territories throughout his career, becoming a trusted main event attraction across the wrestling landscape.

Funk is widely credited with developing the Texas Cloverleaf submission hold, a move that became associated with him throughout his career. His technical innovations and wrestling philosophy influenced generations of wrestlers who studied his matches and learned from his approach to the sport.

While Funk's in-ring accomplishments secured his legendary status, perhaps his greatest legacy comes from his decades of work as a trainer and mentor. Operating the Funking Conservatory in Ocala, Florida, alongside his wife Marti, whom he married in 1989, Funk helped develop countless future wrestling stars. Among the notable wrestlers trained at the Funking Conservatory are Kurt Angle, Edge, Christian, Lita, Matt Hardy, Jeff Hardy, Mickie James, Ted DiBiase Jr., and Mark Henry. Many of these wrestlers went on to achieve tremendous success in major promotions including WWE and other major wrestling companies. Funk's training emphasizing fundamental wrestling, technical proficiency, and professional conduct helped shape the careers of multiple generations of wrestlers.

Despite retiring from regular competition in 2017, Funk remained active well into his eighties. When NXT held house shows in Ocala, Florida, Funk would appear in a non-wrestling capacity. He was even shown in the crowd at a 2025 edition of WWE Saturday Night's Main Event, demonstrating his continued connection to professional wrestling and the industry. His final match took place on August 24, 2024, when he teamed with Osamu Nishimura to defeat Atsushi Onita and Raijin Yaguchi in a double hell current explosion death match at a memorial event honoring his brother Terry and celebrating Atsushi Onita's 50th anniversary in professional wrestling. At 83 years old, Funk proved that his passion for wrestling remained undiminished even in his later years.

Funk celebrated his 85th birthday in February 2026 at his home in Ocala, Florida, surrounded by his wife and the community that had become his home for decades. The Ocala Gazette reported that a public memorial service will be held to honor Funk on Thanksgiving Day, with a private ceremony for family being held sooner. Details for the public event will be provided at a later date.

Dory Funk Jr. is survived by his wife, Marti Funk. His influence stretched across generations and continents, from his historic championship reign that defined the NWA's territorial era to the countless wrestlers whose careers he shaped and mentored. His technical wrestling philosophy, professional demeanor, and commitment to teaching the next generation of wrestlers created a legacy that will remain woven into the fabric of professional wrestling for decades to come. The wrestling world has lost one of its greatest technicians, champions, and ambassadors.

The physical demands of his Hell in a Cell match against Oba Femi took a visible toll on The Beast Incarnate. "I spent the last two days in bed," Lesnar explained. "I got run over by a Nigerian Nightmare dump truck Saturday night. It's time. I'm 49, and I still wanna be able to get around and move."

Rather than continuing to chase championships, Lesnar's priorities have shifted to his family. "I got some hunting lined up and I got two boys in hockey," he said. "I'm gonna support that, their dreams, as long as that's feasible and my daughter — she's got her eye on 2028 Olympics and just gonna spend some more time with the kids and in the woods and just reap what I've sown, you know? Just enjoy life."

The legendary competitor also used the platform to thank those who supported him throughout his career. Beyond WWE and TKO, Lesnar specifically acknowledged Dana White and the UFC, the Minnesota Vikings organization, and the University of Minnesota for believing in him and giving him opportunities across multiple sports and entertainment platforms.

🔄 המשך Speculation about why WWE didn’t announce the attendance for SummerSlam 2026

According to Meltzer, WWE's decision to withhold the attendance announcement is a significant departure from standard practice. He emphasized that when WWE is satisfied with attendance numbers, they announce them, and the lack of an announcement signals disappointment. He noted that WWE revealed attendance figures for last year's SummerSlam and even for WrestleMania despite some concerns about that event, making this year's silence particularly notable.

Meltzer theorized that competitive pressure from AEW is a driving factor in WWE's silence. With All In: London potentially drawing north of 40,000 fans, WWE apparently felt announcing SummerSlam's numbers would only invite unfavorable comparisons. Rather than risk publicizing what they consider an underwhelming figure, WWE chose not to announce attendance at all. Meltzer acknowledged that while the visual presentation of the event was handled well through production, the actual attendance fell short of WWE's expectations.
