AEW Continental Challenge Cup tournament

AEW has officially unveiled the complete ruleset and format for the Continental Challenge Cup tournament during the opening segment of the August 8 episode of Collision. The tournament represents a prestigious opportunity for 16 wrestlers to compete in a straight knockout format with stipulations designed to ensure clean finishes and legitimate competition.

The tournament structure follows a blind draw format throughout its progression. Sixteen wrestlers will begin by competing in eight first-round matches, with the eight winners advancing to the next round. Those eight competitors will then face off in four blind draw matches, with the four winners securing spots in the semifinals. This progression ensures that matchups remain unpredictable until they are officially announced.

Several key rules have been established to govern all Continental Challenge Cup matches. There will be no outside interference allowed under any circumstances, ensuring that victories are determined purely by in-ring competition. Additionally, all matches will have no time limits, allowing wrestlers to compete until a decisive finish is reached without clock constraints. These rules create an environment where the best pure wrestling will determine advancement.

The tournament's schedule spans multiple major AEW events. The first-round matches are being held during episodes of Collision, with three additional first-round matchups confirmed for the August 15 episode. These matches include Katsuyori Shibata versus Nigel McGuinness, Roderick Strong versus Kyle O'Reilly, and Brian Cage versus Hechicero. Earlier in the week on August 8, Claudio Castagnoli, Orange Cassidy, and Eddie Kingston advanced from their respective first-round contests.

The tournament will culminate over two major international events. The semifinals are scheduled to take place in Glasgow, Scotland on August 26, bringing the competition to the United Kingdom. The Continental Cup Final will then take place at Wembley Stadium during AEW All In London on August 30, providing a grand stage for the championship culmination. The winner of the final match will receive the Continental Cup and be crowned the new AEW Continental Champion.

Chris Jericho Reveals Whether He Considered WWE Return Before Renewing AEW Contract

Several months after he returned at an AEW Dynamite episode in Winnipeg, Chris Jericho is firmly entrenched in AEW once again. But it wasn't that long ago that it seemed like Jericho's time with AEW had come to an end, with a return to WWE heavily speculated on and even expected.

During an interview with Beef Vegan, Jericho addressed how close he came to exploring opportunities outside of AEW before ultimately renewing his contract with the promotion. While he didn't reveal extensive details about his decision-making process, Jericho acknowledged that he had considered various options available to him.

"I mean...you know, you weigh all your options, you know?" Jericho said. "But I have skin in the game with AEW. I helped create AEW, and I was never gone. I just was off TV for awhile while we figured out some stuff. But you know, basically just ended one contract and went straight to the other one."

Jericho emphasized the significance of his role in establishing AEW and highlighted the factors that made staying with the promotion an attractive choice. He stressed his positive relationship with company leadership and his investment in the company's trajectory.

"When you're at a place...I like Tony Khan as a boss, I like AEW as a company, I like the growth that we've done, and I helped start the company, you know?" Jericho explained. "So I'll always have, like I mentioned, skin in the game. WWE existed before I was there and it'll exist I was there, and of course AEW will exist after I was there too. But it didn't exist before I was there. So you know, it was...more exciting to be with the company that I helped create."

Since his return, Jericho has been involved in several significant storylines and matches. He teamed with The Elite and Hurt Syndicate to win Stadium Stampede at AEW Double or Nothing. Additionally, Jericho has been engaged in a violent feud with Tommaso Ciampa that has seen both competitors claim victories.

Ciampa defeated Jericho on Dynamite in July before Jericho, donning his Painmaker persona, got revenge on Ciampa in a wild no holds barred match at AEW Redemption. The two currently have one win apiece against each other, leaving their rivalry with unfinished business.

AEW Brawling Birds tag team

AEW's Brawling Birds have become a dominant tag team on the roster over the past few months, with Jamie Hayter and Alex Windsor wrestling a hard-hitting style that naturally compliments the other. Appearing on Close Up with Renee Paquette, the two detailed how their partnership came together.

According to Windsor, the pairing began at an AEW television taping back in January when one of the producers approached her with a simple question. "I think I was at TV, I think back in January, and one of the producers came up to me and was like: 'So, do you want to tag with Jamie?' I was just like: 'Yeah? Sure, count me in! Like, no question about it whatsoever," Windsor recalled.

Hayter agreed with the account, noting that the conversation took place late at night as the two were preparing to leave the venue. She expressed immediate excitement upon learning about the potential partnership. While the two had known each other for a long time prior to being paired together, they hadn't been particularly close before this opportunity arose.

The partnership came together quickly, and the team soon learned what they would be called. According to Windsor, Tony Khan came up with the Brawling Birds name. "It's so British, but it's not too in your face," Windsor said. "I think it was Tony's idea. He was like: 'Ah, the Brawling Birds!' And we was like: 'Birds?' I think being British, we know the reference."

Despite their rapid rise to prominence, the Brawling Birds have yet to capture gold together. However, they have set their sights squarely on the current AEW Women's Tag Team Champions, Divine Dominion. Hayter indicated that the Brawling Birds are focused on becoming the best versions of themselves so they could take on Megan Bayne and Lena Kross, possibly at AEW All In, in their native country of England, later this month.

Grand Slam 2 wrestling event

Grand Slam 2 will bring live professional wrestling back to Kokomo Municipal Stadium on Saturday, August 15, with two championship matches and hometown wrestler Chris Owens facing Jacob Brawn. Owens told the media that the inaugural Grand Slam attracted approximately 600 people in August 2024. After recently teaming with Marti Belle in a winning effort at EMERGE 98: Back In The Saddle, Owens will return home as both a featured wrestler and one of the people responsible for assembling the event.

The first Grand Slam took place at Kokomo Municipal Stadium on August 3, 2024. Owens, Nick Kranz and Nathan Martin are childhood friends who grew up sharing an interest in professional wrestling. Kranz first approached Martin, who was serving as the stadium's general manager, about bringing wrestling to the venue. Kranz then encouraged Owens to compete because his friends and family rarely had an opportunity to watch him wrestle in Kokomo.

Owens explained the origins of the event: "The idea for this show actually came from Nick. He had got with Nathan, who was the GM of the stadium at the time, to talk about doing a show there and asked what we needed to do to make it happen. Nick came to me and said I should do a match there because it is my hometown and I had friends and family that wanted to come see me wrestle live, so the idea for the show kinda snowballed from there. Nick was the promoter, Nathan was the GM of the stadium and I was the wrestler, and I booked all of the talent."

The venue had already captured Owens' attention before the event was proposed. Kokomo Municipal Stadium opened in 2015 at 400 S. Union Street. The facility has 3,000 fixed seats, grass seating for approximately 1,000 people and suites that bring its total capacity to approximately 4,500. Owens said the inaugural event drew approximately 600 people and the response he received afterward convinced him that Grand Slam should return.

"I determined the first show a success by the reaction we got from the audience and everyone that attended," Owens stated. "The amount of messages I got telling me how much they enjoyed it and how they can't wait for the next one." He also expressed his initial impressions of the stadium as a wrestling venue: "We chose the Kokomo Municipal Stadium because I remember when it was first built. The first thought I had when I saw it was, 'This would be a great place to do a wrestling show.' It is a beautiful baseball stadium."

Presenting wrestling inside an outdoor baseball stadium gives Grand Slam a distinctive appearance, but it also creates challenges involving rain and summer heat. Grand Slam 2 will begin later than the inaugural event to reduce the amount of time spectators spend sitting under the sun. Gates will open at 19:00 before the first match begins at 20:00.

Owens addressed the logistical challenges: "The most difficult part of doing a show at a baseball stadium, an outside venue, is just stressing over the weather. I have decided to make the start time a little later this time to avoid the heat. I feel bad making the crowd sit in the hot sun for the first half of the show. Stressing about rain is a difficult part also."

Owens wants the event to remain accessible to spectators who do not regularly follow independent wrestling. Each match is intended to tell a complete story without requiring the audience to understand months of previous developments. "I want Grand Slam 2 to just offer a more coherent and smooth-looking show that a lot of indie shows do not have. We do not like to make storylines difficult to understand. Everything is open and shut that night. No cliffhangers."

Owens personally selected the wrestlers and matches. Marketability, athletic presentation and professional experience were among the qualities he considered while assembling the card. "The wrestlers and matches were hand-picked by me based on how marketable they are. Do they have a good look? We want guys that people will look at and say, 'They are the real deal,' and you can tell the" wrestlers are legitimate competitors.
