🔥 MJF challenges Will Ospreay to title vs. title match at AEW Worlds End

MJF has his sights set on becoming the undisputed AEW World Champion. Following his surprise return at AEW Full Gear over the weekend, Maxwell Jacob Friedman appeared on the November 18 episode of AEW Dynamite, where he continued his war of words with reigning AEW World Champion Will Ospreay. With Ospreay absent from the show due to medical issues preventing him from traveling after Full Gear, Friedman took full advantage of the opportunity to deliver a lengthy verbal attack on The Aerial Assassin.

During the segment, MJF claimed that Ospreay was responsible for his downfall and mocked Ospreay's ongoing mission to restore the feeling in AEW, arguing that he doesn’t need to restore anything because he is the feeling. Claiming that Me, myself, and I literally doubled ticket sales and pay-per-view buys, MJF declared that he was that feeling. Friedman also accused Ospreay of stealing his accomplishments, his championship, and most importantly, his life.

The former AEW World Champion proceeded to remind Ospreay who was named the number one wrestler on Pro Wrestling Illustrated's PWI 500 list, unveiling a massive banner of his ranking while telling Ospreay that the world feels differently about who is best. MJF revisited their personal history, reminding everyone that he defeated Ospreay clean in the middle of the ring during their first encounter at the 250th episode of Dynamite to win the International title, before Ospreay regained it a month later at All In London. He also claimed he had Ospreay beaten in their next meeting before outside interference changed the outcome, and brought up original plans for them to headline All In before Ospreay allegedly cost him everything during his championship match against Kenny Omega at Beach Break earlier this year.

Even though MJF lost the AEW world title back in July, he has refused to accept the fact that he is not the real world champion. He refers to himself as the One True Champion and still carries around his customized Triple-B belt, also known as the Big Burberry Belt. MJF made his return at Full Gear wearing a mask before attacking Ospreay after the latter's successful title defense over Kyle Fletcher, stomping on Ospreay's belt and making his presence felt with his own version of the title.

The former champion ultimately made his intentions clear, telling Ospreay that if he didn't like the truth, he should face him at AEW Worlds End in a title versus title match with both versions of the championship on the line. MJF ended the segment by holding up his championship before leaving the ring, making it clear that he wants more than revenge and is looking to settle their rivalry with everything at stake to crown one undisputed champion.

AEW Worlds End 2026 is scheduled for Saturday, December 19, at the Target Center in Minneapolis, Minnesota, marking the company's final pay-per-view of the year. The challenge has been officially laid out on Dynamite, but Ospreay is yet to respond to the proposal at the time of writing, leaving the question of whether this winner-take-all showdown will actually main event the card.

🔥 Chris Jericho discusses underrated AEW talent

During a recent video interview, Chris Jericho was asked to identify underrated wrestlers on the current roster who deserve more love and television time. Jericho listed several performers, including Mike Bailey, Kevin Knight, Matt Menard known as Daddy Magic, and Sammy Guevara. Jericho expressed that Daddy Magic could become a cult hero, praised Bailey as excellent and Knight as great, but placed special emphasis on Guevara.

Speaking specifically about Guevara, Jericho stated that he should be featured on television more often. Jericho praised Guevara for having a great personality and humorously added that he has a very punchable face, concluding that the promotion should at least try and do something with him. Jericho and Guevara share a lengthy on-screen history, having previously worked closely together in stables like The Inner Circle, Le Sex Gods, and the Jericho Appreciation Society.

Despite once being positioned as one of the original four pillars of the company and a three-time TNT Champion and Dynamite Award winner, Guevara has largely disappeared from regular television programming in recent months. His most recent AEW match took place in June, while his last Ring of Honor appearance was in August. Guevara's absence previously sparked speculation regarding a potential departure, fueled by fan misunderstandings about his social media bios. However, the promotion's leadership dismissed these exit rumors, confirming that Guevara remained under contract and had expressed his love for the company. Meanwhile, Guevara's wife, Tay Melo, recently departed the promotion after requesting her release. Although absent from domestic television, Guevara recently returned to the ring for World Series Wrestling's tour of Australia and New Zealand, expressing excitement on social media about his return and sharing a workout photo with the hashtag WorldChampSeason.

During the same rapid-fire interview segment, Jericho also shared his picks for his favorite wrestling figures and matches. He named Will Ospreay as his favorite wrestler to watch today. For the greatest babyfaces of all time, he selected Hulk Hogan in the 1980s, Ricky Steamboat, and designated Shawn Michaels as a backup. Jericho also noted that Michaels was one of the greatest heels of all time, alongside Macho Man Randy Savage. He awarded the best finisher title to Stone Cold Steve Austin for his Stone Cold Stunner. When discussing his favorite matches, Jericho highlighted his classic 2008 ladder match against Shawn Michaels for the world title as well as the unique Stadium Stampede matches, which he praised for their creativity during the pandemic era when shows had to be produced without live audiences.

🔥 Backstage news on potential location for AEW All In 2027

AEW could be preparing to take its biggest annual event, All In, outside of London once again in 2027. Toronto, Ontario, Canada has emerged as a leading candidate and top potential landing spot to host the major pay-per-view. While discussions have taken place internally within AEW, nothing has been confirmed or finalized yet, and there have been no discussions pertaining to a specific venue that would host the event if the proposal moves forward.

One of the main drawing points and a notable factor in these discussions is Adam Copeland. With his retirement on the horizon and 2027 planned as his final year as an active wrestler, Copeland has communicated both to Tony Khan and others that he would like for his last match, or one of his last matches, to take place in Toronto. Copeland's connection to his hometown could make the city an appealing destination for a major AEW event, particularly if his retirement plans become part of the promotion's creative direction. However, there is no official confirmation that Copeland's retirement match is locked in for All In 2027, and it could simply be one of his final appearances before hanging up his boots. Copeland has previously crossed off several items on his wrestling bucket list, including competing at Arena Mexico, Wembley Stadium, and the former Maple Leaf Gardens, while still keeping goals like the Tokyo Dome and another AEW World Championship reign on his radar.

Another factor surrounding the event is WWE's decision to hold WrestleMania 43 outside of North America in 2027. With WWE's biggest annual event taking place internationally, AEW could potentially benefit from staging one of its major shows in the North American market. Despite that possibility, there are reportedly no active plans to move All In to WrestleMania weekend. AEW traditionally holds All In during the months of July or August.

AEW has previously demonstrated a willingness to move All In beyond its established London location. The first-ever All In event was held in Chicago in 2018, serving as the spiritual predecessor to AEW as it was founded in 2019. The event was brought back under AEW branding in 2023 at Wembley Stadium in London, returning in 2024. The 2025 event was shifted to Globe Life Field in Arlington, Texas, because Wembley Stadium was unavailable due to a prior booking, before AEW returned to Wembley for All In 2026. Following that event, Tony Khan stated he was unsure whether Wembley Stadium would host the 2027 event, though he confirmed that AEW would return to the venue in the future. Meanwhile, the United Kingdom is already scheduled to host at least one AEW pay-per-view in 2027, with Dynasty set for Liverpool on Sunday, February 7.

For now, the possibility of All In heading to Canada remains under discussion, and AEW has not yet officially announced a host city, venue, or date for All In 2027. Whether the company ultimately decides to take its marquee event to Toronto or returns to Wembley Stadium remains to be seen.

🔥 AEW announces eight-man tag match for Winter Is Coming

AEW has officially put together a wild and major eight-man tag team showdown for its upcoming Winter Is Coming special, bringing together four of the biggest tag teams in the promotion. The match was made official during the November 18 episode of Dynamite in Denver, Colorado, setting the stage for AEW's first-ever trip to Monterrey, Mexico. The December 9 event at Arena Monterrey will see the Young Bucks and the Motor City Machine Guns join forces to take on FTR and the newly arrived Kasper Brothers.

The hostility dates back to the Full Gear pay-per-view in Phoenix, Arizona. There, Jacob and Drew Kasper, formerly known as WWE's Creed Brothers after choosing not to re-sign with the company, made a surprise AEW debut. They made an immediate impact by attacking both the Young Bucks and the Motor City Machine Guns following a tag team match before aligning themselves with FTR and manager Stokely Hathaway.

The match took shape when Alex Shelley and Chris Sabin approached Matt and Nick Jackson backstage on Dynamite. The Machine Guns proposed temporarily joining forces with the former AEW World Tag Team Champions under the name Young Guns to deal with their common enemies. Stokely Hathaway subsequently accepted the challenge on behalf of his team, choosing Winter Is Coming in Monterrey as the venue for the showdown.

Adding to their momentum, the Kasper Brothers also made their official in-ring AEW debut on the November 18 episode of Dynamite, picking up a dominant squash match victory over a pair of local competitors. Their arrival alongside FTR has already placed them firmly in the middle of AEW's tag team championship picture.

There is another interesting layer to the upcoming contest as well. The Motor City Machine Guns are also set to challenge the Young Bucks for the AEW World Tag Team Championships at a date yet to be announced, likely for next month's Worlds End pay-per-view. Their temporary partnership could therefore become quite complicated once their individual championship ambitions come back into focus. With eight competitors and plenty of unfinished business involved, AEW Winter Is Coming now has a major tag team attraction locked in as the company heads south of the border.

Queen Aminata retains AEW TBS Title

Queen Aminata is still the AEW TBS Champion after successfully defending her title in a chaotic four-way match against AZM, Maya World, and Mina Shirakawa on AEW Dynamite. Commentary noted that this grueling encounter was actually Aminata's third title defense in a single week.

The action heated up immediately when the champion was taken out of the ring early, allowing World, Shirakawa, and AZM to battle amongst themselves. AZM took Aminata out by sweeping her leg on the apron, knocking her down, before taking a moment to taunt AEW Women's World Champion Mercedes Mone, who was providing guest commentary.

Inside the ring, Shirakawa broke up a submission hold that AZM had applied to Aminata and tried to secure a pin on the champion. When that failed, Shirakawa attempted a spinning elbow strike, but Aminata countered by connecting with the Queen's Crown. Aminata then took down AZM, but World re-entered the match to attempt a rollup on the champion followed by a stomp. World continued her offensive by taking out both Aminata and AZM on the outside, which was quickly followed by Shirakawa wiping out all three opponents with a flying cross body.

The pace remained relentless as World was locked in a figure four leglock by Shirakawa in the middle of the ring. Aminata abruptly broke up the submission by delivering a stomp from the top rope. Moments later, AZM sent the champion crashing face-first into the commentary desk with a dropkick while Aminata was on the apron, though Mone quickly retaliated by taking out AZM with the women's title from behind.

Down the stretch, Shirakawa almost pinned World following a rolling elbow. After World missed a moonsault from the middle rope, Shirakawa capitalized by hitting a Diamond DDT from the ropes. Shirakawa went for another figure four, but Aminata was positioned right behind her and connected with another Queen's Crown, followed immediately by an Off with Her Head to decisively retain the TBS championship.
