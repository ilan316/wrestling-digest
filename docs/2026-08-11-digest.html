Mick Foley AEW Transition

Mick Foley has opened up about his decision to join AEW after his long tenure with WWE, highlighting the stark differences between the two companies' cultures and management styles.

In an interview with TheSportster.com, the wrestling legend explained that he took a hiatus from wrestling for a couple of years after his children had grown up and moved out. Since his kids had been a major driving force behind his engagement with wrestling, their departure prompted him to step back from the industry. During this time away, Foley found himself drifting from his passion for the sport, though he continued to appreciate it from afar.

However, Foley's perspective began to shift when he started observing AEW's emergence and the energy surrounding the company. He began comparing what he saw in AEW to the corporate direction that WWE had taken over the years. This contrast between the two companies ultimately influenced his decision to work with AEW rather than remain exclusively tied to WWE.

Since joining AEW, Foley has found the experience refreshing and fulfilling. He emphasized that what stands out most to him is the ownership's visible passion and enthusiasm for the product. Specifically, Foley noted that he enjoys seeing the owner genuinely happy after matches and expressing clear appreciation for the hard work of his wrestlers. This authentic gratitude and engagement contrasts with the more business-focused management approach he experienced during his WWE career.

While Foley acknowledged that Vince McMahon's management style was effective and successful, he expressed that at this stage of his life, he prefers the environment AEW provides. The combination of the company's infectious enthusiasm and the owner's evident love for watching performers excel has made his transition to AEW a positive one.

MJF Creative Influence in AEW

Backstage News On MJF's Creative Influence In AEW

Recent reports have provided insight into MJF's creative involvement within AEW's booking structure. In the latest edition of a Q&A session, Sean Ross Sapp addressed fan questions about the extent of MJF's influence in the booking of the AEW World Title picture and the main event scene.

Sapp reported that MJF maintains a major influence on the programs he is involved in and contributes creatively to some extent. It remains unclear whether this creative input is formally outlined as part of his AEW contract, but the company has known for some time that he plays a significant role in creative decisions regarding his own storylines.

When asked whether MJF influences the creative booking for other main event wrestlers such as Swerve Strickland, "Hangman" Adam Page, or Jon Moxley, Sapp expressed his belief that MJF does not have creative control over other AEW main event talents. His influence appears confined to the programs he is directly involved in or with talent he has personally worked with, rather than extending across the broader main event landscape.

Regarding how other wrestlers perceive MJF's creative influence, Sapp noted that some wrestlers prefer not to have any talent exerting creative influence within the company. He indicated that he has rarely heard of wrestlers viewing another talent's creative input as a positive development.

MJF has been absent from AEW programming since losing the World Title to Kenny Omega last month at AEW Dynamite: Beach Break. He is scheduled to compete this Wednesday on AEW Dynamite in a three-way match against Nick Wayne and "Speedball" Mike Bailey, with the winner earning the number two spot in the Casino Gauntlet Match at All In: London.

Beyond his wrestling commitments, MJF will appear in the upcoming film Violent Night 2, starring alongside David Harbour and Kristen Bell. The film is set to be released in theaters on December 4th.

CM Punk in AEW: Part 10 – AEW Double or Nothing 2022

This was previously part of a book written and released covering CM Punk's run from debut to first AEW Championship win in All Elite Wrestling, spanning August 2021 until May 2022. This column is being made available online in serialized fashion. You can check out Part 1, Part 2, Part 3, Part 4, Part 5, Part 6, Part 7, Part 8 and Part 9 in this ongoing series.

The match between CM Punk and Hangman at AEW Revolution 2022 would once again see the former WWE Champion being lauded by both fans and critics, with Dave Meltzer of the Wrestling Observer giving the bout four and a half stars overall. The buyrate estimates from WrestleNomics, however, indicate that the show was not the success that Tony Khan and co would likely have wanted, with around 165,000 buys reported, about 10,000 down from AEW Revolution that featured CM Punk vs MJF in a dog collar match. This would finally be the crowning of Punk as the All Elite Wrestling Champion, signalling his arrival as the "official" face of the company going forward.

Before the Double or Nothing event took place, there was a lot of speculation online regarding MJF and his status in the company, with Maxwell reportedly threatening to walk. Despite this, and the extra hype that was anticipated due to MJF maybe no-showing, reportedly did not make a major difference to the overall buyrate. According to that week's Wrestling Observer Newsletter: "It was almost identical to how it was tracking as compared to other shows, as it was tracking at the level of Full Gear (150,000 buys) going in. Basically, there were people who thought all the late pub from the MJF story on social media would lead to a huge increase in buys, but it doesn't appear to have had any noticeable effect."

Another issue that the PPV show faced was that it was going up against Game Seven of the Boston Celtics vs. Miami Heat NBA finals, something that probably would've hindered the buys from a US standpoint. Dave Meltzer noted at the time that "Khan actually studied and found that 79 percent of the time there is not a game seven in the NBA playoffs, so the odds were this would be a free date. But it didn't work out that way." It was a gamble from TK, and it didn't pay off in terms of PPV numbers.

In terms of online hype, the show did around what most of the bigger AEW shows did at the time, with around 200,000 Google searches over the weekend. Most of that was attributed by Meltzer to the ongoing MJF situation, but as he noted: "on the day of the show, Punk blew everyone away with MJF and Adam Page being a tie for second and nobody else close."

It wasn't just the buyrates and online buzz that would signal success for the PPV event, with the live attendance being a sellout of 14,459 fans, making it the third largest show in company history to that point (behind the Arthur Ashe Stadium show and the first Chicago United Center show). However, to that point it was the largest ever for an AEW PPV, and the gate was just under $1.15 million, making it the biggest non-WWE gate in the history of North American pro wrestling up to that point (the previous record was the 2019 NJPW/ROH show in Madison Square Garden).

It is impossible to attribute all of this success to CM Punk, as there were other matches on show that were well-built like MJF vs Wardlow, Thunder Rosa vs Serena Deeb, the Anarchy in the Arena match and The Young Bucks vs The Hardys. But, in a world where most tend to attribute the gate to a strong main event, you cannot dismiss that CM Punk challenging for the AEW Championship, and in a very likely position to win it, drew a LOT of those fans watching in the arena or live at home on PPV.

🔄 המשך MVP AEW World Title Opportunity Rejection

MVP expanded on his reasoning for turning down a potential career capstone opportunity, drawing a clear distinction between his willingness to work tag and trios matches versus the demands of a world championship reign.

The veteran emphasized that being AEW World Champion involves more than just holding a belt. The role requires being the company's poster boy, responsible for selling tickets and delivering in marquee main-event matches. MVP made it explicit that he has no interest in that level of commitment or visibility on his way out.

When pressed on whether his reluctance stems from physical or mental factors, MVP confirmed it's both. While he acknowledged he simply doesn't want to work that hard anymore, he also stressed he wouldn't want to take the title away from younger talent. He's acutely aware that a world championship run demands performances he can no longer deliver at the level he'd expect from himself.

Despite his retirement timeline moving closer, MVP remains engaged with AEW in a less demanding capacity. He's open to continuing his role with the Hurt Syndicate in tag and trios competition, where he can contribute without being the centerpiece of major matches.
