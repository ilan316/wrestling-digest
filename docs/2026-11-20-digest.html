🔥 Jim Ross health and hospital updates

AEW and WWE Hall of Fame personality Jim Ross has confirmed he is out of the hospital, though he admits that his fight is far from over. Ross, 74, has dealt with numerous health issues over the last few years, landing in and out of the hospital frequently for various surgeries. After most recently undergoing another shunt surgery on November 11, Ross shared a personal update on social media. He announced that he left the hospital and went straight to Tulsa to move in with his daughter and her family for a few weeks, noting how supportive his family has been and thanking everyone for their positive thoughts and prayers.

Podcast co-host Conrad Thompson provided additional encouraging news during the opening of Grilling JR, revealing that Ross remains mentally super sharp despite dealing with physical weakness. According to updates from JRsBBQ.com, Ross was laid up in the hospital for the better part of a week, but those close to him are optimistic about his recovery. The team expressed hope that Ross will be healthy enough to resume his hosting duties in time for the Thanksgiving edition of the podcast. Beyond podcasting, Ross is also hopeful that he will be able to return to his part-time role on AEW commentary once he feels better and can travel again.

While his medical battles have been ongoing for years, 2026 has taken a heavy toll on the announcer. Ross underwent brain surgery over the summer to have a shunt inserted to drain excess fluid, which initially helped alleviate balance problems and frequent falls. However, he later developed severe headaches, requiring a second brain procedure in October to address a shunt issue, a brief hospitalization for low oxygen, and a third surgery earlier this month.

Amidst this difficult stretch, Ross is also mourning the loss of a close friend following the passing of Cowboy Bill Watts at age 87 on November 17. Watts served as Ross' mentor in Mid-South Wrestling and helped him break into the industry, prompting a recent podcast episode to feature a re-airing of a past conversation between Ross and his late mentor.

Mick Foley connection with AEW's Nick Wayne

WWE Hall of Famer Mick Foley has revealed a special connection to AEW star Nick Wayne that dates back to Wayne’s childhood. During Wayne’s appearance on the latest episode of The Mick and Kenny Show, Foley reflected on his longtime relationship with the young wrestler and his family, including a holiday tradition that involved personally writing letters from Santa Claus.

Foley recalled first watching Wayne wrestle in 2018, when he was approximately 12 or 13 years old. Wayne noted that he began training under his father, Buddy Wayne, at just nine years old. It was inconceivable, not just that you could do the things that you did, but that they were done so smoothly, Foley said of Wayne’s abilities at such a young age.

The conversation also touched on Wayne’s upbringing around professional wrestling. His father operated the Buddy Wayne Academy out of the family home, allowing Nick to grow up surrounded by the business. Buddy Wayne passed away when Nick was just 11 years old.

Foley then brought up a unique childhood memory involving Wayne, revealing that he had personally written letters from Santa Claus to the future AEW star. You are the only current wrestler who can say this. Who was I to you when you were about 12 or 13 years old? You want to admit this to the world? Foley asked. He’s contemplating because he’s like, If I say this, this means people know I believed until I was 13. I can say it for you, Foley continued.

The wrestling legend explained that he would write dozens of personalized Christmas letters each year, with Wayne being among the recipients. One man cannot be expected to circumnavigate the globe in one night and write letters to every child. He needs help. And so every year I would do about 30 to 40 handwritten letters. And one of them was to young Nick Wayne. And I hope you still have that.

Wayne confirmed that he still has the letter and intends to keep it forever. Of course. I’ll cherish it for the rest of my life, Wayne responded. Foley also revealed that he had written a letter to Wayne’s mother, known to AEW viewers as Mother Wayne. I believe in the handwritten letter, and so yes, I was St. Nick to this young man, Foley added.

Wayne is coming off having defeated Mike Bailey on an edition of AEW Dynamite in his opening Continental Classic match. Foley last appeared on AEW programming as part of the pre-show panel for AEW Full Gear on Saturday.

Val Venis comments on AEW and social media disputes

Former WWE star Val Venis recently voiced strong criticism regarding where All Elite Wrestling has taken professional wrestling, noting that recent violent spectacles like Full Gear failed to win him over. Venis argued that parts of the product have drifted so far from believable wrestling that they resemble demonic horror movies. Taking to social media, he specifically targeted the modern style and suggested that AEW caters to hardcore gamers who prefer video-game aesthetics over realistic athletic competition. He expressed a preference for the old-school approach of the 1950s, sixties, and seventies, where fans could suspend their disbelief during a plausible exhibition. While acknowledging there is a legitimate market for AEW's approach, Venis stated it is not enough to keep him watching. The timing of his critique coincides with ongoing debates surrounding extreme spots at Full Gear, such as the blood-in-the-mouth spot in Swerve Strickland and Hangman Adam Page's Texas Death Match and the use of skewers in Anarchy in the Arena. While Tony Schiavone dismissed concerns about the blood spot by questioning how to define limits in a Texas Death Match, Venis maintains that the overall spectacle entirely undermines believability.

Beyond his critique of modern wrestling styles, Venis became embroiled in a heated social media dispute with journalist Sean Ross Sapp. The conflict began when Sapp criticized Venis on Twitter for accepting money to do an interview with RF Video, a shoot interview company founded by Rob Feinstein, who was involved in a 2004 online sting operation involving an adult posing as an underage boy. Venis fired back with a lengthy post accusing Sapp of guilt by association, arguing that RF Video was simply one of many vendors in the industry and that doing business with a company does not equal an endorsement of its founder's actions. To emphasize his point, Venis listed numerous prominent wrestlers who also participated in interviews with the company over the years.

Venis named an extensive roster of wrestling icons, including Bret Hart, Dusty Rhodes, Eddie Guerrero, AJ Styles, Lex Luger, Rob Van Dam, the Dudley Boys, Arn Anderson, Bobby Heenan, Kenny Omega, Kevin Steen, Karrion Kross, Rey Mysterio, Ricky Steamboat, Scott Steiner, Terry Funk, and Vader, among dozens of others. He questioned whether Sapp would have the courage to confront all of those legendary performers with the same criticism. Furthermore, Venis turned the tables on the journalist by suggesting that interviewers often speak with controversial figures without endorsing them. The argument escalated further when Venis brought up personal politics, attacking Sapp's stance on abortion and accusing him of supporting evil. This social media clash adds to a history of online disputes for Venis, who previously faced backlash and an AEW block over comments regarding PAC, as well as political disagreements with CM Punk.

AEW Full Gear 2026 PPV buys update

Early indications regarding the November 14 AEW Full Gear pay-per-view suggest the event could finish as the company's third-biggest PPV of the year, with the show reportedly performing significantly better than October's WrestleDream event. During an episode of Wrestling Observer Radio, Dave Meltzer discussed the early numbers alongside Bryan Alvarez, pointing to the AEW World Championship main event between Will Ospreay and Kyle Fletcher as a major factor behind the strong performance.

It is probably third biggest of the year, and that was a huge success, Meltzer said. He emphasized Fletcher's emergence as an established attraction, rather than simply someone with the potential to become one. Kyle Fletcher is not the future. Kyle Fletcher is the present. He is a major star. They did not get that number without... it was that main event, that is the key. The blood matches, I am sure the blood matches helped. But the big difference when it comes to pay-per-views is almost always going to be the main event or something on the card that is special when you do a number like that.

Although an official final buy total has not been established, the early projections put Full Gear in some notable company. AEW Revolution on March 15 was estimated to have generated between 170,000 and 175,000 buys. Double or Nothing on May 24 was tracking toward approximately 187,000 to 200,000 buys, while All In at Wembley Stadium on August 30 remains the company's biggest estimated PPV of the year with between 210,000 and 230,000 buys. For comparison, AEW WrestleDream on October 17 was estimated at between 100,000 and 110,000 buys, potentially tying Redemption for the company's lowest total of the year. If Full Gear finishes as the third-biggest show, it would rank ahead of AEW Revolution while falling below Double or Nothing.

Meltzer also highlighted Ospreay's recent track record as a pay-per-view headliner, noting that three of his last four main events have delivered particularly strong business. Will has headlined the last four pay-per-views and three of them have done big numbers. Obviously the Omega did the biggest, but the Moxley did real big, and this one probably did as big or bigger than the Moxley one. And with Kyle, I mean, that is saying a lot. They did numbers in the range of what MJF did with Adam Page with the retirement stipulation, and that was like the biggest non-All In, other than Sting's retirement, in years. And this is in that range.

The performance is particularly noteworthy considering Full Gear featured Fletcher challenging Ospreay in the main event, with the two delivering a championship showdown that helped drive significant interest in the event. Meltzer concluded by making it clear that both wrestlers have established themselves as legitimate pay-per-view attractions. Both of those guys are real drawing cards now. Not theoretical, not, well, let us put them in a spot and hope they grow. They are there now. That was the big message of this week.

Gabe Kidd talks forming a tag team with Pac in AEW

AEW star Gabe Kidd recently opened up about growing closer with the late Pac, sharing fond memories and revealing that the two had discussed forming a tag team.

Speaking on Talk Is Jericho, Kidd recalled first meeting Pac in 2011 in Nottingham at a Dragon Gate UK event while setting up the ring at the Royal Albert Hall. Though they did not cross paths frequently for years after that initial encounter, their bond strengthened significantly in the weeks leading up to Pac's passing while spending time together on the road.

Kidd noted that they bonded over unexpected musical tastes, discovering a mutual love for dubstep and dance hall music. Kidd mentioned that he would bring a speaker into the Death Riders locker room to pump up their energy before matches, which helped spark conversations about their shared preferences.

That connection ran deep enough that the two competitors seriously considered forming a tag team that would lean into their shared British roots. Kidd described the proposed concept as being two rough English boys showcased on American television.

According to Kidd, the tag team idea came up during a dinner outing in Charleston, West Virginia. During the same conversation, they also bonded over their shared experiences working in Japan, where both men felt they truly transformed from boys into adults, recalling milestones like doing their own laundry for the very first time.

Pac and Kidd eventually reunited under the All Elite Wrestling banner as members of the Death Riders stable. Their final in-ring encounter took place on the September 16 edition of AEW Dynamite, where they teamed up with Jon Moxley to face the United Empire.

Jonathan Coachman Calls Jade Cargill Overpaid, Says She ‘Messes Something Up’ in Every Big Match

Jade Cargill recently drew attention when she told fans who think she should have gone to NXT that she makes too much money not to be seen. Jonathan Coachman heard that line and agreed with the first half during a recent podcast appearance alongside Vince Russo. Cargill explained that WWE made the decision to skip NXT and even told fans they could turn around if they didn't want to watch her. Coachman humorously pictured Vince McMahon in the back watching the crowd actually take her up on that offer and wondering how pissed he would be.

Coachman opened the segment by questioning how big Cargill's ego is. When Cargill claimed her name generates money, Coachman asked Russo how often WWE actually put her in a position to draw as champion on the road to WrestleMania. He noted that she won a title in November of 2025 and questioned how many times she defended it during the Royal Rumble and WrestleMania season if she was truly viewed as a major drawing star. Russo chimed in to say that Cargill just buried NXT with her remarks.

Coachman gave Cargill credit for the lucrative deal she landed, but he questioned how she could dismiss NXT given her own limited experience. He recalled her previous statements about having her first match just a few years ago, pointing out the contradiction in calling herself a megastar who does not need NXT while simultaneously telling the world she has been wrestling for less than four years. Cargill actually made her in-ring debut in March 2021 on AEW Dynamite, teaming with Shaquille O'Neal against Cody Rhodes and Red Velvet, famously stating on a podcast that her first match was on TV and that her NXT was AEW.

Contrasting her with proven ticket and merchandise sellers like The Rock, CM Punk, and Cody Rhodes, Coachman took issue with Cargill making public claims about her drawing power. He admitted he has never met her and confessed that her behavior bugs him because other legitimate stars avoid such public remarks to maintain backstage harmony and secure their pushes. Coachman stated that Cargill has never really gotten a push other than money.

Anticipating that fans would respond by compiling videos of her botched spots from the year, Coachman offered his own explanation for her current booking status. He stated that the other reason she has not been pushed is because every time she gets into a big match, she messes something up. When Russo agreed, Coachman emphasized that it is just a factual observation rather than mere hatred, encouraging people to watch the match footage. He closed the segment by naming two veteran women he would like to see pull Cargill aside backstage, stating that Charlotte Flair and Becky Lynch need to smarten her up.

🔄 המשך Speedball Mike Bailey comments on AEW Continental Classic loss

Following his opening-round defeat in the Continental Classic, Speedball Mike Bailey has made it clear that his tournament journey is far from over.

In an impassioned backstage promo released by the promotion, Bailey addressed the loss to Nick Wayne. While acknowledging that Wayne will likely brag about the victory, Bailey pointed out that the round-robin tournament is a marathon rather than a sprint. He noted that Wayne still has to get through the rest of the Gold League, which includes Tommaso Ciampa, Orange Cassidy, Kevin Knight, and reigning Continental Champion Jon Moxley.

Refusing to let the early setback deter him, Bailey stated that the only way to truly lose is to lay down. Declaring that he does not give up, quit, or stop, Bailey vowed to rally and ultimately win the entire tournament.

🔄 המשך Big Boom AJ AEW contract update

Big Boom AJ is open to continuing his time with AEW following his recent match on the Full Gear pre-show.

AJ has wrestled a handful of matches since making his AEW debut in 2024, with the majority of them coming alongside QT Marshall. Following his recent match, in which he and Marshall teamed up with Darius Martin and Matt Sydal to defeat the Don Callis Family, AJ revealed in an interview that his contract is structured around specific appearances.

The deal runs through the end of February and required a minimum of four matches, which he has now completed across major events starting at Revolution, followed by Double or Nothing, All In, and most recently Full Gear. AJ stated that he is happy to throw in more matches and is actively talking with QT Marshall, Tony Khan, and Sonjay Dutt about a potential appearance at Worlds End, emphasizing that he loves it in AEW.

AJ noted that management appears happy with his work and the fans have embraced him and his tag team pairing, leaving him to feel there is no reason why the relationship cannot continue.

That recent Full Gear appearance notably came with last-minute travel drama when an FAA ground stop nearly kept him off the card before Tony Khan intervened with a private jet. Making it to Phoenix, Arizona, allowed AJ to extend his unbeaten run in the promotion, bringing his record to eight straight victories in both singles and tag team competition since returning.
