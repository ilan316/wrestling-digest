🔥 Hangman Page, Brodido, and Bandido win AEW World Trios Championship at WrestleDream

Hangman Adam Page and Brodido, consisting of Brody King and Bandido, regained the AEW World Trios Championship at AEW WrestleDream in Orlando, Florida. They defeated Swerve Strickland and The New Level, featuring former WWE stars Kofi Kingston and Xavier Woods now going by Kofi and Austin Creed, in a grueling two-out-of-three falls match. The New Level and Strickland had entered the event with immense momentum, having successfully defended their championships since winning them in their debut on their first night together at AEW All In: London back in August after coming out last in a Trios Roulette Royal, followed by a successful defense at All Out. However, their fierce rivalry with Page, King, and Bandido proved to be a massive challenge, bringing their 48-day title reign to an end.

The match started with Austin Creed and Bandido before the action shifted to Page and Strickland, whose long-standing bad blood remained a major focal point. Page had been tripped by Strickland from outside the ring, leading to a brawl where members of both teams tried to break things up, though Brody King joined in attacking Strickland until separated, with Page acting as a rare voice of reason. The defending champions soon took control. Creed helped Kofi hit Bandido with a combination of a leg drop and an elbow drop, also known as the Midnight Hour, leaving Bandido in trouble. Kofi pinned Bandido to give his team the first fall. Strickland tried to extend the lead by hitting Bandido with a Swerve Stomp, but Page broke up the cover to start another brawl.

The second phase saw Brodido and Page mount their comeback. King cannonballed Creed and Kofi in the corner, and Page hit Strickland with a clothesline and a Buckshot Lariat, though Strickland avoided the initial impact and was later pulled out of the ring before Page could pin him. Page eventually caught Strickland with a Dead Eye, while King helped Bandido hit a standing 450 splash and monkey-flipped Bandido into a senton on Creed. Bandido pinned Austin Creed to tie the match at one fall each, setting up everything for the final frame.

With the score even, both teams traded big moves inside and outside the ring, with Kofi and Creed attacking opponents near the barricades. Bandido hit an avalanche flipping bodyslam, also described as a Spanish Fly, on both Kofi and Creed. Strickland tried to shift momentum by going after Page, but the match ultimately came down to Bandido and Strickland. Strickland attempted to put Bandido away with a JML Driver or Big Pressure, but Bandido escaped, reversed the attempt, and rolled him up for the three-count to secure the final fall and capture the titles.

Following the match, major drama unfolded as Strickland refused to hand his title belt over to Page, arguing his opponents did not deserve the win and demanding The New Level keep theirs from King and Bandido. The New Level ignored him, respectfully handing their titles over to Brody King and Bandido. Swerve and Hangman shared a tense staredown before Strickland dropped his title in front of Page, bailing and walking out alone without Kofi and Creed. Analysts note that keeping The New Level strong in defeat protects them for a potential future dream match against the Young Bucks in the tag team division, while Strickland's frustration hints at the next fiery chapter in his ongoing rivalry with Hangman Page.

🔥 Will Ospreay retains AEW World Championship in Clockwork Carnage match at WrestleDream

Will Ospreay survived a chaotic Clockwork Carnage match at AEW WrestleDream to retain the AEW World Championship. Adam Copeland came close to pulling off a storybook victory, but Ospreay stole the win at the last possible moment. The eight-man match featured Will Ospreay, Konosuke Takeshita, Speedball Mike Bailey, Ricochet, Darby Allin, Kazuchika Okada, Adam Copeland, and Samoa Joe.

Ospreay drew the first entrance to ensure he would have to battle all seven opponents to defend his title, while Konosuke Takeshita was the second entrant. They had some exchanges before Ospreay sent Takeshita outside with a hurricanrana and followed up with a slingshot crossbody. Speedball Mike Bailey entered third as Ospreay's United Empire teammate and briefly teamed up with the champion against Takeshita, but the latter soon fought back. Ricochet was the fourth entrant and initially looked ready to work with Takeshita, though that alliance did not last. Ricochet tried to catch him with a roll-up but paid for it with a forearm. Darby Allin entered fifth as the TNT Champion and wasted no time, using his skateboard to attack Ricochet before setting up a table at ringside. Allin then hit several Code Reds, while Takeshita took a Coffin Drop on the floor.

Kazuchika Okada entered sixth as the International Champion. After several close calls, Takeshita eliminated Bailey with a Blue Thunder Bomb followed by Raging Fire to ensure he would not progress to the second stage. Adam Copeland entered seventh as the next elimination occurred, when Okada stepped in with a Rainmaker to eliminate Takeshita. Samoa Joe then entered as the final competitor, getting the second stage of the bout started where falls are scored for 15 minutes akin to an Iron Man match, and every pinfall or submission earned one point.

Joe planted Allin with a Muscle Buster to earn the first point of the scoring period. Ricochet quickly pinned Allin to score a point of his own after clearing Joe from the ring. Okada then caught Copeland with a Rainmaker and stole a rare pinfall. Ospreay joined the scoreboard by pinning Ricochet with a Styles Clash, but Allin answered with a Coffin Drop to pin the champion. Allin then caught Copeland with a La Magistral cradle to earn another point, taking the lead. Ospreay responded with a standing Hidden Blade to pin Allin and earn his second point. Ricochet continued to show off his aerial moves, however, Copeland stopped him with a Spear that sent him through the ropes and into the table that had been set up earlier at ringside.

Ospreay tried several times to catch Joe with an OsCutter, but Joe kept avoiding the move. The Samoan Submission Machine eventually locked in the Coquina Clutch, and though Ospreay fought hard to escape, he had no choice but to tap out, earning Joe his second point.

With three minutes left, Copeland changed the match by hitting Joe with a Spear and pinning him for a point, having previously been shut out. He followed that up with another Spear to Ricochet, earning his second point with just over a minute remaining. Ospreay tried to keep up with Copeland, and the champion landed a kick, looking ready to hit the Hidden Blade before Copeland countered with a DDT. Both kept fighting while Okada remained a threat. Copeland eventually landed a Spear on Okada and went for the cover, looking like he might steal the contest entirely. Just as the referee began counting, Ospreay hit Copeland with a Hidden Blade and pinned him with only four seconds left on the clock. That pinfall gave Ospreay his third point, enough to beat Copeland, Joe, and Allin, who finished with two points each, allowing Ospreay to retain the title despite being pinned and submitting earlier in the match.

In the aftermath, tensions flared between Ospreay and Copeland, who had only recently announced he would be calling it quits on his in-ring career next year. After an initial intense exchange where Copeland confronted and shoved the champion, Copeland ultimately showed respect, shook hands with the champion, pulled him closer to indicate he would be coming back for the World title, and walked out while Ospreay celebrated with his belt.

🔥 Jay White returns to ring action during AEW WrestleDream Tailgate Brawl

AEW fans received a massive surprise at WrestleDream when Jay White made his unexpected return to the ring during the Tailgate Brawl pre-show on TNT and HBO Max. The Bang Bang Gang leader had been expected to miss several weeks or up to two months of action after suffering an injury during an eight-man tag team match against The Death Riders at the All Out Tailgate Brawl. The injury was especially frustrating because White had only recently returned after spending more than a year away from the ring. He was originally scheduled to compete in the marquee Clockwork Carnage match at WrestleDream, but injuries to both White and Christian Cage caused plans to change, allowing Adam Copeland to take White's spot in the match against Samoa Joe, Kazuchika Okada, Ricochet, Konosuke Takeshita, Mike Bailey, Darby Allin, and AEW World Champion Will Ospreay.

Despite initial expectations that he would be sidelined for over a month, White beat the prognosis and appeared alongside his teammates to a massive pop. White teamed up with The Bang Bang Gang, represented by Austin and Colten Gunn, and The Conglomeration, consisting of Orange Cassidy, Roderick Strong, and Kyle O’Reilly. They faced a combined team of The Death Riders, represented by Wheeler Yuta, Claudio Castagnoli, Gabe Kidd, and Daniel Garcia, and The Dogs, represented by David Finlay and Clark Connors. Willow Nightingale was also at ringside to help fend off Marina Shafir, though Shafir still managed to assist her team during the chaos.

During the 12-man tag team match, action was non-stop as both sides traded fierce attacks. White made an immediate impact by taking the fight to his opponents, hitting a Dragon Screw on Castagnoli and dropping Finlay with a DDT for a scintillating highlight before attempting a pin that Finlay kicked out of. Throughout the bout, Claudio Castagnoli gave Orange Cassidy the Giant Swing for nearly 40 revolutions, and The Gunns thought they had the match won with a 3:10 to Yuma. However, late in the match, Castagnoli returned to help Wheeler Yuta hit a decisive high-impact move, allowing The Death Riders and The Dogs to score the pinfall victory over Colten Gunn. The fight continued well after the bell had rung, meaning White's exciting return ultimately ended in defeat.

White was not the only star to receive positive injury news during the WrestleDream festivities. During The Buy-In pre-show, Renee Paquette, Jeff Jarrett, and R.J. City welcomed former AEW TBS Champion Maya World for an interview, where she officially announced that she has been medically cleared to return to the ring. World had been out of action since September, with her most recent match taking place on the September 9 episode of AEW Dynamite, following a 35-day title reign that ended at All In when Britt Baker interfered and cost her the championship. Although World has been cleared to compete, no official date has been announced for her return.

In addition to the chaotic 12-man tag, the WrestleDream Tailgate Brawl featured several other notable matches. In the opening contest, Divine Dominion picked up a hard-fought win over The Sisters of Sin. Megan Bayne and Lena Kross worked over Skye Blue for the majority of the match, and once Skye tagged in Julia Hart, the tide turned briefly before Bayne used her sheer strength to German suplex both Sisters of Sin at once and eventually isolated Hart, finishing the job with Heaven and Hell to secure the victory. Finally, the Don Callis Family had a quick night at the office as Josh Alexander, Jake Doyle, and Wardlow made light work of Alex Breeze, Rhys Maddox, and Papi D. The underdogs stood no chance once Wardlow conducted his Powerbomb Symphony on all three opponents, easily earning the win for his team.

🔥 Nick Wayne defeats Chris Jericho at AEW WrestleDream 2026

Nick Wayne has finally settled his bitter rivalry with Chris Jericho, and their trilogy ended with another twist at AEW WrestleDream 2026. The Prodigy defeated the veteran for the second time in a row on October 17, winning their three-match series 2-1. Both stars entered the pay-per-view with one win each, making their third singles match the decisive rubber match, with Jericho having previously promised to apologize to Wayne if he lost.

Chris Jericho wasted no time showing his aggressive side, beating Wayne from pillar to post in the early going. He spat on Wayne right after the opening bell, knocked him down with a right hand, dumped Wayne on the steel steps, and sent him into the crowd. Jericho was so proud of his early work that he gave some Mardi Gras beads to Paul Wight on commentary and even used a photographer's camera to take a selfie. He then hit a dropkick that sent Wayne to the floor and drove him into the barricade.

Back in the ring, Jericho hit a bulldog before avoiding Wayne attempted Swanton Bomb by moving toward the ropes, then climbed to the top turnbuckle and landed a Frog Splash. Wayne fought back with an enziguri and a Moonsault to the outside after pushing Jericho off the top rope, but Jericho quickly regained control, hitting a big lariat after missing the Judas Effect, avoiding a kick to hit a Codebreaker for a near fall, and attempting a Lionsault.

The action escalated as Jericho hit a Crossbody to the outside and later countered an avalanche hurricanrana with a powerbomb for a two count, transitioning it into the Walls of Jericho. Wayne managed to crawl toward the ropes and forced a break, rolling to the outside to grab a bottle of water. Wayne spat the water into Jericho eyes, giving himself an opening to hit a Sliced Bread on the apron and a 450 Splash back in the ring, though Jericho kicked out of the pin attempt.

Wayne followed up with a modified Wayne World which Jericho kicked out of at a count of one, firing up the veteran to lock in the Walls of Jericho again. When Wayne escaped, Jericho launched him face-first into the top turnbuckle and followed with a German suplex and a catapult into the corner. With both men looking for any possible advantage, Wayne distracted the referee and attempted a low blow, but Jericho came prepared and revealed a protective cup hidden inside his tights.

The former AEW World Champion used the cup to hit Wayne. The referee eventually took the cup away, and Jericho used the chance to deliver a low blow of his own for good measure. The veteran looked ready to finish things with the Judas Effect, but Wayne suddenly collapsed before the move could be executed. When Jericho went to pick Wayne up, Wayne ducked a subsequent attack and caught him with a flash roll-up. The referee counted to three, giving Wayne the decisive victory.

After the match, Jericho recovered on the outside while Wayne took a microphone and proudly boasted about his win to the live crowd, cementing a major milestone in his young career. The two first met on the September 16 edition of Dynamite where Jericho defeated Wayne and offered a handshake, only to be met with a low blow from Wayne. Their second match took place at Grand Slam France, where Wayne scored a win after low blowing Jericho and pinning him following a superkick and his Wayne World finish.
