🔥 Eddie Kingston Double Champion and Continental Cup

Eddie Kingston's comeback story took its first major step forward when he defeated Jake Doyle in the main event of AEW Collision on August 8, 2026. Kingston's victory marked his first appearance on AEW programming in more than six months, his previous match coming in a parking lot fight on the February 7 episode of Collision. During his time away from AEW television, Kingston had been working the independent scene and performing frequently for Ring of Honor, teaming with Ortiz in various matches including a challenge for the ROH World Six-Man Tag Team titles at Supercard Showdown on May 9 and a recent victory over Rocky Romero and Trent Beretta at the July 20 tapings in Jacksonville, Florida.

In his Continental Challenge Cup opening round match, Kingston took considerable punishment from Doyle, who carries impressive credentials despite being viewed as a fall guy for the Callis Family. Doyle boasts notable victories including a win over "Speedball" Mike Bailey. The contest was a slugfest that appeared to be heading in Doyle's favor, but Kingston mounted a remarkable comeback. When the momentum seemed to be shifting against him, Kingston uncorked a pair of spinning back fists to stun the burly brawler before slapping on the dragon sleeper for the submission victory. The comeback nature of the win fit perfectly with the narrative of Kingston's return.

Following his victory, Kingston appeared in an AEW digital exclusive where he addressed his extended absence from AEW television and explained the inspiration behind his tournament run. Kingston revealed that his time away was his own doing, though he remained cryptic about the specific reasons. He emphasized that he had genuinely missed the fans and their energy, explaining that their enthusiasm drives him to keep fighting when he doesn't want to continue. Kingston recalled meeting fans on the independent circuit who asked when he would return to television and told them that they inspired him, even though many didn't believe his sincerity at the time.

Kingston personally requested to Tony Khan that he be included in the Continental Challenge Cup tournament, demonstrating his confidence in his ability to compete at the highest level. Much like his previous run in the Continental Classic where he became a triple champion, Kingston has his sights set on winning the inaugural Continental Challenge Cup. He dedicated his entire tournament run to the fans who supported him during his absence and the people he met while working throughout various promotions. In an emotional statement, Kingston declared: "This tournament, this cup, is for every single person that I met in all the promotions that I worked for who came up to me and said, 'I can't wait to see you back on TV again.' This is all for you. I fight for you in this tournament, for you."

The Continental Challenge Cup operates on a blind draw system with no predetermined bracket. Once all first-round matches are completed, the quarterfinal matchups will be announced randomly. Kingston's advancement to the second round was accompanied by victories from Claudio Castagnoli and Orange Cassidy on the same episode. Castagnoli defeated Ace Austin after escaping a triangle choke counter to the big swing and hitting a pop-up uppercut for the victory. Orange Cassidy defeated Matt Sydal in what was described as a chess match of movement, dodging Sydal's shooting star press and countering with the Beach Break for the win.

Several notable first-round matches remain on the schedule. Jay White faces David Finlay on Dynamite on August 12, while Jon Moxley, the current AEW World Champion, faces Jack Perry in a championship match on the same show. Roderick Strong competes against Kyle O'Reilly on Collision on August 15, with Brian Cage facing Hechicero on the same card. Katsuyori Shibata battles Nigel McGuinness in the final first-round match, also scheduled for Collision on August 15.

Kingston's career trajectory before his injury and absence was remarkable. He had previously peaked as a double champion, simultaneously holding the ROH World Championship and the NJPW Strong Openweight Championship. He then risked everything by participating in the first Continental Classic tournament, ultimately becoming the inaugural AEW Continental Champion and a triple champion. That reign proved to be short-lived as Kingston dropped all three titles in the following months. His comeback path has been gradual, with a handful of matches in AEW while working off rust primarily in ROH.

The narrative around Kingston's tournament run presents a genuine Cinderella story for fans. After an extended absence, a serious leg injury that sidelined him for 16 months, and a low-key comeback period, Kingston is positioned to achieve a remarkable redemption. The possibility of Kingston winning the Continental Challenge Cup and becoming the first winner of the inaugural tournament would provide a perfect full-circle moment for the Mad King, especially given that the final will take place at All In, a significant AEW event.

Chris Jericho New Opportunity in AEW

Chris Jericho has nearly done it all in AEW. He is the inaugural world champion. The Ocho won the ROH world championship twice. Jericho is even a former FTW champion. He wrestled in unique matches such as Blood & Guts, Stadium Stampede, and the Mimosa Mayhem Match. The Demo God also shared the screen with MJF for a song and dance number.

This past Wednesday at Grand Slam Mexico, Jericho wrestled as Corazón de León, and he helped Darby Allin pin Kevin Knight for the win. Jericho intercepted Knight's coast to coast dropkick with the Judas Effect elbow strike. Allin finished the job with the Coffin Drop.

Little did Jericho know at the time that his efforts in Arena Mexico would lead to an opportunity to achieve something that he's never done before in AEW. Knight wants payback on Jericho, so he is offering a TNT Championship bout for Dynamite. Jericho has never reigned with that title belt before. AEW officially booked the match for Wednesday, August 12 in Las Vegas.

The story for this match unfolded on Collision. Allin wants another crack at Knight for the TNT title at All In, and he wants a special stipulation. Falls Count Anywhere. The motivation for that stipulation is that Knight attacked him outside the ring when this feud started.

Knight wasn't interested in addressing Allin's request, but he did have other business on his mind. Knight blamed the Grand Slam loss on Jericho, so he wants a chance to wipe that blemish off his record.

On the surface, it seems like Jericho's goose is cooked before it is plucked. AEW is already making a strong push for Knight versus Allin at All In. Well, never say never. The feud with Allin doesn't demand the TNT Championship, so it could be possible that Jericho scores the upset and celebrates with a little bit of the bubbly.

Regarding his future in wrestling, Jericho remains uncommitted to any specific retirement timeline. Over the past two years, several of the last biggest names of the previous wrestling era retired one after the other, from John Cena to AJ Styles to Brock Lesnar. Despite this, Chris Jericho, who debuted long before any of them, is still actively competing in AEW.

When compared to Terry Funk, who similarly had a lengthy career, and asked how many more years he thinks he might have left, Jericho explained his perspective. "I mean, I don't really look at it like that, man. I don't really put a timeline on it," Jericho said.

Jericho pointed to his AEW Redemption match against Tommaso Ciampa, noting that it was probably one of the best matches on the show based on how fans reacted. "Why would I want to step away from that? I still enjoy doing it, and still can do it at a level that's still show-stealing, you know, possibilities," he exclaimed.

Jericho said he might have a run that lasts another week or goes another three years. "I mean, I don't want to do it forever, but as long as I feel that I'm still, you know, happy with the work that I'm doing and not second-guessing myself, then I'll continue to do it," he proclaimed.

The veteran also pointed out that when you're in any kind of show business for a long time, there will always be people who want to see you gone. "Keep going as long as you can still be creatively fulfilled and as long as you still are putting out great work. I feel great ... I feel sore, but in the way that I've felt sore for 35 years. It's just the sign of a good night's work," Jericho said.

AEW Referee Aubrey Edwards and Match Stipulations

AEW Referee Aubrey Edwards Names Hardest Match Stipulation To Prepare For

For as hard as wrestlers work inside the ring, referees work just as hard to keep the match going, and in AEW, Aubrey Edwards has worked harder than most. The first female referee to call a main event match on both television and on pay-per-view, Edwards has called some of the biggest moments in AEW history, but some matches are a lot more complicated than others, and that makes her life very difficult.

During a recent interview with Pro Wrestling Proverbs, Edwards was asked which AEW stipulation was the hardest to prepare for, to which she stated that the most dangerous match in the company is easily the most difficult. "Blood and Guts is probably the hardest one because you've got two rings that are stuck together, the cage around it. The cage is not too big of a problem, we've sort of adapted what Blood and Guts was every year. So the first year we didn't have referees inside the cage, so yelling time cues from 20 feet away to somebody is really difficult and you're out going 'WE HAVE FIVE MINUTES LEFT!' We're trying not to break the fourth wall completely."

There is one more thing that Edwards revealed about the Blood and Guts match, and that's something in the bout she was not prepared for the first time around. If she didn't have a strong stomach, she might have never been fully prepared for it. "The other thing that you're not prepared for is the smell. The amount of blood, because it's literally in the title Blood and Guts, but blood smells a lot like iron. So it's just like 'Where did all those pennies come from?' They're just kind of not used to it."

Hurt Syndicate Business Offer in AEW

The Hurt Syndicate, comprised of Bobby Lashley, Shelton Benjamin, and MVP, have been waiting on the sidelines to monetize their services ever since MVP announced the group was open for business to hurt people in exchange for payment. For six long weeks, there were no takers on their violent offerings, leaving the three men seemingly without employment opportunities despite their obvious financial comfort.

That drought finally ended on Saturday night during AEW Collision on August 8, 2026, when an unexpected suitor came calling. Shane Taylor, the proprietor of Shane Taylor Promotions, approached the Hurt Syndicate with a business proposal that included a substantial advance in the form of an envelope filled with 100-dollar bills. The monetary incentive was enough to get MVP's attention, as he indicated openness to hearing what STP had in mind.

The nature of Taylor's proposal remains somewhat mysterious at this point. Shane Taylor Promotions already boasts eight members on its roster, so the need for additional muscle might seem unnecessary at first glance. However, there are several plausible scenarios that could explain why STP would invest in the Hurt Syndicate's services.

One possibility is that Taylor has set his sights on the AEW trios titles currently held by the Good, the Bad, and the Ugly, an alliance featuring Hangman Page, Brody King, and Bandido. To mount a credible challenge against such a formidable unit, STP would certainly benefit from the kind of destructive power that Lashley, Benjamin, and MVP could provide. Another intriguing angle involves the Casino Gauntlet match scheduled for All In, where a victory could earn the winners a world title shot. If STP members are slated to compete in that match, hiring the Hurt Syndicate could give them a significant advantage in securing the gold.

The exact details of this partnership will likely unfold in the coming weeks as the storyline develops. What remains clear is that the Hurt Syndicate's job search has ended, and they have finally found their first paying customers in Shane Taylor Promotions.

🔥 Alex Wright WCW and WWE History

Alex Wright's journey through WCW and his rejection of WWE opportunities after the company's closure reveals a wrestling career shaped by financial hardship, creative resistance, and the toll of extended injury work.

Wright moved to the United States at just 18 years old with hopes that WCW would launch his career. Instead, he found himself sitting at the Power Plant for months without payment. Despite having a contract, the company left him in significant financial distress, roughly half a year without pay. During this period, Wright lived alone in an airport hotel while his fiancée remained in Germany, accumulating debt to family and friends.

The isolation and frustration took a physical toll on Wright. He drank heavily and gained approximately 40 pounds before Ric Flair intervened, telling him he needed to get back into shape if WCW was going to do anything with him. Wright responded by training twice a day and dropping from around 250 pounds to 190 pounds. Even after transforming himself into television shape, WCW still wasn't paying him. This final indignity prompted Wright to take action.

Unable to maintain professional politeness any longer, Wright made an unannounced trip to the CNN building in Atlanta where WCW's offices were located. He rushed directly into Eric Bischoff's office without an appointment and delivered an ultimatum. "I got really pissed and I went down to the CNN building in Atlanta, had no appointment and just rushed into the office from Eric Bischoff and said, 'Hey, listen Eric, I can lose money in Germany also. I don't have to be in the States for that. So either you pay me or I'm gone.'" Wright believed this aggressive confrontation impressed Bischoff. "I think that really impressed him because from that moment on they paid me."

However, the financial struggles continued despite finally receiving payment. Road expenses remained substantial, and Wright had to make difficult choices to stretch his limited funds. He slept on other wrestlers' hotel floors and ate only once a day to conserve money. Eventually, Wright did become a regular on WCW television, though his path to success was considerably harder than his polished on-screen character suggested.

One of the most recognizable aspects of Alex Wright's WCW career was his signature dancing entrance. Yet Wright initially fought strongly against this gimmick. WCW management first noticed his dance moves when they saw him dancing to techno music at a nightclub. Wright was simply dancing the way he normally would in Germany, but management immediately recognized its potential television value.

"I just danced like I would dance in a dance club in Germany to techno music, and they said, 'What's that? That's different.' I said, 'Well, that's how I dance in Germany.' He said, 'Hey, you have to do that in the ring.' And I said, 'No, I'm not going to do that.'"

Wright came from a traditional wrestling background and rejected the idea of building his character around dancing. He was brought up in pure wrestling with minimal entertainment emphasis. WCW pushed ahead with the concept regardless of his objections, and Wright now acknowledges the company was correct in its judgment.

Three decades later, Wright credits the dance with making him instantly recognizable and particularly popular with younger fans and women. The gimmick ensured his longevity in people's memories. "I'm very thankful that people still remember me, and that's one big part. The dance was different. It made me very popular and everybody knew me very quickly."

However, Wright also acknowledged a significant downside to the dancing gimmick. Some fans never looked past the dancing to appreciate his actual wrestling abilities. "On the other hand, I had a hard time with some fans because they never gave me a chance and looked past the dancing and looked at my wrestling abilities." Despite this criticism, the thing Wright originally resisted has become the primary reason fans remember him decades after his WCW departure.

When WCW shut down in 2001, WWE showed interest in signing Wright. The company approached him not once but twice with contract offers. Both times, Wright turned them down. His reasons stemmed from the physical and mental toll of his WCW years. At the time, Wright remained under a Time Warner contract, and WWE's buyout offer was not financially appealing.

More importantly, Wright knew he was not in the right condition to give his best effort to another major promotion. "WWE approached me twice and asked me if I'm interested, if I want to come to them. But I didn't feel ready at that moment because I knew I can't give 100% for the promotion, I can't give 100% for the fans and I can't give 100% for myself. That wouldn't be fair for any of them to step in the ring because I couldn't perform."

Years of working through injuries in WCW had left Wright completely drained. He had wrestled continuously for many years while carrying numerous injuries, and this sustained physical punishment combined with the years of financial and professional instability had exhausted him mentally as well.

"At that point, I was really mentally and physically burned out because I was straight with WCW. I wrestled many, many years and I had a lot of injuries, and I kept wrestling with a lot of injuries also."

When WWE contacted him again, Wright politely but firmly declined: "I said, 'I'm very honored and thank you very much, but it's not the right time for me.'"

What makes Wright's decision even more significant is what WWE had apparently envisioned for him. During one of their approaches, WWE proposed pairing Wright with Hulk Hogan in a storyline program. For a wrestler in Wright's position, such an opportunity would normally represent a career-defining moment. Working with one of professional wrestling's biggest names could have revitalized his career and provided financial security.

Nevertheless, even the prospect of facing Hogan was insufficient to change Wright's mind. He recognized the significance of what WWE was proposing: "Well, one time they said, 'Well, we would like to do a story with you and Hogan.' So that would have been actually a very big deal."

Instead of accepting the offer, Wright returned to Germany. He took time away from wrestling to recover both physically and mentally. Eventually, he rebuilt his connection to the wrestling business on his own terms, allowing himself to heal before re-engaging with the industry that had demanded so much from him.

Former seven-time TNA Knockouts Champion Gail Kim recently shared her perspective on the six-sided ring during an appearance on "My World w/ Jeff Jarrett," offering a balanced comparison between the unique ring and the traditional four-sided setup.
