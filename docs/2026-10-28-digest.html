🔥 Max Caster provides update on Anthony Bowens' AEW status and his own future

Max Caster has departed AEW, but his former Acclaimed partner Anthony Bowens is still with the promotion. Both Bowens and Caster had their AEW contracts originally set to expire at the start of October 2026. While Caster has left the company and is no longer included on the AEW website roster page, Bowens remains listed. Caster revealed during a virtual signing that Bowens still works for AEW, noting that he is still there, though he did not want to speak too much for his former partner or confirm if a new deal has been signed or what the resolution of Bowens contract situation was. Bowens was recently spotted posting a photo of himself at the NJPW Dojo in California. As The Acclaimed, Caster and Bowens were once Tag Team Champions for AEW, but they no longer team together.

Casters post AEW career took a turbulent turn when he debuted for TNA in a loss to Moose at Bound for Glory on October 11. During his entrance, Caster performed a trademark freestyle rap that included a controversial line about the ongoing violence in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, rapping that this is the firing squad, dropping bombs without needing to fly to Riyadh. By the end of the following day, TNA announced it was severing ties with Caster. Following the fallout, Caster issued a public apology and made a surprise appearance at Awesome Championship Wrestling in Poughkeepsie, New York, where he told fans he did not know what his next move was and would be stepping away from the ring for an extended period of time to go home, go to therapy, and get it right.

Despite currently turning down all wrestling bookings and planning an undefined absence, Caster did not fully close the door on eventually returning to AEW. During his virtual signing, which he noted was agreed to a couple of months ago and fulfilled despite taking a break, he addressed the possibility of returning by stating that it is wrestling and you never say never, though reiterating that he is saying no to wrestling bookings right now. Caster also took time to praise AEW boss Tony Khan, noting that he is still living off AEW money and commending Khan for how he supports wrestlers and helps them make a living. Caster previously held both World Tag Team Championship and World Trios Championship gold during his tenure with the promotion.

Kyle Fletcher credits The Miz as an in-ring influence

AEW star Kyle Fletcher has made a name for himself in the ring, but he also recognizes the importance of mastering the microphone. When it comes to promo skills, Fletcher looks to an unlikely source for inspiration in WWE veteran The Miz.

During a recent podcast interview, Fletcher explained how the verbal expertise of The Miz has directly influenced his current work in AEW. Fletcher noted how much he admires how Miz interacts with the crowd, pointing out that it goes beyond simply turning to the audience and yelling at them. While acknowledging that some performers succeed by memorizing heavily scripted material, Fletcher highlighted that top stars like The Miz and John Cena often go out to the ring without knowing everything they are going to say, letting their promos be entirely impacted by how the crowd reacts and what is happening in the moment. Fletcher stated that this is something he has definitely looked at, been inspired by, and tried to incorporate into his own work.

As a performer, Fletcher admitted that overthinking comes quite naturally to him, particularly regarding promos, which is why some wrestlers choose to memorize their material in-depth ahead of time. Seeing the successful spontaneity of The Miz has inspired Fletcher to do the exact opposite and instead trust in his own speaking abilities.

The Miz has cut a number of impassioned promos during his 20-plus year WWE career, including his now infamous firestorm on Talking Smack in 2016. Since turning heel, shaving his head, and joining The Don Callis Family in 2024, Fletcher has delivered speeches of a similar style while also making them his own.

AEW wrestlers reportedly resisting taking TV losses

A new backstage report claims that certain talent in AEW have pushed back on taking pins on television, with some wrestlers apparently happier getting pushed in Ring of Honor than showing up on Dynamite just to lose.

Speaking during a Self Made Session, Self Made Pro claimed this is not just a case of one or two wrestlers being difficult about doing jobs. According to the report, there are plenty of people hanging around the AEW midcard who are not exactly excited about losing on television every week. They are not the only people that do not love to job. There are plenty of people in AEW populating your favorite midcard that do not like losing on TV every week.

Things apparently get even more interesting inside the Don Callis Family. Self Made Pro claimed there are members of the group who have not won a single match this year but have still turned down taking pins on television. There are people in the Don Callis Family who have won zero matches this year that have also turned down jobbing and eating pins on TV.

While nobody was named, this attitude has also helped keep certain wrestlers over in Ring of Honor. Apparently, some would rather actually get pushed there than pop up on Dynamite just to put somebody else over. There are guys in Ring of Honor mainly because they would rather be on Ring of Honor pushed than jobbing on Dynamite.

Self Made Pro also argued that it is pretty easy for established main eventers like Will Ospreay and Hangman Page to be team players. Those guys already know one loss is not going to suddenly knock them down the card. There is no issues when it comes to being team players when you are Will Ospreay or Hangman Page, because they are main eventers. They are gonna be okay. The no-job people in AEW, or who pout about jobbing, it is easier to be all about the team when your spot is solidified.

For somebody fighting just to keep their spot, though, taking another loss can apparently feel a whole lot different. Self Made Pro said some wrestlers are naturally thinking about their own future before worrying about whether losing makes for a great angle. But when you are worried about your own stock in AEW, it becomes, what happens to me, rather than, like, oh, this is a great angle, because the priority is yourself, because you do not know what is gonna happen to yourself, and there are plenty of people like that.

That concern makes a little more sense with another recent report claiming some lower-card AEW contracts could simply be allowed to expire rather than being renewed. If wrestlers already are not sure where they stand, apparently some of them are not too interested in making that situation worse by becoming the person everybody beats on TV.

What do you make of the reported situation with some AEW talent behind the scenes, and how much control should wrestlers have over their own booking?

Bishop Kaun reveals original plans for AEW faction The Demand

Bishop Kaun recently shared the behind the scenes story of how he and Toa Liona secured their places in Ricochets AEW faction, The Demand, revealing that they were not part of the original plans for the group. During an appearance on The Certified Mark Show, Kaun explained that he and Liona essentially forced their hand by impressing Ricochet after he began teasing the formation of a new faction on AEW programming.

Kaun recalled seeing Ricochet cutting a promo about looking for people to start a group. He and Liona surveyed the landscape to figure out who might be in contention, but ultimately decided the spot belonged to them. Kaun explained that despite their years with AEW, they still feel they are fighting for respect, making every opportunity to showcase their abilities vital because their backs are always against the wall and they cannot afford to mess up.

That drive led them to target a specific match where Ricochet happened to be sitting at the commentary desk. Kaun stated they knew they had to show out for him and take the opportunity to insert themselves into the group. Their strategy worked immediately. Kaun recalled getting to the back after the match and being pulled aside by a producer who told them that Ricochet wanted them in his stable because they had impressed him by beating ass in the ring.

Perhaps the most interesting revelation came when Kaun suggested that two other wrestlers had initially been considered for the spots that he and Liona ultimately secured, noting that they simply stepped over them to take the positions. When asked if he knew the identities of those two wrestlers, Kaun offered an intriguing response by suggesting they might actually be in the group right now. While Kaun did not explicitly name them, The Demand currently maintains an on-screen alliance with Lethal Twist, the ROH World Six-Man Tag Team Champions consisting of Jay Lethal, Blake Christian, and Lee Johnson.

Cash Wheeler explains why FTR re-signed with AEW over returning to WWE

FTR have had plenty of chances to leave AEW and potentially make their way back to WWE, but Cash Wheeler says there is a pretty simple reason they have never pulled the trigger. For him, AEW still gives FTR things they would not necessarily get anywhere else. Speaking in a recent interview, Wheeler was asked why he and Dax Harwood have kept AEW as their home base since arriving in 2020 instead of bouncing between major promotions. Wheeler started with the obvious answer for a guy who has spent most of his career in a tag team, believing that AEW has the best tag team division in wrestling. But there is also something about being part of a company that is still figuring out how big it can become that appeals to him.

Just because it is the home of the best tag team division in the world, and it is a place where it is still so young and growing, it is exciting to be part of that as opposed to being part of something that is already a known entity, that is established, that is always going to be there no matter what, just because it has been part of pop culture now for sixty plus years. Wheeler never mentioned WWE by name there, but the comparison was pretty obvious. Instead of returning to a company that has been established for decades, he likes the idea of helping build something that is relatively new.

Whereas AEW, it is seven years old. It is in second or third grade, technically. It is brand new, and it is exciting to be part of that, to be building that up, and to see the growth the company has had since we debuted there in 2020 to now in 2026. Just to see how the TV show has grown, to see how the attendance has grown, to see how the fan base has grown, and to see new people discovering it.

There is another part of AEW existence Wheeler clearly values too. Having another major wrestling company around means more jobs and, perhaps even more importantly for wrestlers, another place they can negotiate with. And just knowing that it gives so many other people in our industry, not just the wrestlers, but people backstage, so many job opportunities now. And it helps give wrestlers leverage, honestly, which is not something we have had much of for the past twenty plus years that I have been in it.

Then there is the actual wrestling. Wheeler pointed to the number of tag teams FTR have been able to work with during their AEW run, while also noting that being with the company has not stopped them from wrestling elsewhere. FTR have been able to head to places like New Japan Pro-Wrestling and Mexico while still calling AEW home. There are tag teams across the board that you cannot find anywhere else. And then you get a chance, like we have done with our AEW tenure, to go wrestle in New Japan Pro-Wrestling and work those tag teams, or go to Mexico and wrestle some of the luchadors down there.

For Wheeler, that combination pretty much kills any reason to start looking elsewhere. AEW gives FTR the tag team competition they want, the freedom to work outside the company and the chance to help build something that is still growing. It gives you so much freedom to go out and explore this wrestling world. It is just a no brainer for us. It is a win win for us and, I think, for the company. FTR have already spent six years watching AEW change around them, and Wheeler clearly still sees plenty left for them to do there. At this point, it sounds like being part of whatever the company becomes next is a bigger draw for him than going back to something they have already experienced.

QT Marshall acquires partial ownership of Rome Red Cats arena football team

QT Marshall has become a partial owner of the Rome Red Cats arena football team in Georgia. He joins the ownership group alongside Tripp Bennett, who is his business partner in One Fall Wrestling.

The team announced the ownership additions in an October release. Marshall and Bennett will support the existing leadership through marketing, sponsorships, community outreach, and business development.

Alongside his new sports ownership role, Marshall serves as All Elite Wrestling's senior vice president of creative coordination while also continuing his work with One Fall Wrestling.

The Red Cats will compete in the American Arena League in 2027. The Forum River Center, located in downtown Rome, will serve as the team's official home field.
